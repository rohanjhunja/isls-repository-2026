## Abstract

The concept of a “growth mindset” refers to the belief that one’s abilities and intelligence can be developed through effort, learning, and persistence. This systematic review examines the impact of digital games on fostering a growth mindset in pre-K-12 educational settings. Following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines, we analyzed 13 studies focused on digital games designed to enhance a growth mindset. Findings indicate that most games positively influence growth mindset along with other competencies (e.g., creativity and persistence), while a minority show mixed or negligible effects, often linked to the game design or context. The review identifies trends across various game genres, targeted skills, and geographical regions, reflecting a growing interest in growth mindset research. It suggests that digital games represent a valuable avenue for educational interventions which emphasize the need for thoughtful design informed by evidence on effective game features.

## 1. Introduction

The concept of a “growth mindset,” introduced by Stanford psychologist Carol Dweck, refers to the belief that one’s abilities and intelligence can be developed through effort, learning, and persistence. In contrast, a “fixed mindset” reflects the belief that abilities are innate and unchangeable (Dweck, 2006). Individuals with a growth mindset embrace challenges, view setbacks as opportunities for growth, and persist in the face of difficulty. On the other hand, those with a fixed mindset often avoid challenges and may feel defeated by failure, seeing it as a reflection of unchangeable limitations (Dweck, 2008). These mindsets align well with learners’ orientation: learning orientation which includes learners who enjoy learning new concepts and skills, and performance orientation which includes learners who prioritize performance (e.g., getting good grades) (Shute, 2008). The development of a growth mindset is especially important during the formative years of pre-K-12 education, as it promotes resilience, adaptability, and a love for learning—qualities essential for success both inside and outside the classroom (Dweck, 2006).

Early interventions to instill a growth mindset in students have proven highly beneficial, making pre-K-12 education a critical period for fostering this mindset. Numerous studies have explored strategies to cultivate growth mindsets in educational settings. For example, providing feedback that emphasizes effort, strategies, and progress rather than innate ability has been shown to be effective (Haimovitz & Dweck, 2017). Other evidence-based interventions include explicitly teaching students about the malleability of the brain (e.g., neuroplasticity), setting challenging yet achievable goals, and fostering classroom environments where mistakes are framed as valuable learning experiences (Boaler, 2013; Paunesku et al., 2015). These treatments have demonstrated positive effects on student motivation, engagement, and performance.

One promising approach to fostering a growth mindset is through digital games. Games offer engaging, interactive platforms that can reward effort, persistence, and problem-solving strategies, rather than only emphasizing performance outcomes. For instance, a game might reward players with virtual coins or progress points for persevering through a difficult level, regardless of their ultimate success. Such designs align closely with the principles of a growth mindset by reinforcing the value of effort and encouraging resilience in the face of challenges (Granic et al., 2014; Dweck, 2016).

### Current Study & Research Questions
Despite the growing interest in leveraging digital games to promote a growth mindset, no systematic review has yet synthesized the available research on this topic. This study aims to address that gap by systematically reviewing the literature on digital games designed to foster a growth mindset in students. Specifically, this review seeks to answer the following research questions:

- **RQ 1**: What are the trends in using digital games to improve a growth mindset?
- **RQ 2**: What competencies (i.e., knowledge, skills, and other attributes) are targeted by these studies?
- **RQ 3**: What are the effects of promoting a growth mindset through digital games on students’ outcomes?

## 2. Method

This systematic review was conducted according to PRISMA guidelines (Moher et al., 2009) to synthesize findings on the promotion of a growth mindset through digital games in K-12 educational settings. The objective was to explore how digital games may contribute to fostering a growth mindset among K-12 students and to analyze trends and methodologies in this area.

### Database Search & Strategy
To ensure comprehensive literature coverage, searches were conducted in multiple databases: **ERIC, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, ProQuest, Google Scholar, and Scopus**. Search strings combined terms for digital games, growth mindset (e.g., "growth mindset", "incremental theory"), and educational levels (Pre-K through 12th grade).

### Screening Process
The initial search retrieved 2,063 studies across databases. Covidence was used to screen studies. After removing duplicates, 1,342 unique records were screened. Two researchers independently reviewed titles and abstracts, followed by full-text review. Discrepancies were resolved via discussion and consensus. 13 studies were selected for detailed quality assessment.

### Quality Assessment
The Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP) checklist (Singh, 2013) and additional criteria from Pluye et al. (2009) were applied. Studies received ratings of high (>70%), medium (40–70%), or low (<40%). No study fell below 40%; all 13 studies were included in the final synthesis.

## 3. Results

### RQ 1: Trends in Using Digital Games to Improve Growth Mindset
The 13 included studies span 2014 to 2023 and reflect international interest, primarily based in the USA ($n=5$), Taiwan ($n=4$), European countries ($n=2$, UK and Ireland), and Malaysia ($n=1$). Participants ranged from 3rd to 12th grade, with elementary school students most frequently represented ($n=9$), followed by middle school ($n=3$) and mixed middle/high school ($n=1$).

Researchers came from education ($n=5$), computer science education ($n=5$), creativity ($n=2$), and digital media design ($n=1$). Three main game genres were explored:
1. **Puzzle games** ($n=6$): e.g., *Refraction*, *Selene*, *Tangram Treasure*
2. **Serious games** ($n=5$): e.g., *DGLC-A*, *Better Bites*, *Brainology*, *GLS-CM*
3. **Sandbox games** ($n=2$): e.g., *Treasure Hunt*, *Maze City*

### RQ 2: Targeted Competencies
The reviewed studies targeted a wide range of competencies across three dimensions:
- **Knowledge Development** ($n=8$): Understanding growth mindset principles, neuroplasticity, and subject-specific concepts in mathematics (e.g., *Refraction*) or programming (*Maze City*).
- **Skills** ($n=11$): Resilience and perseverance ($n=7$), problem-solving ($n=5$), collaborative group tasks ($n=3$), and health decision-making ($n=1$).
- **Socio-Emotional Attributes** ($n=9$): Self-efficacy ($n=6$), hope beliefs ($n=2$), and emotional engagement and regulation ($n=4$).

---

### RQ 3: Effects of Promoting Growth Mindset on Students’ Outcomes
Of the 13 studies:
- **9 Studies Reported Positive Effects**:
  - *Yeh (2023a, 2023b)* and *Ting & Yeh (2024)* showed gains in creativity self-efficacy, self-determination, and hope beliefs through scaffolded tasks and mastery experiences.
  - *Yang & Xiao (2023)* showed that virtual escape rooms increased collaboration, peer problem-solving, and willingness to embrace challenges.
  - *White & McCoy (2019)* demonstrated significant improvements in math attitudes and achievement through collaborative game-based problem-solving.
  - *O'Rourke et al. (2014)* proved that "Brain Points" incentives motivated effort and strategic iteration in puzzle games, particularly among struggling learners.
  - *Park et al. (2017)* found social robots modeling growth mindset increased student perseverance.
  - *Gorman et al. (2022)* observed higher persistence during coding tasks in disadvantaged schools.
  - *Raffali & Asraf (2023)* highlighted better health decision-making through narrative game choices.
- **4 Studies Reported Mixed ($n=1$) or Non-Significant ($n=3$) Effects**:
  - *O'Rourke et al. (2016)* observed student disengagement when narrative messaging was not well-calibrated.
  - *Leach (2016)* found no significant gains among gifted students, suggesting advanced learners require more complex challenges.
  - *Dowey (2017)* observed no significant differences between whole-school versus intensive mindset interventions in English or Math effort without tailored game components.
  - *Ting & Yeh (2024)* noted that while fixed mindset beliefs decreased, growth creativity mindsets did not show immediate statistically significant shifts, indicating overcoming fixed beliefs may precede cultivating growth-oriented ones.

## 4. Discussion & Implications

1. **Scarcity of Dedicated Interventions**: Despite extensive conceptual discourse on growth mindset and games, only 13 empirical studies rigorously evaluated games specifically designed to foster growth mindsets in Pre-K–12.
2. **Interdisciplinary Breadth**: The domain spans education, computer science, and media design, demonstrating broad applicability across mathematics, coding, health, and creativity.
3. **Design Considerations**: Game mechanics like "Brain Points," scaffolded mastery experiences, and formative feedback on effort rather than outcome are crucial. Generic or uncalibrated interventions risk disengagement or negligible effects.

---

## 5. Conclusion

Growth mindset is foundational for lifelong learning and resilience. Digital games represent an impactful vehicle for cultivating growth mindsets in Pre-K–12 learners when mechanics explicitly reward effort, strategic iteration, and learning from failure. This paper highlights both the promising empirical base and the substantial opportunity for learning scientists to design and evaluate targeted game interventions.
