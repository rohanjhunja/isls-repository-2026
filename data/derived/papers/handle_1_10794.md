# A Comparison Between AI and Human Evaluation with a Focus on Generative AI

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study aims to explore the utility of generative AI in providing formative assessment and feedback. Using data from 43 learners in an instructional technology class, we assessed generative AI’s evaluative indices and feedback capabilities by comparing them to human-rated scores. To do this, this study employed Linear Mixed-Effects (LME) models, correlation analyses, and a case study methodology. Our findings suggest an effective generative AI model that generates reliable evaluation for detecting learners’ progress. Moderate correlations were found between generative AI-based evaluations and human-rated scores, and generative AI demonstrated potential in providing formative feedback by identifying strengths and gaps. These findings suggest the potential of utilizing generative AI to provide different insights as well as automate formative feedback that can offer learners detailed scaffolding for summary writing.

## Introduction

Prompt formative assessment and feedback is an ongo ing challenge in educational settings, particularly  in the context of written assignments (Lewis Sevcikova, 2018). In the setting of summarization, it is especially important to be able to provide adequate  scaffolding. Traditionally, human evaluators have t aken such roles, with their strength in providing holistic judgments (Lewis Sevcikova, 2018). However, this approach is time-consuming and resource-intensive, which can hinder timely assessment and support (Brookhart & Chen, 2015). There is a growing interest in AI’s capacity to support automatic assessment and feedback. Our previous work touched on the potential of using AI-generated assessmen t t hrough the exploration of the relationship between human evaluations and knowledge-based AI evaluations (Kim et al., 2023). We found that each measure provides different insights, indicating a need for both collective and supplementary use for a more comprehensive assessment. AI technology is rapidly evolving, from the previou sly explored knowledge-based systems where summaries were assessed in terms of their similarity to a specified reference model (Kim & McCarthy, 2 021), to machine learning models that can be trained to auto matically extract aspects and provide evaluations o n its own (Nehm et al., 2012), and now to generative AI that could potentially review, grade, and provide feedback (Nah et al., 2023). This study aims to evaluate the practical value of generative AI in formative assessment and feedback. We are particularly intrigued by the prospect of providing detailed personalized feedback with a level of precision beyond previous methods. The research questions that guided this study are as follows:

• To what extent is generative AI-based evaluation able to detect changes between learners’ initial and

final revised summaries?

• How do evaluation measures driven by generative AI relate to other evaluation methods, including

human-rated scores and knowledge-based AI evaluations?

• What kind of formative feedback can generative AI provide to learners?

## Research context and data source

Summary data was collected from learners in an instructional technology course conducted asynchronously online over six semesters. Pre-class activities of reading and summary writing were assigned weekly, with learners using the Student Mental Model Analyzer for Research and Teaching (SMART) for selected weeks. SMART is a webbased formative feedback and evaluation system that  utilizes knowledge-based AI evaluation indices to provide learners with multimo dal feedback and encourages revision (Kim & McCarth y, 2021). The collected data consisted of the initial and final version of 62 se ts of such summaries, totaling 124 summaries submit ted by 43 learners for two separate assignments. Furthermore,  expert summaries, written by subject matter expert s (i.e., instructors), were incorporated to serve as a benchmark for generative AI model selection and prompt refinement. Evaluation indices For human evaluation, e ach summary was evaluated by two researchers on a s cale from zero to five, assessing content quality, content coverage, content coherence, and argument quality (Gao et al., 2019). The consistency of these four indices between raters were computed usi ng Intraclass Correlation Coefficients (ICC). The results showed reliability levels ranging from moderate (0.5-0.75) to good (0.75-0.9) (Koo & Li, 2016), with ICC values of.771,.640,.527, and.776, respectively.

Knowledge-based AI evaluation indices were automatically comp uted using SMART. Ten similarity indices built from graph theory (Wasserman & Faust, 1994) are calculated by comparing the learner’s summaries with the expert summary. Generative AI-based evaluation was conducted using gpt-4-0613, which was trained on data up to September 2021 and was the most up-to-date stable model at the time of this research (Ope nAI Platform, 2023). This model w as explored using LangChain, an open-source framework that simplifies the development of applications utilizing language models (Langchain, n.d.). We used the relevance, correctness, coherence, depth, and detail from the criteria evaluator provided through LangChain as the generative AI-based evaluation indices. The prompts utilized with the GPT model adhered to the guidelines set forth in the Prompt Engineering Guide (n.d.). We directed the generation of scores and textual feedback for summary assessme nt based on the designated criteria and expert summaries. We refined the prompts to enhance specificity  by increasing the point scale for detailed scoring, ensuring clear topic specification, and articulating evaluation criteria explicitly.

## Data analysis

In our analysis to examine generative AI models det ecting changes between learners’ initial and final revised summaries, we utilized Linear Mixed-Effects (LME) models. This was useful to track changes while controlling random effects attributable to individual learners and specific assignments. Additionally, Pearson’s correlation analysis was conducted to explore the relationships and similarities between indices. Finally, we conducted a case study of the produced feedback to assess the nature of the output information.

## Results and discussion

Generative AI-based evaluation Our analysis focused on assessing the capability of  generative AI-based evaluation indices in detecting changes between initial and final revised summaries. Overall, the results of the LME analysis accounting for random-effect variables (learner and assignment) indicated significant improvements in the scores across all five generative AIbased evaluation indices (t = 2.117, 3.693, 3.497, 4.241, 4.690, p =.037, <.000,.001, <.000, <.000, respectively). These results indicate that generative AI-based evaluation indices are capable of detecting improvements in learners’ summaries. This highlights the possibl e utility of such indices in providing formative as sessment. However, it is important to note that the correlations among the five indices and total average were all above 0.7, with the exception of the correlation between relevance and detail, which was slightly lower at 0.69. Relations between evaluation indices Pearson’s correlation coefficients were calculated to explore the relations between generative AI-based evaluation with human evaluations and knowledge-based AI evaluations. It was found that all generative AI-based indices showed moderate correlations with human evaluations (.3 < r <.7), apart from content coherence, which showed weak correlations. This might be attributed to the subjective nature  of coherence judgements, which is further evidenced by having the lowest ICC values among the  human raters (ICC =.527). In terms of the correla tions among generative AI-based evaluation and knowledge-based AI evaluations, the semantic dimension measures all showed moderate relationships. Other moderate c orrelations observed were between coherence and mea n distance, and depth and meandistance (r =.30 and.31, respectively). Overall, generative AI-based evaluation  demonstrated moderate correlations between all huma n evaluations, while most of the moderately correlate d indices pertained to semantic measures with regar ds to knowledge-based AI indices, suggesting its potential to serve as a supplementary measure to human evaluation. Generative AI-based formative feedback Generative AI-based formative feedback was examined through the r eview of specific cases. The provided feedback seems to effectively and consistently delineate the points that were identified successfu lly, as well as those that were absent or required further development, based upon the expert summary. While the feedback consistently adheres to the specified criteria, there appears to be some uniformity. This could possibly be reflecting a lack of distinct separatio n between certain indices. For example, the concept s of ‘depth’ and ‘detail’ often overlap, which could have led to  similar formative feedback. This observation highl ights the need to establish more distinct and precise criteria to enhance guidance targeting learner improvements. ## Conclusion

In this study, our objective was to investigate the potential of generative AI for formative assessment and feedback. We found that generative AI-based evaluation indices were able to identify learner progress in summary revisions and discovered moderate correlations between generative AI-based indices with human and semantic dimension knowledge-based AI indices, suggesting potential to serve as supplementary. Additionally, our case study further demonstrated its capacity to provide precise and actionable guidance. Nevertheless limitations included delays in generating results from lengthy texts, dependency risks on API servers, and practical cost-related challenges. Despite these challenges, the study reaffirms the potential utility of generative AI in educational settings, particularly for assessment and feedback (Nah et al., 2023). Future research should strive to address limitations to ensure a consistent and effective feedback loop, with investigations into how learners perceive such feedback.
