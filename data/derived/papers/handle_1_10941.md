# Little Logic Adventure: A Game for Learning Integrated Math and Coding in Early Elementary Classrooms

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In this poster, we present “Little Logic Adventure," a digital game designed for kindergarten students that integrates computational thinking (CT) with foundational math skills. The game is designed around a cognitive model of early childhood CT and mathematics standards for kindergarten. Children complete tasks where they apply their math and CT skills concurrently. The game is designed for use in early childhood classrooms and to provide teachers with a tool for integrating CT and mathematics.

## Introduction

Integrating computational thinking (CT) and mathematics has potential to help children understand mathematics concepts more deeply (Shumway et al, 2023; Goldenberg et al., 2021). However, there is a dearth of resources for elementary teachers on how to do this in a meaningful way (Israel & Lash, 2020). In this poster, we introduce early stages of a digital game for early elementary  classrooms. The design is guided by the question:  how do we design a developmentally appropriate game for learning integrated CT and math skills? Developmentally appropriate game design In prior work, we operationalized a developmentally  appropriate  cognitive model of CT for early elementary (Shumway et al., 2023). We aligned our CT model with kindergarten mathematics standards and what we call integrated processes (see Figure 1). The design features utilize audio and visual cues t o provide instructions, feedback, and support so that reading ability does not affect game play.  As children play, they compl ete levels that increase in difficulty in terms of the content. We draw on Plaas et al’s (2015) integrated design  framework for playful learning that incorporates cognitive, a ffective, and socio-cultural perspectives to account for the complexity of game design and learning, that we will elaborate on in the poster. ## Conclusion

Teachers need resources for integrating CT and math  easily in their classroom. We are developing a gam e for kindergarten students to develop and practice foundational mathematics and CT skills. The game will also include a reporting tool for teachers to keep track of their student’s progress at both the individual and classroom level.
