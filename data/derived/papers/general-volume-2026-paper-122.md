# Interactive Mosaics: Youth Creative Computing with ArtAlgo

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract

Interactive Mosaics: Youth Creative Computing with ArtAlgo Interactive Mosaics presents a collection of algorithmic artworks created by rural middle school students using ArtAlgo, a creative computing platform that pairs p5.js coding with Aiko, a conversational AI design partner. Over six workshop sessions, students explored color as a computational medium, transforming HEX values and palette generation into deliberate artistic choices. They built interactive mosaics that respond to touch, sound, and motion through Makey Makey physical computing kits, bridging digital abstractions with tangible, embodied experience. Aiko supported students only when asked, preserving their creative agency while offering responsive scaffolding around color, mood, and structure. The resulting works illustrate how computational thinking and creativity develop together when young people use code as an expressive medium. This collection invites viewers to consider how purposeful human-AI partnership and community-centered design can make computational expression accessible and personally meaningful for youth in under-resourced settings. STEM Club from West Middle School, North Carolina OTHERLAND: The Cultural, Perceptual Knowledge ABOUT, FROM, and WITH the Others OTHERLAND is an interactive installation that invites participants to step into a projected North American landscape, transforming static viewing into an embodied encounter. Within a 3m × 3m immersive environment, participants' gestures generate rippling waves of light that respond dynamically to movement. Based on a 2024 prototype by Glorie Gao's team, the installation draws on East Asian landscape aesthetics, in which water symbolizes philosophical depth and relational harmony. Real-time ambient sounds amplify this multisensory dialogue, challenging hierarchies privileging visual or cognitive knowledge over somatic and relational knowing (Abrahamson, 2014; Glenberg, 2010). Through interdisciplinary projects and partnerships, they explore connections between art (especially theatre), education, and the learning sciences, hoping to transform how we educate future generations. The motivation behind all their work is to support growing diversity and to foster curiosity and compassion for the other. In 2025, Kailea graduated with their Ph.D. in Curriculum and Instruction from the University of Wisconsin – Madison, and they are currently pursuing an M.F.A. in Acting from California Institute of the Arts. Kailea Saplan, Ph.D., Tina Kronis, Heidi Scheller Mapping Our Journeys: From Artist to Teacher In this collaborative artist book, we engage in reflective narrative to explore our learning journeys including what drove us as students and artists to want to pursue teaching. We employ connected arts learning to conceptually ground our reflections and artwork in our culturally connected and creative educational opportunities. The context of our learning community is central to this project. We are a cohort of students and instructors at UCLA VAPAE, where we are participating in a sequence of three classes that span a full academic year. As a close cohort, we have developed community norms, made art together, and explored culturally sustaining and teaching, socioemotional learning, and supportive classroom environments. We’ve also shared our surprises, struggles, and successes as we were placed at school sites and taught for the first time. Through our artistic reflections, we hope to share a window to ourselves as a community of artists and teachers.
