# Designing Quantum-Infused NGSS-Aligned Science Curriculum to Support Learning and Engagement of Middle School Students

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Introducing quantum concepts at the K-12 level is an innovative practice that offers varied educational opportunities across different subjects. However, there are persistent challenges, particularly in teaching basic quantum concepts in middle school, with concerns raised by teachers about training, materials, and time constraints. Despite these obstacles, recognizing the significance of engaging students in cultivating science and engineering practices, this study introduced a middle school science curriculum infused with quantum concepts aligned with Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS). Assessing its impact on the science learning outcomes and multidimensional engagement of 873 students revealed significant gains in learning fundamental quantum concepts as well as engagement in different dimensions. This study emphasizes the potential of integrating quantum concepts into middle school science education within the learning sciences framework, offering valuable insights for researchers and educators interested in incorporating emerging quantum technologies and their societal applications.

## Introduction

Quantum technologies are increasingly vital for future industries like materials science, artificial intelligence, and cryptography (Gururaj, 2023), with predictions suggesting signif icant job growth, particularly in quantum computing, by 2040. Preparing middle and high schoo l students for this future is crucial  (Akdemir et al., 2023), necessitating widespread quantum education, as emphasized by the National Quantum Coordination Office. This study integrates Quantum Information Science and En gineering (QISE) into formal middle school science education through a novel teaching-learning sequence (TLS), clarifying the distinction  between classical and quantum randomness concepts. By incorporating Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) and interdisciplinary pedagogy, it aims to enhance students' quantum knowledge and engagement, filling a critical gap in teaching QISE to K-12 students.

Our investigation revolves around two primary research questions:

1. What impact does a quantum-enriched science curriculum have on the learning ou tcomes and

engagement of middle school students?

2. Do learning outcomes differ when comparing quantum and  non-quantum content within this

curriculum?

## Methods

Design This study, utilizing design-based research (DBR), examines how integrating quan tum computing into a science curriculum impacts middle school students' learning and engagement. Over two years, DBR methodically trained in-service science teachers and students, culmin ating in the assessment and evaluation phase presen ted in this paper. Student groups naturally formed based on classroom assignments and data collection timing (Campbell & Stanley, 2015), with assessments and engagement sur veys administered befor e and after instruction to measure progress. Though originally comprising three phases, this paper focuses on the final phase, omitting q ualitative findings from the prototyping phase due to space constraints.

## Participants

Data from 873 students in grades 6-8 across seven public middle schools in the Midwest  region of the United States were collected with the assistance of 10 middle school science teachers, who participated in a professional development workshop organized by the project team. These teachers then implemented the unit at different times based on their annual teaching schedules, with an average overall science teaching experience of 15 years among them, comprising four female and six male teachers.  Only students who completed both pre-and post-measures were included in the analyses.

## Procedure

In Spring 2022, we developed a quantum-infused curriculum unit on randomness and probability in classical and quantum physics, utilizing the explore-before-explain instructional sequence and aligned with a teaching-learning sequence in science education. This curriculum integrated engaging elements such as a fictional Spiderman story to introduce radioactive materials and progressed t o explain randomness in classical and quantum compu ting, including real-world applications like quantum random generators. In Summer 2022, a three-day intensive professional development program was conducted for ten middle school teachers, covering quantum information fundamentals, curriculum implementation, assessment tools, and applied pedagogy. Data collection for the study began in Fall 2022, including a science content asse ssment and an engagement survey, facilitating a comprehensive evaluation of student engagement (Wang et al., 2016) and understanding.

## Preliminary findings

Research Question 1 examined the pre-post comparison of learning outcomes and engagement scores. The paired samples t-test revealed a significant increase in science lea rning outcomes post-assessment, with a moderate to large effect size indicating practical impact (Cohen, 1988). Similarly, emotional, cognitive, and social engagement dimensions showed significant improvements. Research Question 2 focused on comparing learning outcomes by question content, indicating significant increases in both quantum and  non-quantum-based assessments post-instruction. While the instruction notably enhanced understanding of quantum concepts,  improvements in non-quantum areas were comparatively less pronounced.

## Conclusion

The study supports the effectiveness of quantum-infused science teaching and learning in terms of students’ learning outcomes and multidimensional engagement. As Bruner (1966) advocated decades earlier, we were able to generate an educationally sound curriculum that impacted students’ learning and engagement regardless of the educational context. In other words, our TLS was impactful to teach a n abstract and counterintuitive topic to learners who transition from concrete to abstract learning, whic h provided us a new venue for QISE education and engagement.  Our preliminary quantitative findings can suggest t hat incorporating quantum principles into science education using TLS can be a valuable instructional strategy, especially for the scholars interested in the integration of LPs in the context of learning scien ces. Moreover, the found significant change in middle school students’ emotional, cognitive, and social engagement can imply that integration of quantum-related concepts into middle school level formal science education curriculum is promising, opening a window for further research on how these changes occur, and develop qualitatively.
