# Equitable Learning Analytics Dashboards: Roles and Mechanisms in Personalized Learning

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster presents a rapid scoping review of equitable learning analytics dashboards (LADs) for personalized learning. Grounded in the integration of self-regulated learning (SRL) theory and situated learning theory, the review synthesizes findings from ten journal articles published between 2023 and 2025. The findings outline four roles and eight supporting mechanisms through which personalized LADs promote equity. This preliminary synthesis highlights inclusive design consideration to support equitable LAD development.

Learning analytics dashboards (LADs) are sophisticated interfaces that integrate and visualize data about learning processes to support educational decision-making. Personalized learning in the context of LADs  involves presenting dynamically generated data to support adaptation of study strategies and pacing, aiming to foster selfregulated learning  (SRL) competencies (Verbert et al., 2020).  With the development of technology, A rtificial intelligence (AI) analytics and multimodal data integration have significantly  enhanced LAD capabilities, yet critical gaps remain in synthesizing their equity impacts  (Baker et al., 2022; Wise & Shaffer, 2023).  To address these research gaps, this review synthesizes recent  studies on how personalized LADs promot e equity. The research question guided this study is: What are the roles of personalized LADs in promoting equity, and what technical mechanisms support these roles?

The theoretical framework  integrates self-regulated learning (SRL) theory (Zimmerman, 200 2) and situated learning theory  (Lave & Wenger, 1991). SRL conceptualizes effective learning as iterative cycles of forethought, performance, and self-reflection, enabling learners to actively regulate and refine their learning strategies (Zimmerman, 2002). In the context of LADs, SRL provides  a lens to examine how data visualizations and adaptive feedback empowers students to take control over their learning (Lee, 2024). Situated learning theory posits that knowledge is constructed through participation in authentic social contexts, where learners progressing from legitimate peripheral participation toward full membership in communities of practice (Lave & Wenger, 1991). In the context of LADs, this perspective underscores  how LADs foster equity  by scaffolding inclusive participation and engagement. By combing SRL theory and situated learning theory, this study adopted a dual lens to explore how personalized LADs promote equity.

This study adopted a rapid scoping review method to systematically map and synthesize relevant literature. As an accelerated form of the traditional review, this method  streamlines processes to deliver  timely insights into fast evolving fields (Tricco et al., 2015). It  is well-suited to educational technology because rapid technological advances call for up-to-data review that capture emerging trends and developments. This study followed the four steps: (1) Identification: This study followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews  (PRISMA) framework  (Page et al., 2021) to identify related studies. Searches were conducted in three databases: Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and web of science. The Boolean search string used was "learning analytics dashboards" AND "personalized learning" AND “equity” OR “inclusion” with filters applied to limit results to peer-reviewed articles. (2) Screening: Title and abstracts were independently screened by four researchers against the inclusion criteria: (a) published in English; (b) published between 2023 to 2025; (c) focus on personalized learning in LADs; (d) relate to equity; and (e) published in top-tier Q1 academic journals ranked by the S cimago Journal Rank (SJR). (3) Coding: Data extraction was performed using NVivo software with a deductive-inductive coding framework grounded in SRL theory and situated learning theory. And (4) Analysis: Coding was aggregated through thematic synthesis to identify patterns. Four researchers independently coded and analyzed the data, with discrepancies resolved through discussion and consensus. The findings were organized into four roles and eight supporting technical mechanisms that facilitate equitable implication for diverse learners (see Table 1).

They  promote equity by delivering personalized, context-sensitive feedback and bridging accessibility to ensure learners with diverse needs can understand and act on it (Lee et al., 2025). Th e technological mechanism involves  hierarchical content delivery supported by adaptive LADs that monitor progress. For example, Maier and Klotz (2025) used MasteryX adaptive grammar app, which organizes learning into hierarchical course levels  and provides elaborated feedback that enable students to receive actionable feedback.

• Advancing adaptive personalization. Personalized LADs enable scalable, individualized learning

pathways that adapt to diverse learner needs. This role is supported by technological mechanism such as AI-driven personalization and natural language processing (NLP). For example, Tretow-Fish et al. (2023) demonstrated the use of AI for evaluation and adaptative content delivery. S imilarly, Weidlich et al.

(2025) employed NLP to analyze Moodle forum interaction and identify indicators such as academic

terminology usage, facilitating responsive and equitable support.

• Integrating multimodal data. Personalized LADs process diverse multimodal inputs such as text, video,

audio to address different learner backgrounds and accessibility needs. Key supporting mechanisms include multimodal data processing and bias-aware algorithmic design. For example, Bauer et al. (2025) analyze multimodal learner data, including log files, eye tracking, videos, and texts, to enable responsive and inclusive support. Küchemann et al. (2025) employed large multimodal models as middleware to facilitate real-time scaffolding. Similarly, Wu and Chiu (2025) explored the integration of generative AI tools, including ChatGPT for text generation, Midjourney for image creation, and Grammarly for writing assistance, to deliver interactive feedback directly through user interactions. ## Significance and future research

This review contributes to the literature by synthesizing recent studies on personalized LADs and their roles in promoting equity. By integrating the perspectives of SRL and situated learning, this study highlights how personalized LADs function not only as tools but also as mechanisms that foster inclusive participation in learning environments. Future research should further examine the long-term impact of personalized LADs on learners across diverse contexts and investigate potential biases embedded in A I-driven analytics systems. In addition, mixed-methods approaches are needed to better understand how students interpret dashboard feedback and use these insights to regulate their learning.
