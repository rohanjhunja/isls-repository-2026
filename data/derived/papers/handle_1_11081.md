# The Process of Constructing Interdisciplinary Thinking Through Graduation Research in an Interdisciplinary Department

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study aims to investigate how students in an interdisciplinary department construct interdisciplinary thinking through their graduation research. Using Trajectory Equifinality Modeling (TEM), grounded in social constructivism, we conducted a qualitative analysis from the perspective of interdisciplinary thinking. The analysis revealed two types of processes in the construction of interdisciplinary thinking through graduation research. The first involves integrating a secondary field through external resources provided by supervisor, while the second entails developing interest in a secondary field due to the interdisciplinarity of seminars or supervisors. Students expanded their research themes by maintaining their primary field and incorporating insights from secondary fields. This study provides valuable insights for the development of interdisciplinary education designed to foster interdisciplinary thinking.

## Introduction

With the increasing complexity of global issues, there is a need for interdisciplinary education in higher education that fosters the ability to integrate diverse disci plinary insights to address multifaceted problems. Students themselves need to integrate new connections of knowledge and skills, and there is a global focus on integrating undergraduate research (UR)  into the curriculum, which refers to all research a ctivities conducted by undergraduate students (Sengpta et al. 2022).

However, previous studies have not clarified how in terdisciplinary thinking is formed in the course of UR. Interdisciplinary thinking is " the capacity to integrate knowledge and modes of thi nking in two or more disciplines or established areas of expertise to pr oduce a cognitive advancement  in ways that would have been impossible or unlikely through single disciplinary means" (Boix Mansilla and Duraise 2007). The purpose of this study is to investigate the formation process of in terdisciplinary thinking through graduation researc h among interdisciplinary undergraduate students. In doing so, we will apply Trajectory Equifinality Modeling (TEM), based on the definition of interdisciplinary thinking, to data collected from interviews with seven interdisciplinary undergraduate students who completed their graduation research, a representative UR in Japan.

## Methods

This study targeted students from the interdisciplinary department at P University, where graduation research is a requirement and students are planning for postgradu ate studies. Seven fourth-year students working on their graduation research for postgraduate education part icipated. Two longitudinal interviews were condu cted, in March 2022 and October 2023, each lasting approxima tely 120 minutes. These interviews covered the proc ess from the commencement of graduation research to res earch activities in the master's program. The inter views primarily focused on  the relationship between their  research topics and interdisciplinarity. The study employed Trajectory Equifinality Modeling (TEM) as a central  tool to understand the developmental paths of interdisciplinary thinking among undergraduates. Ba sed on cultural psychology, TEM identifies a specif ic point as the Equifinality Point (EFP) and depicts multiple paths leading to this point, clarifying why certain paths were chosen at pivotal experiences.
