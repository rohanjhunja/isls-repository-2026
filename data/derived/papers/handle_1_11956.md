# Designer-of-Text: An Integrated Approach to Idea Improvement and Written Composition in Knowledge Building Communities

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster introduces an integrated approach to foster the dynamic improvement of idea and written composition in knowledge building communities. The approach encourages the creation of new phrases and sentences as design products in design and belief modes. The strength of this approach lies in its promotion of higher-order thinking about meaningful language-content patterns and connections, emphasizing the role of language as a dynamic driver of thought and knowledge advancement.

## Introduction

The previous study investigated language and content development with two novice writers of English in Japan, using commercial translators to foster active engagement in complex language and content problem solving (Tsuji, 2022). Through discussion of translated ideas and i ntentions, the writers engaged in progressive improvement of their text, what the author has termed “Designer-of-Text.” This concept reflects Sharples’ (1996) notion of writing as creative design, Larsen-Freeman’s (2017) characterization of language as co mplex, self-organized systems, and Knowledge Building interactions between linguistic and conceptual problem spaces. Knowledge Building is a socio-constructivist approach to knowledge advancement with a collective responsibility mindset (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2006), supported by idea-centered discourse and analytics for meta-discourse. Knowledge building aligns with an emerging perspective that language production/development is a constructivist act of creating new language patterns from existing texts (Cameron & La rsen-Freeman, 2007). Under this view, the approach to support writing development is to facilitate the self-organizing process in which writers build on existing language resources. Thus, knowledge building could provide an environment that fosters the dynamic improvement of idea and language in parallel.

The current study extends the previous research by exploring the d esigner-of-text approach, practices, and tool designs to support and examine novice writers’ creation of new language patterns with writing supports. Specifically, it aims to foster intentional focus on phrases or sent ences as design products and the invention of new words or terms to accommodate novel ideas or concepts. Designer-of-text process One way to facilitate the emergence of new language  patterns is to engage them in design thinking with their written materials. Writing has been linked with  design, as Sharples argued (1996): both writing and design processes generate new ideas and knowledge-embodying materials, with existing resources, materials, and tools. To facilitate content-language knowledge creation, the approach that assumes writing as design should engage students in design (e.g., creation, refinement) and belief (e.g., validation, question) modes (Bereiter & Scardamalia, 2003; Bereiter & Scardamalia, 2014). Thus, t he initial model of the  designer-of-text process integrates the two modes into Sharples’ account of writing as creative design  to facilitate the emergence of new ideas and language patterns in parallel  (Figure 1). The circular shape with the writer as a designer in  the center indicates that the writer accesses cognitive action s at any time in any way to produce and refine new ideas and language patterns. Cognitive actions include plan-engage-synthesize (design mode) and evaluate (belief mode) at the time of composition. “Engage” requires writers to “think with texts” in Sharples’ terms (emphasis original) to produce written texts that represent their ideas. Sharples originally categorized “plan” as a reflective activity; the current model reframes it as another intentional design activity  because planning written materials to represent ideas involves early conceptualization of texts  and ideas. “S ynthesize,” rather than Sharples’ original term “contemplate,” is used to emphasize that writers actively seek novel language-content patterns, associations, and connections that reframe or extend their thinking. “Evaluate” refers to  activities in belief mode, such as questioning, validating, accepting, or rejecting the writer’s representations. This involves critical judgement of representations: assessment of  their accuracy, clarity, appropriateness, and effectivene ss.  Resources, materials, and tools available to the writer are essential in the design process to extend the writer's cognition. ## Implications

The integrated approach fosters students’ agency in advancing content and language knowledge, positioning students as “a creative thinker and a designer of text (Sharples, 1996, p.1).” It could embrace learners’ language diversity, creativity, metalinguistic awareness, and expressiveness by approaching writing and language learning beyond error correction. The strength of this approach lies in its promotion of higher-order thinking about meaningful language-content patterns and connections, emphasizing language as a dynamic driver of thought and knowledge advancement.
