# Portraitures of STEMcyclists Youth: Joy and Liberation for Critical and Transformative STEM Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Out-of-school, informal STEM education opportunities have potential to move beyond patterns of didactic, teacher-focused, factual recall that have been a mainstay in traditional STEM teaching and learning contexts. In this study, we present portraitures of two Black youth who participated in a one-week STEMcyclists summer program across two years, designed to circumvent status quo modes of teaching and learning by honoring minoritized youths’ lives, genius, and joy through science and engineering activities related to the bike and community biking. Through the portraiture method and cycles of descriptive and deductive coding informed by Muhammad’s (2023) cultivating genius and joy framework, we uncovered youths’ experiences in the program. Findings from these portraitures surfaced the STEMcyclists program as a context for learning that amplifies youth past experiences, identities, and knowledge while repositioning and cultivating youth as STEM knowledge possessors who engage in re-seeing, rebuilding, and forging new knowledge.

## Background

In the Technology, Knowledge and Learning Special Issue  Introduction, “Knowing and Learning with the Technology (and on Wheels!), ” Lee (2013) highlights how a group of scholars exami ned the bicycle as a rich domain to study technology and learning. Essentiall y, this approach is undergirded by constructivism t hat posits that because the bicycle is generally a familiar to ol, and bike riding is a rite of passage for children; it provides a context to develop more complete and expert understandings of phenomena. Lee notes that bicycles and bicycling are fine – perhaps even exceptional candidate objects and domains for eliciting ideas. Relatedly, Lee argues that the bicycle is an impactful medium because it is a symbol of cultural identities and informs community  spaces and places. Thus, the bicycle, while a powerful and culturally enduring technology, has remained an understudied, technological medium for science teaching and learning. In response, this study examines the experiences of Black and Brown youth in a STEM bicycle and biking progra m in a midsized Northeastern city. More specifically, we documented how youth narrated their understandings of related scientific  and engineering phenomena, their experiences of the biking program, and how these ex periences informed their disposition s for STEM learning. The following questions framed the study: What were youths’ experiences with the STEM cyclists summer program? How did STEMcyclists youth re-present their experiences, learning opportunities, and STEM identities using the bicycle as a medium for STEM teaching and learning in a STEMcyclists summer program? In alignment with the above questions, the STEMcyclists program, an informal learning experience that focuses on the science and engineering of bikes and biking, draws from  cultivating genius and joy framings (Muhammad, 2023) that honor asset-based understandings of the role  and affordances  of place, along with youth’s sociocultural, communal ways of knowing that support urban STEM learning (Adams et al., 2017).

## Method

Dixson et al. (2005) note that “through portraiture researchers c andemonstrate a commitment to the research participants and contextualize the depictions of in dividuals and events” (p. 17). Dixson and colleague s explain further that portraitures function on a partialit y that “re-presents” the research participant (Lawrence-Lightfoot,

1983) through a subjective and critical lens of the researcher. The context for this study was a one-week summer

program (Summer 2022 and Summer 2023) that focused on the science and engine ering of the bicycle. Youth rebuilt their bicycles and learned about the associated STEM phenomena related to their rebuild. At the end of the program, the youth took the bikes  home that they rebuilt. Altogether, twenty Black and Brown rising ninth and tenth graders across two years of the summer p rogram participated in the study. A first reading of the data was organized according to research questions. The inte rview transcripts for all the participants w ere organized in a matrix according to the research questions. The data was analyzed qualitatively using an iterat ive process of inductive descriptive coding (Saldaña, 2021).

## Findings

Below we present four main themes that encapsulate the experiences o f STEMcyclists youth. We provide a portraiture of Jamel (pseudonym) to reflect his specific experiences. Of memories: Spaces that center what youth know Jamel was quiet and deliberate. He noted that a teacher recommended the bike program and while he did not know what to expect, he showed up. Soon, he would make connections with his broken bike at home, and he reminisced about “riding with my brother or friends down the s treet,” up a hill and around a dirt patch. The bike provi ded accessibility to spaces and places that are mostly hidden to others, and thus, it was a foundational connection to nature and kinship.

Re-seeing, re-building and forging pathways for knowledge building In reflecting on his STEMcyclists experience, Jamel  described it as fun, with a paradoxical layering o f “I hated like a few parts that were really hard, but otherwi se it was fun. Something good to do.” When prompted  on his favorite part of the program, he quipped: “I think it was the second class where we learned about the tires and stuff and took them apart and put them back together and stuff like that.” For science, he stressed big ideas related to “momentum, aerodynamics, weight and stuff.” Mentor affirmations, safety, and joy Jamel was explicit about his interactions and relational experience with instruc tors and mentors during the STEMcyclists program. For Jamel, the highlight was that instructors and mentors “made it easy to learn and how to do that.” On his return as a peer mentor, Jamel ’s messaging was consistent: “It was mostly fun and really enjoyable.” When prompted to elaborate on his notion of fun and enjoyable, he offered: “People, they were easy, very easy to talk to and willing to learn more about it and talk about it.” Critical identities: Youth as the knowers, doers, and owners As a final point, Jamel noted that he learned that he is good in engineering and good at fixing things. Jamel developed a keen facility with understanding the bike, fixing, and remixing the bike. Thus, it was no surprise that Jamel returned for year 2 as a peer mentor, and in this role, he rotated across all of the groups assisting, supporting, and serving as a role model. What is critical here was the emergence of a blossoming youth who was comfortable in his knowledge, skills, and contributions to his peers.

## Discussion

We argue that the STEMcyclists program via the bike provided an impactful STEM experience for Black and Brown youth due to its keen familiarity, cultural identity as a disruptor, transparent, technological medium to fix, rebuild, and re-mix opportunities for ownership, and foster joy and liberation in the process. The STEMcyclists program leverages the bike as a familiar and transp arent technology (Clark, 2013) and provides a tangible ecosystem to elicit youth’s ideas about engineering principles and science knowledge and practices.
