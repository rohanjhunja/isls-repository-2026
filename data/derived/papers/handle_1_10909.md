# A Chemistry-Based Escape Room as a Tool for Teachers to Assess Student Thinking

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores chemistry teachers' formative assessment (FA) knowledge and approaches in a collaborative dynamic learning environment through professional noticing and FA frameworks. Observation worksheets and perception questionnaires were collected from 36 high school chemistry teachers. A mixed method design is employed. Five categories were identified based on the results of the teacher perception questionnaire. Furthermore, three dimensions of noticing were identified. Our study will contribute to teachers' professional knowledge about assessment and FA.

Research rationale Using FA activities it is a challenge for teachers (Duckor & Holmberg, 2023). To conduct FA effectively, teachers need guidance and support (Duckor & Holmberg, 2023; Murray et al., 2020). In this study, we examine teachers' FA knowledge through the professional noticing framework in a physical, face-to-face chemistry-based escaperoom (ER), designed for high school students. This pedagogical environment is an example of a collaborative and dynamic learning environment. In this environment, teachers can focus  beyond content knowledge, on students' discourse and 21st century skills, such as collaboration (Brady & Andersen, 2021; Roig et al., 2023) and listening skills (Avargil et al., 2021).

The main goal of this study is to investigate chemistry teachers' FA knowledge and approaches in a collaborative dynamic learning environment. The research questions are: (1) during an observation in a chemistry-based escape room (ER), what do chemistry teachers notice as important to assess? (2) what are the characteristics of chemistry teachers’ interpretation?

Research setting The research setting includes a one-week professional development program (PD) called “ Chemistry-based escape room as a pedagogical tool for assessment.” The goals for the teachers were to: (1) experience the ER as learners, designing a map of the puzzles, and gain ing knowledge of the chemical content, process, and str ucture of the ER, (2) observe a video of students participating in the ER activity, as well as learning about FA and teacher noticing, (3) discuss FA and teachers’ professional perceptions of student activities in the ER.

## Research participants

In this research, 36 high school chemistry teachers were deliberately selected using the judgment sample method Their experience range d from two to more than 31 years. Selection criteria included participation in the PD, availability, teaching experience, motivation, gender, sectoral diversity, and interest in leading a change.

## Research tools and methodology

We used a mixed method with sequential transformative designs (Creswell, 2003). The phenomenon was investigated using an exploratory approach. The research tools were teachers' perception questionnaire and their observation worksheets during the PD. The data was analyzed using factor analysis, the Cronbach’s alpha for each category was: factor 1 (0.863), factor 2 (0.726), factor 3 (0.737), factor 4 (0.720), factor 5 (0.688). Additionally, the qualitative data was analyzed using a direct content analysis  (Hsieh & Shannon, 2005). Two researchers independently coded a small portion of randomly selected occasions of noticing from each teacher's observations to ensure trustworthiness. Then they reviewed and resolved discrepancies  to reach consensus about the coding scheme and the coding of each teacher. The unit of analysis consists of phrases describing what the teachers notice while students experience a chemistry ER and an explanation of their description.

## Analyses and findings

Based on analyzing the teacher’s perception questio nnaire five categories were identified  (Figure 1). According to the Friedman test, a nonparametric statistical analysis, there were significant differences between the categories (ꭕ2(4) = 26.704, p<.001).

## Discussion and contribution

According to the findings, chemistry teachers focus ed on the emotional aspects of enjoyment, communica tion, and leadership skills among their students while so lving chemistry puzzles in the ER. According to Brady and Andersen (2021), the ER is a collaborative problem-solving activity that increases student engagement. Students also enjoyed the ER activity and had the opportunity to review chemistry concepts. In addition, Roig and colleagues (2023), argue that ERs are the highly effective assessment tool for motivating and engaging student s and improving their performance. We found that teachers emphasized the students' collaboration and sharing skills more than the challenges associated with the ER learning environment. In a physical ER, students can collaborate spontaneously through sharing information, eye cont act, and laughing (Haimovich et al., 2022). Our stu dy also shows that, teachers were able to pay attention to student thinking through the experience in the ER and identified three noticing dimensions: Chemical thinking notici ng (Murray et al., 2020), 21st century skills notic ing and affective noticing. This study could promote theoretical knowledge about FA and assessment knowledge through teachers’ professional noticing and specifically in  learning environments that promote collaboration a nd teamwork in problem solving. Practical contribution s include identifying different dimensions of notic ing and interpreting that may be applied in various collaborative and dynamic learning environments.
