# Students Knowledge Building Engagement and Learning Outcomes in Business English Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Knowledge building (KB) environment was a principle-based learning environment which places emphasis on idea improvement and knowledge creation. Students’ engagement in knowledge building inquiry can be assessed both by learning indices in Analytic Toolkit (ATK) and use of discourse moves in Knowledge Forum (KF) notes. In this study, KB principles were introduced to a business English class, and 25 students conducted KF inquiry over a whole semester. Mixed methods were used to examine the students’ changes in domain knowledge, learners’ engagement in KF inquiry, and correlations between KF engagement and written examination. The results showed students improved their domain understanding significantly. In addition, some of students’ ATK indices and discourse moves were correlated to students’ written examination scores. This study shed light on how to design a principle-based KB environment and what particular discourse moves should be developed to promote students’ learning outcomes.

## Introduction

Knowledge Building (KB) emphasizes collective knowledge advancement and continual idea improvement, and Knowledge Forum (KF) has been designed and developed as a principle-based online  learning environment to support KB inquiry (Chan, 2011). As an innovative model in computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL), KB has drawn much attention among education research community in China in re cent years. Classroom innovations guided by KB have demonstrated Chinese learners can engage in offline and online KB inquiry and improve both domain knowledge and collaborative skills (Authors, 2018). In the context of Covid-19, KB has been more frequently adopted to support Chinese students’ online collective inquiry, especially in higher education. However, most KB studies in tertiary classes discussed students’ learning in the context of educational technology courses and students’ engagement was mostly examined by ATK indices. Whether students’ knowledge building engagement other than ATK data can predict students’ learning outcomes is less investigated, and there is also a need for studies of KB supporting different disciplines. Business English is an interdisciplinary area which integrates business conte nt knowledge and English skills. In 2020, Ministry of Education of the People's Republic of China published a new  course syllabus for business English, which emphasized principles of “being student-centered, output-oriented, and continuously improved”. The core ideas were consistent with KB model which encourages sustained in-depth inquiry and idea improvement.

In this study, KB principles were used for instruction design and online KF inquiry. In addition to offline face-to-face instruction, students were engaged in online KF discussion thr oughout the whole semester. Three research questions are addressed: (1) What are the effects of princip le-based KB environment on students’ conceptual understanding? (2) How are students engaged in online KF activities? and (3) What’s the relationship between KF engagement and students’ academic achievement?

## Participants and context

Participants included one class first year undergraduate students (n=25) from  a university in Shanghai, China. Students were divided into five groups, and each group had 5 students. They attende d KB class and conducted KF inquiry in a business English class over a period of 12 weeks. Each we ek, the students spent 90 minutes on in-class discussion and inquiry, and each student wrote at least two KF notes a fter class. The teacher was an experienced KB teacher and English was used as the medium of instruction and discussion in class and online. The curriculum was content-based, helping first-year undergraduate students under stand basic business concepts and theories. In this study, students mainly learned two big themes. The  first theme is about the PEST model, which is a business analysis framework from politics, economy, society, and technology perspectives. The second theme is centering on some representative management theories. Learners are engaged in the class to share, CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 242 build on, and improve their ideas. And they continued discussion after class in KF  by posting notes, providing feedbacks, referring tonew information, and building on peers’ ideas.

## Design and data collection

Principle-based KB environment was designed to support students’ in-class and KF activities. “Real ideas and authentic problem” is a principle of KB model which emphasizes creative w ork that learners really care about, rather than issues totally from textbooks. Therefore, learning materials linked to  real commercial projects were presented to students for in-class KB inquiry. Also, authentic business cas es from daily life were selected to promote students’ further inquiry on KF. “Improvable ideas and rise above” is  another principle guiding the instruction. Students were asked to refine ideas and track how these ideas were dynamically developed over time. Moreover, students were encouraged to identify future direction of inquiry base d on continuous revision and reflection. “Embedded and transformative assessment” is KB principle guiding the design of assessment activities. In this study, reflective assessment was designed to help learners refle ct on collective inquiry and report knowledge creation work. Students were engaged in in-class reflective presentati ons and KF e-portfolio writing to analyze collective knowledge advancement. Several other KB principles were also adopted to design the instruction, including “community knowledge & collective responsibility” and “KB discourse”. Students’ learning outcomes were assessed by business domain test and final written examination. Business domain test was designed to examine students’ understanding of key conc epts relevant to the business. The test included two sections, asking the learners “ what they have known” and “what they would like to know ” about core concepts in business. To cite an example, one question in the first section asked students “What comes to your mind when we mention business management”, while another question in the  second section asked students to answer “What more you would like to know about business management”. The domain test was conducted in a pre/post manner, at the first week and final week of the semester. A written examination was held during the last week of semester. It was a case analysis to examine to what extent students could use business analytical model solve ill-defined problems. The background materials briefly introduce d the development of a company called Pop Mart. And the students were asked to use PEST model to  write a business analysis report, making decisions on whether investment should be made to Pop Mart in the coming five years. Multiple data sources were used in this study, including KF indices collected by ATK tools, KF notes, business domain test, and final written examination. In addition, all in-class instr uctions were audiotaped and videotaped.

## Discussion

The results demonstrate that principle-based KB design has played a positive role in improving domain knowledge of business English studies. Students significantly improved their understanding of business concepts and theories in offline and online KB inquiry. In addition, students’ engagement in KF inquir y indicated they participated in KF inquiry in different forms with various discourse patterns, with most of  their KF notes focusing on putting forward theories and improving ideas. Students also developed metacognition and collabora tive learning skills since they used KB meta-discourse moves to track collective knowledge advancement and identify future inquiry direction. Correlation analysis uncovered that students’ engagement in certain KF  activities had positive relationships with the written examination. When students read, wrote, and revise d notes with scaffolds, they developed understanding-based memorization and analytical skills. Moreover, the quality of discourse moves was also correlated to written examination. Discourse moves referring to theory bui lding, idea improvement, and reflection on collective inquiry process improved their performance in coordinati ng ideas and creating new knowledge, so that students improved performance in writing task. Principle-based KB environment affected the design of inquiry activities in-class and online. Students have sustained inquiry and continuously co-construct new knowledge in KF supported by discourse moves. This study proved that KB environment played a pos itive role in improving tertiary students’ learning outcomes. Further analysis may explore how various types of KF discourse affect l earning outcomes and how to develop students’ use of good KB discourse moves. Moreover, whether the same findings  can be ob tained in other disciplines deserves further exploration.
