# Leveraging Influencer Groups to Examine Learning Networks on Twitter

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Our study builds on theories emphasizing connectivity for learning on social media to study learning networks on Twitter (e.g., Haythornthwaite, 2019; Siemens, 2005). Considering the nature of power law distribution on social media (Yoshida, 2021), we focus on a group of influencers from the Non-Fungible Token (NFT) community to uncover online learning networks and their emergent practices. By employing an exploratory Social Network Analysis (Nooraie et al., 2020) and qualitative analysis, we examine the way in which influencers form learning relations through conversational interactions and content, and the modalities and discursive strategies used in their tweets. We find that influencers built a strong learning network through sustained interactions and content relations among themselves and with a wide community outreach. This analysis contributes to the way that learning networks are analyzed and found on Twitter.

## Introduction

Online, networked technologies have transformed communication and information sharing, and challenged traditional notions of formal, non-formal, and informal learning (Haythornthwaite, 2022; Prestridge et al., 2021). Theories emphasizing connections between people offer frameworks to explore emerging processes of learning on social media, highlighting the need to analyze conversational interactions within online learning networks (Haythornthwaite, 2022). In this paper, we focus on Twitter, an open and well-known online  platform, which supports learning or information sharing via multiple avenues, most commonly via hasht ag (#) searches (Kimmons et al., 2021). While hashtags are a key feature for aggregating information, retweets and mentions offer additional ways for users to learn and connect on Twitter (Luzón & Albero-Pos ac, 2020). However, research on learning congregations on Twitter beyond hashtags is underreported. To address this gap, we focus on a group of NFT influencers on Twitter, as they can exemplify networked learning (Cheng, 2013) and can reveal different ways that people connect and learn together online. We aim to answer thr ee research questions: (1) What conversational interactions exist among influencers? (2) What content relations are present in this group? (3) And what modalities and communicative tools are used for these relations? Using the Twitter API, we capture a digital footprint of connections and analyze the resulting data to uncover emergent practices and learning networks within the Twitter environment.

## Theoretical framework

Our study builds on networked learning and learning network theories. Networked learning, whose principles are grounded in connectivism (Cheng, 2013), extends beyond the individual connections of people (Sieme ns, 2005) to also consider relationships with other people, groups, their context, and their le arning resources in pursuit of enhancing learning outcomes (Cheng, 2013; Haythornthwaite, 2019). Learning networks, which are the collections of connections formed between individuals or individuals and objects, emerge through self-organized ties (Schreurs et al., 2019). In examining connectivity and learning on social media, conversations, and the interrelation of ties, learning relations, and network formations are important. Learning r elations form in several multi-directional ways. Conversations form the basic ties among actors which support learning communities (Haythornthwaite, 2022). Information, communication, and discursive practices can constitute learning relations (Haythornthwaite, 2019; Schreurs et al., 2019). They accrue from the information and content exchanges between people, the self and platform, or from a collective practice that is construed in a platform. Multiple relations form an interwoven relationship of social ties that support a learning community network (Haythornthwaite, 2019). This paper narrows down on the interrelations of ties among influencers to understand learning networks and the focus on influencers is guided by the nature of communication online. Online networ ks follow a power law distribution in which a few users have the most connections while most other users are spread out with sparse connections (Yoshida, 2021). Influencers, individuals who regardless of level of expertise garnered a following of people around a particular interest-driven domain (Marcelo & Marcelo, 2021), make up most of the content on a social network. They direct the flow of trends and conversations (Kimmons et al., 2021), have a critical role in the dissemination of information (Shen et al., 2017), impact learning, and spread the ir influence through both a commitment to sharing information and collective practices (Haythornthwaite, 2019; Prestridge et al., 2021). ## Methods and data sources

In this study, we investigate the interactions of Non-Fungible Token (NFT) influ encers on Twitter. The NFT community has been of interest to us as it is composed of groups of individuals th at, through both online and offline interactions, have learned enough to establish a complex and wide-ranging dom ain with real-life implications, and continue to learn within their formed domain ecology, entangled with their daily life environments. We feel that such interactions can exemplify networked learning, and s tudying learners in this community can reveal different and dynamic ways that people connect and learn to gether online, which has potential for informing pedagogical practices and the design of open collaborative platforms. We followed a group of 93 NFT influencers suggested by an insider-NFT influencer, aggregated through  a Twitter List. To capture prolonged interactions within a day and contents of interest across different time periods, we randomly collected 1000 tweets from each of 4 consecutive days over 4 months, capturing tweets from 69 of the influencers on the list. We then employed an exploratory social network analysis (SNA) (Nooraie e t al., 2020) to examine the conversational interactions that could frame the learning relations among our influe ncers. We programmatically built the network ties based on directional mentions and replies, and constructed our network representation using the Force Atlas 2 algorithm in Gephi.

To identify the content relations through which these influencers build their learning (Haythornthwaite, 2019), we employed an open and exploratory coding approach (Sharma & Land, 2019). Twe ets with more than 10 replies were coded by the authors, resulting in a data set of 586 tweets analyzed for the second question in our study. The tweets were independently coded by the authors and through a process of  comparison and discussion for consensus, we identified six main categories of codes: 1) Asking Questions (AQ), 2) Giving Information (GI),

3) Social (SL), 4) Sharing Opinion (SO), 5) Promotions (PR), and 6) Miscellaneous (MI). AQ involved questions

related to NFTs and topics, while GI included tweets providing relevant information about NFT, cryptocurrency, and Web 3.0. SL included community-specific, humor, motivational, emotional, and celebratory tweets, whereas SO involved opinions based on thinking or experience. PR promoted NFT/collections and invited users to engage, and MI included anything unrelated to NFT or NFT topics.

SL, MI, and SO make up the most tweets and 58% of all replies, showing that influencers and their  extended network widely engage in social and conversational behaviors both in domain and non-domain specific topics. ## Discussion and conclusion

Our study found that the learning relations among influencers on Twitter are consistent with those found in other online learning communities, including seeking and giving information and engaging in dialogue (Haythornthwaite, 2022). The influencers in our study formed content relations around giving information, asking questions, and discussing social topics. Our Social Network Analysis (SNA) showe d that these content relations were sustained through a complex network of interactions among influencers and th e larger community. Additionally, qualitative analyses revealed that influencers were committed to contributing domain knowledge and showcasing practices of the collective community (Prestridge et al., 2021). By examining learning networks through the lens of influencers, our study contributes to understanding how learning networks can be found on Twitter. While previous research has focuse d on hashtag congregations (Kimmons et al., 2021), we found that most influencers in our network built their connections  through mentions and replies, suggesting the need to explore shared connections to other influencers  as a tool for finding learning networks on Twitter. Moreover, our study found that the use of multimedia in NFT inf luencers' tweets is significant, with multimedia present in tweets almost 40% of the time across all tweet categories. This highlights the need for further analysis to understand the meaning and context of tweets th rough multimodal discourse analysis. Therefore, alternative methods of data collection and analysis, such a s multimodal machine learning, could be explored to gain a more comprehensive understanding of communication practi ces among niche communities on social media.
