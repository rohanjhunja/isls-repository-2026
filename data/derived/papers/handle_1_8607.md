# Teacher Discourse in Scaffolding a Knowledge Building Group：A Case Study

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In a group of one teacher and 30 students engaged in collaborative knowledge building, the teacher’s 165 posts in Knowledge Forum were parsed out for analysis. A coding scheme was developed to categorize the posts into three patterns, namely, managerial, low order, and high order. While managerial and low order discourse seemed inevitable, high order discourse was more pervasive. The posts also suggested a shifting role of the teacher in scaffolding knowledge building over time.

## Introduction

One important goal of a Computer-supported Collabor ative Learning (CSCL) environment is to foster productive discourse among the learners. A wealth o f studies has examined the effectiveness of a desig ned environment through the analysis of student discour se; however, very few studies have examined the rol e of teacher discourse in fostering productive discourse in CSCL environments. Knowledge building posits that a learning community  shall look ideas as improvable and get them enriched, revised and refined through productive di scourse in Knowledge Forum® (KF), a technologysupported discussion platform. However, people ofte n find it difficult to implement the knowledge buil ding approach because there are no scripts to follow. In knowledge building, learner agency is often stressed and the role of a teacher is described as a facilitator or student helper. While a teacher is suggested to sha dow its existence and let the students fully play their potentials in progressive inquiry, it is important to investigate how a teacher can achieve such a role. This poses a dilemma particularly in the Asian educational context in which a teacher is often regarded as the authority and expected to assume much responsibility in leading the class. To obtain productive interaction and quality discourse, teachers should try to ask more high-cognitivelevel questions. Wang and colleagues (2016) identif ied a few examples of teachers’ classroom questions  that have promoted knowledge building principles. It is also found that higher-order-thinking questions could foster student’s deeper thinking and develop their understanding; and when questions are responsive to studen ts’ earlier contributions and builds on from them, they  can encourage elaborated interaction and promote productive talk activity (Chin, 2006).  Traditionally, teachers’ questioning practices are exercised verbally with students on face-to-face classroom occasions; howev er, many CSCL environments are usually mediated by discussion tools where written messages are exchanged and recorded asynchronously. Therefore, it is important to investigate how a teacher may interact with his students in a discussion forum, aiming to prompt th e whole community towards collective knowledge advances through productive and deep discourse. Two research questions are addressed: (1) What char acterizes the teacher discourse in Knowledge Forum when knowledge building approach is implement ed in a group of Year-1 university students? (2) Ar e there differences on teacher discourse over time (Semester-1 versus Semester-2)?

## Method

This study is based on an earlier knowledge buildin g study in Shanghai, China (Lei & Chan, 2018), whic h showed students in the knowledge building environme nt were engaged in productive knowledge building discourse and made collective knowledge advancement. For this study, the teacher’s KF posts are retrie ved separately for analysis.

Over two semesters and with a group of 30 students  engaged in knowledge building, the teacher contributed a total of 165 KF posts, either in the form of a KF note or a KF annotation. A KF note is typical in the forum, i.e. when a post is composed and submitt ed, it will appear as a tiny square () in the worki ng space where every one of the community could see. While a n annotation will not appear in the open space, it is an attached message to an existent note (); only when a note is clicked open, the annotation can be seen.  All the findings are based on the analysis of the teacher’s  posts in KF; students’ KF notes, especially those prior to or build on the teacher’s posts, are frequently revisited in order to understand the interactional contexts.

In other words, student to student interactions were more salient in the dataset. A qualitative analysis was conducted and teacher’s discourse was ultimately categorized into three patterns in terms of its nature and purpose:(1)  managerial, to encourage and maintain students’ KF writing; (2) low order, to invoke students’ low-cognitive, quick response s; (3) high order, to promote knowledge building principles, i.e. involving students’ collective res ponsibility in pursuing new knowledge. ## Implications

Similar to teachers’ classroom questioning, teachers’ forum discourse alsoneeds to orchestrate students’ inquiry rather than prescribing what they should do (Wang et al., 2016). Traditionally, a majority of teacher questioning is managerial or low-order; however, high order discourse needs to be prevalent in order to stimulate knowledge building principles and productive student discours e. The teacher’s posts were significantly decreased  in semester-2, which suggested less teacher presence a nd more student-to-student interactions in the prog ressive inquiry process. There is a shifting role of teacher and a turning of high level agency to the students over time.
