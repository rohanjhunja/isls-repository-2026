# EnactingBoard: AI-mediated Dialogue for Developing Empathic Negotiation Skills in Higher Education

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study proposed a three-step learning method to cultivate the skill of sustaining dialogue with emotional opponents and implemented it in the form of the EnactingBoard web application. This system integrates generative AI to simulate adversarial dialogue, reflection, empathic understanding, and counterfactual analysis. This study demonstrates how AI-mediated dialogue can provide a novel approach for developing empathic negotiation skills in higher education.

In contemporary society, which increasingly emphasizes  diversity and inclusion, individuals must collaborate with others who hold different values and perspectives. In such contexts, the ability to express opinions, persuade others, and build consensus is essential, and higher education is expected to cultiva te these abilities (UNESCO, 2020). A major obstacle to consensus-building lies not in logic or evidence but in engaging with emotional opponents, who may reject arguments regardless of reasoning. This can lead students to view such opponents  as “monsters” or incomprehensible beings, causing dialogue to collapse. Therefore, the ability to sustain dialogue and pursue consensus with emotional opponents is crucial for university students. However, training in this area has largely been limited to professional conflict resolution (cf., Winslade et al., 1998) and rarely appears in general higher education. This study proposes a learning method supported by an AI-based tool to foster empathic understanding of emotional opponents as a foundation for sustaining dialogue under emotional tension and disagreement.

## Proposed method

We propose the following methods to operationalize three elements. (1) Safe and realistic dialogue practice with emotional opponents: Learners engage in adversarial dialogue with a generative AI acting as an emotional opponent whose persona is defined by the learner. Compared with peer role-play, generative AI can produce more unexpected responses and safely evoke stronger emotions. (2) Scaffolding for e mpathic understanding of emotional opponents: This process unfolds in three stages. First, learners analyze the emotions and background circumstances underlying both parties’ utterances in an adversarial dialogue. Next, they explore past experiences that may underlie the opponent’s hostility while also reflecting on their own past experiences that led them to adopt views opposed to those of the emotional opponents, thereby considering possible similarities between themselves and the opponent. This design draws on prior research demonstrating the role of perceived similarity between the self and others in fostering empathic understanding (Gerace et al., 2015; Hoffman, 2000; Nickerson, 1999). Finally, based on the understanding developed through these analyses, learners re-engage in dialogue to test the adequacy of their interpretation. (3) Expansion of dialogue possibilities for sustained dialogue: Sustaining dialogue requires not only empathic understanding of emotional opponents  but also the ability to anticipate a range of possible dialogue trajectories. To support such reflection, the system generates two counterfactual dialogues, one leading to continued dialogue and the other to rupture, allowing learners to compare actual and possible paths toward sustained dialogue (List, 2006; Morris et al., 2000). Preliminary investigation The proposed method was implemented with 18 university students. Each student identified an opponent, engaged in dialogue, and analyzed the interaction. Generative AI was used for the dialogue and present ation of dialogue trajectories. To examine empathy toward the opponent, students rated the perceived distance between themselves and the opponent using the Inclusion of Other in the Self Scale (Aron et al., 1992) at four time points: before the dialogue task (Step 0), after Step 1, after Step 2, and after Step 3. The mean ratings were 5.40 (0.43), 6.06 (0.33), 3.80 (0.46), and 3.73 (0.39), respectively, with higher values indicating greater distance. Analysis of variance showed a significant effect of step (F(3, 42) = 12.13, p <.001). Holm’s multiple comparison test showed that the perceived distance increased after the dialogue experience and then decreased after Step 2. No significant change 704 was found between Steps 2 and 3. These results suggest that the AI-based dialogue provided a realistic sense of adversarial tension. Furthermore, dialogue analysis and analysis of similarities between the self and the opponent may enhance empathy toward the opponent. Counterfactual reflection did not further increase empathy, possibly because this step served a different function, namely, expanding awareness of dialogue possibilities, although further investigation is needed to confirm this interpretation.  This implementation also suggested the importance of reducing learners’ procedural load for further evaluation of the proposed method. System development: EnactingBoard To support the implementation of the proposed method, w e developed EnactingBoard (Figure 1), a web-based application built on the ChatGPT API. The system provides five modules. (1)  Dialogue: Learners define an emotional opponent’s persona and engage in dialogue through a WhatsApp-style interface. (2) Behind the Lines: The system presents the dialogue transcript for annotation of the emotions and circumstances underlying each utterance (Figure 1 [A]). (3) Persona Exploration: The system displays the opponent’s persona and an AI-generated estimate of the learner’s persona from the opponent’s perspective, supporting reflection on past experiences and possible similarities. (4) Re-dialogue: The system generates a new dialogue session with a hidden emotional key, which learners attempt to identify through continued dialogue. (5) Counterfactual Reflection: The system juxtaposes the learner’s dialogue with two AI-generated alternatives—an ideal end and a disastrous end— while supporting written reflection and the linking of relevant dialogue segments (Figure 1 [B –D]).
