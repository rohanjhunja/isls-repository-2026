# Designing Supports for Science Teachers to Integrate Mathematics into Storyline Science Units

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We examine what professional learning (PL) experiences and resources can support middle school science teachers to integrate mathematics powerfully in storyline science units. We describe the design of PL activities and resources aimed to help teachers integrate mathematics reasoning into storyline science instruction. An analysis of teacher questions and questionnaires from the PL workshop illustrate potential benefits of the resources to teachers.

## Introduction and rationale

Mathematics reasoning is essential for scientists t o engage in scientific practices such as analyzing data and developing models. Science teacher education programs have not typically focused on integrating STEM subjects (Czerniak & Johnson, 2014). Partly as a result, science teachers may not be prepared to support students in using mathematics to engage in scientific practices (e.g., Sagrans et al., 2022). Science teachers will need  professional learning (PL) experiences and supporting resources in order to integrate mathematics in ways that are powerful for investigating, modeling, and explaining scientific phenomena. Storyline science units (Reiser et al., 2021) such as OpenSciEd (Edelson et al., 2021; OpenSciEd, 2024) offer promise for coherently integrating mathematics and practice-based science. Storyline units anchor students’ science work in student-generated ques tions, position students in the role of figuring ou t explanations of phenomena, and compel students to iteratively refine models and explanations through classroom discussion and consensus-building. These curricular features enable students  to use mathemat ics for scientific sensemaking as they engage in scientific practices to generate powerful explanations of phenomena. In this paper, we address the research question: What PL experiences and resources can support middl e school science teachers to integrate mathematics powerfully in storyline science units?

To inform the design of mathematics supports for Op enSciEd teachers, we first conducted a lesson-bylesson analysis of all 18 OpenSciEd middle school u nits. Two key mathematics content areas emerged fro m this analysis: graph comprehension (GC) and covariational reasoning (CoV). GC is the ability to extract meaning from visual representations of data (e.g., Shah & Hoeffn er, 2002), while CoV is the ability to coordinate t wo varying quantities and reason about how those quantities ch ange in relation to each other (e.g., Carlson et al., 2002). Our analysis of the units indicated that GC and CoV are  especially powerful for supporting students in wor king with data and developing models of phenomena. We focus h ere on the design of the PL activities and three ty pes of supporting resources we anticipated would effective ly prepare teachers to support students’ reasoning with GC and CoV in OpenSciEd lessons: (1) mathematics learn ing trajectories, (2) unit analysis supports and (3) data routines. We describe these resources below.

Design of the PL workshop and supporting resources In order to create a meaningful context for emphasizing mathematics in OpenSciEd, we developed a storyline for our teacher PL workshop (Table 1). Engaging teacher s with a PL storyline models storyline-based pedagogy for teachers and helps them to see how tools for teachi ng help address their own questions about teaching.  To help advance teachers’ ability to integrate students’ re asoning with GC and CoV into their science instruct ion, we created the following resources to support teachers’ engagement in the PL storyline activities. GC and CoV learning trajectories described students’ proficiency with these domains at different levels of sophistication and included examples that are re levant to the focal OpenSciEd units. Teachers used them to support their own scientific sensemaking, anticipat e students’ mathematical  strengths and gaps, guide their analysis of the units, and inform discussion prompts and questioning approaches. Unit analysis supports  served as scaffolds for teachers to figure out (1) which unit lessons offer opportunities for students to use GC and CoV powerf ully when engaging with data, modeling, and explana tion and (2) how GC and CoV are consequential to the goals of specific lessons. The unit analysis supports ultimately informed teachers’ lesson adaptations that connect GC and CoV to the lesson phenomena. The Data Routine  (Penuel et al., 2023) helped teachers structure the ir students’ engagement with data into four elements: Framing the Question (e.g., ide ntifying key quantities, making initial predictions), Orienting to the Data (e.g., orienting to and evaluating the features of graphs), Exploring the Data (e.g., crea ting graphs, identifying patterns, making preliminary claims), a nd Engaging in Sensemaking about the Data (e.g., su pporting claims with evidence from data, making predictions). We adapted a pri or version of the Data Routine designed for high school teachers to support the focal units for this study with middle school teachers. In the PL workshop, teachers used the Data Routine as a tool for explor ing the teacher guide, identifying prompts and qu estions that represent each element to inform their lesson adaptation and enactment.
