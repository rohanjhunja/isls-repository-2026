# Placemaking in Dentistry: Enhancing Patient Engagement through Collaborative Art and Digital Storyboards

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This project explored how placemaking principles and participatory art can transform dental offices into engaging and collaborative environments. Over four months (May-September, 2014), 15 patients and four dental specialists co-created digital storyboards, building connections and reducing anxiety within the space. By integrating art and storytelling, the initiative enhanced patient engagement and learning. The findings demonstrate how creativity and collaboration can redefine a healthcare space as an inclusive place that fosters a sense of community.

## Introduction

Placemaking has been explored as a transformative a pproach in various fields, including urban planning  and healthcare. In this context, placemaking refers to the intentional design and adaptation of spaces to foster engagement, interaction, and community-building. Building on the foundational concepts of H enri Lefebvre, scholars have examined how placemaking reimagines spaces to foster engagement and interaction. In healthcare, Rosenberg (2016) demonstrated how collaborative des ign improved patient satisfaction and created more welcoming environments. Collaborative art and desig n, in this study, involve participatory creative pr ocesses where patients actively engage in storytelling and visual expression to enhance their connection to th e space. Inspired by these principles, this study integrates  placemaking with participatory art practices to ex plore how a dental office can evolve into a vibrant community s pace. Specifically, Participatory Art Research (PAR) is used as a structured methodology that actively i nvolves participants in the creative process to doc ument their experiences and foster interaction. In this paper, we describe our exploration of how placemaking prin ciples and participatory art practices can transform dental offices into engaging and collaborative environments. Placemaking principles in healthcare settings Scholars have identified the benefits of collaborative design in health care, including reducing patient anxiety and promoting a sense of comfort and inclusion. For ins tance, participatory arts have shown to give voice to individuals’ health experiences and improve patient engagement (Research Involvement and Engagement, 2023). Similarly, collaborative care models in primary hea lthcare settings have addressed mental health issue s through structured management plans and interprofessional c ommunication (BMC Primary Care, 2020). By integrati ng participatory art practices, healthcare spaces can evolve into inclusive environments where patients f eel valued and engaged.

Building on these principles, this study applied PA R to reimagine a dental office as a collaborative a nd inclusive space. Patients were invited to create digital storyboards featuring artwork, narratives, and AI-generated content using DALL⸱E. Over four months (May-September 2024), 15 participating patients (aged 11 to 70) were guided to co-create AI-assisted digital storyboards sharing their dental visit experiences and lessons learned from dentists or hygienists, with the researcher who fac ilitated the process. Patients contributed individually during their visits, adding to an evolving collection displayed in the clinic. They responded to guided prompts about their experiences, reflections, and the impact of display ed digital artwork on their comfort. T heir responses were transformed into AI-generated visuals using DALL·E through three modes:  verbal responses facilitated by staff, written responses later converted into images, or direct engagement with AI. The storyboards were compiled into a Google Slideshow and displayed in the clinic lobby, allowing patients and staff to engage with the collection. Patients shared perceptions, questions, or requests  about dental visits through the storyboards or cou ld create content of their choosing using methods they  preferred. The storyboards were reviewed and respo nded to by dental professionals and/or other patients. This  approach encouraged active participation, alleviat ed anxiety, and promoted shared learning by enabling patients and professionals to engage with and respond to one another’s contributions. To analyze the impact, patient interactions with the storyboards were observed, informal interviews were conducted with patients and staff, and thematic coding was applied to the narratives and artwork to identify key findings. Rooted in placemaking principles emph asizing co-creating inclusive spaces (Lefebvre,  1991; Rosenberg, 2016), this study reimagined how a dental office can be  as an engaging and collaborative environment.

## Findings

The findings demonstrate that integrating placemaking principles into a dental office transformed a clinical setting into a supportive space. Storyboards and patients’ artwork displayed in the lobby reduced anxiety and encouraged connection. Patients and staff reported that collaborative activities and digital art projects improve d the overall atmosphere, creating a welcoming environment. Emotional comfort and connection.  Patients commented on how the redesigned space made  them feel more at ease and connected. Ryan said, “Getting my smile back” (personal communication, June 14, 2024), reflecting the emotional significance of their visi t, while Leo remarked, “(the digital s toryboard) created a sense of community that we are all connected” (personal communication, August 26, 2024). ## Discussion and conclusion

This study demonstrates that placemaking principles  can transform healthcare environments into collabo rative and engaging spaces. It presents the dental office as both a clinical and participatory learning environment, where connection and emotional comfort are encouraged. Contributions such as artwork and narratives supported shared learning and engagement, with co-created storyboards helping to reduce anxiety and b uild a sense of belonging. This study demonstrates how participatory arts and design can improve patient satisfaction and transfo rm healthcare settings (Research Involvement and Engag ement, 2023; Rosenberg, 2016). These practices refl ect Lefebvre’s (1991) understanding of spaces as shaped  through  interactions and lived experiences. This project adds to existing literature by positioning placemaking as a practice that involves learning through engagement in the healthcare setting, a combination rarely addres sed in either field. The poster presentation descri bes how placemaking was incorporated into a dental space, h ow storyboard contributed to communication about de ntal care, and what this approach may suggest for creating environments that reduce barriers and support participation. Institutions may benefit from considering clinical spaces as settings that respond to both educational  and emotional needs across diverse communities.
