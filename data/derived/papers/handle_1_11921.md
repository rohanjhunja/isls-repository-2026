# Behavioral Transition Patterns in Online Collaborative Problem-Solving: A Lag Sequential Analysis of High School Teams

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Understanding behavioral patterns in collaborative problem-solving (CPS) is crucial for developing effective educational interventions. However, previous studies have not fully explored the most basic behavioral transition patterns. Here, we present a lag sequential analysis of behavioral transitions in online CPS tasks among high school students (229 teams). Using the PISA 2015 CPS framework, we identify 34 significant behavioral transitions, revealing four patterns. Our findings suggest effective CPS interventions should strengthen planning and monitoring behaviors while minimizing task-irrelevant interactions.

## Background

Collaborative Problem-solving (CPS) has emerged as a critical 21st-century competency  (OECD, 2017). Analyzing sequential patterns in CPS activities hel ps to understand how students conduct cognition, de cisionmaking, and problem solving in the collaborative pr ocess. Among the many sequential pattern mining methods, Lag Sequential Analysis (LSA)  can uncover students' underlying characteristics throug h behavioral patterns. However, previous LSA studies usually use CPS tasks involvin g physical domain knowledge  (Chang, Chang, Chiu, et al., 2017), while some studies have shown that the process of collaboration and problem-solving may be affected by the domain knowledge of team members re lated to CPS tasks (Funke & Frensch, 2007), so the most basic CPS collaboration patterns cannot be obtained. This study uses a task that is not related to domain knowledge to obtain process data in the most general case. By analyzing process data from high school students in an online CPS task to identify and characterize behavioral pa tterns in collaborative problem-solving. By examining these patterns, we aim to enhance understanding of team behavioral differences across varying CPS skill levels.

## Participants

840 students aged between 17 and 18 were recruited from a high school in Hefei, China, rando mly assigned to 280 teams, collaborating with 2 anonymous peers. Af ter excluding 21 teams that left early or experienc ed abnormal disconnections and 30 teams showing irregular operations or passive participation, 229 remained.

## Task design and data collection

The study employed an online collaborative problem-solving task called "Mondrian's Geometric Fantasy" (Qizhi et al., 2024), requiring three randomly assigned participants to c omplete a jigsaw puzzle. They could see the puzzle areas of other members but not their own. Pa rticipants could only translate or rotate their own  puzzle pieces. The task lasted 40 minutes, requiring completion of  both levels within the time limit, with the final score determined by the total number of assembled pieces.  The data was co llected between September 5-7, 2023. A total of 241,906 rows of valid process data were collected, including 25,332 rows of valid dialogue data.

## Coding process and data analysis

To explore the CPS capabilities represented in the p rocess data, this study adopted the PISA 2015 CPS skill framework, in which  CPS skills are organized into a matrix comprising 4 problem-solving dimensions and 3 collaborative dimensions (OECD, 2017). Dialogue irrelevant to the task was c oded as 'U' for "unrelated". Two coders were trained to code the process data. In the first round, they coded 8 data sets (kappa =  0.772). In the second round, 5 data sets were coded (kappa = 0.777). Afterward, they randomly distributed the remaining data. This study used LSA to analyze student process data to find out students' basic behavioral transformation patterns in this CPS activity. Then the network analysis software Gephi 0.10.1 was  used to perform community detection and identify the pattern relationship between important behavioral sequences. Finally, the study divided the teams into a high-performance group (the first 27%, 68 teams) and a l ow-performance group (the last 27%, 65 teams), and used independent sample t-tests to analyze the inter-group differences in the frequency of their transformations.

## Results and discussion

According to the theory of LSA, the study found 34 significant behavioral transitio ns, including 12 self-transformations and 22 transitions between differen t behaviors. Based on the community detection resul ts, the study constructed a behavioral transition diagram.

Different colors  represent different communities. Finally, the independent sample t-test results identified 7 significant behavioral transition frequencies, among which the high-score group C1-C2 and C2-D2 had a higher frequency of mutual transitions, th e C2B2 had a higher frequency of transitions, and the low-score group C3 and U had a higher frequency of self-conversions. Based on the above results, the study identified the following behavioral trans formation patterns. First, planning and action coordination are the core activities of the team, and the high-scoring group tends to make and execute more plans; second, goal setting and task c larification are the basic activities of the team; third, the feedback-driven monitoring cycle is very important in CPS activities, and the high-performance group shows a greater tendency to monitor and reflect on the resu lts of plan execution; fourth, the task focus inter ruption phenomenon is also significant in CPS activities, a nd the poor-performing group may have more task-irrelevant conversations. However, more analysis is needed in other assessment situations without domain knowledg e to find commonalities in CPS behavior patterns and obtain more universal CPS behavior change patterns.
