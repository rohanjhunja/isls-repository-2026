# Traces of Transformation: An Artist-in-School-Community Project

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract

T races of Transformation: An Artist-in-School-Community Project Traces of Transformation is a co-created artist-in-school-community project emerging from Fall of Artica (FoA), developed through a partnership between Artivition Projects (represented by artist Kathy H. Zhou) and the interdisciplinary Art and Design course (IDC) at University of Toronto Schools, led by teacher Robin Michel. Building on the Culture Shoes Project, students designed shoes embodying their FoA avatars, using gameplay to surface ideological tensions — safety vs. freedom, now vs. future, utility vs. culture. Through research, discussion, and critical reflection, each student imprinted a personal message onto their shoe sole. These Adobe Illustrator designs were realized as laser-cut rubber stamps at the UTS innovation hub. Echoing Allora and Calzadilla's politically engaged interventions, this critical-making work asks: What marks do we leave behind as learners, and how do these traces shape the paths available to those who follow? Zhou, Robin Michel, Rubina Khan Latina/x Voces: Comunidad, Creaciones, y Casa “Latina/x Voces: Comunidad, Creaciones, y Casa” is a celebration of a multi-year Participatory Design Research project aimed at amplifying the presence of Latina/x youth in museums through co-creation of exhibitions. This exhibition includes selected artworks created by Latina/x youth during after-school programs and a summer camp between 2021-2025. These artworks were displayed previously at the Krannert Art Museum in Champaign, IL. Each year, the artists identified themes that represented their collective works. The “Comunidad” exhibition showcased languages, community spaces, and cultural foods. The “Creaciones” exhibition explored the intersection of Latina/x and youth cultures. Finally, the “Casa” exhibition examined what it means to gather at home for formal and informal occasions. In addition to increasing authentic representation of Latina/x youth in museums, this project has contributed to research about multilingual and multicultural literacy practices, participatory methodologies, and visitors’ appreciation for and sense of belonging in exhibitions designed by marginalized groups. Project co-directors are Catherine Dornfeld Tissenbaum, Mónica González Ybarra, and Idalia Nuñez. “Core” artists include Darly Chen Man, Evolet Cruz Hernandez, Ashley Rebolledo Hernández, Kasey Rivas Lagos, and Mayra Valencia. Past artists include Gabriela Aguilar, Marvel Briones-Rebolledo, Maria Francisco, Leilani Hernandez, Rubi Lopez Felix, Yuridia Lopez Felix, Joyce Miralez. Culture & Identity: Creating Meaningful Spaces Through Collaborative Art This mural encompasses a variety of emotions, feelings, and perspectives from artists in their path to understanding culture and how it shapes their identity. I believe that creating a collaborative mural is a beautiful visual because it showcases how culture can connect and bring us closer together while also celebrating the differences in all of our cultures and identities and how that uniqueness makes us who we are. Additionally, I think the collaboration and reflection allowed for us to also express the nuances of culture, such as: how cultures can be mixed, how culture can be more than race, gender, sexuality but that communities also build culture, and that culture is not a one sized fits all experience but an ever evolving and growing feeling that constantly changes through time. Sanjana Vinjamuri, Allison Starks
