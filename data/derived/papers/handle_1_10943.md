# Leveraging Generative Artificial Intelligence to Support Students in Learning Programming: A Chatbot Pilot Study

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In the age of generative AI (GenAI), it is crucial to prioritize the prevalence of GenAI and its effect on student learning. This study explores how students can benefit from feedback generated by GenAI in learning programming. Applying a qualitative descriptive approach in research, the findings show that students' interactions with the GenAI-chatbot, their attitudes, and the amount of time they spend reading, comprehending, and applying the bot's feedback all impact how effective the bot is. The findings of the study will help instructors understand how best to support their students in learning programming with a GenAI-chatbot and provide crucial evidence to advance the field of educational technology development.

## Introduction

In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape, GenAI is at the vanguard. GenAI has been explored and used in many ways in education such as a cognitive exten der and a collaborative tool (Lodge et al., 2023). GenAI has even been suggested as a remedy to solve the difficulties teachers encounter in meeting the needs of each student (Kaplan-Rakowski et al., 2023). Indeed, GenAI has several a ffordances such as being able to be adaptive and provide personalized responses to students.  However, there are biases and other issues with GenAI but these can be harnessed appropriately (UNESCO, 2023). In the d omain of computing education, GenAI models have the potential to revolutionize the way computing course s are taught and how students learn programming. Ge nAI models can be used as a personal tutor for students  to learn programming to strengthen their reasoning  and logic skills. However, these models are highly reliant on  the input prompts they receive, and the skill of c rafting effective prompts that yield accurate solutions will become crucial for students. Studies have looked at how GenAI chatbots can be utilized in education, but there is  a scarcity of studies analyzing the outcome, effec tiveness, or how GenAI chatbots affect the learning process (Yil maz & Yilmaz, 2023). Therefore, we aim to address t he knowledge gaps by examining the effect of GenAI cha tbot feedback to help students learn computer programming.

## Research design and method

MyBotBuddy is a chatbot developed based on the GenAI model to scaffold and provide students with step-by-step instructions on completing programming tasks. Prompt engineering was leveraged in the development  of MyBotBuddy which involves programming large language models through tailored prompts. The tailored prompts include breaking down a programming problem into smaller pr oblems and giving step-by-step instructions on completing the task. The training data was preprocessed and filtered t o exclude damaging or biased language. Additionally, the training data sources were divers ified, and bias detection and mitigation approaches  were included. MyBotBuddy allows students to check mistakes in their written code and query line(s) of code they do not understand. The GenAI chatbot  is also able to generate code based on students’ re quests, simplify the generated code, clarify and elaborate on code, and break down the programming problems. MyBotBuddy was pilot trialed in a secondary school in Singapore.  A quasi-experimental study was conducted to examine the impact of feedback from th e GenAI model on students' programming learning.  Due to the limited number of students, we did not design a comparison group. Ethical consent was obtained from students, parents and the government ministry. As a half-day in-class program, students participated in a pre-test, followed by an intervention using MyBotBuddy and a post-test and survey. The pre-test was to use computational thinking approaches where students candecompose the problem into smaller parts and abstract the important information to solve a validation problem. They were to use algorithmic thinking to sequence th e steps to validate an ISBN-13 number and implement the algorithm in Python code using Google Collaboratory. The intervention involved using MyBotBuddy to improve their pre-test code. The post-test was a knowledge test requiring students to validate an ISBN-10 number. Based on the overall intervention, this study identified two contrasting cases: student X (with score increment from pre to post-test) and student Y (without score increment). The study used an observ ational and qualitative approach to analyze the differences in student interaction with MyBotBuddy.

## Findings

The Python codes for the pre-and post-tests were written before and after the interventio n respectively. The knowledge test questions were graded based on the r ubric prepared by the subject matter experts. Stude nt X had an increase of 33% score from the pre to post-test, while student Y had no increase in scores. Student X learned to craft a solution using the new  concepts (eg: enumeration) through interaction with MyBotBuddy, which resulted in a considerable improvement in t he pre-test performance. Student X took time to study the feedback and debug the code, eventually a dding more lines of code for it to work. The intera ction involved several clarifications and iterative learn ing. Student Y interacted differently with MyBotBuddy and did not seem to learn from the intervention. Student Y hard-coded answers for both pre-and post-tests, asking MyBotBuddy to simplify and further simplify the code. Despite receiving a rating of 60 for the code and how the code could be improved, student Y did not utilize t he feedback but asked non-task-related questions. The interaction with MyBotBuddy was an iterative process to improve the code and introduce new concepts.

## Discussion and conclusion

This paper highlights two different contrasting cas es of student interactions with MyBotBuddy, a GenAI model for learning programming. Student X clearly used the chatbot to learn new computing con cepts and scored well while student Y did not. It seemed that student  Y did not know what to ask and focus on during the ch atbot interaction and was perhaps not ready to learn more  advanced computational concepts. These two contras ting cases raise several questions regarding GenAI in te aching and learning. What cou ld possibly motivate and steer student Y in a more successful direction? Could more human in tervention in the form of a teacher who would have more potential to understand the context and need of learners be the added resource? Or could there be other ways that the bot could have scaffolded the student ? As for student X, who demonstrates the power of GenAI to be a personal tutor, and collaborated well with GenAI (Lodge et al., 202 3), would the GenAI chatbot be the new defacto way of learning, superseding traditional didactic methods? MyBotBuddy has shown potential in teaching and learning, generating feedback and serving as a virtual tutor. However, its effectiveness depends on studen ts' interaction, attitudes, and time spent reading and understanding the bot. Students need to study the code carefully and convert abstract problem descrip tions into executable steps. GenAI models candevelop into mor e capable tools, but they must be used alongside hu man supervision and direction. Marrying GenAI with human computer interaction and pedagogies is therefore essential for leveraging GenAI to support all students and enhance their learning.
