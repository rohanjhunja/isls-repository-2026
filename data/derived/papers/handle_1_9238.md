# Misinformation-Based Dialogical Construction of Misconceptions on the Internet. A Literature Review Based on Automated Publication Analysis

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Aiming at a deeper understanding of how Internet users construct misconceptions starting from misinformation, we combine dialogism, a social knowledge construction theory, and knowledge in pieces, a theory describing misconceptions. In search of empirical findings pertaining to both theories, we applied natural language processing methods to identify the most central publications that we subsequently analyzed manually. The findings reveal six variables involved in the dialogic development and consolidation of misconceptions through online discussions. This suggests a model of dialogical construction of misconceptions that will be validated in further research.

## Introduction

The Internet includes many spaces for dialogic knowledge construction. At the sa me time, misinformation is prominently disseminated on the Internet. Seeking to understand more in-depth the  negative influence misinformation may have on individual and social everyday life, we assumed that dia logic knowledge construction based on misinformation creates, consolidates, and propagates misconceptions. One of the theories explaining the social construction of knowledge is dialogism (T rausan-Matu et al., 2021), in which “voice” is the central concept. A voice is an expression of a n idea, and can be uttered by one or more dialog participants, who can have themselves one or more voices. In the dialog, voices converge and diverge, appear and disappear, creating an intertextual structure similar to polyphony in music. This view allowed the development of automated dialog analysis procedures and software, such as ReaderBench (Dascalu et al., 2015). At cognitive level, diSessa (2018) describes misconceptions in his theory of knowledge in pieces (KiP). Accordingly, individual knowledge includes so-called phenomenological primitive (p-prims) that can be achieved by everyday experience or intuition. As information is further received, it can connect to p-prims resulting in fragmented misconceptions. Integrating even more information, these misconcept ions become coherent, stable, and hard to correct. So far, KiP allowed identifying knowledge types involved in misconceptions, understanding failure in knowledge construction, and making recommendations for instructional design. These two theories have initially different scopes and, to the best of our knowledge, were not explicitly combined so far. Nevertheless, the research literature might contain  findings that pertain to both. Therefore, we asked the following research question: According to current research literature, which factors may influence the dialogic development and consolidation of misconceptions in online discussions?  Answering this question may allow the development of instruments that automatically identify spaces on the Internet, such as echo chambers (Lewandowsky et al., 2020), where misconceptions are dialogically constructed based on misinformation. Moreover, additional knowledge on the dialogical construction of misconceptions may ground inf ormation literacy interventions. Both consequences may help combating misinformation and its consequences.

## Method

The literature search started by entering the keyword “online user comment ” to five journals with highest relevance for commenting behavior and four international academic databases. Exc luding studies focused on participatory journalism, social bots, and clinical psychology, we yielded a sample consisting of 151 studies published in English language between 2006 and 2021. This corpus was subjected to automated content analysis based on natural language processing, in order to identify the most central, i.e., representative publications. We used the processing pipeline from ReaderBench (Dascalu et al., 2015), version 0.11 (https://pypi.org/project/rbpyrb/) building a publication graph based on the semantic similarities of the abstrac ts. We manually analyzed the article content in decreasing order of the article degree centrality, codi ng the most frequent discussion topics, CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 375 attributes of the information presentation in dialog, and the effects of the comments on users and discussions. Saturation was reached after the first 26 articles.

## Findings

Our publications analysis yielded so far six variables involved in the dialogic deve lopment and consolidation of misconceptions through online discussions: the discussion topics, the presence of  disagreement, the degree to which opinions are justified, the politeness vs. uncivility of expressions, the ne gativity of information presentation, and users’ perceptions of opinion camps and majority. More specifically, politics represented a ubiquitous discussion topic, paired w ith frequent negative presentation and controversies. Within this context, opinions often carried a strong and ne gative emotional load, rather than being knowledge-based and justified.

Politely worded, disagreements could encourage the expression of justifications, reduce dissent, and lead to longer discussions. On the one hand, uncivil comments attracted attention a nd triggered even more uncivil comments and negative emotions. At the same time, uncivil comments shortened  discussions and demotivated users with different opinions from online participation. In consequence, the users sustaining an opinion by uncivil comments became the majority in online discussion groups. The perception of opinion camps in discussions also influenced commenting behavior, such that users who perceived themselves as belonging to a majority tended to post statements with little or no justification, which also shortened discussions and provoked uncivility.

## Discussion and conclusion

Within the conceptual framework consisting of dialogism (Trausan-Matu e t al., 2021) and KiP (diSessa, 2018), the findings of the publication analysis suggest a model of how misconceptions can be socially constructed starting from misinformation. The process may begin with misinformation providing Interne t users with p-prims, which results in unstable fragmented misconceptions. Secondly, users with a fragmented misconception integrate the pprim and create coherent misconceptions (diSessa, 2018). Thirdly, in the online environment, proponents of the misconceptions suppress divergent voices by means of uncivil opinion expressions, i.e., verbal violence, creating a community of likeminded people confirming each other’s views, i.e., convergent voi ces in a  so called echo chamber (Lewandowsky et al., 2020). The divergent voices become a minority, get silent, and disappear from the discussion (Trausan-Matu et al., 2021). In this cyclic process, each repe tition leads to higher availability of the coherent misconception, which in turn increases the salience and applicabi lity of the misinformation (diSessa, 2018), which thus becomes familiar, feels true, and gets intuitively confirmed in discussions. The validity of our study is potentially limited by the strongly selective method of publications analysis and, from a larger perspective, by the current state of knowledge in dialogic knowledge construction and misinformation research. Further research is called to substantiate th e model we propose and, methodologically, to additionally validate the automated analysis. For educational practice, the proposed model may be a ground for approaches to information and media literacy. These may include teaching and tra ining cognitive strategies for evaluating online information, as well as behavioral strategies for dealing with dominating voices and supporting opinion diversity and critical thinking in online environments. Algorithms and software may be developed to spot dialogic spaces on the Internet where misconceptions are developed and disseminated.
