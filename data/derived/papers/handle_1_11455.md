# Developing a Framework for Assessment of Learning for Flourishing with Living Heritage in Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This practice paper shares our insights about the journey we took in co-designing an innovative model of assessment for learning with living heritage as a foundation of learning. Although attempts to integrate learning with heritage in curriculum have been documented, there is paucity of information on assessing these practices. This paper reports the development process for an assessment practice tool which draws from the co-design process carried out with teachers in the two selected case study countries, Namibia and Zimbabwe, and the pilot online course gathering an international reference group of teachers. Teachers made significant contributions in re-structuring and strengthening the assessment model through the implementation of focus group workshops, surveys, and of the toolkit, resulting in the addition of two critical petals to the Education for Sustainable Development assessment for significant learning model, which we originally utilized as an anchor to the work that we were doing.

## Introduction

This practice paper shares work undertaken to innovate a model of ‘assessment for learning’ (Earl, Lorna, 2003), with living heritage as a foundation of learning (UNESCO, 2024). The project was developed in partnership with UNESCO in the context of the global program on Safeguarding Intangible Cultural Heritage through Formal and Non-Formal Education. This program is part of and contributes to UNESCO’s work for strengthening educational policy, foregrounding appreciation of cultural diversity in advancing sustainability, as a foundation of Sustainable Development Goal 4 on inclusive and quality education, and more specifically, Target 4.7 (UN, 2015). The rationale for our project and the current paper is that culture, and in specific, living heritage, is included as a foundation of quality education in re vised and new international policy frameworks (UNESCO 2003, 2022, 2023 and 2024), contributing to increasing its integration into formal and non-formal education (Moreeng, 2014). However, asses sing living heritage’s contribution to learning outcomes  is not yet  sufficiently covered by formal assessment systems (UNESCO, 2021; King, 2024), which, in turn negatively affects its longerterm integration into curricula. This is in line wi th Redman et. al (2021) who content that despite its widely acknowledged importance, assessment is substantiall y underdeveloped both in theory and practice. Adapted and innovative assessment approaches on living heritage contribution to learning outcomes in education are required (Inoue, 2004, UNESCO, 2021).  Our paper focusses on the first objective of th e project, which was to develop a framework with an innovative set of tools for teachers and educators to assess the specific learning outcomes and added value of learning with living heritage  in the classroom. In this limited number of pages, we will share insights and reflections about the process of co-designing the “Assessment of Learning for Flourishing” as a living tool for transformative assessment practices for te achers which involved three stages: first, review of history of assessment and significant learning framework; seco ndly co-designing the framework; and lastly, finalizing the framework.

Before unpacking each of these stages, we want to briefly mention ou r understanding of some of key concepts of this paper. Our approach to living heritage draw s from the UNESCO Convention (2003), but also echoing the growing body of research on culture as a foundation of learning (Gutiérrez & Rogoff, 2003; Nasir et. al, 2020), and the more recent approaches on living heritage as part of the global public goods, having the capacity to the realization of the sustainable development goals (UNESCO, 2022; Wulf, 2025). Assessment for learning in our research is referred to as an approach, integrated into teaching and learning,  which creates feedback for learners and teachers in order to improve learning and guide  their next steps (Wiliam, 2009). Finally, addressing quality education with a transformative lens, requires partnership work between the research team and the main beneficiaries of this work, which are the teachers who will put into practice the results of this short research project (Heron & Reason, 1997; Nind, 2011; TESF, 2021). This also responds to our interest in conducting research that has the potential to positively impact teaching and learning practices and educational policy (TESF, 2021). Review of history of assessment and the significant learning framework The project started with a brief review of existing work on  history of assessment  to build a clear narrative to reframe assessment in a way that will be relevant to assessment learning with living heritage as a foundation. We sought to understanding traditional forms of assess ment associated with learning practices in local co mmunity contexts (Gutiérrez & Rogoff, 2003; Rosado-May et. al, 2020) and traced more recent forms of assessment to the advent of formalized and individualized assessment approaches that developed with the modern (colonial) educational institution  (Nasir et. al, 2020, Shumba et. al, 2021; Redman et. al, 2021), to contemporary trends involving AI assessment tools.  In this research we developed the toolkit with an u nderstanding that assessment functions as an "act of communication about what we  value" (Boud, 2000), and what gets assessed and gr aded forms the "real curriculum" that shapes learners' strategic rationales for allocating time and focus (Serrano et. al., 2018). Thus, we developed a critical analysis of this in relation to the inte ntions and objectives of integrating living heritage into education  as a decolonial and culturally relevant praxis that  can advance quality education (Moreeng, 2014), seeking out ways of thinking assessment as integral to processes of transformative learning or as assessment as initiator of change (Wulf, 2025). We also drew on recent work done on framing more expansive assessment models for social learning and for a wider framing of transformative learning that involves the socialemotional, the cognitive and action-oriented intentions as per the transformative learn ing approach (Schnepfleitner & Ferreira, 2021).

To develop these paradigms further in relation to transformative education intentions, we initially worked with a model that was developed in southern Africa by Education for Sustainable Development practition ers in the UNESCO Sustainability Starts with Teachers prog ram (Shumba et. al, 2021) adapted from the work of  Fink

(2003) who introduced the idea of assessing significant le arning in a more holistic manner. Th is model involves

six dimensions of assessment including foundational  knowledge, application, integration, human and eco logical relations, caring / ubuntu (empathy and compassion for self, other li fe forms and the world around him/her), and learning to learn, which were adapted to an ESD approach. While this research offered progress in conceptualizing transformative assessment for learning with living heritage, we noted that there were still elements that remained under-developed in the frameworks, notably:

1) how these integrate the positive psychology concepts of ‘fullness of being’ and ‘flourishing

2) how these integrate to support relational identity formation and personal and collective well-being as

key aspects of living heritage foundations

3) how these integrate to support cultural and situational resonance

We also noted a need to interrogate and develop these in relation to:

4) the intentionality of cultural transmission as encompassed by communities’ living heritage

5) how transformative assessment of learning with living heritage  can advance notions of quality

education as envisaged by SDG 4.7 We identified that these 5 dimensions were crucial for advancing assessment of learning with living heritage, which can significantly enhance and transform teacher assessment practices  for more humanistic education outcomes. The humanistic intentions promote iterative relationship between pedagogy and assessment is considered as learning (assessment as learning m antra). With this as background, we noted that for assessin g learning with living heritage, it is therefore impo rtant to explicitly transgress normalized forms of assessmen t practice and develop alternative metaphors that are  attuned to the intention of foregrounding living h eritage in education as a foundation of learning. The review and conceptualization process above, were complemented by a search for appropriate metaphors  for communicating a broader, more holistic and situ ated view of assessment. We felt that this was necessary as dominant theorizing and practices of assessment tend towards an unfortunate reduction to attend individual cognitive development, which leaves assessment being equated to measuremen t and judgement of the individual  (Nasir et. al, 2020). Drawing from positive psychology, in which flourishing is ‘when people experience positive emotions,  positive psychological functioning and positive soc ial functioning, most of the time,’ (Frederickson & Losada, 2005), and taking inspiration from the long history of symbolism and cultural meaning that has been ascribed to flowers by many cultures around the world, we ended up using a flower metaphor. This metaphor communicates the transformative and holistic approach to assessm ent and helped us conceptualize the framework of ‘Assessment of Learning for Flourishing’. This background work was crucial to establish posit ive starting points for our practice project to then engage with teachers to further adapt the framework, and related language and tools. The key question for this study was: How can we and teachers co-develop a Framework for Assessment of Learning for F lourishing with Living Heritage?

Co-design of the assessment of learning for flourishing framework and toolkit The second step in the process was the co-design (Nind, 2011; TESF, 2021; Wardak et. al., 2024) of the Assessment of Learning for Flourishing framework with teachers through two face-to-face workshops in Namibia and Zimbabwe, tested during a global online course  addressed to teachers. Since this practice research aims to impact the assessment practices of teachers, we considered vital to develop the framework along with the tools (i.e. toolkit and online course)  that could enable them to use it in a meaningful way.  The framework and toolkit are seen as an interrelated practice tool.

Face-to-face workshops in Namibia and Zimbabwe Building on the preliminary research and framing work outlined above, the workshops were carefully designed to focus on pre-testing the ESD assessment for significant learning framework as an alternative assessment approach in the context of teaching and learning with living heritage experiences. To start the process of data gathering, protocols were observed with the parent Ministries and with the support of partner organizations. This process involved engaging teachers grounding them in their own living heritage and experiences, learning how they are working with living heritage in situating their lea rning and assessment activities in their teaching p ractice. Furthermore, the exchanges proceeded to sharing knowledge and experiences on h ow teachers addressed living heritage in an ethical and generative manner. This extended to exploring teachers’ assessment practices and overall experience of assessment for them and for t he learners. Having descried  the overall experience of assessment as “stressful”,  we r eflected on the need for innovative and transformative asses sment practices, grounded in the formal curriculum, and how could these enhance flourishing of the learners. The flower metaphor had significant resonance with teachers views and b ased on the ESD Assessment for Significant Learning Framework (Figure 1, image a), we explored what other components could be added based on their learning with living heritage experiences. In total, they identified 12 additional dimensions (Figure 1, images b and c). After the workshop, the research team worked on reviewing teachers input to improve the framework. Some contributions could be encompassed in the six existing dimensions, but others pointed the need to further highlight living heritage role in personal and collective identify formation, and in developing learner’s agency. A first draft of the framework and related toolkit was then developed (see Figure 2 on the side) which was constituted by the following combination of elements (e.g. 1 e ducators’ workbook, 6 information sheets, 5 domain small cards, 6 activity small cards, 19 transformative pedagogy small cards, 9 transformative assessment strategy small cards).

Testing and refinement through a pilot online course Once the toolkit was complete in its first draft, integrating UNESCO minor feedback, we developed an online course as a mediating tool to test the framework (van Staden & Lotz-Sisitka, 2023), which was then run for 23 educators from 8 countries, who had previously engaged in living heritage learning experiences. The framework and toolkit guided the development of the course which, in turn, was used to pilot and refine the framework. Based on the post course survey, participants considered that the framework practice tool improved participants´ understanding of living heritage learning experiences, and transformative assessment, meeting their expectations. Some reflected that it took them back to their own living heritage expe riences, and this was interesting. Others showed st rong interest in the Assessment of Learning for Flourishing framework, and the transformative pedagogies. Others also reflected on the online course tools and modalities that were learner-centered and interactive. Finalizing the framework Teachers’ input following the interactive and iterative codesign and pilot testing process  showed that the ESD framework could be improved by adding contextual features making a structure reminiscent of a flower, hence the flower diagram which was consolidated into the Assessment of Learning for  Flourishing Framework (see Figure 3). Importantly, we found that teachers related well to the metaphor and concept of ‘flourishing’ when applied to assessment and this allowed them to engage more creatively with concepts of assessment, and its application to living heritage learning experiences.

Reflecting the above-mentioned inputs, thinking and experiences of the teachers regarding the contributions of living heritage to learners learning, we included two additional petals. The “Cultural Identity and Self-Esteem” petal highlights the importance of developing a sense of identity and belonging, as well as self-esteem, and love for their  and other  cultures, communities, and family roots, in the flourishing of learners. The petal “Relationality and Learners’ Agency” further strengthens our understanding of how learners can personally and collectively shape their learning experiences towards agency for change and build more substantial relations for co-engaged meaning-making.

## Conclusion

This work contributes to advancing transformative approaches of assessment at practice, conceptual, and political level, while promoting recognition and visibility of communities living heritage as a foundation of an education for the common good and for all. Following teachers’ feedback, we identified three dimensions to take this practice tool forward:

• Further pedagogical adaptation: There is need to consider options of cohorts of teaching educator s

(primary school teachers, secondary school, teacher  educators,  among others) for more sustained engagement with participants and planning for adapt ation to their needs. Considering special needs is another stream to enquire requested by teachers.

• Build back-end data: There is need to follow up by engaging in a larger process to gather and analyze

data from the Change Projects and the Course to bui ld the case for how living heritage supports improved learning outcomes that are related to the curriculum. It will also be important to further learn on how this framework relates and aligns with  national curriculum assessment frameworks contributing to a transformative shift in learning assessment. This should be constituted as a longerterm evaluation process associated with teachers’ feedback.

• Expand scope and reach of the work: There is alsoneed to train country-based trainers of trainers and

facilitators to cater for teachers situated in area s where there are challenges in accessing internet facilities. The course should be translated and dis seminated in a variety of forms and languages, contextually adapted as a constant living and interactive tool.
