# Human–AI Learning Alignment in Situated Contexts: A Human-Centered Framework

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
As generative artificial intelligence (AI) transforms education, many existing approaches to AI in education emphasize task completion while overlooking learner agency and the sociotechnical nature of learning. This gap limits our ability to understand how AI supports learning in situated contexts. In this paper, we define human–AI learning alignment and present TEACH-AI (Trustworthy and Effective AI Classroom Heuristics) as a learner-centered set of analytical dimensions spanning interactional, learning-oriented, and sociotechnical perspectives. Through an illustrative case analysis, we discuss how this perspective can be used to analyze AI-supported learning settings. This work contributes a conceptual foundation for understanding how generative AI supports learning in situated contexts.

As generative AI (GenAI) becomes integrated into learning environments, existing approaches emphasize task completion while overlooking learner agency, interaction dynamics, and pedagogical goals. Prior work highlights the importance of contextualized, lea rner-centered support (Shaffer & Resnick, 1999; Papert, 1993). However, generative AI introduces challenges such as over-reliance, limited socio-emotional support, and risks such as bias and inaccuracy (Bender et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024). At the same time, many HCI and human –AI approaches rely on general criteria (e.g., clarity, usefulness), domain-specific systems, or standardized tasks (Amershi et al., 2019; Faruk et al., 2025), limiting their ability to capture how AI behavior aligns with pedagogica l goals in authentic learning contexts (Rosenfeld, 2021; Ribera et al., 2019; Puig et al., 2020). This paper asks: what characterizes effective human –AI learning alignment, and how can these insights inform the design and understanding of generative AI sys tems? We argue that current approaches fail to capture this alignment, particularly in situated learning contexts. To address this gap, we introduce human–AI learning alignment, which characterizes how AI-generated support aligns with learners’ cognitive needs, pedagogical objectives, and interaction context in situated settings (Wang et al., 2024; Ding & Magerko, 2026). We present TEACH-AI, a set of analytical dimensions that integrate pedagogical goals and interaction dynamics to support context-aware learning alignment and describe it through TEACH-AI as analytical dimensions built in the literature from AI, HCI, and the learning sciences; (b) we show how this perspective supports context-aware, pedagogically grounded Following Arksey and O’Malley’s (2005) framework for scoping reviews, we synthesize prior work to identify gaps in understanding generative AI in learning contexts. We reviewed Google Scholar and major venues (e.g., CHI, IDC, AIED) using keywords related to human-centered AI and educational AI systems. In total, we reviewed 126 sources across HCI, education, responsible AI, and generative AI from the pre-LLM era (before 2017) to the generative AI boom (2023–present). We iteratively coded the literature through thematic grouping and identified recurring concepts across sources. Disagreements in categorization were resolved through discussion and consultation with a senior faculty advisor to ensure consistency. Based on this synthesis, we organized the framework into three levels with eight components capturing human– AI learning alignment, and illustrated it through a case analysis.

## Illustrative case study

To illustrate this perspective, we show how TEACH-AI can be used to analyze generative AI assistants in situated learning contexts. The framework can be flexibly applied across different learning scenarios and priorities. For example, an AI assistant may guide learners step-by-step toward a solution but limit opportunities for independent learning, indicating a misalignment with pedagogical goals. This example highlights how TEACH-AI supports context-sensitive analysis of human–AI learning alignment. In summary, we introduce human– AI learning alignment and TEACH-AI, a framework for analyzing alignment across feedback, learning, and sociotechnical dimensions in context. Future work will focus on validating this framework in real-world settings.
