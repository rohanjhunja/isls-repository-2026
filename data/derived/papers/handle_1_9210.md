# Promoting Undergraduates’ STEM Understanding in Knowledge-Building Environment

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study designed a Knowledge-Building Environment (KBE) to promote undergraduates’ STEM understanding. 171 undergraduates worked in different groups in terms of their interests in STEM topics and collaboratively produced the artifacts. The quantitative findings suggest that the designed knowledge-building environment promoted students’ STEM understanding successfully. The quantitative findings suggested that the designed KBE promoted students’ STEM understanding successfully. Specifically, knowledge-building activities and artifacts fabricating facilitated their STEM understanding. The qualitative analysis illustrated the way a KBE helps students implement STEM projects. Implications of developing knowledge building approach for STEM learning are discussed.

## Introduction

A better understanding of STEM is crucial for education students who will teach S TEM courses in elementary school. Mostly, STEM education often concentrates on final artifacts, rather than visible documentation of iterative characterized fabricating activities (Tseng, 2016). At the same ti me, merely engaging in disciplinary projects does not allow students to master STEM’s epistemic nature suff iciently (Bell et al., 2016; Pleasants & Olson, 2019). Instead, it should be taught through an explicit educational approach (Akerson et al., 2000). We assumed that Knowledge-building (KB) theory with a Knowledge Forum (KF) supports students’ further inquiry and fabrication of artifacts. KB, as an educational pedagogy, engage learners  in collaborative work to improve their ideas and advance the state of knowledge in a community (Bereiter, 2002; C han & van Aalst, 2018; Scardamalia & Bereiter, 1994, 2014). Knowledge Forum (KF), as a mutual knowledge s pace, was designed to support creative interactions in communities (Scardamalia, 2004; Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2014). A KnowledgeBuilding Environment support students work with ideas that help them better understand the problems and design and facilitate them to document the process of artifacts with images and vi deos. Eventually, students engage collectively in reflecting on the process of understanding the artifacts and domain. The following questions were examined: (1) Did a designed KBE promote students’ STEM understanding succ essfully? (2) How did students engage in KB discourse move, and what was the relation between students’ KF participation, KB discourse move, students’ STEM artifacts, and STEM understanding? (3) How did the KBE help  students implement the STEM projects and gain a better STEM understanding?

## Methods

This study was conducted in a Mainland China university. There were 171 junior students from four undergraduate classes: Class A (n=39), Class B (n=40), Class C (n=41), and Class D (n=51). All of the classes studied the same course, “STEM Education”, over 4 months with two lessons weekly. Figure 1 KF View and Note This study attempted to design a KBE to support students’ STEM learning. Firstly, they were encouraged to think of new ideas about what they would like to do with respect to their experience. Based upon their interests, they were divided into different groups and determined the subjects of the ir projects. In phase II, the students CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 270 worked on the project and used the “Theory Building” scaffolds to propose new ide as or improve others’ ideas by “building-on” (Figure 1). In phase III, they reflected collectively on the knowledge building and STEM understanding that they acquired in STEM projects. A mixed methods was used to analyze the data collected. To investigate the way a KBE supported students’ STEM understanding, 7 themes in a  specific case, “culture diffusion of YAO minority”, are introduced to characterize the processes of KB-supported STEM learning. Theme 1: Posing initial ideas and forming groups based upon interests. In KBE, students could assume epistemic agency to post the initial ideas that related to their life experience. They formed different groups based upon their interests and engaged in further inquiry in the groups. (e.g., S18: fewer and fewer people are willing to take the initiative to learn about it; maybe we cando something. Theme 2: Searching for information and knowing the topics’ backgroun d. Students searched for information about the topic separately, developed their own understanding, and posted their new ideas and related information on KF for peers’ review. With the scaffolds of theory building, students could build upon each other’s ideas and improve theirs progressively. (S22: [My theory] The Long Drum and Dance of the Yao Nationality, as a unique national culture, is vanishing because of the aged and small number of inheritors.). Theme 3: Converging different ideas and scoping the core problem. KBE provided a mutual space for students to co-construct conceptual problems. Group members published their th oughts about the problems, converge their ideas and scope the core problem. In the “culture diffusion of YAO minority” group, they focused firstly on the way to inform outside people about the Long Drum (S18: [My theory] How can those outside the Yao nationality obtain information about the Long Drum culture effectively?). Then, another student found that the transmission routes were very rare. (S21: [My theory] The form of transmission is not sufficiently innovative. The transmission channels are not diversified sufficiently). Lastly, student S22 summarized all of the ideas that group members posted. (S22: [Put our knowledge together] How to carry forward the Yao Long Drum culture?) Theme 4: Finding solutions to the core problems and developing conc eptual ideas. As the students scoped the core problems, they attempted to find solutions to them. With the support of theory building scaffolds, the students engaged in progressive inquiry to identify an approach to solve the problem. The students in the “culture diffusion of YAO minority ” group, wanted to carry forward the Yao minority’s  Long Drum culture. Specifically, they needed to find a way to spread the culture to outside people. (S19: [My theory] Making cultural and creative products related to the elements of Long Drum.) Theme 5: Sketching the design and fabricating the artifacts. The students began a modeling phase as they decided what kinds of artifacts they wanted to create. Firstly, they sketc hed the conceptual ideas and visualized the design in a draft, and then explored and collected the kinds of  materials and methods that they would use to fabricate the artifacts. In KBE, KF allows students to document the fabrication, as they can organize the structure of views in KF and post multiple forms (words, pictures, and videos) to present their artifacts. Further, documentation of collaborative design and fabrication helps students engage in collective reflection and optimize their artifacts. In the “culture diffusion of YAO minority ” group, they decided to innovate some emojis based CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 272 upon images of Long Drums (see Figure 4). The students personified the Long Dr ums, and they had different expressions like human beings. This help people access to Yao minority’s Long Drum culture. ## Conclusion

This study designed a KBE to facilitate students’ STEM learning and under standing. The quantitative findings suggested that KBE supported KF participation, high-level KB discourse, and experience  with artifacts that can promote students’ STEM understanding. The qualitative analysis characterized the processes through which KB facilitates students’ collective inquiry, fabrication of artifacts, and STEM understanding. The focus of this study highlighted the artifacts-oriented learning experience and found evidence that KBE can facilitate students’ production of artifacts and domain understanding thr ough embodied learning. In KBE, the students were encouraged to assume their epistemic agency and col lective responsibility to contribute to developing the ideas and fostering the advancement of community knowledge. Future r esearch could explore cross-group activities for further inquiry into the design-based model to produce artifacts.
