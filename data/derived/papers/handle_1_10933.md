# Using Digital Games to Enhance 21st-Century Skills Development in K-12 Education: A Systematic Review

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In today’s rapidly evolving landscape, 21st-century skills such as creativity and problem-solving are indispensable. Digital games present a promising medium to cultivate these essential competencies. In this systematic review, we examine the impact of digital games on nurturing K-12 students’ 21st-century skills, drawing on a rigorous analysis of 81 selected studies. This paper offers a synthesis of our initial findings.

## Introduction

Equipping our children with 21st-century skills is crucial for their success in toda y’s dynamic job market and beyond (P21 Framework Definitions, 2009). These ski lls encompass a broad spectrum including learning a nd innovation abilities (e.g., critical thi nking, creativity, collaboration, and communication), proficiency in information, media, and technology (World Economic Forum, 2016). However, disconnect between school curricula and the demands of the 21st-century is one of fundamental issues in educational systems (Squire, 2005).

To address this issue, well-designed digital games are emerging as promising ve hicles for fostering 21st-century skills (Gee, 2003). Essential attributes of such digital games include ongoing interactive problem-solving, specific goals and rules, adaptive challenges, player autono my, timely feedback, uncertainty, and sensory stimu li (Shute & Ke, 2012). However, the efficacy of digital games  in nurturing 21st-century skills is still debated. While some studies present mixed or negative findings (e.g., Dindar, 2018), others report positive outcomes (e.g., Connolly et al., 2012; Sitzmann et al., 2011). This systematic review aims to identify key trends in using digital  games to foster 21st-century skills, while also exploring the effectiven ess of digital games and considering design aspects crucial for targeting such skills. To that end, we address the following research questions: (1) Which 21st-century skills are targeted by digital games? (2) What game  genres effectively foster lear ners’ 21st-century skills? (3) Which design elements of digital games have been em phasized in studies? (4) What learning theories hav e informed studies’ game design? and (5) What impact do digital games have on the development of 21st-century skills?

## Method

Following the PRISMA guidelines (Moher et al., 2009), we conducted a systematic review of the relevant literature to identify studies on digital games and  21st-century skills. We searched various databases and u sed tailored search strings (e.g., (“game” AND “learning” AND “21st century skills” AND “K-12”); (“digital game”) AND (“education”) AND (“21st-century skills”)). The inclusion criteria were studies that focused on 21st-century skills, in K-12 context, and were published between 2014-2023 in English.  This range was chosen because a previous study on the same topic was conducted in 2016 which included studies from 2010 to 2014 (Qian & Clark, 2016). Therefore, the aim of the current review is to examine studies published after 2014, expand the  scope of the research questions asked by Qian and Clark (201 6), and provide an updated understanding of the eff ects of digital games on 21st-century skills. The exclusion criteria were non-English studies published before 2014 or studies with low quality. Two resea rchers independently screened the abstracts and ful l text of the articles, resulting in 81 studies for review, including 14 hand-searched studies. A spreadsheet was then used to extract and synthesize the data, with separate columns for each  paper's variab les. The information from each paper was independently extracted by the two researchers. A c ontent analysis (in-depth reading of each paper) was performed to gain a deeper understanding of each st udy and answer the research questions. The research ers met and resolved their disagreements through iterative discussions to reach 100% agreement.

## Results

Our systematic review encompassed 81 studies on the  use of digital games to develop 21st-century skills, involving participants ranging from first graders to high school students. In relation to RQ1, our findings revealed that the primary skills targeted a cross these studies were problem-solving (n = 19), creativity (n = 16), collaboration (n = 13), critical thinking (n = 11), and communication (n = 7). A notable number of these studies (n = 15) explored the interplay among multiple skills.  Addressing RQ2, the digital game genres cited with in the 81 studies were as follows: serious games (n = 22), simulations (n = 12), adventures (n = 10), sandboxes (n = 8), puzzles (n = 7), role-playing (n = 3), strategies (n = 3), board games (n = 2), problem-solving games (n = 2), action games (n = 2), and other genres (n = 10). Regarding RQ3, studies have emphasized vario us design elements in digital games, including game mechanics, visual aes thetics, narrative, incentives, musical score, and learning objectives integrated with relevant content and ski lls.To address RQ4 we found learning theories such as behaviorist, cognitive, constructivism, and motivat ional and psychological have informed studies’ game  design. Finally, our finding regarding RQ5 shows that overa ll research indicates a positive impact of digital games on 21st-century skills development.

## Discussion and conclusion

The aim of this systematic review was to provide educators with a roadmap for using digital games and designers with guidance for creating games that effectively e nhance 21st-century skills among K-12 students. The review examined 81 studies and found that digital games have an overall positive impact on developing problem-solving, creativity, collaboration, critical thinking, and c ommunication skills. The most common game genres us ed were serious games, simulations, adventures, sandboxes, puzzles, role-playing, strategies, board games, problem-solving games, and action games.This diverse range of genres highlights the potential for various types of games to foster 21st-century skills. The studies emphasized various desi gn elements, such as game mechanics, v isual aesthetics, narrative, incentives, musical score, and learning objectives, which are crucial for the effectiveness of digital games in promoting 21st-century skills. Understanding the role of these des ign elements is essential for creating engaging and educational games. Learning theories, including behaviorist, cognitive, constructivism, and motivational and psychological theories, have infor med game design in the studies. To effectively use digital games for fostering 21st-century skills, educators, researchers, and designers should carefully select game genres, learning theories, and game design elements that align with the targeted skill development. This systematic review contributes to the growing body of research on the use of digital games for developing 21st-century skills and provides a comprehensive map connecting game genres, design elements, and learning theories, empowerin g stakeholders to harness the potential of digital games for fostering critical skills in K-12 students.
