# Conceptualizations of Teaching and Learning in Impact Statements for Tenure and Promotion

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigated conceptualizations of teaching and learning using complex systems theory and network analysis. In 226 impact statements for promotion and tenure in one university we found two conceptualizations. In the Content-Processing conceptualization, teaching is the delivery of content and learning is acquisition of knowledge. In the FramingFacilitation conceptualization, teaching is preparation of students for future careers and imparting truth, and learning is skill development. We also identified leverage points within each conceptualization.

## Background

In complex systems interdependent elements interact in ways that lead to emergent phenomena which cannot be explained through linear cause-and-effect analysis (Bar-Yam, 2016). The interdependent elements in com plex conceptual systems are related ideas which together form conceptualizations, leading to emergent phenomena in the form of practices. Because practices are emerge nt from conceptualizations, changing practices requires that conceptualizations must change. In prior studies we  found two dominant conceptualizations of learning (Donaldson & Allen-Handy, 2020). In the Transfer-Acquisition conceptualization, learning is the acquisition and manipulation of knowledge objects. We found this co nceptualization to be dominant among the general pu blic (Donaldson, 2018), as well as among STEM educators (Donaldson & Allen-Handy, 2020). In the learning sciences we found widespread use of the Construction-Becoming conceptualization (Donaldson, 2019) which sees learning simultaneously as becoming and as construction of meaning. This study seeks to identify which conceptualizations professors across all disciplines in a Research 1 university use to relate the impact of teaching and learning.

## Methods

We collected 226 anonymized impact statements from faculty being considered for tenure and promotion t o associate professor across 16 colleges in a Researc h 1 university in the United States from 2015 to 20 19. In MAXQDA Analytics Pro we coded the data using emerge nt coding processes within the following categories: non-metaphorical characterizations of teaching and learning; metaphors of teaching and learning; practices in teaching and learning; and value statements. The coding stage resulted in a final codebook which contained 291 codes covering 9,383 coded segments. We then calculated code co-occurrence correlations using Pearson’s r for all pairs of codes within each category. Symmetrical correlation matrices were created at the p < 0.05, p < 0.01, and p < 0.001 confidence levels. UCINet network analysis software was then used to analyze the matrices as 1mode networks. We used Girvan-Newman cluster analysis to identify network clusters (Girvan & Newman, 2002), which were visualized using the NetDraw network visualization software with different colors of nodes indicating membership in a cluster. To identify leverage points within each conceptualization, we developed an innovative methodology: semantic network maps in which node sizes were adjusted such that nodes with higher betweenness centrality measures were larger, and nodes with high betweenness values are key leverage points.

## Findings

Girvan-Newman analysis identified two conceptualiza tions of teaching and learning (see figure 1) at Q= 319. Although they were significantly distinct non-overlapping clusters, these two conceptualizations of teaching and learning had so much semantic overlap that we suspected they are actually two variations of one conceptualization, which was further supported by the finding that bot h conceptualizations were aligned with the Transfer-Acquisition conceptualization found in prior studie s. In the current study, the Content-Processing conceptualization of teaching and learning (blue cl uster-88.9% of the professors) saw teaching as in volving creating courses and structures for delivering and explaining knowledge. Professors who used this conc eptual story often seek to improve their courses and teaching practices, often with a focus on trying to motivate students. These professors believed that learning involves acquiring and using knowledge, and often value adjusting their teaching according to their students' learning styles. It often framed teaching and learning in terms of content— ICLS2022 Proceedings 1824 © ISLS content which is conveyed to students, which forms a foundation or body of knowledge. This content com es in the form of pieces of information which students ob tain. A major focus was covering material which stu dents receive and process. This conceptualization was aligned with the Transfer-Acquisition conceptualization found in prior research. We also found a Framing-Facilitatio n conceptualization of teaching and learning (83.6%  of professors-red cluster) focused on facilitating student development of skills to prepare them for future careers. It used a perspective on learning which sees learning as a process which takes place on the level of the individual student, and the role of the professor is to impart truth and correct facts to students. This conceptualization used visual metaphors such as exposure, illumination, and lenses. This conceptualization was also mostly aligned with the Transfer-Acquisition conceptualization found in  prior research, but to a lesser degree than the Co ntentProcessing conceptualization. ## Discussion and Implications

This study used an innovative methodology which leverages network analysis tools to investigate these questions, and we were able to further develop the methodology such that it provides insights about key leverage points in complex conceptual systems. We found two conceptual izations of teaching and learning used by professor s in impact statements for tenure and promotion. By virt ue of identifying these conceptualizations we gain insights into both 1) the motivations for faculty teaching a nd learning approaches and 2) how faculty members perceive their colleagues value teaching and learning during the promotion and tenure process.
