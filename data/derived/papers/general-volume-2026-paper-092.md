# The Look Again Quilt

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract

T he Look Again Quilt For millennia, humans used pictures to tell stories. Narrative quilts are a traditional form of pictorial storytelling, dating back centuries. The Look Again quilt is a modern example of a narrative quilt used for science communication and engagement. Since blankets are familiar and often evoke positive feelings, quilting as a science communication media may help avoid top-down cognitive processing that results in public disengagement with science. In addition, quilts can be gamified and create not only storytelling, but interactive opportunities. The Look Again quilt features two realistic eyes, one open and the other closed at the top of the quilt, above an array of blocks, each representing different aspect of biology. The closed eye side is monochromatic and represents the angst and prevalence of stereotypes when many consider biology. For example, chemical equations represented as spinning blocks and straight-line stitching to represent rigid structures. In contrast, the open eye side is full color with blocks designed to evoke a sense of wonder and curiosity at the ways biology is part of one’s life. For example, including blocks with wavy stitching and layered fabrics that represent the ecosystem at a beach. Overall, the quilt encourages people to look at biology again and see the beauty of biology around them. Engagement with the quilt can come in the form of (A) Finding a block and using it to tell a biology story about the concept presented there (B) Playing co-designed I Spy (find a block that you think represents genetic engineering) (C) Asking people to reflect on what they notice about their perceptions and feelings of biology when they look again. How people engage with the quilt presents many opportunities for research on how people make sense of the relevance of biological information to their lives. Melanie Peffer Patchworking conversations We created this piece with the goal to spark conversations about art, creativity, and connections. We invite everyone to participate in the making of an evolving artwork throughout the conference, embodying the theme "Partnering with purpose" by blurring boundaries between artists and observers, and acts as individual or collective. The work challenges the idea that artistic talent is innate and instead takes an exploratory approach to creation; a capability of expression set in motion through partnering with self, others, and materials. The collage we began by affixing pieces of fabric to paper grows when we ask all patrons to "find a material that matches your feeling and attach it onto the collage". In addition to becoming an exploration of material agency and a document of a collective journey, this work demonstrates that artmaking is not limited to self-identified artists, and that artmaking can foster compassion through participation, reflection and connection. Anastasia Y. Goodwin, Lexie Zhao, Naomi Field, Lana Ćosić Reflexivity as Collaborative Poetic Process Grounding in learning sciences scholarship on the embodied, affective, and relational nature of learning, we invite conference attendees to explore their own learning in and through their research through the co-creation of a collaborative poetic reflexivity installation. To begin, the installation will include a collection of poems written by members of our research team and others (with their permission) in response to prompts including: What does your/our research mean to you? What feelings, images, memories call you to your work? What in your work are you grappling with? We offer poetic processes of personal and collaborative inquiry as cognitive–affective tools for reflexive transformations that can strengthen our connections to our own complex humanity, to our research partners and their complex humanity, and to our aspirations for ourselves, our work, and our worlds. Sofia Tancredi, Nicole Louie, Rosanne Luu, Siqi Huang, The Mattering Collective
