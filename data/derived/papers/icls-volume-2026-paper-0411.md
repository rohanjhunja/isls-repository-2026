# Catalyzing Professional Identity: The Impact of Authentic Assessment in Teacher Education

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Developing professional identity in pre-service teachers is a common challenge in teacher education. Authentic reflective assessment practices serve as powerful catalysts for transformative learning and identity formation. Analysis of reflection papers from eleven early childhood education students revealed shifts in their professional perspectives and evidence of critical self-reflection. Our findings suggest that assessment design fosters professional identity, expanding its pedagogical role in teacher education.

There are challenges in teacher education not only in imparting pedagogical skills, but also in fostering professional identity within pre-service teachers. It is difficult to become an effective educator if you cannot see yourself as an effective educator. More traditional approaches frame assessment as a tool to measure learning outcomes. This poster argues for us to rethink how we view and give assessments. Authentic, reflective assessment practices can serve as a key catalyst for transformative learning  and professional identity formation. We suggest these practices do more than teach assessment skills, but change the way pre-service teachers see themselves in their future careers and how they understand the children they observe. Transformative learning theory (Mezirow, 2000) examines how people change foundational assumptions and beliefs. It is not about adding new beliefs, but is about a fundamental shift in how a person perceives the world. In this context, pre-service teachers in an early childhood education course engage in an “Assessment Instrument Application Assignment.” After documenting and analyzing children’s behaviors, the pre-service teachers reflected on their observations answering questions about what worked, what they struggled with, and what the experience meant to them. We propose that assessing children and reflecting on that experience shifts core assumptions about child development, assessment practices, and professional identity. The dynamic systems model of role identity (DSMRI; Kaplan & Garner, 2017) says that identity is fluid and flexible, and changes as we interact with our environment and reflect on those interactions. We propose that the authentic, field-based assessment these pre-service teachers completed provided opportunities for students to explore and discover their professional identities. The act of doing and reflecting can lead to the “broadening of perspectives,” which is an indicator of identity development within the DSMRI framework. We also used cognitive constructivist learning theory (Piaget, 1952) to account for aspects of individual knowledge construction and constructionist learning theory (Papert & Harel, 1991) to account for aspects related to learning by making. Pre-service teachers at the University of Alabama at Birmingham wrote reflections on their experience within their course on assessments. They were asked to consider the weekly application of assessments in the course, then to answer: what worked well? What struggles did you have? And what did this experience mean to you? After collecting the reflections from eleven (N=11) students, we used the MAXQDA coding software to assign codes at the sentence level to each student’s responses to find recurring themes and high-frequency codes. Codes came from three categories: Principles from theory, struggles, and what worked. We took each sentence from each of the students' responses and assigned applicable codes to each sentence. Some sentences had multiple codes applied. Coding resulted in 32 codes covering 90 coded segments. Once the sentences were coded, we looked for common themes by conducting frequency analysis.

After analyzing students' reflections, we found a pattern of learning outcomes that support the role of authentic assessment in catalyzing professional identity formation and transformative learning. The most common experiences found through the coding process align with the DSMRI and transformative learning theory framework. Many students reported shifts in their professional perspectives with top codes being the DSMRI principle of broadening perspectives, the aspect of the experience that worked well of r elevance to studies or the field, and the aspect that worked of relevance in terms of career. In one student’s reflection they wrote, “[The 2662 assignment] gave me valuable insights into how life of a teacher will be, and gave me all the necessary practice!” The transformative learning theory principle of critical self-reflection was evident as well. Areas that students identified as having room for improved instruction, were shown through reported struggles. The most frequent struggles were with a perception of unclear instructions or expectations and wanting more examples, samples, or templates. Our study suggests that educators should design assessment tasks with more than the intention to evaluate, and instead should aim to create strategic experiences that catalyze critical self-reflection and the construction of their students’ professional identity. Our findings support the argument that authentic assessment assignments can be used as pedagogical tools that can help pre-service teachers expand on and discover their professional identity. With the frequency of identity shifts shown in codes regarding broadening perspectives and self-reflection, it is evident that these types of assessments and assignments foster transformative learning and professional role identity. In practice, teacher educators should consider assessment not as a means to give a number that determines how much the student learned or failed to learn, but as an on-going process for development. Integrating authentic fieldwork with structured reflection prompts can maximize growth in pre-service teachers. Addressing the logistical struggles with clearer instructions and examples will enhance engagement from the students.  By reframing assessment as an identity-formation engine, we contribute to the learning sciences by demonstrating an approach that fosters reflective and adaptable educators who are continuously learning.
