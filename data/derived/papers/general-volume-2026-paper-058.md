# Playful Learning in the Community: An interactive tour of community based early learning installations

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Playful Learning Landscapes (PLL) transforms everyday community spaces like parks, bus-stops, and grocery stores into conduits for caregiver-child interactions and early learning. During this workshop, attendees will visit a series of PLL installations in Santa Ana, CA, which were co-designed by researchers from UC Irvine and Temple University, and families from the Santa Ana Early Learning Initiative, a local grassroots non-profit community organization. Attendees will engage with the PLL installations and learn about the design process and impact of the installations on community members and caregiver child interactions at PLL sites. The session will also demonstrate how this project was conducted in partnership with the City of Santa Ana’s Parks and Recreation and Public Works departments who own and maintain the installations and have begun to replicate them using public funds in other community spaces. Challenges, lessons learned, plans for sustainability, and practical and theoretical insights will be discussed. Playful learning in the community: An interactive tour of community based early learning installations Informal learning environments like children’s museums, zoos, and maker spaces are powerful contexts for supporting young children’s science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM) learning and identity. Unfortunately, children from low-income families have less access to these spaces due to a variety of barriers like admission prices, transportation challenges, or even an awareness of their existence. In addition to these barriers, linguistically and ethnically minoritized families can feel alienated in these spaces which rarely center their languages, cultural values, and practices. Playful Learning Landscapes is an international initiative that brings informal learning installations to everyday spaces like parks, bus-stops, grocery stores, laundry mats, medical clinic waiting rooms, sidewalks, and walls (Hassinger-Das, 2018; Bustamante et al., 2019). Playful Learning Landscapes promote the caregiver-child conversations and interactions that decades of developmental and learning sciences research suggest lead to learning, such as conversational turns, STEM language, and hands-on engagement (Bustamante et al., 2020; Hassinger-Das et al., 2021; Pesch et al., 2025). In addition to designing PLLs based on the latest research in developmental and learning sciences, they are also designed in partnership with local families, so the installations build from families’ values, routines, and cultural practices (Bermudez et al., 2023). Understanding learning as a cultural process situates family’s participation in learning environments and design as dictated by values, routines, and cultural practices (Bang & Medin, 2010; Rogoff et al, 2007). Leveraging cultural assets, practices, and values can therefore increase relevance and significance for families, in turn increasing inclusivity, sustainability, and engagement. Partners The PLL work in Santa Ana, CA, is conducted in the context of a long-term research practice partnership between the Santa Ana Early Learning Initiative (SAELI), the University of California Irvine, and the City of Santa Ana. SAELI is a grassroots non-profit community organization bringing together local families, non-profit organizations, and leadership from the Santa Ana Unified School district to support families with children between 0-9 years of age. SAELI is led by director Wendy Gomez and uses a multigenerational approach through which promotoras (e.g., Chen et al., 2023) promote wellness through initiatives on child development, connecting families with resources, and local advocacy projects. On the university side, these projects are led by UC Irvine S chool of Education Professors Andres Bustamante and June Ahn and Temple University Psychology Professor Kathy Hirsh-Pasek. This research team brings expertise in child development, early education, participatory design, and research practice partnerships. The City of Santa Ana supported the project through their Parks and Recreation Department and Public Works Department, with support from the City Manager’s Office, Mayor's Office, and City Council. Other organizations and businesses included in this work were Northgate Gonzalez Market grocery chain, the Santa Ana Unified School District, First Five Orange County, and Orange County Health Care Agency. In this RPP, we endeavored to center families’ voices in all phases of the process from designing the installations, to deciding their locations, to the evaluation research, and dissemination of the findings. Importantly, trust and relationship-building with families were central to the design sessions. Personal stories and family values were shared through informal conversations over meals. These informal opportunities to connect allowed trust to be built over time, which uniquely positioned researchers and families as equals. We believe this allowed families to share openly and addressed traditional power dynamics between researchers and families. Decisions were taken jointly through parallel conversations with partners and interested parties, requiring transparency, trust-building, multiple channels of communication, rapid problem-solving, adaptation, and other forms of invisible relational labor. However, tensions emerged throughout the process particularly when navigating the intersection of large bureaucracies including federal funding, university systems, and city government. The project faced multiple challenges, including changing project timelines and material constraints, changes in organizational leadership, physical limitations of the public spaces designated for installations, and the complex social and political contexts of the community. Given existing power differentials between governmental partners and community stakeholders, our university team played a critical intermediary role, advocating on behalf of the community in certain cases. Whereas in other cases, our community partners stepped up and advocated for the project using their influence as voting constituents and activists in their community. Ultimately, the political capital of all stake-holders was necessary to bring this project to fruition. Research design in the community The Santa Ana PLLs were supported by an Advancing Informal STEM Learning (AISL) award from the National Science Foundation. The project began in 2020, and due to the pandemic, all design sessions in the first year of the project were held on Zoom. We held seven 90-minute co-design sessions where we learned about families histories and experiences with their community spaces, and used participatory design activities such as bags-of-stuff, photo-elicitation, story boarding, and storytelling to encourage families to share their goals and routines in their community spaces (for a detailed description of design process see Bermudez et al., 2023; Garcia et al., 2022; Chew et al., 2022). Through an inductive thematic analysis of these co-design sessions, we surfaced community values, practices, and aspirations such as promoting intergenerational learning, and increasing family time. As part of these themes, we also surfaced ways in which families' own routines and practices already involved STEM concepts and practices. We iteratively combined these themes and existing practices with the science practices and crosscutting concepts from the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) and principles from developmental science for creating high-quality early learning environments that are active, engaging, iterative, social, meaningful, and joyful (Hirsh-Pasek et al., 2015). While a single design could not meet all the criteria, we sought balance across community values, developmental principles, and science learning goals (see Bermudez et al., 2023 for more detail). Further, we were intentional to align these practices with SAELI’s three step design model that included engaging parents in discovery & dreaming, designing, and creating a sustainability plan. Using these design goals and guiding frameworks, we conducted prototype ideation sessions with SAELI families and then subsequent sessions where we created low-fidelity mock-ups of the prototypes for play testing and iteration during the second and third years of the project. Children and parents were interviewed to hear their feedback on the designs and learn about their experiences playing with the physical prototypes. These sessions were key to evaluate factors from usability to how and if families’ interactions aligned with their values and practices. These sessions were held in person at their local Community Center, started with a shared meal, followed by a design activity, reflections and share outs. Through these sessions we iterated and refined the prototypes until they were ready to be shared with our fabrication partners. Fabrication and launch For fabrication, we contracted a Los Angeles based museum installation design shop. We worked closely with their engineers to adapt the co-designed prototypes for durability, usability, safety, cost efficiency, and regulation compliance. All designs were reviewed by City of Santa Ana Parks and Recreation or Public Works departments and UC Irvine’s Facilities Management department. We worked collaboratively with the City of Santa Ana and S AELI to decide where the installations would go, taking advantage of existing city renovation efforts to save costs and create efficiencies when possible. We also worked with legal representation from UCI and the City of Santa Ana to execute a Memorandum of Understanding that the PLL installations were being donated to the city by UCI and the City of Santa Ana would own and maintain them. This process took approximately two years from the beginning of the fabrication process to installation in public spaces. The installations were implemented between Fall 2024 and Spring 2025 with a grand opening ribbon cutting ceremony held on May 10th, 2025, as part of the NSF’s 75th anniversary celebration and Mother’s Day in many Latin American countries. The ceremony was hosted at Madison Park in Santa Ana and attended by the Mayor, Director of Parks and Recreation, several city council members, and the Dean of the UCI School of Education. Data collection Our data collection protocol included baseline observations of STEM language and caregiver child interactions at each of the five installation sites pre-installation as well as a matched comparison site (e.g., for a bus-stop installation we observed a comparison bus stop in the same neighborhood). We also trained SAELI parents to collect surveys at all PLL and control sites of caregiver’s self-efficacy in teaching STEM to their children, perceptions of safety and learning opportunities in their community spaces, and attitudes around the connection between play and learning. We then performed the observational and survey data collection protocol post-installation to examine the impacts of the PLL’s on caregiver child interactions and experiences in community spaces. While post-installation data collection is on-going we expect to present findings from these analyses at the ISLS workshop in June 2026. For a more detailed description of our analytical plans please refer to our project pre-registration on the Open Science Framework. Building on this momentum, we are working with city officials on a resolution to integrate PLLs into the city’s on-going process for new public amenities leveraging existing budget from local institutions. We plan to position SAELI as drivers of future PLL efforts where they engage local community members in a design process of new public amenities that center early learning and build from local families’ funds of knowledge. This work represents a scalable and community centered vision for designing public infrastructure to promote child development and civic engagement.
