# Applying the Understanding by Design (UbD) Model to Support Critical Thinking and Multidisciplinary Inquiry in the Chinese High School Biology Curriculum

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper presents a method of guiding teachers to develop “critical action inquiry” curriculum lessons, using a UbD (Understanding by Design) approach. This method is being developed at South China Normal University, as part of an ongoing collaboration with researchers in the Critical Action Learning Exchange (CALE) project, at the University of Toronto. In this design-based research involving 12 faculty and 92 students, the research team and the pedagogical team accompanied teachers in their development of pedagogical scenarios and use UbD as a framework. We present our application of UbD to the design of a Critical Action Inquiry project called the Dongguan Coastal Flooding project. We show how the design of CALE activities can be guided by UbD, and discuss our next steps for applications with teachers in southern China.

## Introduction and theoretical framework

In China, the Standard for General Senior Secondary Biology Curriculum (2017 Edition) states that students need to develop critical thinking, and actively engage with real-world problems. Paul (1988) defined critical thinking as a kind of self-directed thinking.  Facione (1990) defines critical thinking as a purposive, self-regulated thinking process. STEM teachers are generally challenged to integrate  inquiry and critical thinking into their curriculu m, in part because of the challenge associated with adding inquiry into their curriculum (Slotta & Linn, 2009). Since 2021, we have applied the CALE framework to help Ch inese teachers design and implement multidisciplina ry inquiry lessons that include a dimension of critical thinking (Carvalho et al, 2023). We also applied the principles of Understanding by Design (UbD), an approach to backw ard design of curriculum proposed by Wiggins & McTighe (2005). UbD advocates focusing on learning outcomes first, starting with the end in mind, so t hat teachers begin thinking about, organizing and designing instructional content in terms of learning outcomes.  Zhao & Tian (2022) constructed a reverse instructional design model for accurate teaching. This paper reports on a collaborative effort between research teams from two universities-one in South China and one in North America. The Chinese team coordinated a teacher professional  development community using the pedagogical framework of the North American group (Carvalho et al, 2023). The focus of this research is the application of the UbD model in developing a  secondary biology curriculum  unit that emphasizes critical thinking and multidisciplinary inquiry. The paper p resents a case study of a group of teachers who designed an inquiry project on coastal flooding in South China, with a specific emphasis on conservatio n and sustainability. The project also aimed to engage students in critical thinking about the causes of flooding and various approaches to the conservation of mangrove forests. It is important to note that this paper represents work in pr ogress, as the researchers are currently working with the teachers to implement, evaluate, and refine their designs. Additionally, interviews with the teachers are being conducted to gain insights into their understanding of the project.

## Methodology

This design-based research project involved the participation of 12 teachers and 92 students from one high school in three distinct iterations corresponding to the f ollowing semesters: summer 2021, 2022 and 2023.  A mixed methodology relying on both task sheets  and interviews (teachers) was deployed.  The program integrates classroom instruction and outdoor expeditions to provide an immersive learning experience for students. The team of researchers and educational developers assisted them through collaborative scenario design activities in three disciplines: biology, geography, and technology. After engaging in these pedagogical scenarios,  t he students submitted their task sheets.

## CASE Study: Applying UbD to design a multidisciplinary STEM inquiry lesson

In the first stage, teachers need to distill the core big concepts of the subject based on the curriculum standards, formulate teaching objectives, identify the core concepts and skills that students need to understand and master, and design basic questions based on real-life situations that can promote students' understanding of the big concepts and knowledge transfer.

In the second stage, teachers need to design approp riate indicators and scales based on the teaching objectives to collect evidence during the teaching activities to assess the achievement of teaching objectives. Three forms of assessment were identified: (1) video production of components and functions of mangrove ecosystems;

(2) open-ended questions and responses to measure understand ing of the structure and function of mangrove

ecosystems, as well as the current measures to prot ect mangrove ecosystems, to recognize the gap between their own abilities and the reality, and to show critical thinking; (3) expressive tasks, in which students apply what they have learned to analyze the functions of mangrove e cosystems and the current threats they face, and pr opose solutions.

In the third stage, it is necessary to design a tea ching program that is both effective and engaging t o students, based on the teaching objectives and assessment strategies. UbD instructional design emphasizes the use of WHERETO elements to structure and test instructional activities, Specific instructional activities are as follows:

(1) investigate what species are found in mangroves (W); (2) classify the organisms and describe the basis of your

classification (W); (3) Pick an organism that inter ests you, look closely at it and describe a characteristic or  a behavior that is harmful or beneficial to the organism's survival (H, T); (4) Identify inanimate factors that maintain the normal structure and function of mangrove fores ts (E); and (5) Based on the above investigation and discussion, explain the scientific term "ecosystem" in your own words (R).

## Discussion and next steps

In this study, we applied UbD as a theoretical framework to support the teaching of critical thinking and interdisciplinary inquiry. We asked the question of  whether UbD (and backward design generally) could be a useful methodology to support teachers in gaining experience and understandings of inquiry and critical thinking. By focusing on important outcomes of the design-including critical thinking-it is possible to design those elements into the curriculum, and teachers can gain  confidence and positive experiences creating learn ing activities to support those elusive elements (e.g.,  21st century competencies, critical thinking).  This can be especially vital for teachers who lack experience a nd understanding of these ideas and approaches. Ide ntifying assessments of students' critical thinking through open-ended questions and expressive tasks, and then designing corresponding teaching activities based on the expe cted results and assessment tools, UbD provides a f easible path for teachers to participate in the design of curriculum that cultivates students' critical thinking. As this work moves forward,  we will study the im pact of such experiences on teachers’ understanding s and classroom practices.  Ultimately, we hope to extend this model to more di sciplines and a wider community of teachers, applying ideas of Design-based Implementation Research to help wide the scal e for such models of curriculum design.
