# The Role of Teacher Feedback in Supporting Cocreative Processes

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Cocreation is a key 21st-century skill, yet few studies examine how teacher feedback supports it in classrooms. This study examined the role of structured, dialogic feedback in fostering students’ creativity and collaborative knowledge building. Twenty-five twelve-year-olds engaged in a technology-enhanced, project-based learning intervention, creating digital infographics using Viacocrea technology. Feedback, guided by the T-SEDA (2022) framework and aligned with Guilford’s creativity dimensions (1950), fo stered idea generation, reasoning, and collaboration. Findings highlight teacher feedback as essential for cocreative learning.

Creativity is a key competence in education, but it emerges through social interaction rather than individual processes (Sawyer, 2021). Teachers play a crucial role as facilitators who can stimulate idea generation, encourage reflection, and foster collaborative thinking (Major et al., 2018). In this context, feedback becomes an interactive process that helps students refine and expand their ideas (Espasa et al., 2019). The T-SEDA framework (2022) has been widely used to analyse collaborative dialogue. This study proposes a new perspective: interpreting T-SEDA as a tool to foster creative dialogue, where feedback encourages students to expand, reframe, and challenge ideas. In this sense, T-SEDA categories can structure teacher feedback that promotes fluency, flexibility, and originality.

Despite recognition of the importance of dialogue for creativity, little research has examined how teacher feedback can intentionally scaffold cocreative dialogue in classroom interactions. This study contributes to the CSCL field by reframing T-SEDA as a pedagogical tool for cocreativity, linking dialogic feedback moves with Guilford’s creativity dimensions (fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration). This perspective provides a new  analytical lens for understanding how teacher feedback can structure  collaborative idea generation and development in classroom practice.

This study adopts a case study design in a sixth-grade classroom (25 students, aged 12) in a public school in Lleida (Spain). Students worked in small groups over nine sessions within a project-based learning intervention, cocreating a digital infographic about their school and culture for peers in the United States, supported by the Viacocrea platform (Pifarré, 2019). The teacher, who also acted as a teacher-researcher, received prior training in dialogic teaching to support cocreative classroom interactions. Data were collected through audio recordings of group interactions and students’ collaborative documents.

To analyse transcripts of teacher feedback, a coding scheme was developed combining theoretical and data-driven approaches. Initial categories were informed by the T-SEDA framework (2022) and J. P. Guilford’s creativity model (1950), focusing on fluency, f lexibility, originality, and elaboration. These were iteratively refined through transcript analysis to better capture how teacher feedback supports cocreative dialogue. Elaboration-Expanding and reframing contributions Teacher feedback emerged as a key driver of students’ cocreative processes. Analysis of classroom dialogue revealed five feedback patterns that shaped how students engaged in collaborative idea generation: Invite to build ideas: “Tell me why you are so interested in Paella.”: Students initially named the dish (“It’s a Spanish dish”), but after the prompt, they expanded the discussion to ingredients, origins, and cultural meaning, showing an increase in the number and diversity of ideas generated (fluency). Guide the direction of dialogue: “Are you taking into account everything related to the school?”: The feedback redirected the group from listing school spaces to discussing cultural and social aspects of school life, prompting students to reconsider overlooked perspectives and demonstrat ing greater flexibility in their exploration of ideas.

Express or invite ideas to move forward: “OK, so think about school traditions…”: This prompt encouraged students to extend previous contributions by proposing new traditions and experiences, broadening the shared idea space and supporting original contributions within the group dialogue. Challenge: “Is this a unique activity from Lleida?”: The teacher’s challenge led students to question whether their example was unique to Lleida, prompting them to compare local and national traditions and generate more distinctive interpretations (originality).

Invite reasoning: “But why do you want to know when it started?”: Students responded by explaining their motivations and clarifying the relevance of historical information, leading to more elaborate explanations and deeper collective understanding.

Teacher feedback played a central role in students’ cocreative processes. Feedback strategies such as inviting reasoning, guiding dialogue, challenging assumptions, building on ideas, and expressing or inviting ideas to move forward supported fluency, flexibility, originality, and elaboration (Guilford, 1950). These findings align with Mercer (2019) and Sawyer (2021), who highlight that creativity and knowledge building emerge through dialogue. By linking T-SEDA dialogic moves with creativity dimensions, this study claims that teacher feedback can function as a dialogic scaffold for cocreative processes, shaping how students generate, expand, and refine ideas collaboratively in classroom interaction.

This study contributes to knowledge about the central role of teacher feedback in fostering cocreative processes in project-based, technology-enhanced learning. Teacher dialogic strategies supported fluency, flexibility, and originality, guiding and catalyzing students’ creative thinking. Future research could explore AI-based analysis of teacher-student dialogues to identify recurring patterns and better define effective cocreative dialogue in classrooms.
