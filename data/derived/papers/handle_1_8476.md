# Indigenous Learning Lab: Inclusive Knowledge-Production and Enacted Utopias Towards Decolonizing School Systems

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The United State was built on settler colonialism, slavery, and capitalism. Settler colonialism strives for the dissolution of Indigenous societies by establishing a new colonial society on seized land with the elimination of Indigenous societies as an organizing principle. Through the education system, Native Americans experienced downward assimilation and cultural genocide. Today, Native American youth disproportionately receive exclusionary disciplinary actions more frequently and severely in schools. Disproportionality in school discipline is a result of historically accumulated contradictions such as widening disparities in health, justice, housing, and wealth. As an enduring systemic contradiction, disproportionality demands sustained collaborative systemic transformations led and owned by local stakeholders. This paper presents a new formative intervention model, Indigenous Learning Lab. In a partnership with an Anishinaabe nation, Indigenous students, educators, and family and community members and non-Indigenous school staff, our research team worked at a rural public high school to collectively design and implement a new, culturally responsive, decolonizing school-wide behavioral support system.

Objectives Settler colonialism strives for the dissolution of Indigenous societies by establishing a new colonial society on seized land with the elimination of Indigenous societies as an organizing principle (Wolfe, 2006). Through the education system in U.S., Native Americans experienced downward assimilation and cultural genocide. Today, in the U.S. education system, Native American youth receive exclusionary disciplinary actions more frequently and severely for more subjective reasons such as disrespect (Whitford et al., 2019). Disproportionality in school discipline is a result of historically accumulated contradictions arising from a racist, settler colonial society (Bal, 2017). Addressing inequity requires a situated conceptualization to help develop systemic solutions led and owned by community members in response to school communities’ diverse and often opposing histories, interests, and goals.

## Methodology & Study Context

In this study, we examined how Indigenous Learning  Lab, a community-based, inclusive systemic design intervention that aimed to facilitate the inclusive design of a culturally responsive school-wide behavioral support system in partnership with a tribal nation of the Anishinaabe people and school staff at Northwoods High School, a rural high school in U.S. We addressed the following question: How did Indigenous Learning Lab facilitate the collaborative problem-solving and systemic design process with Indigenous and non-Indigenous members of the school community for the systemic transformation?

## Theoretical Framework

Indigenous Learning Lab is a formative intervention model (Sannino et al., 2016). It is grounded in CulturalHistorical Activity Theory (CHAT) and Decolonizing Epistemologies (Engeström, 2016; Smith, 2012). Indigenous Learning Lab builds upon “refusal” of metanarrative in social science research and leverages “resistance, reclaiming, recovery, reciprocity, repatriation, [and] regeneration” (Tuck & Yang, 2014, p. 244).

## Methods

Indigenous Learning Lab included 14 participants (See Table 1). Members participated in 11 video recorded sessions. They followed a cycle of expansive learning actions consisting of forming Learning Lab, questioning, analyzing, modeling, testing, and stabilizing new solutions to resolve the systemwide problems of practice (Bal, 2017).

## Results

Forming Inclusive Problem-Solving Group The research team worked with the community and school liaisons to hold initial meetings on the reservation to disrupt asymmetrical power relations embedded in the bureaucratic school system. Each session started with a pipe smudging ceremony by lighting up traditional tobacco and sage. The American Indian education mentor led the pipe ceremony. As suggested by the Anishinaabe community members, the research team introduced the ICLS2022 Proceedings 1317 © ISLS Seven Grandfather Teachings of the Anishinaabe as inclusive group norms to run a mutually respectful problem-solving process. The Seven Grandfather Teachings are orally passed-down moral and ethical principles elders have used to educate future generations on living a good life: Nibwaakaawin (Wisdom); Zaagi’idiwin (Love); Minaadendamowin (Respect); Aakode’ewin (Bravery); Gwayakwaadiziwin (Honesty); Dabaadendiziwin (Humility); and Debwewin (Truth).

The collaborative examination of systemic contradictions (e.g., racial disparities) and the creation of new instrumentality aimed to resolve daily disturbances and conflicts resulting from the inner contradictions. Learning Lab facilitated the expansion of the school discipline system’s object from the punishment and exclusion of students with challenging behaviors to prevention and inclusive, positive support for all students and adults in a school community. A new, culturally responsive system was designed around the expanded object. The following academic year, the Learning Lab members worked on the implementation of the new system.

Analysis of Systemic Contradictions in Settler-Colonial School System Learning Lab utilized multiple mediating tools to stimulate a critical analysis. Members mapped out the school’s exiting discipline system and identified multiple systemic breakdowns in the existing system that contribute to (re) production of the racialization of school discipline. Designing Systemic Solutions for an Enacted Utopia Members designed a new, culturally responsive school-wide behavioral support system. Members used the system map to generate solutions for the inner contradictions in their system. In the 2020-2021 academic year, the school implemented the new system.

## Significance

To offset the cumulative effects of colonial harms through education systems, critical indigenous scholarship has emphasized the necessity of building culturally decolonizing systems (McCarty & Lee, 2014). Learning Lab can be utilized to address historically accumulated contradictions through strategic community-schooluniversity coalitions for building a socially just school systems. In the learning sciences, design-based research moved toward more situated, collaborative forms of research (Bang et al., 2016). Forming inclusive, productive, and sustained desig n teams requires time, commitment, interest convergence, negotiations and more importantly robust methodologies and theories of organizational learning and transformation (Adler, 2015). The present study shows how CHAT and formative interventions can be instrumental in this paradigm expansion and may contribute to recently emerging inclusive, community-based research methodologies such as DBIR and social design experiments. Indigenous Learning Lab may add more to the field’s toolbox for facilitating the decolonizing knowledge-production activities and systemic transformation of the oppressive social systems towards buen vivir – collective well-being.
