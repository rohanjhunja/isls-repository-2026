# Design Principles of Argumentativo: Designing an Intervention Focusing on Explicit Writing Instruction and Collaborative Writing

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In the present study, we provide in-depth insight into the content and structure of a class-wide teacher-led educational intervention on argumentative writing. The computer-supported intervention focuses on explicit writing instruction and collaborative writing, both proven to be effective instructional approaches in educational research. The intervention is systematically and analytically described by means of blueprint-based design principles for pedagogical interventions. Following this procedure, this writing program is described by defining design principles, instructional teaching activities, and student learning activities.

Improving 11th-graders’ argumentative writing by combining explicit writing instruction and collaborative writing Prior research indicates that higher-order argumentative writing remains  cognitively challenging in secondary school and at least a relevant part of secondary school students do not have fully developed and persistent writing strategies (Ferretti & Lewis, 2013). Recent research also showed that argumentative texts are often weak: they are not factual accurate, have a weak level of persuasiveness, include bad writin g mechanics, or do not include any counterarguments nor rebuttals (Landrieu et al., 2022). Not withstanding students’ disappointing argumentative writing performance and their persistent writing difficulties, research indi cates that these can be overcome by instruction, with explicit writing instruction and collaborative writing as complementa ry approaches (Ferretti & Lewis, 2013; Granado-Peinado et al., 2019, 2022). More particularly, explicit writing instruction appears to have positive effects on the quality of students’ argumentative writing and on their self-efficacy for writing (De Smedt et al., 2020; Graham et al., 2013). Furthermore, providing peer interaction during writing by creating opportunities in which students collaborate and assist one another while writing revealed bene ficial effects on students’ individual writing and their self-efficacy (De La Paz & Graham, 1996; Van S teendam, 2016). As Graham and Perin (2007) showed that explicit strategy instruction has an effect size of.82 and collaborative writing has an effect size of.75, the present study combines these two effective instructional approaches in secondary education in order to enhance 11th graders' argumentative writing skills. Up to date, we only found the study of Elving and vanden Bergh (2015) that successfully combines both approaches in secondary education. Through this computersupported intervention study, it was inter alia investigated what the effect is of  explicit instruction and collaborative writing practice on students' (a) individual writing performance and (b) self-efficacy for argumentation. The design of this study was based on the study of Granado-Peinado and colleagues (2019) in higher education.

Systematically reporting the design of an intervention through the construction of design principles To analytically and systematically describe the design of the educational writing intervention, we elaborate on the underlying design principles (Rijlaarsdam et al., 2017). These design principles are theoretically and empirically driven and constitute the blueprint of our instructional intervention: (1) what is the focus of instruction (i.e., what writing and collaboration processes are central), (2) what is the mode of ins truction (i.e., what instructional practices are central: what do teachers have to do to stimulate students' argumentative writing processes), and (3) what is the sequence of instruction (i.e., what is the structure and timeline of  the intervention). Based on this blueprint, we will describe the concrete instructional activities and learning activities in the intervention program (i.e., the lessons on collaborative, argumentative writing). In 2021, the intervention study "Argumentativo" was conducted in Flanders (Belgium): a class-wide, teacher-led educational intervention study on argumentative writing. 8 lessons of each 50 minutes were developed and throughout the intervention, students (n=400) wrote argumentative texts, by using (digital) informative source texts. In the argumentative writing lessons, students wrote (collaboratively or individually) argumentative texts on their laptop.

Regarding "explicit writing instruction", teachers have to implement a pre-desig ned teaching package focusing on (a) activating students' prior knowledge, (b) offering challenging writing ta sks, (c) pointing out the importance of argumentative writing, and (d) providing explicit strategy instruction on how to construct an argumentative text, (e) providing genre knowledge on argumentative writing and (f) pr oviding text structure knowledge. The learning activities describe what students are expected to do to r each the intended learning outcomes, such as (a) observing, listening and comprehending while watching knowledge clips, (b) exercising and writing the strongest possible argumentative text, (c) actively using the ar gumentation guide, (d) discussing strategies with their peer, and (e) internalizing strategies. Regarding "collaborative writing", teachers have to script the collaboration between students by defining which phases of the writing process are done individually or collaboratively (depending on the research condition, students are (a) collaborating through all phases of the writing process, or (b) alternating between individual and collaborative writing). Additionally, teachers had to pair up students into productive duo's (based on writing skills and personalities). As to the learning activities, students are expected to (a) work together in productive pairs, (b) follow the scripted collaboration, (c) discuss, listen and negotiate with their peer, (d) understand each other's point of view and (e) write together in an online Google Docs. As a concluding remark, we would like to point out the movement towards more clarity and transparency in the design of (writing) interventions, since only few authors (e.g., Rijlaars dam et al., 2017) are providing extended information on their intervention studies. Additionally, we aim to point out the importance of reporting design principles in research publications (De Smedt et al., 2020).
