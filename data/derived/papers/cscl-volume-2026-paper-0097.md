# Fostering Cocreativity in Primary Education Through Viacocrea: Effects on Students’ Creative Products

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores how the Viacocrea technology platform enhances cocreative processes in primary education. Twenty-five twelve-year-old students participated in a project-based learning intervention, creating digital infographics collaboratively. Students’ creative outcomes were assessed using Guilford’s creativity dimensions (1950): fluency, flexibility, and originality. Findings show that Viacocrea effectively supported idea generation, adaptation, and originality, embedding creative processes throughout the project. This study demonstrates that technology can foster cocreativity within the curriculum, offering practical insights for teaching collaborative and creative skills in primary classrooms.

Creativity is recognised as a key educational goal, fostering cognitive flexibility, originality, and innovative thinking (Cremin & Chappell, 2019). From a sociocultural perspective, it is a collaborative process shaped by dialogue and interaction (Sawyer,  2021; Glăveanu, 2010). In this view, cocreativity emphasises the joint generation of ideas and knowledge, going beyond individual contributions. Digital technologies have increasingly been used to scaffold creative collaboration by supporting idea sharing, visualization, and iterative design processes in learning environments. Viacocrea addresses this gap by providing a shared, multi-user platform that fosters engagement and collaboration on real-world challenges. Through graphical representations of cocreative processes and a comprehensive toolkit, Viacocrea enables students to generate, refine, and combine ideas effectively, promoting iterative thinking and problem-solving (Pifarré, 2019).

The platform structures cocreation into six interconnected phases, such as Start up, Define, Design, Build Up, Sum up, and Communicate, supported by visual techniques and guidance for both divergent (e.g., brainstorming, idea elaboration) and convergent thinking (e.g., idea selection, reasoning). Viacocrea also systematically models a cocreative language, encouraging iterative thinking, questioning, exploration of perspectives, and reflection, which collectively cultivate collaborative and original outcomes. This study aims to examine how a technology-enhanced cocreative pedagogy supported by Viacocrea affects primary students’ creative processes and outcomes, specifically investigating its impact on the fluency, flexibility, and originality of their final products.

This research followed a case study methodology in a sixth-grade classroom in Lleida (Spain) with 25 students (aged 12). Over the course of nine weeks, groups of 4-5 students created digital infographics to present aspects of their school and culture to peers in the United States. The project adopted a project-based learning approach, combining inquiry, collaboration, and creative use of technology. Viacocrea supported the process by enabling shared brainstorming, refining, and consolidating ideas. Final products were analysed using a rubric aligned with Guilford’s creativity dimensions (1950): fluency, flexibility, and originality (see Table 1).

Students’ final products were collected and assessed with a creativity rubric. These products took the form of digital infographics created collaboratively using Canva, an online multi-user design platform for creating visual content.

## Data analysis

The final infographics were evaluated using a rubric based on Guilford’s (1950) creativity dimensions: fluency, flexibility, and originality. Each dimension was operationalised into four criteria (see Table 1).  Each criterion captured observable character istics of the final infographic (e.g., diversity of ideas, inclusion of multiple perspectives, or novelty of content and design), allowing the creativity dimensions to be systematically assessed. 612 Each criterion was scored on a 0-2 scale (0 = not observable; 1 = emerging; 2 = effective). Thus, each dimension had a maximum of 8 points, and total group scores could reach 24.  Inter-rater agreement was established through independent scoring followed by discussion of discrepancies.
