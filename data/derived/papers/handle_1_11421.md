# Towards InCoRe - An Interactive Co-Regulation Model for training Teacher Classroom Communication Skills

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This short paper introduces InCoRe, a conceptual and early-stage implementation of an interactive co-regulation model aimed at supporting teacher training in classroom communication and socio-emotional conflict management. InCoRe is embedded within MITHOS, a hybrid AI training system combining psychodynamic theory and computational affect modeling to interpret teacher emotional experiences during classroom conflicts. Grounded in Operationalized Psychodynamic Diagnostics and conflict resolution theory, the model identifies internal conflicts, lead affects, and behavioral intentions to guide virtual student responses. We present findings from a Wizard-of-Oz study validating the mapping between subjective emotional experiences and conflict regulation styles. Results demonstrate the feasibility of InCoRe for real-time interpretation of co-regulation behavior, setting the stage for more comprehensive system deployment and large-scale validation.

## Introduction

Socioemotional interactions form the foundation of human experience, particularly in caregiving relati onships such as between teachers and students. Teachers, mu ch like parents, are central to children’s socioemo tional development and serve as key role models for emotion regulation. Teachers who are able to reflect on and regulate their emotions, motives (i.e., emotional needs of oneself), and intentions (i.e., desired affect on others) can better understand student behaviors and take their perspec tives (Nišponská, 2023; Talia et al., 2021; Park & Tsovaltzi, 2022). In doing so, they support students in co-regulating their emotional responses (Asen & Fonagy, 2021; Nišponská, 2023). Understanding these internal dyna mics—teachers’ motives and intentions for co-regulation behavior—is therefore crucial (Sullivan et al., 2014).

Despite increasing awareness of emotion regulation in the learning sciences, most professional development programs and existing training systems neglect the relational and subjective complexity of teacherstudent conflict. While agent-based models such as  Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) focus on top-down reasoning or observable behavior, they lack mechanisms for capturing the internal conflicts and motivational structures that drive co-regulatory communication. To address this, we integ rate the Operationalized Psychodynamic Diagnostics framework—developed and refined across its editions (OPD, 2001; OPD-2, 2008; OPD-3, 2024) for identifying internal emotional conflicts and confli ct resolution theory (Blake & Mouton, 1979) for mod eling interpersonal regulation strategies based on concern for self and others.

In this paper, we introduce InCoRe—an interactive co-regulation model embedded within the automated version of the MITHOS training system (Chehayeb et al., 2024) that was developed based on the Wizard-of-Oz version of the MITHOS system (Bhuvaneshwara et al., 2023). InCoRe bridges internal psychological mechanisms with observable conflict resolution strategies to s upport teacher self-reflection in real-time. We report findings from a Wizard-of-Oz study that investigates how teachers’ subjective  emotional experiences align with their coregulation behavior in classroom conflict scenarios. Our aim is to establish the conceptual and empiri cal foundation for InCoRe and demonstrate its feasibili ty as a framework for supporting real-time teacher training through the automated MITHOS training system, the architecture of which is provided in Figure 1. InCoRe model summary The InCoRe  model interprets teacher co-regulation behavior by grounding emotional experien ces in the Operationalized Psychodynamic Diagnostics framework  (OPD-2, 2008; OPD-3, 2024). It infers internal conflicts—such as control vs. submission —based on observable behavior and subjective reports, identifying the lead affect (e.g., anger, shame) that shapes the teacher's response. This process is informed by emotion regulation theory, which highlights how stress can shift respo nses from deliberate to automatic modes (Gross, 200 2; Gross, 2015), shaping how internal conflicts manifest in classroom co-regulation. These lead affects are then mapped to underlying in tentions, revealing how teachers aim to influence student emotions or actions during conflict. Confli ct resolution strategies (Blake & Mouton, 1979) gui de this interpretation by situating teacher responses along dimensions of task and relational focus. InCoRe uses this triad of conflict, affect, and int ention to dynamically generate student-agent responses that reflect a psychologically grounded interaction  loop. Social norms, contextualized within the clas sroom setting, further modulate this process by interpret ing the teacher’s behavior in terms of expected obl igations and role expectations. For instance, a teacher's emotio nal response to rule-breaking may be processed through norms emphasizing fairness or authority, shaping how the student-agent reacts  in turn. These insights feed into a feedback and control loop embedded within the automated MITHOS system (Chehayeb et al., 2024)—our hybrid AI training architecture—which synchronizes multimodal inputs and drives real-time student-agent adaptation. Figure 1 illustrates the overall system architectur e: multimodal signals from the teacher (e.g., voice, posture, gaze) are processed through components suc h as the AffectToolbox (Mertes et al., 2024) and th e Geni:OS1, then passed to InCoRe, which models co-regulation behavior by combining affect interpretat ion from ALMA (Gebhard, 2005) with the aforementioned Operationalized Psychodynamic Diagnostics framework (OPD2, 2008; OPD-3, 2024). The resulting output is used by InCoRe, implemented in Visual SceneMaker (Gebhard et al., 2012), to orchestrate synchronized, context-sensitive student responses. This model-driven automation enables InCoRe to function as a hybrid AI driven and psycho logically grounded component within  the automated MITHOS system, supporting scalable, real-time co-regulation training for teachers.

## Methodology

To evaluate the conceptual foundations of InCoRe, w e conducted a Wizard-of-Oz (WoZ) study involving 60 teacher trainees engaged in simulated classroom conflict scenarios using the first version of the MITHOS training system (Bhuvaneshwara et al., 2023). Participants interacted with a virtual student in a mixed-reality environment while a human "wizard" interpreted their behavior i n real time. After the interaction, semi-structured interviews based on the Operationalized Psychodynamic Diagnost ics framework (OPD-2, 2008;  OPD-3, 2024) were conducted to explore participants’ subjective emotional experiences, including internal conflicts, lead affects, and behavioral intentions. Additionally, participants c ompleted a lead affect questionnaire, and trained i nterviewers completed a countertransference assessment. An annotation scheme was developed to categorize internal conflicts 1 https://www.semvox.de/technologies/genios/ and align them with expressed motives and intention s, serving as the empirical foundation for InCoRe’s interpretive engine. ## Key findings and implications

Analysis of the Wizard-of-Oz study revealed that the most frequently observed  internal conflict among teacher trainees was Submission vs. Control in its active m ode, accounting for 26.4% of all annotated instance s (Table 1). This pattern reflects a strong inclination among teachers to assert authority and maintain control in challenging classroom moments—highlighting a key psychological dynamic that InCoRe is designed to recognize and respond to. Other notable conflicts included passive expres sions of the Oedipal Conflict (18%) and active Need for Care vs. Autarky (14%), each shedding light on emotional  undercurrents influencing co-regulation strategies. These distributions are visualized in Figure 2a and 2b, w hich show both the frequency of conflict types and their modulation as active or passive.

Importantly, participants’ reported lead affects aligned significantly with the annotated conflicts. Across multiple categories, teachers’ dominant emotional r esponses (e.g., anger, helplessness, shame) consist ently matched the conflict types inferred from their inte rview narratives and interaction behavior. This ali gnment supports the psycholo gical validity of the OPD-driven annotation scheme (OPD-2, 2008; OPD-3, 2024)  for modeling teacher emotional experiences and behaviou r thereof based on internal conflicts, and reinforces its use as a foundation for affect-driven behavior modeling.

Preliminary results from the automated InCoRe syste m further demonstrated promising alignment with the annotated data from the WoZ study (Bhuvaneshwar a et al., 2023). Following the results, the final a utomated system (Chehayeb et al., 2024), implements the  most frequently detected conflict Control in its ac tive mode, to capture the key affective and intentional responses  found in the Wizard-of-Oz study, and renders the automatic real-time interpretation of teacher co-regulation behavior, paving the way for scalable, psychologically grounded training tools that offer tailored feedback and support within simulated classroom environments.
