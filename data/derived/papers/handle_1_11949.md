# The Impact of Peer Relationships in Adolescent Virtual Social Media on Academic Self-Identity

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigates the influence of adolescent social media interactions on academic self-identity in China. It explores the relationship between peer relationships, online social capital, and academic self-identity, considering demographic factors. Through retrospective analysis and questionnaires, significant correlations were found between peer relationships and academic self-identity, and between online social capital and peer relationships. Peer relationships mediate the impact of online social capital on academic self-identity, with variations by gender, major, and region. The study contributes to understanding the role of virtual peer relationships in academic identity formation within an East Asian context.

## Research design

Retrospective analysis method (Brown, 1982) was adopted in this study to investigate how recent high school graduates perceive the online social capital, peer relationship and academic self-identity during their high school life. A questionnaire that contains with basic demographic information (such as age, gender, major and region) and 3 sub-scales was used to collect data.

Data was collected by online survey and participants were selected from three typical economic regions in China by the stratified sample technique (see figure 2). 1268 valid respondents were returned with an average age of 20.5 years (SD = 1.55).

Figure 3 Interaction of Peer Relation and Academic Self-Identity on Major, Region and Gender This study extends social capital theory by demonstrating how online peer interactions in collectivist cultures serve dual roles: as emotional anchors in high-stress academic environments and as competitive benchmarks for self-evaluation (Hou & Qin et al.,2024). Contrasting with Western findings (Nesi, 2018), Chinese adolescents’ academic identity is more susceptible to peer-mediated social capital due to systemic pressure, highlighting the need for culturally tailored educational interventions.

## Limitations and future directions

First, the retrospective design may introduce recall bias. Second, the suboptimal reliability of peer relationship scales (α =.62) calls for caution. Third, data collected may not capture recent trends. Future studies should adopt longitudinal designs with real-time data.
