# Turning Ourselves Inside Out: Reading and Creating Graphic Medicine in Community

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
An interdisciplinary team designed a graphic medicine project to address health inequalities, implementing it in public libraries with underserved populations. Participants read graphic novels about experiences with illness, then create a 4-8 panel comic about a healthrelated experience of their own. Research questions are 1) what personal insights about health arise when reading and discussing graphic narratives? and 2) what community insights about health arise when creating and sharing stories in the comics format?

## Issues addressed and potential significance of the work

The recent pandemic has brought mainstream attention to the health disparities beneath the surface of our wealthy nation. Researchers attending to these issues know that underserved populations such as Black, Latinx,  and Indigenous communities and those from lower socioeconomic backgrounds suffer not only from lack of access to health care, but also access to health information. This paucity of information is actually a two-way street – while these populations receive less health information, their health experiences remain underrepresented in  medical research. The health perspectives and experiences o f underserved groups must be brought forward and ma de visible to decrease persistent health inequalities.

An effective tool for addressing this inequality in health information comes from the emergent field of graphic medicine. Graphic medicine refers to health narratives and medical information created and shared in the format of comics, which, when collected, create a sequential art story called a graphic novel. Originally conceived as a way to increase the empathy of medical student s, it has expanded to include the communication of health information to various populations as well as a too l for participatory personal expression of health e xperiences. In their Graphic Medicine Manifesto, the interdisciplinary field’s founders state, “Gr aphic medicine is also a movement for change that challenges the dominant methods of scholarship in healthcare, offering a more inclusive perspective of medicine, illness, disability, caregiving, and being cared for” (Czerwiec, et al, 2015). This paper reports the initial findings from a graphic medicine project hosted by public libraries in three communities with underserved populations, including  rural and urban locations. The project curriculum was iteratively designed by an interdisciplinary team, which includes researchers from learning sciences, information science, English, journalism, and health services. The project research questions are 1) what personal  insights about health arise when reading and discussing graphic narratives? and 2) what community insights about health arise when creating and sharing stories in the comics format? Theoretical and methodological approaches Our research is guided by sociocultural-historical theories, bringing the focus of the analysis to peo ple learning in context through mediational means, in this case,  language and images (Cole, 1996). Graphic novels l end themselves to Bakhtinian analysis through their het eroglossic structure that “borrows pervasively from  other cultural genres and discourses, and in doing so appropriates a range of cultural and intellectual ideas and narrative and discursive forms" (McCallum, 1999, p. 258). Thr ough the lens of “funds of knowledge,” we view the participants as “competent, they have knowledge, and their life experiences have given them that knowl edge" (González, Moll, & Amanti, 2005, p. x). Each participant brings their funds of knowledge to the library program and actively produces meanings for the texts they r ead, discuss, create, and share. As researchers, ou r close attention to these activities in context illuminates the informal learning experience. This qualitative research project takes the form of  a comparative case study (Merriam, 2009) in three public libraries. A maximum of 10 patrons sign up t hrough each library location, for a total of 30 par ticipants. Data includes a) pre-and post-program surveys abou t experiences with graphic novels/comics and with h ealth and health care, b) video recordings of the eight o nline program sessions, and c) the four to eight pa nel comic stories created by participants.

The research team qualitatively codes data from the  surveys, sessions, and participant-created comics through the lens of the previously discussed theore tical framework. Team members complete a round of o pen coding, then compare results to create a series of closed codes. Using these closed codes, team members re-code ICLS2022 Proceedings 2164 © ISLS the data corpus until an acceptable level of agreement is reached (Merriam 2009). This process will be completed by June 2022.

## Findings and implications

Initial findings come from a rural library, where t he solicitated participants were older adults in th eir 60’s and 70’s. Led by a member of the research team, the gro up first discussed the book Cancer Made Me a Shallower Person: A Memoir in Comics  by Miriam Engelberg, followed by My Degeneration: A Journey Through Parkinson’s by Peter Dunlap-Schohl. In the earliest conversati ons, participants often commented on new knowledge gained about the disease discussed, i.e., “I didn’t know that…” As discussions continued, participants shared more of their personal health experiences the had some similarities to the experiences of the comic authors. A common theme amongst these senior citizens was concern about losing greater control of their bodies and the burden it will place upon others.

While initially apprehensive about their ability t o create a comic, they all reported feeling capable thanks to the step-by-step coaching from another member of the research team. In the next to last session, this member asked everyone to reflect on a moment from their experiences in the pandemic in order to create a storyboard. As they shared their drawings, common themes arose of anxiety and its impact on the body.
