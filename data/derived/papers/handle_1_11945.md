## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This proposal conceptualizes a system to further Knowledge Building as a pedagogical approach in education. The system is named Knowledge Network, which will structure knowledge into manageable "nodes" within a network-based model. This model enhances epistemic agency providing personalized learning paths, with content curation by authoritative domain experts, and real-time interactive learning with analytics incorporated to optimize collective learning experiences over time.

## Introduction

Knowledge Building serves as an approach to place s tudents as active participants in constructing thei r learning (Scardamalia & Bereiter 2006). The traditional writ ing forum and discussion approach fosters collabora tion and creativity but faces challenges with scalability issues, as well as lack of persona lization of content to tailor to individual student's strengths and weaknesses. The rise of digital learning environments brings both n ew opportunities, emerging technologies like Large Lan guage Models (LLMs) offer new avenues for knowledge construction, yet their role in classrooms is still being explored. This paper proposes a solution built on a network of classrooms, to measure and track students' learning path and to exchange curated content. This network would enable collaborative learning communities to exchange knowledge asynchronously, and scale efficiently, fo stering an unified standardized approach to teachin g and learning.

## Background

Creating a centralized and easily accessible knowle dge repository for the community is a critical aspe ct of Knowledge Building, realistically this is done via technological mean s. (Scardamalia, Bereiter, & Lamon, 1994). However, indigeneity and lived experiences often shape knowledge, each s tudent could potentially bring in fresh perspectives on an old topic, but without guidance the usefulness of t hat perspective is diminished. The overhead of managing the exploration also bogs down the teacher's effort with limited resources. The early success of knowledge-building methodologies largely stemmed from integra ting technology with pedagogy (Scardamalia, 2004). Visual representations of knowledge hierarchies p roved essential, enabling students to grasp subject structures and appreciate their broader implications. Knowledge Forum (Scardamalia & Bereiter 1994) are infrastructures that are actively used to create conditions for knowledge building communities. Future research in pedagogy must therefore focus no t only on adapting technology for educational use but also on pioneering technological advancements that address existing challenges in education. A key theme in this evolution is unity, the internet, the rise of MOOCs, and the knowledge forum all share this theme. The vision of a connected network is not simply about deliveri ng online content; it’s about harnessing the benefi ts that connection brings, where one can break down the physical traditional barrier of the classroom,  and share each classroom’s unique contributions with the broader community. Current challenges ● Assessment challenges: Difficulty in assessing coll aboration, creativity, and deep understanding using traditional methods (Scardamalia  & Bereiter, 2021). This limitation creates a scalab ility issue, as each additional student exponentially increases the assessment workload for teachers. ● Time-Consuming: Teachers must devote significant time to  facilitating knowledge-building activities, often conflicting with the demands of a rigid curriculum. (Cennamo & Brandt, 2012) ● Unequal Participation: Collaborative work often suffers from unequal participation, where some students dominate discussions while others remain passive, h indering the collaborative learning experience. (Gallifa, 2018) ● Difficulty Mapping Individual Profiles: Translating individual knowledge, personality, and interests into a computerized model remains challenging.

Proposed solution Design requirements and the knowledge network The core requirements for this system are as follows: ● Function as a Network: The platform must connect cl assrooms, schools, and knowledge building communities.

● Modularized Knowledge: Knowledge should be broken down into manageable, modular units. ● Support a Multi-Level Structure: The system must allow collaboratio n across classroom, school, and global levels.

● Enable Personalized Path Creation: Teachers should be able to design custom views to address curricular needs, and students should have pathways that reflect their personal learning journey. ● Provide Collaborative Tools: Tools should support c ommunication and collaboration at different community levels.

By breaking knowledge into modular "knowledge nodes ”, each node developed by a group of domain experts, the node with condensed version of more common ground truth, would alleviate teachers from the burden of creation of classroom materials. The  modularity approach enables the creation of persona lized learning paths that cater to students’ individual preferences while aligning with course structures. These learning paths can also be analyzed and aggregated to define a unique “learning signature” for each student. Advanced data analytics and AI models could then provide real-time feedback and insights based on this data, enha ncing the learning experience.

This model’s viability is evident in the success of  social media platforms, and Reddit communit ies. Similarly, an educational platform could be designed to meet diverse learning needs. Community and feedback mechanisms Each node could incorporate a discussion board with a community-driven voting system, similar to platforms like Reddit or Stack Exchange. This approach would enable continuous evaluation and improvement of content based on collective feedback. The community-building aspect of knowledge building is a key component of the teacherstudent relationship. Personalized learning paths and course management Personalized learning paths and course content management, by navigating nodes in a specific sequence, teachers candesign course content, while students explore w ithin a predefined area of knowledge. These paths c an be calculated to form the unique signature of both a course and a student’s learning journey. By measuring progress through the number of knowledge nodes completed, teachers can track a student’s development and tailor content accordingly. This re duces the time teachers spend on customizing materi al to match each student’s expertise level.

Values generated for educational stakeholders The knowledge network simplifies continuous content development, allowing teachers to focus more on engaging with students and enhancing instruction. The tool’s visual representation of knowledge nodes intuitively navigates students to grasp complex relationships between con cepts, supporting a deeper understanding of the mat erial. Teachers can also create personalized learning paths, aligning content with students' unique interests and learning styles. Educational institutions also benefit from the tool’s advanced data analytics, which track student progress in detail, offering a comprehensive view of learning outcomes. Concerns and challenges A key concern is the high cost and complexity of maintaining such systems. The requirement for high availability and reliability translates into significant societa l costs. However, the potential to reduce resource redundancy compared to existing approaches, despite potentiall y high maintenance efforts, might represent a  worthwhile trade-off.

Another concern involves predefined paths potential ly restraining the discovery of new knowledge and limiting exploration. This system aims to streamlin e the learning process for existing knowledge, and fast track more students towards researching deep topics. Therefore increasing the overall discoveries on community level. Overall, this paper presents an early introduction to the theoretical foundation for the Knowledge Network system. Its implementation and technical de velopment are currently underway. With current advancements in narrow Artificial Intelligence, the  technical challenges of such a system are perceived as les s formidable than in the past.
