# A Scoping Review of Interdisciplinary Learning Research 
in Higher Education Contexts

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study conducted a scoping review on 19 interdisciplinary education/learning research articles in higher education contexts. Citation network analysis and qualitative analysis revealed that 1) the articles were categorized into three clusters, 2) only 14 articles were cited more than twice, and 3) each cluster cited different articles with different focuses. These findings suggest that interdisciplinary education/learning research is in the phase of establishing shared vocabularies and engaging with conceptual tools.

## Introduction

The importance of interdisciplinary education /learning in higher education has been repeatedly emphasized  to tackle complex social needs such as climate change  (e.g., Repko & Szostak, 2020). Several review articles on interdisciplinary learning have been published in the past few decades (Heen & Nustad, 2023; Spelt et al., 2009). However, scholars repeatedly criticized that interdisciplinary learning lacks foundational theory (e.g., Lyall et al., 2016; Markauskaite et al., 2024). While several researchers rely on theories (e.g., Activity Theory in Papendieck & Clarke, 2024) or conceptual tools (e.g., knowledge object in Muukkonen & Kajamaa, 2024), this issue persists as a central, unresolved theme in interdisciplinary educational research.

To address the lack of theory in interdisciplinary education, it is crucial to clarify how interdiscip linary education/learning research has constructed their arguments using previous  literature. For instance, if a specific theory in an article is frequently cited in subsequent articles, this theory is likely to be positioned as a foundational theory in the future interdisciplinary education  research and practice. Therefore, we selected and analyzed interdisciplinary education/learning  articles, especially those targeted in past reviews, and identified what and how literature has been referenced in those articles. The following two research questions were addressed: RQ1. What articles are commonly cited in the context of interdisciplinary education/learning in higher education? RQ2. How have these referenced articles been cited?

## Method

The first step was to select interdisciplinary lear ning articles, particularly those targeted in past review studies. We collected articles reviewed by Spelt et al. (2009) and Heen and Nustad (2023). We also selected three articles focusing on higher education from the JLS special issue (Markauskaite et al., 2024). In total, we obtain ed 25 accessible articles focused on interdisciplinary education/learning in higher education (hereafter called target articles). The second step wa s to visualize how the target articles cited previous publications.  Bibliographic coupling analysis in VOSviewer (https://www.vosviewer.com/) was used for cluster visualization, based on the number of references shared across the reference lists of 19 target articles retrieved from the Web of Science. Six articles were excluded because their data were unav ailable in Web of Science, making them unsuitable f or VOSviewer visualization. The third step was to check the reference lists in the 19 articles and list articles in the reference cited by  more than two target articles (hereafter called reference articles). The fourth step was to interpret how reference articles were cited in target article s to construct the argumentation in each article. We examined the in-text citations of each reference article and picked up relevant sentences.

## Discussion

Our analysis revealed the three clusters. However, any specific theory frequently cited in subsequent articles was not found. We attempt to interpret the state of interdisciplinary education/learning research using the perspectives on academic disciplines presented by Repko and Szostak (2020). Repko and Szostak (2020) stated that several components define academic discipline: phenomena, epistemology, assumption, concept, theory, and method. Our study revealed that a consensus is now being built on the concepts of interdisciplinarity and interdis ciplinary understanding and recent articles in the JLS starts to use concepts that can characterize interdisciplinar y education. As this result shows, w e believe that the learning sciences field has great potential to advance interdisciplinary education/learning research by especially providing epistemology (e.g., acquisition metaphor, knowledge  creation metaphor), concept (e.g., epistemic boundary, boundary objects, and knowledge objects), theory (e.g., activity theory), and method s (e.g., discourse analysis, quantitative ethnography).

The findings indicate that interdisciplinary education/learning research  has only recently emerged to establish shared vocabularies and started engaging with conceptual tools. For this line of research to advance, it is essential to cite each other’s studies, build ac ademic networks, and foster the accumulation and sh aring of insights. In this regard, the perspectives on academic disciplines  presented by Repko and Szostak (2020) may offer valuable insights.
