# Toward a Theory-Grounded Associative Model of AI Literacy

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This conceptual paper introduces the Associative Model of AI Literacy (AMAIL) for developing AI literacy. Through analysis of literature and empirical data, four interdependent domains containing sixteen competencies are proposed. Principles from learning theories provide insights into AI literacy development. AMAIL integrates constructs with these principles, situating learning in authentic contexts. Further research should investigate AMAIL's real-world implementation. AMAIL can guide pedagogy to develop multidimensional AI literacy as AI proliferates.

## Introduction and background

This conceptual paper presents work towards development of an associative model of AI literacy that is grounded in principles from learning theories. The rapid proliferation of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies necessitates the development of AI literacy among learners in all contexts. As AI becomes further ubiquitous in various facets of life, a nu anced understanding of its affordances, limitations, and societal implications is crucial. However, traditional technology, data, and computer science education often do not provide learners with opport unities to cultivate robust AI literacy. To address th is gap, we introduce the Associative Model of AI Li teracy (AMAIL), grounded in the literature, empirical data, and principles from multiple learning theories. AMAIL emphasizes the interconnected, contextual nature of AI literacy de velopment. This different iates it from existing models which focus on distinct and disconnected AI literacy cons tructs. Previous models have made important contrib utions, delineating key competencies like recognizing AI, u nderstanding its logic, and considering its ethics (Long & Magerko, 2020; Touretzky et al., 2019; Heyder & Pos egga, 2021; Wang et al., 2022). However, more work is needed to address the complex interplay between the se competencies and the need to situate AI literacy  in authentic, learner-driven contexts. Principles from transformative lea rning theory (Mezirow, 2008), constructionism (Papert & Harel, 1991), social cons tructivism (Vygotsky, 1978), and cognitive construc tivism (Piaget, 1977) provide crucial insights into the le arning processes through which AI literacy emerges.  Through analysis of empirical data from an AI literacy deve lopment intervention, we synthesize these principle s with AI literacy constructs, thus proposing a more integrative and contextualized model.

## Methodology: Literature sample and student data analysis

Through our analysis of AI literacy literature, we identified four AI literacy models that are promine nt (Long & Magerko, 2020; Touretzky, Gardner-McCune, Martin, & Seehorn, 2019; Heyder & Posegga, 2021; Wang, Rau, & Yuan, 2022). We then collected a sample of literature reporting empirical studies in which AI was the focus of educational activities (n=57) and coded it accordin g to categories from the four models, principles fr om transformative learning theory, constructionism, so cial constructivism, and cog nitive constructivism, and emergent codes. Then we collected and coded reflection data (n=338) from an after-school intervention in which elementary school students in Indonesia learned abo ut AI (for details, see Relmasira, Lai, & Donaldson, 2023). We used  network analysis with clustering (Girvan & Newman, 2002) to aid our axial coding process of investigating the relationships between concepts related to AI literacy development.

## Findings: The AMAIL Model

Our analysis resulted in a set of four AI literacy constructs in which we found sixteen interdependent  and associative competencies. Some of these competencie s were included in the four prominent AI literacy m odels, but some were not. The use of learning theories to frame our analysis was particularly useful. Our analysis resulted in a network map of correlations of codes for theor y principles and AI literacy constructs at P < 0.05 with 3 clusters at Q = 0.44. Our analysis led to the devel opment of the Associative model of AI literacy  (AMAIL), emphasizing the integration of foundational AI conc epts with principles from supportive learning theor ies. Core to the AMAIL framework are four domains-Recognizing AI, Explaining and Evaluating AI, Interacting with AI, and Ethics Regarding AI. Each domain intertwines wi th principles from transformative learning theory, constructionism, social constructivism, and cogniti ve constructivism including embedding, scaffolded l earnerlearner and learner-AI collaboration, framing, fostering a safe environment, and encouraging creativity and making. Domain 1-Recognition of AI: Central to AMAIL is the recognition of AI, a competency underlined by Long and Magerko (2020), Touretzky et al. (2019), H eyder and Posegga (2021), and Wang et al. (2022). T his competence facilitates an understanding of what constitutes AI and distinguishes it from non-AI technologies. An inherent capability is the recognition of products produced by or with the aid of AI, a facet identified by Long and Magerko (2020), Touretzky et al. (2019), and Wang e t al. (2022).  Domain 2-Explaining and Evaluating AI: Within the domain of explaining and evaluating AI, several competencies emerge. Articulating the limitations of AI, highlighted by Long and Magerko (2020), is cruc ial, as is explicating the strengths of AI. A profo und understanding is required for the roles AI plays in  juxtaposition with human actors, a point of conver gence between Long and Magerko (2020) and Touretzky et al. (2019). Additionally, the potential futures of AI, outlined by Long and Magerko (2020) and Touretzky et al. (2019), and the data AI utilizes, identified by the same sources, as well as its underlying logic recognized by Heyde r and Posegga (2021) and Wang et al. (2022), are es sential facets. Domain 3-Interacting with AI: The realm of interaction with AI spans a wide spectrum of competencies. Learners should be capable of leveraging AI to addr ess problems, a competency cited by Long and Magerk o (2020), Touretzky et al. (2019), and Wang et al.  (2022). Generativity, emphasizing the use of AI for  creative purposes, is outlined by Long and Magerko (2020) and Touretzky et al. (2019). Additionally, the abilit y to collaborate with others using AI is a new finding f rom our research, not previously cited in existing models. Moreover, the capacity to use AI for art and expres sion and competency in using AI for fun were also u nique discoveries from our investigation.  Domain 4-Ethics Regarding AI: Ethics, in relation to AI, is paramount. Learners are called upon to critically assess AI fr om an ethical lens, a competency underscored by Lon g and Magerko (2020), Touretzky et al. (2019), Heyder and  Posegga (2021), and Wang et al. (2022). This inclu des questioning power dynamics, evaluating the broader societal impacts, and discerning the explicit and i mplicit purposes of AI implementations. Transformative learning theory, which hinges on questioning and revising one's beliefs and assumptions, finds deep resonance here (see red circles cluster). Ethical considerations compel learners to challenge existing paradigms, leading to paradig m shifts in their conceptual understanding. It's no t just about how AI functions, but the larger implications of its integration into society and its potential ramifications.

## Discussion, implications, and conclusion

The AMAIL model builds upon recent AI literacy fram eworks, emphasizing interconnections within constru cts and competencies. Rooted in empirical analysis of learner experiences, AMAIL provides guidance on pedagogical strategies to develop AI literacy in mea ningful, context-driven learning. Adopting AMAIL poses challenges, requiring educators to facilitate active inquiry and access to resources. However, the benefits outwei gh the barriers, reinvigorating STEM education and empower ing students to leverage AI for good. Further resea rch should investigate AMAIL's real-world implementation across diverse educational set tings. As AI advances, developing AI literacy grounded in learning theory principles will only grow in importance.
