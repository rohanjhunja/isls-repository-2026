# Connected Learning: A Dual Reflexive Pedagogical Identity

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper utilizes an interdisciplinary lens to explore self-learning as a critical pedagogical mechanism. The relational aspect of connected learning is specifically explored through Vygotsky’s sociocultural paradigm and Heinz Kohut’s theory of self psychology. Kohut views identity formation as a dual reflexive relational dynamic. Pedagogical identity construction, then, is a two-pronged relational sociocultural process. Exploration through this framework critically situates identity within learning.

Sociocultural immersion in connected learning: Positioning identity through relationships Some prominent learning sciences  perspectives of identity  include Milner’s (2007) work, which explores researcher identity positionality; Polman’s, which discusses the zone of proximal identity development within the context of Vygotsky's zone of proximal development (2010) and other community-based theorizations of identity (2006); and Nasir’s (2006), which describes identity as situated within the context o f race, culture, and learning. These authors’ postulations are strongly situated within a broader learning community context. Most notable for this paper, however, is the  emphasis on work related  to more intentional identity exploration in less traditional environments  (Halverson, 2004; Tierney, 2020). I draw my closest parallels to Halverson's work on the relationship between narratives and ide ntity as a site of learning. Halverson presents a case analysis of a youth who constructs an oppositional identity that ultimately  is entwined in their pieces of evidence of learning. In this paper, I  call to attention the need for more frameworks that  situate spaces for intentional identity exploration, exclusively and c ollectively, before delving into identity in its learning process. A critical understanding of self is necessary to situate conscious and aware learners. Connected learning, as developed by Ito et al.  (2013), proposes a fluid framework of learning that combines various elements of formal and informal le arning contexts. These elements are represented through an exploration of learners’ interests, opportunities, and relationships.  “The social dimension of consciousness is primary in time and fact. The individual dimension of consciousness is derivative and secondary” (Vygo tsky, 1979, p. 30). Vygotsky’s sociocultural theory of learning asserts that learn ing cannot occur independently of its sociocultural contexts. As such, learning must acco unt for the social and cultural dynamics that paral lel pedagogical processes and  spaces. The sociocultural theory of learning necessitates t he exploration and embodiment of lived experiences across cultural and  historical dimensions. The emergence of the pedagogical identity, then, is a shared process that reflects the duality of learning in the context of self and others. Sociocultural connected learning: The pedagogical identity Heinz Kohut’s s elf psychology asserts the dual reflexive definitions o f self and identity – one that exists exclusively and independently, and the other that is defined based on social interactions and others in one’s context (Kohut, 1997). My proposition builds on connected learning’s relational component, grounded in sociocultural theory, and operationalized through self psychology ’s core tenets of the exclusive self and the self i n the context of others – what this paper terms “the pedagogical identity.”

## Implications and significance: A pedagogy of self learning

Connected learning comprises the integration of and  connections between interests, opportunities, and relationships to enhance diversified tracks of peda gogy for learning to enhance equitable approaches. The facilitation of these relational aspects as a pedag ogical medium affirms identity formation and construction. The pedagogical identity model explicates the importance of identity within the learning and design context. This intersectional model of connected learning offers a  perspective that grounds the learning process with in the confines of this complex yet meaningful interaction  of phenomena. Learning is most meaningful when new pedagogical processes are embedded within existing schemata, subjectively determined across the components of interests, opportunities, and relationships, and further bounded by sociocultural contexts. Many traditional and contemporary approaches within  the learning sciences often describe pedagogies from a range of formal and informal perspectives, according to various epistemologies. However, the explicitness of intentional identity discovery is underexplored. Even more elaborately,  pedagogies that call attention to self exploration, and identity deconstruction and reconstruction are often overlooked and deemed as more fitting to other “less educational” contexts. Nonetheless, ide ntity is inseparable from learners ’ contexts and inevitably affects pedagogical dynamics.  Learning and design systems that intentionally addr ess and encourage identity exploration and (re)construction are needed to facilitate the discovery and development of identity. Learning and design systems that incorporate intentional identity exploration The capitalist system of education follows methodologies tha t operate through very traditional philosophical orientations. Tracks for degree programs are laid out and follow traditional requirements to ensure standardization across competencies and fulfilments. Courses follow traditional requirements set forth b y course outlines and syllabi to demonstrate expertise in intended content areas. These methods and educational system designs negate space for other critical personal and more formaliz ed development al requirements and milestones. Moreover, shifting to a micro-perspective, there often is not the space within existing course requirements  for students to learn more about the criticality of their stories a nd identities and how those position them as learne rs in their pedagogical contexts. The experiential and existential dimensions of human experience cannot be mutually excluded from its existence within the professional  and academic sphere.  Learning and design f rameworks and paradigms that intentionally create spaces for identity exploration position learners as more conscious and active consumers of knowledge in articulating their pedagogical identities. Identity exploration as a medium of social justice and equity for design Intentional identity exploration and construction a lso serve as a medium for supporting social justice and ultimately equity for learning and design systems. When learners are encouraged to explore themselves and their identity deeply, in their exclusive contexts and their share d social contexts, it creates a critical lens to delineate oppressive environments. Particularly for historica lly underrepresented and minoritized groups, identi ty exploration is a critical tool in raising awareness  around issues of social justice and equity within their learning contexts. These shared learnings may serve as a means of connective solidarity in advocating for more diversified and inclusive learning spaces that more equitably support those subjected to oppressive states. Identity exploration as a medium of healing, resilience, and community The pedagogical identity most crucially serves as a site of healing, resilience, and community through learning processes. There is great power in the stories that  connect individuals, and their shared definitions of indivi dual and collective identity.  More emphasis must be placed on co-creating authentic spaces that welcome the raw delicacy and beauty that is founded in the coalition of personal and professional lived experiences. Lea rning and design systems that truly seek to create authentic pedagogical environments are the key to developing a prodigy that represents learning as the cornerstone of healing, resilience, and community within the learning sciences.
