# Sociotechnical Efficacy in Modeling 3D Interactive Environments

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines sociotechnical efficacy in 3D environment modeling through the Roblox Developers Forum. Ito & Reich’s (2017) equity-centered principles explore how youth learning participation is fostered in creative spaces. Available curriculum, plugins, and developer feedback built around “hanging out”, “messing around”, “geeking out” (HOMAGO) emphasize the “hanging out” dimension (Ito, 2009). Platforms enhanced by modular, adaptive assets foster equitable access and social connectedness, shifting learning between participatory HOMAGO to supporting agency and connectivist models.

This study examines how developer forum affordance’s structure participation and learning within environments focused on youth-based sociotechnical efficacy. We analyze contemporary sociotechnical patterns in forum commentary to understand user satisfaction with participation. The discussion associate’s constructionist technological design with connectivist learning theory (Harasim, 2017). It frames Roblox as a desirable platform that serves as both a creative metaverse and a social medium. As Roblox Studio incorporates generative AI technologies for mainstream 3D development, youth engagement in computer-mediated environments increasingly blends creation, collaboration, and play. The HOMAGO framework situates this engagement spectrum within distinct learning dispositions  (Ito et al., 2019). Rather than isolated innovation, youth creators engage in iterative, modular technical feedback loops with peers, enhancing agency (constructionist making) and connection (connectivist learning) (Peppler & Thompson, 2024). Generative AI assets amplify this  dynamic by simplifying creation, freeing cognitive space for relational and aesthetic collaboration. This grounded study proposes the research question: in what ways do generative AI tools and modular 3D environment design features in Roblox Studio influence literacy development in youth-centered digital communities?

## Theoretical framework

Ito et al.’s (2019) HOMAGO study remains foundational for understanding and documenting how youth learn through interest-driven participation in digital media. Insight into “ Equity by Design ” calls for learning technologies that align home, school, and community contexts to bridge socio-technical divides (Ito & Reich, 2017). Together, these frameworks support a sociotechnical lens that values open participation and access. Roblox’s plugin-based systems illustrate Ito and Reich’s (2017) principle that 'open ≠ equitable.' The platforms community-driven structure enables learners to co-develop technical literacies; however, it implies that it will increase accessibility for all over time. While the tools are technically available for free, equitable learning arises only when their use connects to youth interests, identities, and peer mentorship.

## Case context

This poster presents findings on Roblox’s Updates & Community Events DevForum post “2025 Year in Review!”. The post highlighted new tools introduced to Roblox over the last year  including: custom emotes, server authority/ban API, events detail page, emissive maps, speech-to-text API, text-to-speech API, and physically-based rendering for accessories. These tools lower barriers to participation and create conditions for peer-to-peer engagement, described as “ geeking out ” mode, where learning is distributed across design communities and individual experimentation is reinforced.  Findings suggest that modular embedded design tools, like plugins, transform ‘messing around’ behaviors into collective “geeking out” practices. This study will employ  thematic analysis of developer comments  on the Roblox DevForum. Using a deductive approach, a priori  themes aligned with HOMAGO categories  guide analysis by identifying  patterns of sociotechnical affordances in 3D development workflows. Each utterance is coded, then placed in category that aligns with an overarching HOMAGO theme. Of 123 comments, 113 utterances  from the post were analyzed using thematic analysis to examine how the seven  tools transform technical literacy practices into socially meaningful interactions. ## Implications

The social interaction sample highlights the need to assess equity in technological cultures rather than assuming open-source ensures inclusion. Emerging generative AI systems within creative 3D platforms like Roblox risk reinforcing hierarchical participation, where technical proficiency candiminish collaborative engagement, leading to reduced motivation and negative environmental affordances for users, and a larger technology gap. Practices like co-creation and other accessible forms of ‘hanging out’ should be leveraged as inclusive practices that extend beyond isolated production toward shared experience.
