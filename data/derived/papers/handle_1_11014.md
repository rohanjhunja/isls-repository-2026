# Exploring Duolingo User’s Learning Experience Through Text Mining

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study of 24,390 Duolingo reviews employed text mining to analyze users' motivational and cognitive expressions, classifying feedback by thumbs-up count for agreement levels. The findings underscore a substantial difference in feedback among agreement groups, particularly in users’ cognitive expression. Reviews garnering more thumbs up proved more relevant for learning, emphasizing their importance in tailoring platforms for educational needs.

As a popular linguistic application, Duolingo has been used by more than 500 million users worldwide  (Blanco, 2023). Duolingo is a game-based learning application with a high rating score  (4.7) and a total of 19.5 million reviews in Google Play.  Although several studies have proved its effectiveness (Ajisoko, 2020; Munday, 2017; Vesselinov & Grego, 2012), a lack of studies focus on its users’ learning experience regarding their cognition and motivation. Research has suggested that psychological effects such as motivation and cognition are core factors influencing users’ experience in online learning. For example, Ryan and Deci’s Self-Determination Theory (2000) can be used to explain how autonomy, competence, and relatedness can influence the intrinsic motivation needed for prolonged engagement in online learning platforms (Sun et al., 2019). Therefore, it is essential to build on this understanding of  users’ cognitive aspects and motivation for improving the understanding of an individual's learning experience in the online learning environment (Geng et al., 2020). To understand user s’ learning experience on Duolingo, this research aims to present a text mining approach to identify user s’ cognition and motivation through user reviews  by answering the research question: How does user feedback differ among agreement groups, inferred by thumbs-up received by community members, in cognition and motivation domains?

This study analyzed the Duolingo app’s review data (N = 24,391) using LIWC (Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count) (Tausczik & Pennebaker, 2010). The anonymous review data was collected from Google Play. The review contents were restricted to English language only, and  emojis also being excluded. The data has several key variables including review ID, review content,  and number of thumbs up per review. Reviews are displayed on the Google Play website, and users could express their agreement by answering the question aligned with each review: "Did you find this useful?" if they thought the individual review was helpful. Based on the distribution of the number of thumbs up for each review, the study classifies reviews into three groups: high agreement (thumbs up count > 2), low agreement (0 < thumbs up count ≤ 2), and no agreement (thumbs up count = 0). This research selects two dimensions from LIWC-22 Dictionary for analysis: cognition (e.g., insight, certainty, causation)  and drives (e.g., power, achievement, affiliation).The drives category represents users’ learning motivation and the cognition category refers to users’ thinking on this app. Table 1 provides a statistical description of agreement groups, including frequency of keywords in each LIWC category. The means reflect the standardized scores of each category, which have been converted to percentiles ranging from 1 to 99. More than 85% of reviews have not received any thumbs up from other users and 9.5% of reviews only received one  or two thumbs up. Only 5.3% of reviews receive d more than two thumbs up. Among the reviews of the high agreement group, 9.8% of them received more than 100 thumbs up and the highest number of thumbs up for an individual review is 6,720.

The study tested for the homogeneity of variances because of unequal sample sizes. It shows that the distribution of each category is different across all categories of agreement groups. Thus, to compare the three agreement groups, we used the Kruskal-Wallis test and found that both motivation and cognition were significant (p <.001) different across groups. The study revealed distinct patterns in the linguistic features associated with different groups of user agreement. Specifically, reviews with more thumbs up are more relevant for learning and should be considered specifically for that need.  It contributes to the existing research on educational applications and underscores the need for a more comprehensive understanding of users' cognitive and motivational dynamics. It also helps app developers save time in selecting valuable reviews and better understand users' needs for potential improvement. I nsights from this study can inform the design and optimization of educational applications to enhance user experiences and, consequently, learning outcomes. In conclusion, this study sets the stage for further exploration into the intricate interplay between cognitive and motivational elements, laying a foundation for app developers and researchers to find more effective user feedback, and offering valuable implications for the future development and improvement of educational applications.
