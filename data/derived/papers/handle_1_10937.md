# Fostering a Sense of Belonging in Children of Persian-speaking Immigrants through an Interactive Mobile App

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper examines the challenges faced by the children of Persian-speaking immigrants to learn their heritage language, and provides them with a culturally rich, multi-modal pedagogy. Being a complex language, along with the absence of engaging learning resources, makes learning Persian challenging outside of Iran. In solution, an interactive mobile app is proposed, immersing children in a joyful learning experience that seeks to enhance their Persian language acquisition and foster their sense of belonging.

## Introduction and objectives

Persian (ﯽﻓﺎرﺳis one of the major spoken languages of Iran, however, its reading and comprehension come with challenges. Reading is one of the most basic ways for a child to connect with the world, but many young kids experience difficulty in learning to read Persian. This situation becomes more challenging when learners live outside their native Persian-speaking place. Many Persian-speaking Iranian families have migrated to other countries, and they wish their children to learn Persian to connect with their culture and heritage. However, the available resources are neither effective nor engaging for children. The complexity of the Persian language poses even more challenges to the learners, such as identifying the letters visually, distinguishing words with similar pronunciation, combining consonants and vowels, recognizing similar words with different meanings (ﺮﯿﺷ lion, ﺮﯿﺷ faucet, ﺮﯿﺷ milk), Syllable awareness, and Reading comprehension. As language is a conduit for comprehending and embracing culture, this slow drift from Persian is eventually causing immigrants to feel marginalized and disconnected. Studies show that using the language of origin at home can positively impact the integration and academic achievement of the children of immigrants (Portes & Hao, 2004). To cater to the mentioned challenges, this project aims to equip gesture-based videos and digital multimodal games to m ake learning Persian easier, fun, and engaging. The access issue will be addressed by providing a mobile-based Persian learning application as one of the outcomes of this project. This research is guided by the following questions: (1) How can we make the Persian learning experience effective, engaging, and fun for learners with limited daily exposure to Persian? (2) How can culturally inspired content as gesture-based and interactive games foster a sense of belonging among learners?

Theoretical frameworks The access  to culturally relevant resources is limited within the formal education system, which can hinder children’s ability to integrate their ethnic identity with the curriculum being taught. The evidence from research on gestures and learning has highlighted the potential to aid the comprehension of complex language (Kelly, 2001), better performance in remembering details while reciting stories (Stevanoni & Salmon, 2005), and has proven a great deal of potential in STEM education and language learning in recent years (Alibali & Nathan, 2012; Goldin-Meadow, 2011; Kita et al., 2017). Multimodal learning emphasizes the i ntegration of multiple-sensory modalities, such as visual, auditory, and kinesthetic to facilitate effective learning and improve learners' understanding of the received information. There is evidence of the benefits of multimodal learning and different modes of presenting content knowledge including verbal and non-verbal. Accordingly, this research aims to understand the amalgamation of these prominent frameworks to make learning how to read Persian easier, accessible, and engaging  for children. ## Methods

This is a design-based study with mixed-method data that runs in iterative processes of content development, testing, data collection, data analysis, and revisiting the pedagogy and the app. Currently, the research is in the content development stage. Thirty children 6-10 years old born into immigrant Persian-speaking families will be recruited for the study. The intervention tool is a mobile application-based digital environment that is being developed by the team. These children are expected to have a lit tle familiarity with the language and have heard ICLS 2024 Proceedings 2205 © ISLS a few basic words in their home, school, or through the media. The app assesses child’s familiarity with some Persian words and then progresses accordingly. Children get to choose from a wide range of games,  making it non-linear to foster agency. All modules are based on storytelling, puzzles, and interactive games, and children will learn through play. The learning design is inspired by the teaching pedagogy developed by Ali Sedaghaty (http://amookhayat.com/),  dividing the app into 6 modules comprising (1) Vocabulary expansion, (2) Introduction to 22 Persian letters, (3) Vowels and mixing them with 22 letters, (4) Introduction to exceptions and 10 remaining letters, (5) Word building, (6) Reading skills and comprehension. This method has been tested and used by community facilitators over the past 12 years to teach children, youth, and adults in Iran and Afghanistan. In the app, each module is further divided into submodules as per specific requirement. In each submodule/module, children get numerous videos, games, and other interactive elements to play. Once they completed the learning objectives of that module, they proceed to the next module. To gain insights on the learning gain, motivation, engagement, user experience, children's attitude changes, etc, the study will be using (i) a Pretest-posttest questionnaire for children to assess their learning gain, (ii) A questionnaire for the parents to learn about their kids’ prior knowledge, (iii) A short semi-structured interview for the children before and after using the tool for the usability (iv) A semi-structured interview with the parents to know about the attitude and behavioral changes in the children.  Moreover, qualitative data will be gathered through the screen recording, video recordings, and note-taking of the interaction between children, the screen, and their environment.  After analyzing, the refinements will be made on the tool to test again.

## Anticipated findings and conclusion

The primary goal of this project is to offer an accessible and joyful language learning experience to children of immigrant Persian-speaking families to connect them with their culture and create a sense of belonging. This study will provide insights into the multimodal pedagogy employed in the app's design on the engagement of children in Persian learning. The use of culturally inspired content will help formulate specific design guidelines to support children’s connections to their cultural identity.
