# MakingSEL: Integrating Maker Education with Social Emotional Learning in Teacher Professional Development

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This research poster delves into the integration of Maker Education and Social Emotional Learning (SEL) through the lens of the MakingSEL program. Aimed at educators and teacher educators, the program blends hands-on making activities with SEL principles to enrich teaching practices. Through physical and online sessions, participants explored collaborative making experiences, fostering creativity, problem-solving, and interpersonal skills. The poster presents the MakingSEL program’s structure, observed outcomes, and implications for practice and future education research.

## Introduction

Despite an upswell of work in maker education and social-emotional learning (SEL) over the past decades, studies on how these two pedagogical methods interact remain sparse. The MakingSEL program explores a convergence of these frameworks and offers a plat form for focusing on creativity, critical thinking,  collaboration, emotional well-being, and holistic educational experiences. The primary research question this paper is trying to address is: How can Maker education and SEL be integrated into teacher professional development in such a way as to create a MakingSEL curricular unit that feels "unified" to participants? Maker Education emphasizes hands-on learning and fosters skills like problem-solving and collaboration (Halverson & Sheridan, 20 14). Rooted in  earlier educational philosophies such as those of Dewey and  Montessori, it also promotes Social Emotional Lear ning (SEL) competencies (Zins, 2004). Social Emotional L earning (SEL) focuses on skills for emotional and s ocial well-being (Denham, 1998). Notable frameworks include CA SEL, which outlines five core competencies, and the RULER model, which emphasizes emotional intelligence and has positively impacted adolescent development (Durlak et al., 2011). MakingSEL combines maker education and SEL; this integration enhances social-emotional skills such as collaboration and problem-solving. MakingSEL is designed as a unified system in which maker activities and SEL are mutually reinforcing. The Ma kingSEL program was led by two teacher educators – Avi Peeri (a SEL expert) and Roey Kafri (a maker education expert). It included 20 educators from diverse educational backgrounds. The program, conceived and executed ov er 10 weekly 4-hour meetings, involved a series of collaborative making activities paired with refle ctive discussions to integrate SEL principles and m aker challenges.  The program’s syllabus paired each skill with a maker challenge related to it. Sessions were allocated so that those dealing with self-awareness and self-management would be conducted onl ine, while sessions revolving around social skills would be held in per son in order to allow participants from remote area s to take part in the program.

The design approach: A look at the second session The program's design approach is exemplified in its second session. The focus of the second session was on selfawareness. This session, conducted via Zoom, featur ed a maker activity centered around 3D modeling usi ng the TinkerCad program. TinkerCad facili tates the creation of 3D models on a computer, serv ing as an initial step in digital production processes. One of the objectives  was to introduce the notion of "maker pedagogy" (B likstein,

2018) through the lens of inquiry-based learning. The intention was  for the students to begin individual learning

as soon as possible to avoid frustrations among stu dents with prior knowledge or a different level of proficiency (differentiation) related to the teacher’s pace. Ro ey emphasized direct peer learning. A reflec tive discussion concerning the query succeeded in this segment, "Ho w did we learn?" and the practice of two self-awareness tools. The first tool, the Mood Meter, developed by  RULER (Brackett & Rivers, 2013), encompasses an em oji board representing diverse emotional states from which students select one mirroring their current sentiment. This exercise is meant to assist students in becoming cognizant of how their emotions fluctuate throughout the day and how that impacts their actions. The second reflecti ve activity entailed a self-awareness reflection page, on which students documented their strengths and weaknesses encountered during the maker challenge; the artifac t from the second activity was subsequently shared with the group.

## Discussion

The MakingSEL teacher PD aims to integrate Maker ed ucation and SEL into a unified curricular unit. Our evidence suggests that all interviewed participants found that learners’ social and emotional elements surfaced in the MakingSEL activity. Throughout the activity, th e participants all reported that they had found man y ways to connect their making behaviors to their social-emotional learning.

## Conclusion and future research

In this study, we ran a set of activities (MakingSE L) meant to foreground connections between Maker ed ucation and Social Emotional Learning (SEL). We found that participants all perceived a strong connection betw een Making and SEL. We detailed this in one showcased a ctivity, designed to demonstrate to the participant s how self-awareness skills can be addressed in a learning pro cess, even within a large group. The observed outco mes highlight the potential benefits of integrating Mak er Education and SEL into professional development curricula more broadly. That said, this study was limited: th is is a small sample of a small study. Nevertheless, we are excited to see that the connections “felt authentic ” to our participants. We believe that a larger, mo re comprehensive study may show deeper benefits in connecting Making and SEL.
