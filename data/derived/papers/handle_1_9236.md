# Systematic Literature Review on Teacher’s Roles in AI-Supported Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The widespread adoption of ChatGPT, an artificial intelligence (AI) language model capable of generating tailored responses to various inquiries, has raised serious concerns among teachers and educators. With the possibilities that AI has to offer, students can write essays, attain high scores in tests and generally substitute their intellectual work with the work of AI. To what extent will such opportunities influence the role of the teacher? According to Luckin et al. (2016), AI will serve as a catalyst for the transformation of the teacher’s role. The implementation of AI in education may require not only the acquisition of new teacher’s tasks and competencies, but also the obsolescence of certain teacher’s responsibilities, which can ultimately lead to a shift towards new emphasis of the teachers' work. This paper proposes a systematic literature review on the teacher’s role in AI-enhanced learning settings. It includes a definition of the teacher’s role, problem statement, research questions, described methodology and next steps for the research. The systematic review results will be visualized as a map of teachers’ roles and competencies at the ISLS conference.

Defining the teacher’s role As in all formal learning environments, teachers are crucial to provide fruitful learning to students and, according to Lameras & Arnab (2022), this is also the case in AI-supported learning settings. However, their role has often been neglected in research (Holstein & Aleven, 2022). In recent studies, the necessity to purposefully investigate and deconstruct the teacher’s role is stressed (ibid.). Karsenti, for exam ple, indicated that “for AI to make a real contribution to academic success, and for all students, the teacher’s role remains as central as ever, perhaps more than ever” (p.110, 2019). Although it is often mentioned that the teacher is key for succe ssful learning, limited research focuses on unraveling that role and this study aims to meet this gap. To commence with, we need to define the teacher's role. A preliminary analysis of papers within the ISLS repository (https://repository.isls.org/) demonstrated that ‘teacher’s role’ has rarely been explicitly defined in the literature. Rather, a teacher’s role represents an umbrella term referring to a variety of concepts, such as ‘competencies of teachers’, ‘teacher behavior’, ‘teacher routines’, ‘teac her’s practices’, ‘teacher functions’ and broader ‘what teachers are doing in classrooms’, without explicitly defining these concepts. In the current review, Kaendler et al. definition of a teacher’s role is used as consisting of competencies, which are understood as “the ability to perform tasks in a specific situati on, for instance, a classroom situation” (Kaendler et al., 2015).

AI-supported learning: Changes in teacher's role Some researchers argue that the list of teachers' professional competenc ies needs to be expanded by including new tasks such as interpreting data collected by AI tools (Luckin et al., 2016) or managing and reprogramming cobots that serve as teacher assista nts (Mozelius & Humble, 2019). In contrast, some traditional teacher’s tasks, such as checking homework and grading (Lazarus et al., 2022; Malach & Javorc ik, 2019), facilitating student interaction (Holmes et al., 2019) or providing instructions (Luckin et al., 2016) can be ‘outsourced’ to AI. As AI tools are capable of analyzing vast amounts of data quickly and accurately, it is crucial to critically evaluate how to reconfigure the teacher's role to empower teachers rather than subdue or replace them. This study utilizes two frameworks: the Implementing Collaborative Learning in the Classroom (ICLC) proposed by Kaendler et al. (2015) and “The Framework for Teaching Evaluation I nstrument” developed by Danielson (Danielson, n.d.). The current systematic review aims to examine  the differences in teachers' roles, tasks and competencies in AI-supported individual and collaborative learning settings us ing both Danielson's framework for individual learning and Kaendler's framework for collaborative learning, as this framework provides a comprehensive picture of one teacher’s role. The overarching r esearch question is: “What is the state of the art of research on the impact of artificial intelligence on the teache r’s role?”. The following sub-questions are put forth:

(1) How can the research currently available be categorized with respect to country origin, educational

level, type, themes, methodology?

## Methods

Article selection and search strategy The systematic literature review was conducted in the Web of Science and followed the PRISMA guidelines (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses)  procedure. We screened articles for compliance with the following in-and exclusion criteria: (1) any education context; (2) AI is the main technology;

(3) focus on the teacher’s role; (4) pedagogical considerations, rather thandes criptions of AI software; (5) not a

computer science discipline; (6) article published in English. Only thos e papers that either involved a teacher’s role in the research question or relied on it methodologically (e.g., studies including teacher observation, teacher reflective journals, and teacher questionnaires) were considered to be included. The search query is illustrated below. The search string was consolidated by a Boolean AND.

• TS = (teacher* OR instructor* OR educator* OR tutor* OR coach* OR assistant* OR trainer* OR

mentor* OR facilitator*)

• AND TS = (role* OR competenc* OR skill* OR capabilit* OR task* OR behavio* OR routin* OR

practice* OR function*)

• AND TS = (artificial AND intelligence OR AI).

The search query resulted in obtaining 367 results (as of January 25th, 2023). In a ccordance with inclusion and exclusion criteria, 314 articles were eliminated. The leading reason for exclusion was criterion (3), as the articles did not address the changing nature of the teacher’s role. Following this, we assessed the full texts of the articles and implemented a backward snowballing method. Ultimately, 36 ar ticles were selected for a comprehensive review.

Coding To examine the selected articles, we are employing a mixed-method methodology that incorporates both bottomup and top-down approaches. Initially, a bottom-up approach was utilized to extract data  in accordance with the developed code scheme, which reflects and expands the research questions. We  are currently using a top-down approach by examining the extracted codes against the suggested frameworks to identify discrepancies between their competencies and tasks. We look forward to interacting with the CSCL &  ISLS community to improve the data analysis process.
