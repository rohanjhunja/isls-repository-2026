### Abstract

OctoStudio: Mobilizing Creative Learning in Community Contexts Cecilé Sadler, Karishma Chadha, Téo Xisto csadler@mit.edu, kchadha@media.mit.edu, teox@media.mit.edu Massachusetts Institute of Technology Abstract: OctoStudio is a mobile coding app co-designed with a global community of educators to provide equitable access to creative learning experiences for young people from all construction materials for creating interactive multimedia projects using block-based programming, device sensors, and culturally-inclusive visual assets. The app has been integrated across informal and formal learning environments globally, supporting crosscurricular learning, and helping to spread creative learning ideas and values. This interactive demo invites participants to create their own OctoStudio projects that combine physical and digital materials, explore applications for research and practice across diverse contexts, and engage in discussion about inclusive creative learning experiences that offer multiple pathways into computing for learners with varied backgrounds and interests. Supporting creative learning experiences for young people of all backgrounds requires equitable access to tools, resources, and learning environments that honor learners’ unique identities and community contexts. In partnership with community-based educators around the world, OctoStudio was developed as a creative learning mobile app to support young people in creating what they imagine, transforming their phones and tablets into construction materials. Guided by the shared knowledge and expertise built through a community-of-practice, OctoStudio was designed to foster inclusive access to creating with technology (Rusk et al., 2024). This interactive demo aims to share how OctoStudio supports young people in engaging in creative learning experiences across diverse communities and learning contexts.

Educational theorists and practitioners have long advocated for hands-on, project-based learning experiences. Constructionism was introduced as an alternative approach to traditional classroom instruction, theorizing that learning (in both formal and informal learning environments) happens most effectively when the learner is actively engaged in creating a meaningful artifact (Papert & Harel, 2002). Creative learning builds on this idea, describing a process where young people learn through making projects connected to their unique interests and passions, while collaborating with peers and embracing a playful spirit of tinkering and iteration (Resnick, 2017). Creative learning aims to support young people of all backgrounds in developing as creative, curious, collaborative, and caring learners (Resnick, 2025).

Constructionism and creative learning advocate designing for “low floors” for learners to get started easily, “wide walls" to create in connection with learners' diverse backgrounds and interests, and “high ceilings” to allow for increasingly complex creations (Resnick & Silverman, 2005). The creative learning philosophy has inspired the design of tools and communities such as the Computer Clubhouse (Resnick et al., 1998), the Brazilian Creative Learning Network (Valente & Burd, 2019), and Scratch, an online community and programming environment (Resnick et al., 2009).

During the COVID-19 global pandemic lockdowns of 2020 and the ensuing years, usage of online learning platforms like Scratch increased as young people sought to connect, create, and adapt for meaningful learning outside of school (Resnick, 2023; Scratch Foundation, 2020, 2021). However, the global pandemic also exacerbated existing disparities for learners around the world who did not have access to computers or a stable internet connection at home. Thus, educators saw a need for new tools that supported creative learning experiences in contexts where young people’s primary access to technology was a mobile device (Rusk et al., 2024). Applying the affordances of mobile devices to constructionist learning theory and creative learning principles, OctoStudio has been developed to address this gap. O ctoStudio overview OctoStudio is an app-based creative learning tool leveraging mobile devices as construction materials for young people to create with code, anytime, anywhere. Designed in a community-of-practice including educators around the world, OctoStudio aims to expand access to creative learning experiences with digital technologies, supporting young people who have often been excluded from such opportunities (Rusk et al., 2024). The app is available on phones, tablets, and Chromebooks and can be used offline after download. This feature is central to OctoStudio, addressing the realities of communities with no or intermittent internet access while broadening the environments in which computationally-rich learning can occur, from parks and trails to buses and trains. The block-based coding environment allows young people to get started without prior coding experience and combines colorful blocks with icons and text, translated to over 31 languages. To create a project in OctoStudio, young people can work independently or collaboratively to first select a background or sprite. These can include emojis, photos, drawings, or images. The app leverages a standard emoji library and extends the default options with custom representations for a broader diversity of body types, hair types/styles, headwear, and cultural symbols (see Figure 1).

Figure 1 Example Standard Emojis, Paired with OctoStudio Adaptations Sprites can be animated through programming blocks to add movement, sound, visual effects, and physical interaction. The coding environment builds on principles of designing for tinkerability (Resnick & Rosenbaum, 2013) allowing learners to develop their understanding of computation while iteratively creating complex and meaningful interactive projects. OctoStudio enables new ways of creating and sharing on mobile devices by leveraging the portable form factor and sensors (e.g., accelerometer and gyroscope) embedded in mobile devices to support creation that combines physical and digital materials (see Figure 2). Young people can export their project as an animated GIF or video and share it with others using existing communication channels (e.g. WhatsApp, SMS, email, Bluetooth). Project files can also be shared between devices, supporting iteration, collaboration, and remixing.

Centering the perspectives and cultural practices of educators working in communities globally has enabled creative learning to spread in varied contexts around the world. This section will provide an overview of examples demonstrating OctoStudio as a part of learning experiences. The examples are situated in both informal and formal l earning environments and depict how OctoStudio has been used in research and practice to explore connections between computer science and other disciplines.

Informal Designed to embrace the affordances of mobile devices, OctoStudio has been leveraged to support facilitated and drop-in style learning experiences in informal learning contexts. These include out-of-school time (OST) spaces, museums, libraries and community-based organizations.

In Massachusetts, USA, OctoStudio has been explored in public libraries through workshop themes like “bring a book to life”, where learners created projects that remixed book covers, animated literary characters, and extended book plots. Workshop facilitators collaborated with local librarians to select books that were popular in their community. The books were used alongside craft materials, inspiring a variety of projects (see Figure 3). OctoStudio’s multi-modal design supported children’s creative engagement with literacy beyond traditional written responses. Children could draw, animate, record their voices, add music, and program interactivity, choosing the modes of expression that best conveyed their understanding of and connection to the stories they read.

Figure 3 Children Using OctoStudio with Physical Craft Materials and Books. Formal OctoStudio has been used in schools for cross-curricular learning experiences to integrate computing into subjects like English language arts (ELA) and history.

Seeing their students’ interest in OctoStudio, 4th-grade educators in Massachusetts, USA connected OctoStudio with their existing poetry curriculum. After writing odes and limericks, students created OctoStudio projects to perform and visualize their poems. They included art and photos to make their poems more personally meaningful. Additionally, OctoStudio features like voice recording and speech synthesis enabled learners to engage with their writing in new ways. Educators noted that animating their poetry in OctoStudio encouraged students to revisit their writing and iterate on their poems. In another instance, educators in Chile have leveraged OctoStudio for place-based learning experiences outside of the classroom. On a field trip, middle school students explored a local historical park with OctoStudio. They photographed plants, insects, textures, and objects that caught their attention, and used these images as sprites and backgrounds in their projects (see Figure 4). OctoStudio’s offline and mobile design allowed the physical environment to become a part of students' digital creations. Interactive demo In this interactive demo, ISLS attendees will have the opportunity to participate in a learning experience with OctoStudio. We will begin by sharing an overview of the app and its features, followed by a brief group demo where the participants collaboratively make a project with the presenters. Participants will then be invited to explore OctoStudio individually and with others nearby to create their own projects that combine physical and digital materials. After this exploration, participants will share and reflect on their experiences, connecting to how they might envision using OctoStudio with the young people they support in research and/or practice.
