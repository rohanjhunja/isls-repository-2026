# Productive Entanglements: Patterns of Partnership for Learning Technology Innovation

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract

Impactful learning technology innovation doesn’t happen in isolation; it grows through relationships, among researchers, educators, students, technologists, designers, and institutional leaders. While some collaborations are carefully structured, others take root more organically, evolving to meet shifting needs and opportunities. In this keynote, Dr. Alyssa Wise explores how purposeful partnerships drive innovation across the technological, social, conceptual, and practical dimensions of learning, tracing three kinds of partnerships that have unfolded over the past decade in her work. Flexible Coupling articulates how institutional partnerships can leverage complementary strengths without becoming over-structured. In a longstanding collaboration with a university learning analytics service unit, in vivo studies of instructors’ analytics use informed both theoretical models of sense-making and applied tool design. Constructive Responsiveness describes how partnerships with educators can evolve around a shared object of inquiry when goals are not yet fully defined. In work with dental educators implementing an online reflection tool, the collaboration advanced from examinations of reflection quality to generate domain-specific insights into failure attribution and professional identity formation. Finally, Generative Pluralism characterizes interdisciplinary partnerships among researchers or co-designers where the goal is not convergence but conceptual creation. These collaborations embraced multiplicity, surfacing productive tensions, methodological bridges, and new vocabularies for imagining learning technologies. Together, these patterns illustrate partnerships as adaptable forms of collaboration, each suited to different problems, people, and purposes. This talk highlights how designing, studying, and sustaining learning with the support of technology can be supported by purposeful partnerships that bridge disciplines, organizations, and stakeholder perspectives. Bio: Dr. Alyssa Wise is Professor of Technology and Education at Vanderbilt University’s Peabody College, with a courtesy appointment in Computer Science in the College of Connected Computing. She also serves as Director of the LIVE Learning Innovation Incubator, Vanderbilt’s interdisciplinary hub for impactful learning technology innovation. Her research productively integrates tools of computational data science, theories of learning sciences and collaborative learning, and practices of human-centered design to build and study analytic and artificial intelligence systems that promote powerful learning for individuals situated in social context. She also conducts mixed-method investigations into how educational practices are being reshaped by new sources of data and the growing availability of new generative artificial intelligence capabilities.
