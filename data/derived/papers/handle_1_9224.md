# Description of Instructor Intervention Using Individual Audio Data in Co-Located Collaboration

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Collaborative learning in face-to-face classroom settings requires instructors to monitor student groups and intervene with the group to ensure they make progress with the activity. One way learning analytics could help in facilitating such classrooms is by providing speech-based solutions to help instructors monitor this. In this paper, we investigate audio data collected from individuals in group work, processed using voice activity detection (VAD), used to describe student collaboration before, within, and after instructor intervention. Individual audio data were collected from classes focused on collaborative problem solving that were part of an introductory undergraduate engineering course. Analysis of 22 groups of individual audio data using VAD indicate that individual audio data could provide critical information on how students interact before, within, and after intervention from a facilitator using metrics like turn taking, turn overlap, and turn duration of individual students.

## Introduction

Collaborative learning in face-to-face classroom setting typically involves teachers whoneed to monitor multiple groups of 3-4 students. To better facilitate such classrooms, teachers c ould be assisted with tools to help them identify groups that collaborate well and groups that need help. However, teachers cannot monitor more than one group at a time. Therefore, effective intervention cannot be provided for clas srooms with more than few groups (Kaendler et al., 2014). The emerging field of learning analytics and Educationa l Data Mining has the potential to identify new ways to detect and understand the collaborative learning activities of students which could lead to better possibilities for intervention and feedback. While learning analytics has focused mostly on computer logfile data, multimodal learning analytics brings in other sensors and data streams  to better understand learning and teaching. Students talking to one another in face-to-face environments is a  natural setting for classroom collaboration and speech-based solutions can provide necessary information to f acilitate such classrooms (D’Angelo et al., 2019). In this work, we investigate the usefulness of audio data collected individually f rom students in groups who are working collaboratively in a face-to-face classr oom setting. We discuss the utility of using individual student speech data to better understand teacher-student inte rventions and the implications this might have for future supportive teacher tools.

## Theoretical background

Collaborative learning builds on constructivist learning theory, specifically social constructivism. Research on collaborative learning has shown that when groups of students construct knowledge through it erative social interactions, it can lead to higher levels of understanding (Roschelle, 1992). Collaborati ve learning is a joint meaning-making process between members of a group. Students need to be supported during group work to effectively collaborate (Kreijns et al., 2003).Teachers play a major role in how well students collaborate and learn. For instructors to intervene, they must monitor and diagnose the student’s curre nt understanding of the content prior to providing the support (Snow & Swanson, 1992).

Voice activity detection (VAD) is essential tool for pre-processing spee ch audio for functions like automated speech recognition (ASR) and speaker identification (Dinkel et al., 2021). It is achieved through the detection of voice segments and differentiating between non-speech segments. Recent research on the use of VAD in classroom settings involves classroom activity detection which categorizes w hether the instructor speaks, the student speaks, there’s no speech,  as well as the duration of the talk during the class. VAD was also used in language learning settings to capture oral and listening exercises by students. Spea ker recognition and identification in group settings is particularly difficult due to overlap of speec h between the individuals in the group (Medennikov et al., 2020). Not much research has been done using individual audio da ta to understand group discussion patterns, especially within formal educational settings. Considering this gap in the literature, our research questions focus on investigating how teaching assistants (TA) and course assistants (CA) interact with a group and how audio data can be used to describe the interaction process. To understand this, we will answer the following research questions:

1) What can individual audio data provide to describe details to the teacher-student intervention process?

## Data collection

This study described here was part of a larger study designing and researc hing collaborative experiences in required engineering courses. Data was collected in a 16-week introductory undergraduate engineering course. Students worked in groups of three or four on synchronized tablet software on ill-str uctured collaborative tasks developed by members of the research team to support collaboration. One graduate student teaching assistant (TA) and two course assistants (CA) (undergraduate students who excelled in th e course in previous semesters) facilitated each discussion session. Each student who consented wore a head-mounted noise-cancelling microphone (Audio-Technica PRO 8HEx). These headsets are designed to minimize the collection of background noise (such as other students in the classroom) to ensure a clear audio signal of speech. Some students did not wear the head-mounted microphones properly (e.g., wearing it around their neck) or did not keep it on during the whole class session and these students and the ir groups were excluded from the analysis. There were also some students who were not present for a discussion session which led to student groups either being a group of two or being merged with other student groups. Thus, a total of  58 students in 22 groups with fairly complete data (14 groups with four students and 8 groups with three stu dents) over five weeks of instruction were used for this analysis.

Data processing Data processing involved extracting features from both the video and audio data. Video data were coded for interventions by the TAs and CAs every 20 seconds (Paquette et al., 2018). The  individual audio data collected were processed using the openSMILE toolkit (Eyben et al., 2010), which contains in-built VAD to obtain speaker and turn information. The audio features were aggregated at the 20 second level as well. Voice Activity Detection (VAD) VAD is used in speech-based systems to discriminate human speech from non-speech sounds. The VAD is based on Long Short-Term Memory Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM-RNNs) using th e openSMILE toolkit (Eyben et al. 2010). The background speech from other groups is moderated using the thresholds in VAD. To setup the thresholds and the number of frames of the audio data required by the VAD to detect speech, RMS energy from the audio data was computed and the optimum value was sele cted by adjusting the threshold such that the turns detected by the VAD were meaningful. ## Results

A total of 149 interventions were coded for the various TA-student interactions. Type of intervention was coded based on majority of the duration of the intervention being dominated by one of these patterns. If they were equal, the one occurring first was assigned. Of the 149 interactions, 48 were focuse d on scaffolding, 52 on providing solution and 49 on others. 98 of the 149 interactions were IRE while the rest were non-IRE. To describe the relationship between pre and post TA/CA intervention, we looked the TA-student interaction and the audio features. Several plots were generated to visualiz e the relationship. A duration of two minutes was chosen for pre-intervention, which is an estimate of the average duration of moni toring by TA/CAs before interventions. A duration of two minutes after the intervention was chosen as a reasonable time to see the effect of the TA/CA intervention on the student discussion patterns. ## Discussion

This exploratory study set out to describe and understand teacher student interventions in classroom collaborative activities. We used audio data features to explore how we candescribe  the changes that occurs before and after instructor intervention in a noisy real-world classroom environment using high quality individual audio data. Features extracted from the individual audio data regarding turn and speaker information using VAD proved to be informative in describing how the students were interacting pre a nd post intervention by TAs and CAs. Students tended to mostly decrease in talk post intervention. This might sugge st that the TAs and CAs are not effective when trying to understand the group interactions and figuring out the nec essary intervention. Collaborative learning is a complex process and the TAs and CAs need to be provided with the necessary training to support these student collaborations. TAs and CAs initiating the conversation led to a decrease in overall talk post intervention when compared to student-initiated intervention. This shows that ther e is a clear need for facilitator training to understand the appropriate time to intervene.

## Conclusion

Individual audio data provided insights into turn taking and how it can potentially capture effectiveness of teacher student intervention. This study showed that turn taking, and speaker features fr om VAD could potentially be used to describe interventions by facilitators in collaborative setting. Future work involves using prosodic features to further explore the use of audio data to describe interventions by teacher s in collaborative setting along with the current features. This could be used along with other modalities to further enhance the richness and accuracy of the description. This can be used to enhance the effectiveness of intervention thereby improving the experiences of students and teachers in such classrooms.
