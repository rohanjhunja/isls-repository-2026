# Design-Based Research for Scenario-Based, Generative AI-Augmented Simulation in Nursing Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Simulation-based learning is vital in nursing education but faces scalability and authenticity challenges. This study explores using generative AI as a role-playing agent in nursing simulations. Using a design-based research approach, we developed an evaluation framework and question set to evaluate AI model performance. Findings highlight accurate role-based dialogue but limitations in common-sense reasoning. Recommendations include clearer evaluation criteria, diverse question sets to comprehensively evaluate ambiguity handling, and refining scenarios iteratively for consistency and clarity.

## Introduction

Simulation-based learning can benefit learners in cognitive, a ffective, and psychomotor domains, improving clinical skills, critical thinking skills, self-confidence, etc., especially when using high-fidelity simulations (Guerrero et al., 2021; Kim & Yoo, 2022; Molloy et al., 2021; Saragih et al., 2024). However, the high costs of running simulations, paired with limited funding and reliance on human operators pose a challenge (Doolen et al., 2016; Gaba, 2004). The labor-intensive process, alongside having adult professionals portray patients of different ages or genders can compromise the realism of the simulation, which may affect the quality of the training. Generative artificial intelligence (generative AI) can potentially offer a solution to address these challenges (Jung, 2023). As a dialogue-based role-playing agent utilizing the pre-existing scenarios that provide background and contextual information, AI agents could generate responses aligned within a defined role, such as that of a patient. If scenarios are well-structured and the limitations of the technology such as accuracy, response consistency, role maintenance are adequately addressed, generative AI could potentially reduce the labor demands placed on simulation operators.

The goal of this study is to explore ways to design and evaluate conversations in scenario-based, generative AI-augmented simulations for nursing education using a  design-based approach. Starting with a contextualized scenario from an undergraduate nursi ng program, we developed an evaluation framework fo r AIaugmented conversational simulations. Guided by the framework, we developed a set of simulation interaction questions through iterative discussions. The finali zed question set was used to generate AI responses,  with the generative AI simulating the role of a patient. These AI-generated responses were evaluated using the rubric  to assess the authenticity of the AI’s role-playing capabilities. The research questions guid ing this study are: (1) What kind of evaluation framework can inform the design and evaluation of generative AI-augmented simulation? and (2) How effectively can generative AI models perform authentic patient role-play within a defined scenario?

## Methods

This study employs a design-based research (DBR)  approach to iteratively develop and evaluate a scenario-specific application of generative AI for simulatio n-based learning (Anderson & Shattuck, 2012; The Design-Based Research Collective, 2003). An expert consultation approach was used to validate findings from literature and refine the framework based on expert feedback (Falender & Shafranske, 2004). Two primary groups of researchers participated: one simulation operation professional and three learning sciences researchers. The used simulation scenario is one currently implemented at the simulation center of a public university in the southern United States as part of the senior  year curriculum in the Bachelor of Science in Nurs ing (BSN) program. The primary aim of this simulation activit y is to guide students in conducting comprehensive assessments that incorporate social determinants of  health and critically evaluate the relationship be tween healthcare delivery and living conditions. The simulation scenario used in this study centers around Renee, a nineyear-old girl hospitalized for asthma-related complications. The scenario includes comprehensive details about Renee’s family, medical history, living environment, school, and social interactions.

## Results

Evaluation Framework for Scenario-Based, AI-Augmented Simulations The evaluation framework  development process was initially guided by concern s surrounding the use of AI models in dialogue-based question-answer scenarios. The primary challenges identified in utilizing generative AI for simulation-based learning included issues such as accuracy and  hallucinations, consistency, and response adaptability (Bengesi et al., 2024; Hicks et al., 2024). After the initial version of the framework was reviewed by the united  experts, the ability of AI to logically infer and integrat e unstated but reasonable elements into its responses, while maintaining alignment with the sce nario, was highlighted. The finalized evaluation framework, incorporating these refinements, resulted in six criteria: accuracy and precision, contextual relevance, respo nse consistency, dynamic response adjustments, role portrayal, and common-sense reasoning. The development of the question set began with the scenario provide d by the simulation expert, accompanied by an initial pool of 60 example questions and sample answers. Questions were deliberately designed to test the generative AI’s ability to per form across criteria, including questions to assess the model ’s consistency and ability to stay in character, particularly its adherence to the characteristics of a nine-year-old patient. Based on feedback, questions to address common sense reasoning  and the simulation’s objective  were added to test the AI’s ability to provide contextually appropriate, logically inferred, and role-consistent responses. After incorporating these refinements, the final simulation interaction question set comprised 64 questions. Assessing generative AI in simulation scenarios The evaluation framework was then used to assess the generative AI’s perform ance using the simulation interaction question set across the defined evaluat ion criteria. Overall, the AI demonstrated notable strengths in maintaining its role portrayal and answering contex tually appropriate questions, though areas such as inference-making and tone adjustment showed room for further improvement. In terms of accuracy and precision, the AI frequently struggled to strictly adhere to the given scenario when speci fic and explicit details are absent. Contextual relevance posed challenges when the AI m issed opportunities to connect scenario details to its responses. However, the AI successfully flagged out-of-scenario questions, such as those about a nonexiste nt older brother or swimming lessons. The AI ’s response  exhibited mostly positive response consistency, but contradictions arose in responses about whether her school had a nurse. Regarding dynamic response adjustments, the answers showed strength in adapting to follow-up questions, demonstrating the AI ’s understanding of pronouns and ability to maintain the flow of conversation. For role portrayal, it embodied the role of a nine-yearold’s conversational style convincingly, using vocabulary such as ‘kinda’, ‘thingy’, or ‘um’. Despite this, the AI adopted an overly cheerful tone, inconsistent with Renee’s context of recent hospitalization, ongoing health challenges, and socio-economic difficulties. Finally, common-sense reasoning was a more divisive category, with varying assessments from evaluators. Certain responses blurred the lines between accuracy, contextual relevance, and common-sense reasoning, leading to differing interpretations among evaluators. For instance, responses about Renee’s birthday were considered as an example of effecti ve common-sense reasoning by one evaluator but flagged as inaccurate by another.

## Discussion and implications

This study employed a DBR approach to explore ways to support scenario-based simulation learning in nursing education using generative AI. The findings offer several recommendations and impl ications for advancing AI integration into simulation-based learning:

• Evaluation framework refinement: Articulate clearer distinctions between criteria to  avoid overlap and

ambiguity during evaluation, particularly for areas  like inference capabilities and common-sense reasoning.

• Simulation interaction question set design: Include a mix of in-context and out-of-context questions to

test AI ’s ability to navigate ambiguities and align questio ns with evaluation elements to ensure comprehensive evaluation.

• Scenario design: Utilize feedback from AI interactions to refine unc lear areas and ensure consistency

between scenario details and example answers throug h iterative evaluations and improvements (e.g., define clear boundaries for what elements remain strictly within the scenario and what can be reasonably inferred).

This research serves as a foundational step in addr essing the technical challenges of integrating AI systems into nursing simulation infrastructure (Jung, 2023). By focusing on iterative development and evaluation, this study provides insights into leveraging genera tive AI to enhance scalability and authenticity in simulationbased learning. Future work should build upon these  findings to further refine AI models a nd optimize their application in nursing education.
