# Integrating Introductory Structural Engineering Courses in Architecture Program

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigated the cognitive learning challenges and concerns of architecture program students taking introductory courses in Structural Engineering. The outcome will contribute to our knowledge of the suitable pedagogical approach and instructional strategies to develop engineering analysis and design skills among architecture students.

## Introduction

In an architecture program accredited by the National Architectural Accrediting Board, students typically engage in design studios, where they apply creative princi ples to various projects alongside delving into the oretical frameworks. Among the few engineering-based courses offered in an architecture program, teaching courses on "Structures" is distinctly challenging. In many architecture schools, these courses are primarily lecture-based, and occasionally, the theoretical contents are flavored by using phys ical models, some design-and-build projects (Adriaenssens et al., 2015), and hands-on activities (Khodadadi, 2015). However, student's cognitive challenges in introductory structure courses, particularly those faced by minority or underrepresented students, have not been explicitly identified. These shortcomings in literature hinder curriculum development and limit opportunities for innovation and inclusivity within the educational system. This study aims to address the three following questions:

1. What cognitive learning challenges are experienced by Architecture students in introductory Structural

Engineering courses?

2. What are the specific learning challenges faced by minority or underrepresented students in architecture

programs, particularly in the context of engineering-based courses?

3. How can educational approaches be tailored to address these challenges?

## Methodology

This research delved into the learning experiences of both underg raduate and graduate students at Portland State University over a three-year period (2020-2022) within the framework of an introductory course entitled "Building Structures." The course represents theoretical conc epts on force paths, material, and geometrical prop erties of building structures. This 10-week course is tailored for senior undergraduate students and second-year graduate students enrolled in the 3.5-year M.Arch program, particularly those whose  undergraduate background differs from Architecture. To gather comprehensive insights, a pre  and post-attendance survey featuring open-ended questions was employed. This survey probed into students' demographic information, affiliation with minority or underrepresented communities, academic backgrounds,  motivations for taking the introductory course, and anticipated challenges. The survey findings aimed to address research questions 1 and 2. In response to identified challenges in 2020, the course and teaching strategies were redesigned in 2021. The instructional approach incorporates a blend of lectures, demonstrations, k inesthetic activities, homework assignments, and a construction/testing project. Students actively eng age in hands-on and kinesthetic-based learning, emphasizing problem-solving and basic calculations. Comprehension assessments i nvolve two tests, while the construction/testing project provides a platform fo r applying theoretical concepts to physical design.  The impact of these strategies was  assessed through a post-term survey, answering research question 3. Subsequent adjustments to the course design in 2022 were made,  and the effectiveness of these adjustments was eva luated through a post-term survey.

## Findings and discussion

A substantial 74% of the 82 research participants anticipate encounterin g learning challenges in the course. Interestingly, only 25% of the entire participant c ohort and 40% of those expressing concerns about le arning expect these challenges to be related to their math ematical background. Moreover, although the gender ratio among survey participants is nearly equal (47.5% female and 46.3% male), 54% of concerned students are female, while 45% are male. Additionally, within all underrepresented groups, 100% of neurodivergent students, 71% of new immigrant students, 50% of student parents, and  35% of low-income students have concerns about their mathematical capabilities. They express that their expected learning challenges stem from limited expo sure to effective math education in high school and prolonged peri ods of non-utilization of mathematical knowledge. Besides, 26% of concerned students anticipate difficulties  adapting to the teaching and learning environment, which differs intrinsically from the design studio format. Adaptation challenges arise from the technical nature of structural engineering, which may lack the inherent  appeal of the more creative aspects found in archi tectural design studios. Furthermore, there is a notable concern about solidifying theoretical knowledge and its practical application in real-world design situations. Additional concerns, not extensively explored in existing literature but diagnosed in this study, include course expenses, the course pace, and the unique challenge faced by slow learners in a lecture hall setting, along with limited interaction with peers and restricted assistance during lecture sessions. Upon analyzing the outcomes of the first round of surveys in 2020, a set of pedagogical approaches were deployed to effectively tackle the identified conce rns. ## Conclusion

This study has three major implications. First, integrating structural engineering into the architecture curric ulum encourages students to see the connections between different STEM disciplines. This approach could set  a precedent for more interdisciplinary learning exper iences, breaking down traditional silos in educatio n. Second, making structural engineering concepts more tangibl e and applicable in architecture can contribute to a broader effort to increase STEM participation among students with diverse academic interests. Third, the successful results of this designed-based research can inspire educators to explore cre ative teaching methods in other STEM disciplines, which can lead to making STEM education more engaging and accessible to a wider audience.
