# Exploring Cross-Cultural Collaborative Learning at the Bridging Cultures to Defeat COVID-19 Virtual Global Health Conference

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Cross-cultural collaborations are essential to solving complex global health problems like the COVID-19 pandemic. Virtual academic conferences can enable collaboration and facilitate cross-cultural collaborative learning. This study will explore the success of a virtual global health conference as it pertains to cross-cultural collaborative learning from the perspective of student participants and conference designers, in addition to identifying practical considerations for educators designing and implementing similar cross-cultural collaborative learning experiences for medical trainees.

## Introduction

Today, collaboration is widely recognized as a crit ical educational and professional competency in the medical and health sciences. Morley & Cashell (2017) summar ize collaboration as “an integration of activities and knowledge that requires a partnership of shared authority and responsibility,” and it requires four critical elements: coordination, cooperation, shared decision-making, and partnerships (Morley & Cashell, 2017, p p 208). Promoting collaborative learning and developing col laborative skills in medical education builds the f oundation for trainees to have future success in medicine, re search, and patient care. The knowledge, skills, and values acquired through collaborative learning will help medical and health science  trainees function professionally as effective members of multidisciplinary teams, thus improving healthcare outcomes overall. However, collaboration does not simply occur sponta neously. Rather, collaboration is a learned experience and active learning strategy. Collaborative learning requires the active participation of each learner in a group-based, real-world context, where learners strive to accomplish shared goals, exchange reasoning, and observe patterns of thought (Chang, 2023). The COVID–19 pandemic highlighted the urgent need for successful collaborations among medical professionals throughout the world (Frenk et al., 2022). Although challenging, global collaboration among medical professionals is  imperative for better understanding and management  of humandisease and public health worldwide (Barry, 2017). The COVID-19 pandemic  also highlighted that medical professionals must transce nd socio-cultural boundaries; therefore, learning how to collaborate across cul tures is an important aspect of how medical knowledge can be shared across the world. Building cultural competence for medical professionals is an important learning goal because many medical professionals  not only have to share knowledge across cultures fo r professional advancement, but also must treat patients from many different socio-cultural backgrounds. Therefore, medical and health science trainees must learn cult ural competence. Cultural competence is one’s ability to effectively interact with people having diverse values, beliefs, and behaviors (Health Research & Educational Trust, 2013; Nair & Adetayo, 2019). Having skills i n cultural competency can promote cross-cultural collaborative learning by improving trainees’ social, international, and professional competencies that are required to be effective in today’s globalized world (Gyasi et al., 2021; Scager et al., 2016). As stated above, collective action is required to address global  health challenges, and this can only be accomplished by creating the space to build collaborations. Academic conferences are one such space that provide a beneficial opportunity to enable collaboration and learn cultural competence. Health-related academic conferences are typically structured to offer a variety of valuable opportunities for le arning, networking, and professional development, and play a crucial role in advancing the careers of  students and researchers (A PhD’s Guide to Conferences, n.d.; George, 2022; Niner & Wassermann, 2021; Sch reiner et al., 2022; Viglione, 2020). The knowledge imparted during conferences also has a direct impact on patient care (Sarabipour et al., 2021). Conference participation provides a mechanism for clinicians and scientists to stay informed about the latest research, treatments, and guidelines in the ir rapidly evolving field s, and can expose trainees to different cultures, values, and ways to practice medicine, all of which was critical during the emergence of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Rationale To address this need, my colleagues and I at an academic medical center organized a virtual global conference on COVID-19 to provide a dedicated space to share knowledge,  resources, lessons learned, and, ultimately, build cross-cultural collaborations. The Bridging Cultures to Defeat COVID-19 virtual conference (BCVC) offered collaborative academic-style lectures, professional development, and cultural exchange opportunities for trainees and faculty from the United States (US), Libya, and  the United Arab Emirates (UAE)  (Campagna et al., 2024). Given the demands of our globalized world coupled with the complexity of the COVID-19 pandemic, the need to foster dialogue and international cross-cultural collaboration to solve global health probl ems has never been greater (Campagna et al., 2024).

Although there has been a dramatic increase in the field of global health as both an academic and clinical discipline over the past twenty years (Adams et al., 2016), educational programs that feature virtual global health experiences have been limited (Wu et al., 2021). O f those, virtual educational programs focusing on c ultural exchange are even more scarce (Ambrose et al., 2017; Keynejad, 2016). A systematic review focusing on virtual global health programs in graduate medical education identified the “crucial need for evidence about virtual global health education activities planning, implementation, and continuation…to guide global health educators and the creation of global health programming” (Umphrey et al., 2022, pp 231). It is also important to underst and how well these programs build cultural competency, faci litate intercultural learning, and create “meaningf ul and transformative cultural learning experiences that enhance graduate intercultural capabilities” (Hyett et al., 2019). An essential aspect of this conversation is explori ng how virtual platforms, like the BCVC, can successfully and comprehensively achieve these goals.

## Methods

This study will explore the success of the BCVC as it pertains to cross-cultural collaborative learning. Success will be explored from the perspective of students a nd conference designers, addressed through the foll owing research questions: (1) Did cross-cultural collaborative learning at the BCVC impact students' perspectives on culture?; (2) What specific design elements did the planner s for the BCVC incorporate to help students improve their knowledge of global cultures and promote cros s-cultural collabo ration?; (3) What did the conference planners for the BCVC learn from designing a conference centered on cross-cultural collaborative aspects of global health?

The study will use a parallel, mixed-methods design and will be interpreted through a pr agmatic interpretive framework. Pre-and post-conference surveys from conference participants inf orm the quantitative and qualitative analys es, and semi-structured interview s with the conference planners inform the qualitative analysis. The Deardorff Intercultural Competence Mo del (2006) will be used as a theoretical framework.  The Intercultural Competence Model is a research-based framework used to guide educators in developi ng an intercultural curriculum and can be used to assess student learning outcomes. The model aims to improv e intercultural competence and provide “meaningful fe edback” to students participating in the curriculum  while emphasizing that developing intercultural competence is a lifelong process (Deardorff, 2006). The model features five elements that can be assessed at different points in time and over a period of time: (1) attitudes, (2) knowledge,

(3) skills, (4) internal outcomes, (5) and external  outcomes. This research will primarily focus on th e first four

elements of the model and may be expanded to the fifth.

## Implications

This research comes at a pivotal moment when we can effectively harness global health's growth as an academic field, as it has been developed, strengthened, and dissemi nated by the demands of our globalized world with t he lessons we have learned from navigating the COVID-19 pandemic. The COVID-19 pandemic reinforced the importance of collaboration for effectively solving complex global health problems, and reminded us of the multicultural and borderless impacts of humandisease. It also transformed how educators de sign and approach global medical education worldwide, by emphasizing the importance of developing internationalized curr iculum that fosters learning in cultural competence, socia l justice, health inequities, and interdisciplinary  methods. It is important to prepare medical professionals for the real-world global health challenges we face today and will face in the future. Understanding the success of the cro ss-cultural collaborative global health conference fro m the perspective of students and conference designers may be an effective way to accomplish this goal. The results of this study will help to document and analyze the po tential of virtual academic conferences that promot e global health, medical education, and cross-cultural collaboration, in a ddition to providing the space to discuss the practical considerations for global health educator s when designing and implementing similar virtual c rosscultural collaborative learning experiences for sci entific and medical trainees  beyond what was necessi tated by the COVID-19 pandemic.
