# Elementary School Students’ Mental Models of Human-AI Relationships: Tool, Servant, and Companion

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explored the mental models of elementary school students on human-AI relationships, which might influence how they interacted with AI in education and everyday lifes. For this purpose, this study provided students a task of drawing the life with AI at home in 10 years, which was followed by interviews. In this study, a total of 51 students from 4th to 6th grades in an elementary school participated in the drawing task, and 27 of the students participated in the interviews. Elementary school students' mental models for the relationship between humans and AI were categorized based on the relationship metaphor, relational distance, and the possibility of conflict. As a result of the study, three mental models were found: User-Tool (UT), Master-Servants (MS), and Companion-Companion (CC).

## Introduction

The social impact of AI (Artificial Intelligence) i s increasing in various fields, and ‘AI education for all ’ is being emphasized in anticipation that these changes  will accelerate in the future. In particular, majo r countries around the world are striving to provide systematic  AI literacy education from elementary school for f uture generations who will be AI developers, users, and p roviders. AI literacy is the ability to make critic al understanding and efficient use of AI at the curren t level. As there are growing concerns about prejud ice and discrimination through AI, the risk of hallucinatio n and anthropomorphism of ChatGPT, AI literacy educ ation is required to provide comprehensive and balanced educ ation on AI ethics, socioemotional competencies alo ng with the ability to understand and utilize it (UNES CO, 2022). This is because AI technology, unlike ot her traditional devices and tools, can be developed to simulate human forms and behaviors, and can be prod uced to replace human roles, especially interactions. In ot her words, humans can establish various relationshi ps with AI in the process of interacting with intelligent agen ts or ambient intelligence. Therefore, it is import ant to refine AI literacy education in consideration of relationa l aspects, and it is necessary to understand studen ts' perceptual patterns and grounds for their relationship with AI. This study aims to contribute to the design of AI  literacy education and the development of educational AI by analyzing the mental models of relationships with A I for elementary school students who are new to AI litera cy education. The results of this study can serve a s basic data for AI curriculum design and student education  counseling, and guide teachers by referring to the  types of relationship recognition of students with high or l ow AI literacy. In addition, technology can be used  or redesigned in consideration of how the interaction with educational AI affects not only knowledge acquisition but also relationship formation.  The research question of this study is as follows: What are the elementary school students’ mental models of the relationships between humans and AI?

## Literature review

Bansal and colleagues (2019) said that user experie nce and trust in AI could affect AI interactions and even future relationships with AI. In addition, research  that empathic interactions with AI affect trust and acceptance of AI allows us to assume that the relationship wit h AI may be highly related to AI usage patterns (Pe lau et al., 2021). On the other hand, as Shin and colleagues (2018) reported distrust of AI mediates attitudes and digi tal literacy, negative emotions (e.g., distrust, aliena tion, deficiency, dependence, addiction) can also a ppear in interactions with AI. However, previous studies sim ply focused on the perception of positive, and nega tive attitudes, or external images about AI (Shin et al., 2018). Also did not address the relationship betw een students and AI in inquiring about which part of the teacher 's role can functionally be replaced (Kim et al., 2 018). In addition, no interview was conducted on what elemen tary school students think of AI beyond robots in k inds of literature that they felt intimacy and trust in the ir interactions with intelligent robots or improved  learning motivation and academic performance (Van Straten et  al., 2020). The relationship between students and AI is important because AI can cause various tensions and  conflicts in the educational context and may cause changes in educational goals, learning activities, and inst ructor roles  (Cho et al, 2023). Therefore, it is necessary to categorize how students represent AI and specifical ly analyze the mental models of the relationship be tween humans and AI.

The mental model is an ideological model of oneself, the environment, others, and interacting objects and is formed through experience, education, and tr aining (Young, 2008). Because people's behavioral motivation, thinking, and background of thinking can be learned through the mental model, the understanding of the learner's mental model can be the basis for pre scriptive instructional design. Exploring the menta l models of the relationship with AI can help check the interac tions with current AIs, design future utilization a nd development strategies, and understand the emotional and philosophical background of people's behavior.

## Participants

This study was conducted based on picture data and one-on-one interview results collected from 4th to 6th graders of elementary school, an AI education leadi ng school located in Seoul, South Korea. First, three classes were selected for each of the fourth, fifth, and si xth graders who received AI education for a year, a nd the task of drawing a picture of how AI will exist at home i n 10 years was presented. A total of 51 paintings w ere collected, including 18 in the 4th, 15 in the 5th, and 18 in the 6th. Among them, a total of 27 studen ts participated in interviews, including 7 in the 4th, 10 in the 5th, and 10 in the 6th.

## Data collection

Recruiting interviewees was conducted in an online space where AI classes were conducted non-face-to-face. After obtaining consent from students and parents i n advance, students were asked to draw pictures of "AI at home in 10 years" and semi-structured interviews were conducted based on the d rawing task. The interviews were performed using the real-time online video conference system. Additional que stions were asked considering student's explanations of what was happ ening and why such interactions appeared in their p ictures. Furthermore, changes caused by the spread of AI and  the impacts on their lives were questioned. Interv iews were conducted once for each student for about 60 m inutes, and all interviews were recorded and transc ribed after the interviews were completed and used for analysis.

## Data analysis

This study qualitatively analyzed data to explore t he coexistence factors between humans and non-humans in the AI era and the background that enables such recognition. Elementary school students were not appropriate to apply the direct elicitation method of the mental m odel, such as drawing a cognitive map or diagram, s o two researchers analyzed pictures, explanation notes, a nd verbal texts from interviews to define an indivi dual's cognitive structure (Carley and Palmquist, 1992). T hen the categories were refined by two experts with  PhD in educational philosophy and computer science.

## Findings

As a result of analyzing the mental models of the r elationships between humans and AI in elementary sc hool, it was elicited three types; User-Tool, Master-Servant, and Companion-Companion. The first corresponds to a mental model called User-Tool (UT). This type of student recognized AI as a passive object with low similarity to humans, no agency, and utilized for human purpos es by substituting traditional human-tool interactions into AI. While acknowledging the excellent functions of AI and its abilities for work efficiency, it took f or granted a hierarchical relationship in which self-conscious humans have an edge over AI. Since the pi ctures contained one-way interactions according to human instructions or  directions of use, they didn ’t take into account the possibility that conflicts with humans occurred wit h the machine's desires. Students with the UT menta l model objectified AI by associating it with a cognitive t ool or automated system. There is no anthropomorphi c metaphor for the relationships between humans and AI as distributed cognitive theory suggests. The second is the Master-Servant (MS) mental model which is represented in a  student’s saying “The relationship between humans and AI is like a subord inate relationship (S26) ”. Students with the MS mental model conceptualized coexistence by showing several  AI robots busy working at home in their pictures. AI robots that replace human housework and care, such as cooking robots in the kitchen, guard robots in f ront of the gate, cleaning robots, and learning assistance robots, were recognized as necessary for  maintaining a home. However, AI is hierarchical as a worker and servant  to humans, so AI remains in the role of obeying hu man instructions and responding quickly. AI in the MS m ental model is capable of two-way interaction, such as “What would you like for breakfast today (S09)? ” and behaviors change depending on human answers, bu t MStype students believed that AI ’s behaviors were only calculated responses based on  voice and face recognition, not emotional empathy. This conception divided MS-type students into different ideas about conflict w ith AI. Some students thought they didn't have to be afraid  of AI because it was passive and only followed hum an instructions without empathy. Others thought that A I could attack humans and destroy human society by dissatisfaction with humans. The idea argues that A I’s behaviors are based on human morality. Excessive labor or hierarchical environments may cause resistance to humans as AI learns the principles of human society. Finally, the third mental model is Companion-Companion (CC) as the S15 student said “AI is a substitute for humans, a replica modeled after huma ns, and companions to give people convenience ”. For CC-type students, AI is an equivalent and complementar y partner who knows them well. As a companion, frie nd, or supporter, AI affects humans ’ better lives, but it is recognized that humans also  contribute to the maintenance and development of AI, so the hierarchy does not ap pear in the relationship between the two. Interacti on results between equivalent subjects can be represented across different spectra. Students thought that depending on how AI ethics were applied, the relationship between hu mans and AI could be maintained continuously or an opportunity for antagonism could be created. In add ition, it is regarded that if complex relationships  among humans are reflected in AI’s decision-making, it could lead to wrong results. CC-type students were considering the possibility of a change in the relationship bet ween AI and humans after the singularity because th ey thought AI’s emotional empathy would be possible within 10 yea rs and expressed concerns about a society where AI is more central than humans. ## Discussion

Looking at the mental models regarding AI among future generations is meaningful in checking the educational reality so far and specifying the direction of AI education. In this study, the relationship with AI, which will actively interact with humans, was categorized as a  UT, MS, and CC mental model. Students with the UT mental model mainly recognized simple manipulation or remote control rather than in-depth interaction with AI. The MS mental model involved multi-purpose AI devices that were busy following commands, and assu med interaction via voice and face recognition. In the CC mental model, appearance was not important, ther e may be no appearance for AI, and above all, individualized  relationships were more important. Consequently, s tudents tended to recognize the relationship with AI as a subordinate when AI seems similar to humans and relational distance was relatively far. The idea that relation al distance was close and the possibility of confli ct small made students feel AI within Interdependent and non-competitive supportive relations. In addition, when the similarity between AI and humans is extremely high, the possibility of co nflict increases. The uncanny valley theory is reflected in the CC model that a change in a relationship can occur, and students are afraid that the hierarchy can be overturned. This study will be the basis for res earch to design and develop AI literacy education o r EdTech software. It also gives a foundation for predicting  how children divided into three categories will in teract with social robots or voice assistants.

Through the research findings, the following three implications were derived. First, it is necessary t o use AI educationally after understanding the relational re presentation of learners regarding AI. What kind of relationships are needed for teaching and learning effects should be considered from the EdTech develo pment stage. Second, since mental models can change dynam ically, it is important to continuously track learn ers' mental model changes. Learning activities to closel y grasp learners' prior knowledge and experiences a nd resolve inconsistencies between new knowledge and e xisting mental models are required, and teachers ne ed to provide enough experience to construct various ment al models to form learners' scientific concepts. Th ird, educational programs should be developed to foster human-AI collaboration competencies (Song & Cho, 2023). It is needed to cultivate the ability to improve th e quality of the relationships with AI through educ ation that makes students critically think about its effects, side effects, and personal and social impact. The three mental models are not a matter of right o r wrong, but a practical and philosophical problem of how to use AI. As various opinions are being dis cussed in academia about human-centered AI and the posthumanism agency of AI (Bansal et al., 2019), it  will be important to understand the mental models of future generations which give insights into education. To prevent differences in ideas among society members from causing conflicts in the future, efforts should be made to establish proper relationships with AI and to seek AI ethics for coexistence.
