# Can Educational Researchers Help Reduce Accident Rates in a Big Construction Company?

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
It is not common for big construction companies to call upon educational researchers to collaborate with them in efforts at reducing worksite accident rates. In this paper, we describe one such collaboration, including the background and rationale for it, and the progress that the small team of educational researchers and construction company managers have made in the first 7 months of the project. We outline some of the processes through which strategies for addressing the problem have evolved, and consider how it may be possible for a project like this to manifest characteristics of the four dimensions of quality that Furlong and Oancea (2005) proposed for applied, practice-based educational research (epistemic, economic, technological, and capacity building and value for people). Our overall aim is to reflect on the extent to which educational researchers may be able to contribute to a more diverse range of applied research.

## Introduction

Many educational researchers engage in applied rese arch, but such activity is almost always firmly foc used on the development of new knowledge for teaching and learning (Dodd & Epstein, 2012). However, there are cases where the research remit is broader and goes beyond addressing teaching and learning challenges. In this paper, we describe such a research project: one in which educational researchers collaborate with management of a big construction company in an effort to reduce accident rates in that company.

## Background to and rationale for the project

The collaboration originated from a discussion betw een the fifth author, who is the Manager of the Saf ety Management Department of Toda Corporation, and the second author, who is a doctoral candidate and owner of a private tutoring school, who have known each other for many years. They discussed a pressing challenge being faced by Toda Corporation: how to reduce its accident rates. The company was established 140 years ago and is one of the major construction companies in Japan at the present time. Although, compared to the average of all constructi on companies in Japan, Toda Corporation’s rate of occupational accidents is lower, it is nevertheless at a level that is considered too high by those in the construction industry. Its frequency rate of accidents (which is measured as the rate of occurrence of an accident per million hours of work) was 0.51 in 2020, but its target rate is 0.30. Reducing accident rates is extremely important for a number of reasons, including the company’s ethical responsibility for ensuring safety for workers and the general public, and its liability on many fronts – civil, c riminal, and social – whenever accidents occur. In the past six years, the company has implemented a number of stra tegies, including the development of a new system o f classifying the causes of human errors (based on Takagi, 2006), the appointment of a director to manage general safety and health, implementation of a database for  accident information sharing, and use of web camer as for remote monitoring of work places. However, the company had not directly engaged with academic researchers. The main purpose of the research collaboration described in this paper is to identify and address relevant human factors that may hinder or promote safety. In the original discussion between the second and fifth authors, they realized that although the company may have been addressing various work tools/resources and procedures to improve safety, they had not adequately consider ed human factors (e.g., worker perceptions, beliefs, and motivations). In subsequent discussions with the first author (who is the second author’s doctoral supervisor), the idea of addressing such factors to promote greater safety was therefore developed. Progress to date At the time of writing this report (7 months from the start of a 2-year project funded by Toda), the “research team” involved in this collaboration comprises the first author (university professor in educational psychol ogy), the second author, another two of the first author’s doctoral students (who are able to participate only in very limited capacities because of their other work and study commitments), three part-time research assistants (two working with the first author – one of whom is the third author; and one working with the second author) and, on the Toda ICLS2022 Proceedings 1994 © ISLS Corporation side, the fourth author (Deputy Section Manager of the Safety Management Department) and the fifth author. Two other senior staff members of the company participate in main meetings that are held. Strategies in development The educational researchers have proposed several s trategies to the Toda staff which they are now work ing on together for implementation and evaluation. One of those is to make some modifications to the requirements for contract bidding and decision making. Many smaller construction companies bid for contracts to undertake work for Toda Corporation, but the safety performance records of such companies have not been taken directl y into consideration in the award of contract work. This s trategy is therefore to determine ways to realistic ally but effectively incorporate safety as a key consideration in bidding and awarding of contracts so as to better align the company’s message of valuing safety and its management practices in awarding contracts. Another strategy in development is aimed at improvi ng the training provided to prevent worksite accidents, the most common of which involve injurie s from the use of machinery, structural collapse, a nd falls when working in high places. Toda staff already know essentially why those accidents occur (e.g., taking careless or risky actions especially when in a hurry, ignoring safety protocols) and training of various forms is already in place. However, such training focuses primarily on information provision, and so workers may well learn about the dangers and what they are expected to do, but t hey may not sufficiently understand the direct rele vance to their own selves and the seriousness of the consequences of such accidents. Hence, the objective of this strategy is to improve training methods to more actively engage workers in the learning process. At the time of writing this report, surveys/questio nnaires are also being developed to find out specif ic details about various aspects of workers’ beliefs and perceptions, interactions, and work environments. Identifying variations in these, particularly in relation to accident rates, can then be used for the development of interventions to further reduce accidents. For instance, factors such as team leadership, sense of belongingness and cohesion, levels of trust, and coordination and sharing of knowledge may be related to workers’ sense of responsibility not only for themselves but also those they work with (e.g., Fruhen & Keith, 2014). Finding out such information can help in the development of team building strategies and in the cultivation of safety-oriented worksite leadership. Evaluating the success of the collaboration Furlong and Oancea (2005) proposed four dimensions to consider when evaluating the quality of applied and practice-based research in education: (1) epistemic, (2) economic, (3) technological, and (4) capacity building and value for people. As this research is still in its early stages, it is not yet possible to properly ev aluate its quality according to these four dimensions. However, we can  consider how this research might be able to meet t hese quality dimensions, and examine some indicators tha t might indicate whether the project is progressing  in the right direction. Where the epistemic dimension is concerned, the team is constantly considering the cause-effect chains associated with planned interventions and how it may be possible to reliably document and measure those. Concerning the economic dimension, the research pro ject is focused on reducing the accidents rates of the company which, if achieved, would certainly bring m ultifaceted benefits to the company. Satisfying thi s dimension will depend on the effectiveness of strat egies that are developed and implemented, and the e xtent to which favorable changes (i.e., reductions) in accident rates can be attributed to those strategies. We also believe that there is potential in research projects like the present one to provide knowledge, new ideas, and other useful contributions to the practice of education, and hen ce to satisfy the third, technological dimension of  research quality. Finally, in preparation for writing this s ubmission, we (the authors) undertook individual reflections on our perceptions about the progress of the project to date, including our own roles and progress in relation to work that needs to be done, and our feelings about what has gone well and what can be improved. These refle ctions, which were written down, provide clear indications that this research is contributing toward the fourth dimension of capacity building and value for people (our team). Thus, we have reason to believe that a collaboration like this with a broader research remit can meet the quality dimensions proposed by Furlong and Oancea (2005), but we will need to continue monitoring our progress until the project’s conclusion to more accurately determine that.
