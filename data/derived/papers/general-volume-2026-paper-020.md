# Resonate: An Interactive VR Tool for Experiencing Inclusive Education Through the Student Perspective

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Resonate is a virtual reality (VR) design tool developed to enhance educators’ understanding and implementation of inclusive teaching practices. By enabling educators to experience classroom environments from the perspective of students with disabilities, Resonate bridges critical gaps in teacher preparation and professional development. Leveraging immersive VR technology, the tool extends beyond traditional disability awareness training by fostering structured observation and authentic, experience-based empathy. Distinct from generic simulations, Resonate supports contextualized instructional reflection by allowing educators to test their own teaching materials within simulated disability scenarios. Grounded in Kolb’s experiential learning theory and Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles, the tool supports customizable parameter configuration, dynamic content integration, and in-situ annotation. This paper describes Resonate’s features, applications, and contributions to learning sciences research, aiming to support educators in moving toward more actionable inclusive practices.

Resonate addresses a long-standing challenge in teacher preparation: non-disabled educators often design inclusive teaching based on an “imagined deficit,” resulting in well-intentioned yet frequently ineffective solutions (Valle & Connor, 2019). While educators may be equipped with inclusive theory, they often lack embodied experience of navigating educational environments with disabilities. To bridge this gap, Resonate harnesses immersive VR to provide embodied perspectives, helping educators identify hidden instructional barriers.

Unlike generic simulation tools, Resonate’s core innovation lies in enabling contextualized instructional reflection. The system processes educators’ uploaded teaching materials and integrates them into Unity 3D classroom templates. By systematically modulating the original content, such as reducing specific audio frequencies or blurring visuals, the tool simulates the learning experience of students with specific disabilities within real instructional contexts.

Within the field of learning sciences, Resonate contributes to teacher professional vision and inclusive pedagogy. It demonstrates how experiential learning reshapes educators’ mental models, transforming abstract empathy into concrete instructional design improvements. Beyond a single VR experience, Resonate is designed as a reusable workflow and research infrastructure that supports iterative, data-informed reflection across lessons and disability-related barriers. This paper will illustrate how Resonate leverages these capabilities to help educators refine their teaching through continuous testing, advancing the practical application of learning sciences in inclusive education.

## Theoretical foundations and related work

Resonate is grounded in Kolb’s Experiential Learning Theory (1984), which posits that transformative learning occurs through a cycle of concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualization, and active experimentation. This is particularly relevant for developing cross-context empathy, as direct experience reshapes assumptions. The tool also applies UDL principles (CAST, 2024), advocating for multiple means of representation, engagement, and action/expression. By experiencing how disability impacts these dimensions, educators deepen their understanding of the necessity of UDL. Current research indicates that general education teachers receive limited special education content in pre-service training (Alharbi & Iqtadar, 2024), often leaving them to rely on assumptions to understand student needs (Hwang & Evans, 2011). Meanwhile, VR has been effectively used in disability education (Serafin et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2022), and studies suggest that teachers who undergo disability simulations via VR are more likely to comprehend the real-life difficulties faced by their students (Stavroulia et al., 2023). However, despite VR’s proven potential for UDL, studies focusing on how educators can employ VR to explicitly improve instructional design remain scarce. Resonate fills this gap by providing a systematic solution that integrates immersive experiences, structured reflection, and UDL-aligned feedback. Design features Figure 1 summarizes Resonate’s end-to-end workflow and data flow. Educators first provide instructional artifacts (e.g., slides, video, audio, text) and select a parameterized disability simulation profile. These inputs are mapped onto a classroom template and experienced through a Unity-based VR simulation. During the experience, the system captures in-situ annotations with automatic timestamps, which are aggregated into a timeline heatmap and used to support replay and generate UDL-informed recommendations, with exportable outputs. • Representation: Configures barriers related to information perception, including visual impairments,

hearing loss, and processing delays.

• Engagement: Adjusts factors affecting focus and motivation, such as attention regulation difficulties,

sensory sensitivities, and specific anxiety triggers.

• Action & expression: Simulates challenges in navigation and communication, including motor

The system dynamically integrates the educator’s uploaded materials while simulating specific perceptual and environmental challenges:

• Dynamic content filtering: Applies real-time adjustments to audio and visual clarity based on the input

disability profile. The system modulates the user’s sensory input, such as blurring visuals or reducing specific audio frequencies, to simulate the learners’ perspective.

• Information delay: Replicates the real-world Communication Access Real-time Translation (CART)

services by introducing a 2–6 second lag between spoken audio and caption display.

• Environmental distractions: Simulates the interference caused by real-world classroom dynamics, such

as ambient noise and overlapping conversations during group discussions. Reflection-in-action annotation During immersion, users can activate a lightweight in-headset annotation panel via simple gestures to instantly annotate recognized issues (e.g., “unclear slide text,” “teacher speaking too fast”) (see Figure 3). All annotations are automatically time-stamped, supporting reflective observation by transforming fleeting subjective perceptions into trackable data points.

Figure 3 First-person VR view of the “Reflection-in-action” annotation panel V isual analysis and scaffolding feedback The system converts user annotations into a visual timeline “heatmap.” Annotations are stored with timestamps and tags and aggregated into fixed time bins, allowing the heatmap to highlight periods of high-frequency barriers in the classroom. Beyond providing replay functionality for review, the system generates specific instructional adjustment recommendations based on identified obstacle types and the UDL framework, helping educators translate experiences into concrete teaching design improvements. Application Resonate facilitates an inclusive instructional design process, bridging the gap between experiential empathy and evidence-informed revision. By enabling educators to engage with authentic teaching artifacts through immersive simulation conditions, the tool supports evaluation from the perspective of students with disabilities. This process surfaces otherwise “invisible” instructional barriers, allowing educators to pinpoint specific obstacles and translate these insights into actionable classroom strategies. Resonate flexibly integrates into professional development scenarios:

• Pre-service training: Helps trainees understand and design courses using inclusive design principles.

• In-service workshops: Facilitates team collaboration to redesign courses and discuss barrier

identification.

• Special education certification: Serves as an empathy-building tool, allowing trainees to experience

diverse student perspectives before entering classrooms with students with disabilities. Livedemonstration planduring the interactive demonstration, we will provide an overview of Resonate’s design philosophy and workflow. Participants will then use Meta Quest 3 headsets to experience a representative 3-minute classroom scenario from the perspective of a student with disabilities. This scenario will demonstrate how standard instructional materials are transformed by the system, featuring barriers such as caption delays, high-frequency audio loss, and environmental distractions. While one participant is immersed in VR, others will act as “Codesigners” using a tablet interface to view the live simulation feed and discuss potential interventions. Following the demonstration, we will collaboratively review the heatmap and automatically generated enhancement recommendations. Discussions will center on potential barriers and feasible classroom adjustments. We will also open the floor for feedback on the current user experience, as these insights are vital for shaping future iterations. Hardware requirements are minimal, consisting of 2–3 Meta Quest 3 headsets and several tablets for co-designers. A 360° video backup solution is available on-site.

By combining experiential learning theory with practical instructional design support, Resonate transforms how educators understand and respond to student diversity. The integration of immersive simulation, structured reflection, and iterative design refinement creates a comprehensive system for developing both empathy and practical skills. Future development will expand the library of disability profiles and develop longitudinal tracking of implementation outcomes. Through continued iteration based on feedback and research findings, Resonate aims to provide a comprehensive system for educators to translate inclusive aspirations into more concrete, evidence-informed design decisions.
