# Makerland: Mathematics in Makerspace Resources to Enhance Maker-Based Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This work investigates the Math concepts that are applied when using the resources of a Makerspace. The research highlights Math concepts that are usually employed throughout the modeling, prototyping and production processes. An exploratory study was conducted on a Brazilian high school, and the results indicate that students can identify the Math concepts and that the systematization of these subjects can be used as a learning object.

## Introduction

Recent studies show that different factors lead students to have poor performance in Mathematics. Ziegler & Loos

(2017) indicate that the difficulty that students h ave in their learning is related to the lack of con nection of the

subjects covered at school with the real world. Niss and colleagues (2017) point out that the need for Mathematics to be taught within a functional context has been the basis of educational reforms in some countries. To collaborate with this discussion, this paper seeks to relate Mathematics in the Makerspace resources with the Mathland metaphor presented by Seymou Pape rt (2020), that compares the Math language learning process with the way a person learns French when gr owing up in France. Thus, inspired by the term coin ed by Papert, this work presents the Makerland metaphor, when considering that the Makerspace and its resources are elements to enhance the learning of Math concepts while students develop their projects. Based on this reflection, two complementary points in the prototyping of learning objects stand out. The first is the development of projects that aim at the explicit application of Math concepts. These projects are related to the production of resources that aim the learning of the subject during the planning, execution and presentation stages. The second point is the learning of Math co ncepts in the use of Makerspace resources during th e prototyping and production process. In the Laser Cu tter, for example, the relationship between the spe ed of the equipment while cutting a resource and the laser po wer, as relationships between variables and Cartesi an coordinates, are usually applied. This paper discusses this second point. Theoretical foundation Considering the great advance of new information and communication technologies, Seymour Papert's theory of learning, Constructionism, stands out. The theory is based on Piaget's Constructivism and highlights that learning happens more effectively when students are co-autho rs of their own knowledge and share it with their p eers (Blikstein, 2008). The constructionist learning theory was forged on the exploration and teaching of Math concepts through technological resources and contributed to point out a new path for Math Education. Thus, the Logo Language was created by Papert's res earch group for a teaching process where students a nd educators are learners, and everyone learns from th eir mistakes (Papert, 2020). Similarly, the Makersp ace's prototyping resources also provide for the creation  of Microworlds (Papert, 2020) for learning Mathema tics. In this sense, Maker-based Learning helps learners dea l with unexpected problems for which there are no p reestablished explanations, acquiring the necessary skills to participate in the construction of new skills (Gavassa, 2020).

So, when it comes to Math Education in Brazil, the country has undergone significant reforms. In 2018, the Brazilian Ministry of Education approved the Co mmon National Curricula Base (CNCB – BNCC in Portuguese), a document that defines the essential knowledge that all K-12 students have the right to learn. In the field Mathematics and its technologies, the BNCC conceptualizes the subject as: “Human science, the result of the needs and concern s of different cultures [...] and a living science, which contributes to solving scientific and technological problems and to underpin discoveries and constructions, including impacts on the world of work”. (BRASIL, 2018, p. 267) In the Brazilian reality, the Mathematics subject is organized into 5 Thematic Units (TU). They are:

(i) Numbers: developing skills related to numerical thinking; (ii) Algebraic Thinking: identifying the dependency

relationship between two quantities; (iii) Geometric Thinking: interpreting and moving a figure on the Cartesian plane; (iv) Quantities and Measures: building and e xpanding the notion of measure by studying differen t quantities; (v) Probability and Statistics: building sample space for equiprobable events. In addition, the CNCB also includes Computational Thinking (CT) in the scope of Mathematics. The CT is an approach used to carry out thinking processes  and can be applied in areas of knowledge that go b eyond Computer Science.

## Methods

An exploratory study was conducted with 21 students, users of Makerspace, from a high school in a state capital in Brazil. A questionnaire was designed for this study by observing the routine of students when prototyping their projects in the Makerspace. The first questions in the questionnaire investigated how much students perceived the use of Math concepts in the Makerspace, and at whic h stage of project development their use was notice d. The remaining questions related to a preliminary study, which identified the main Mathematics issues that are explored during the use of resources in the Makerspace. These subjects were organized in Thematic Units.

## Collected Data and Discussion

The responses from the questionnaire revealed impor tant insights into the interaction of students in t he Makerspace using Math concepts. 57.1% of students indicated the most important equipment for their projects is the Laser Cutter, followed by Low Tech Resources, 3 D Printer and finally Robotics & Automation. Regard ing the perception of the use of Mathematics in the process of carrying out projects, 57.1% stated that its use is very frequent and 19% reported that it was only frequent.

Regarding in which stage (Modeling and/or Production) students noted the greatest use of Math concepts, 61.9% of respondents said that for them in both stages Mathematics is used, and 38.1% said that during Modeling stage only. ## Conclusions

This study sought to analyze the relationship between the application of Math concepts and the resources available in the Makerspace, from equipment to software. The application and analysis of the questionnaire pointed to the relationship between the Mathland metaphor and the proposal of a Makerland where resources used in the Makerspace are Microworlds and topics related to Mathematics are naturally explored. The students identified the use of Math concepts du ring the Modeling, Prototyping and Production processes and their perceptions aligned with what was identified in the preliminary study. Next steps of this study include systematically organizing these issues so t hat they can be explored in an objective way when s tudents carry out projects in the Makerspace, so that assessment and evaluation can take into account what is applied and learned during the prototyping process.
