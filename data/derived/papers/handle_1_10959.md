# Ableism in Education and Epistemic Cognition: Development of the AEECI Instrument

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The Ableism in Education and Epistemic Cognition (AEECI) instrument was developed based on relevant literature in epistemic cognition, disability studies, and special education fields to measure teacher ability to identify ableism, and to support the identification by teachers of the perceived root causes of those ableist interactions. The development and content validation of the instrument will be shared and discussion of the ongoing research will be included.

## Background and development

Learning as a cornerstone of healing, resilience, a nd community requires that barriers to learning for  all populations be addressed. The AEECI  was developed to identify points of intervention and advocacy for pe ople with disability. More than 50 years of research shows that inclusive  education provides the best academic outcomes, social skills, and vocational prospects a fter graduation for students with dis/ability (Van Mieghem et al., 2018). Despite this inclusive education is not being implemented effectively for many students with disability (US Department of Education 2021). As a result, teachers are often implicated as a barrier to inclusion (Carrington et al., 2014) despite research showing that the climate of education settings interact with teachers' practice, beliefs, and attitudes regarding dis/ability and inclusive e ducation (Sandoval, 2016; van Steen & Wilson, 2020). One important component to identifying ableism and its causes in the education system is the perspectives of teachers because they are in the middle of the system and can identify barriers to its success (Rodden et al, 2018). EC is a set of processes in which individuals deter mine what they know and how and why they know it (Hofer, 2015).  This process  utilizes the person’s dispositions, beliefs, skills, and experiences to help them determine what is knowledge (Chinn et al., 2011). An understanding of EC illustrates how contextual elem ents embedded in education settings, such as those relat ed to dis/ability and the social or educational nor ms in a particular school, can affect the practices and interactions that can lead to ableism and inequity, despite espoused epistemic beliefs. Brownlee et al. (2011) noted that systemic and contextual factors interact with teacher-espoused epistemic beliefs and the practices they enact whil e teaching. These contextual factors can change dec isions, trigger inherent biases, and contribute to educatio nal inequities. Beuhl and Beck (2020) noted that th e apparent inconsistencies in espoused epistemic beliefs and t eacher practices are a result of how the context of  the educational system in which a particular teacher is operating filters their ability to apply practices that align with their epistemic beliefs about how learning may occur, and for who. This then results in inconsistencies in practice (Buehl & Beck, 2020), in which sometimes teachers i mplement practices that do not align with their epi stemic beliefs about how knowledge can and should be creat ed or justified, but they are required by the n orms of the system to use the practice anyway. Lunn Brownlee et  al. (2019) looked at the ways in which EC may affe ct reasoning, specifically when related to children and inclusion, and observed that when children have m ore subjective beliefs they are three times more likely to be inclusive. T his occurs despite their awareness of social norms or constructions about the acceptability or v alue of the child’s identity, indicating that EC ma y be key in determining why ableist interactions occur in a given educational setting, as it may be related to epistemic beliefs, or may be related to the norms and practices of a system at odds with those beliefs. The AIR (Aims, Ideals, and Reliable Processes) model of EC is a helpful tool to identify the underlying EC processes that occur in the implementation of teacher practices (Chinn et al., 2011). This model includes three principal components which come together to illustrate how individuals may det ermine the nature of knowledge in a given situation (aims), and the justification of knowledge creation and sources (ideals and reliable processes). The result of EC then is an epistemic product (EP). Barnes et al. (2020) stated that these EPs can be the final goal for the teacher (knowledge gained and physical embo diments of knowledge) or the “practical application  of the results of epistemic cognition to teaching” (p. 3).  Therefore, given the complex interaction of societa l constructions of identity and how the inherent powe r relationships of these identities relate to the s ystems and contexts in which teachers’ EC is activated, as wel l as to the identification of ableism and its root causes within that same system, we developed the Ableism in Education and Epistemic Cognition instrument (AEECI). The development goal was a flexible and contextual instrument. Every school is comprised of a unique group of individuals, policies, cultur e, and other factors that may contribute to ableism (Ainscow, 2020). This meant that the instrument would need to allow teach ers to fill in the salient contextual factors from their school in a given scenario (Marques Nascimento Macedo & Bispo, 2022) and allow them to use those factors in considering the root causes of ableism. To do so vi gnettes were created and content validation was und ertaken with 5 experts from the fields of disability studies and special education to ensure each was appropriately designed for the instrument. The poster will include examples (See Figure 1) identifying the survey layout and design, as well as the content validation process.
