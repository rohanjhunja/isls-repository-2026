# Methodological Progress in Co-Regulation and Socially Shared Regulation of Learning Research: A Systematic Review

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Self-regulated learning (SRL), co-regulation (CoRL), and socially shared regulation of learning (SSRL) have been essential for successful learning and collaboration. However, it is challenging to objectively capture the “invisible” cognitive and emotional processes at the foundation of learning regulation. To better understand and support SSRL, the study reviewed 118 journals published between 2003 and 2020. Results highlighted the need for methodological development of reliable multilevel analysis methods to analyze multimodal data at different proximal levels.

## Introduction

Regulation Learning Theory has moved beyond the scope of cognitive process and outcome, to defining SRL as a social, interactive, dynamic, temporal, and developing process (Malmberg et al., 2017) that require a shift toward new forms of instructional tools, data collection, data analysis that acknowledge social entities and shared tasks. Sequencing data and state-of-the-art data mining algorithms are thus utilized to study and represent this transition. Increasing acknowledgment of the interactive role o f motivation and emotion (Järvenoja et al., 2020) l ikewise leads to the exploration and inclusion of physiolog ical measures such as electrodermal activity data, facial recognition of affective triggers and triangulation  of multichannel data as a new approach to capturin g critical phases of SRL.

Although recent methodological progress has been ma de to better examine (S)SRL and extend our understanding of the SRL processes, it is still somewhat unsatisfactory. The methodological progress and related challenges have been subject to considerable discussion, and there is an increasing demand for innovative methods with high reliability to advance the field of study. Accordingly, this systematic review highlights th e relevant research gaps in guiding the methodological develop ment in (S)SRL research. This study seeks to addres s the following questions: (1) how has research methodology in CoRL and SSRL of learning progressed over the period between 2003-2020? and (2) What are the key methodological challenges for CoRL and SSRL?

## Data Analysis Need for integrated multilevel analysis 11

The Need to Examine (S)SRL at Different Proximal Levels The majority of SRL studies do not take a longitudi nal approach. Since SRL features learners’ ability to take ownership of their learning processes, temporal behavioral changes are insufficient to constitute the longitudinal trajectory of change in regulatory efficacy and beliefs. Therefore, we argue there is a need to measure (S)SRL at various proximal levels: capture critical temporal small-scale moments that trigger regulated learning, and longitudinal studies to examine protensive changes of learning behaviors and strategies influenced by these.

## The Need for Multimodal Data Collection

Although there are merits concerning the different data collection methods for SRL studies, researcher s have noted the limitations of the self-reported data approach, whereby data collected may not be accurately measured as learners overestimated their SRL skills and the lack of domain-based skills measures. The multimoda l data approach to CoRL and SSRL research would be a fruitful area for further research. The Need for Reliable Multi-Level Analysis By nature, SSRL associated with metacognitive, cogn itive, behavioral, and motivational negotiation and adaptation are intertwined with individual, social and environmental conditions that influence and are influenced by regulatory activities. Thus, nested levels of individual data in that group should be explored to determine the reciprocal relationships among different measured v ariables. Multilevel analysis shows its efficiency in recognizing hierarchical structure and partition of such individual-level and group-level variables.
