# Improving “Classi in rete” Model through Teachers’ Reflections: A Design Based Research Case Analysis

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
“Classi in rete” is an innovative organizational model designed to overcome the social and cultural isolation of small schools. By using a Design-Based Research methodological approach, the study aims to identify the strengths, critical points and proposals for improvement related to the first iteration of this model and to define the changes to be introduced for its second iteration. The reflections of 20 teachers produced at the end of the three periods of activity in an online environment in the first iteration of the model has been analyzed through a coding scheme created with reference to the Conjecture Mapping technique and applied by two independents. The results of this analysis allowed us to identify the changes in the “Classi in rete” model to be introduced for the second iteration. Implications for the model refinement are discussed.

## Introduction

Collaboration between rural, or more in general, small, isolated schools, enabled by digital technologies, can offer a promising solution to address  challenges such as limited resources, small student populations, and di fficulties in attracting and retaining staff (Whalley & Barbour, 202 4). The “Classi in rete” model, inspired by the Quebec “Écoles en réseau”  (EER) (Laférrière et al., 2012; Pieri, 2022), was first i mplemented in a network of small schools in Abruzzo, Italy, to counteract social and  cultural isolation due to their territorial charac teristics (Cacciamani et al., 2022; Mangione et al., 2022). I t is based on three core elements: Classroom as a K nowledge Building Community, Problem-Based Instruction, and dialogue within/between clas srooms using technology. This study aims to identify, through teachers' reflections and using a Design-Based Research approach (Anderson & Shattuck, 2012) with the Conjecture Mapping technique (Sandoval, 2014), potential refinements to the model. The research questions are: What are the strengths, critical points and proposa ls for improvement about the different elements of the embodiment of the model i n the first iteration from the teachers’ perspectiv e? What are the aspects to be changed in the aspects of the embodiment of model for the second iteration?

## Participants and context

Twenty teachers from primary and lower secondary schools participated in the first model iteration, involving 18 classes in Abruzzo with 222 students during the 202 0–21 school year. Four communities were formed, each addressing a different topic: their town square (past, present, future), town history, issues of indifference to racism and marginalization, and characteristics of stars. The Embodiment components of the model included:  1. Tools and Material: a. Knowledge Building as the theoretical model driving the innovation; b Knowledge Forum and Webex as t online environments selected to support respectively asynchronous and synchronous interactions; c.a Design Format (Canevas) based on the EER experience as a scaffold to support the teachers’ design activity. 2.Task Structures: collaborative  design among the teachers     to develop a k nowledge bui lding activity in a community of classrooms. and collaborative inquiry activity to be developed in each classroom’s community. 3.Participants Structures:  a. Groups of Teachers  as the constitutive component of each  community and that worked to create each of the four design s; b. a community for each design, composed of a group of classrooms. 4.Discursive Practices: online discourse, both synchronous and asynchronous, supported by scaffolds to promote knowledge building.

The “Classi in rete” activities have been distributed in  three phases of work: 1 training teachers in the Knowledge Building model and digital technologies to support experimentation; 2. teachers’ designing activities for the classrooms, supported by the Canevas and the research team feedback and   which included creating the four communities, each dedicated to a specific theme 3. the inquiry activity of the classrooms within each community. The inquiry activity was distributed into three one-month periods with a meeting with the research team for each period, to monitor the innovations in troduced. KF enabled asynchronous workspaces for note-sharing, linking, and building-on. Teachers posted reflections in a dedicated KF view after each cycle, highlighting strengths, critical issues, and improvement suggest ions. These were then discussed in Webex meetings with researchers to address emerging issues.

## Procedure

The data analyzed  for the first research question pertains 53 contributions in KF written by teachers  in the reflective views at the end of each period of activity. In each text, in the first phase, syntactic units (or segments) have been identified by two independents coders through the punctuation used by the author (Cacciamani et al., 2018; Strijbos et al., 2006). The degree of agreement bet ween coders in the segmentation was 95.2% and controversial cases were resolved with a third coder. Through content analysis, each syntactic unit was t hen categorized by the same two independent coders, distinguishing segments indicating strengths, critical points and suggestions for improvement, with a degree of agree ment of 94.9%; the controversial cases were resolve d by appeal to a third coder. In addition, the two independent s coders analyzed the specific content of the segments using a coding scheme, built based on the categories of the Conjecture Mapping technique (Sandoval, 2014). The coding scheme included four main categories: 1. Tools and materials (tools for mediating the act ivity), 2. Task structures (elements of structuring the task), 3. Participation structures (elements of structurin g participation in the task), 4. Discursive practices (recurring discursive activities introduc ed by the project). Each category was articulated in subcategory.

## Results

With reference to the first research question, there is a statistically significant difference in t he distribution (Chi square (6) = 67.79, p<.001) of strength and weaknesses among the categor ies. Specifically, strengths are more frequent in “Participation structures” category (z= 5.4, p<.01) and critical points are more concentrated in “Tools and materials” category (z=6.6,  p<.01). Concerning critical points, within the  Tools and materials category, the highest frequency is concentrated in the subcategor y "1.2 Technological tools" (N= 51). Through the content analysis, it emerged that the critical issues indicated by the teachers are the poor quality of the connection and the difficulties that the students had in using KF and Cisco Webex. In addition, the younger students had problems memorizing the procedures to be able to enter the KF and enter notes and those with specific learning disabilities in some cases had reading difficulties and  to write comprehensible texts. Finally, the proposals for improvement are distributed without significant differences amo ng the  categories. For the Task and materials category,  the improvement proposals are more concentered in the “ 1.2 Technological tools” subcategory (N=14).  Among the suggestions emerged from the content analysis, the teachers indicated, on th e one hand, making the interface of the KF platform more intuitive and attractive and optimizing it for mobile devices, and on the other hand, having a good connection. Finally, the participants also highlighted the need to provide tools that pay greater attention to the weakest students. Within the “Task Structures” category, the improvement proposals are mainly located in the subcategory "2.2 Work phases of the path ” (N= 6): The teachers suggested detailing the variou s phases of the experimentation path during the start-up phase, to provide participants with a clear understanding of the workload required. Finally, with reference to the “Participation Structures” category, the improvement proposals are mainly located in the subcategory "3.5 Teacher-trainer collaboration” (N=5), where the teachers un derlined the need to strengthen the communication between trainees and t rainers, for example by activating a shared noticeboard for urgent requests for support in the various testing phases. Concerning the second research question, the second iteration of the model will take place in a new Ita lian region with the following changes:1.Tools and Materials:

a) introduction of  a group  of “Compensatory tools for students with learning disabilities” as new element, through

a list developed and shared among the teachers, b) creation of  video tutorials for students about the use of Knowledge Forum. 2. Task Structures: creation of a shared agenda of activities in collaboration among teachers and research team at the beginning of each phase of  the project to prevent an excessive workload. 3. Participant Structures: creation of virtual spaces for communication among teachers and  research teams after an analysis of the different actual virtual spaces to better identify the best tools for this function.

## Discussion

The results confirm the model’s capacity to address the educati onal and social needs of small, isolated schools, while also revealing areas requiring adaptation —particularly concerning technological access and su pport for inclusivity. It could be interesting to analyse the second iteration, also with reference to the students’ activity, to determine how the results will be aligned with the teachers' reflections. In addition, it could be important to collect, in the second iteration. indicators of reduced social and cultural isolation  to verify the efficacy of the “Classi in rete” model to overcome these kinds of problems.
