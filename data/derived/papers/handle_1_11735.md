# Integrating Student Interests into Curriculum Design: A Mixed-Methods Approach to Fostering Creativity and Innovation for Addressing Global Crises

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores the integration of students’ interests into 21st-century curricula to foster creativity and enhance problem-solving skills. Using a mixed-methods approach within a design-based research framework, the research examines how aligning educational practices with students' interests bridges the gap between formal and informal learning, empowering students to address community challenges effectively. Drawing on foundational theories, including Wallas's (1926) four stages of creative activity and Csikszentmihalyi's Flow Theory (1990), the study emphasizes the role of motivation, curiosity, and cultural norms in shaping creativity. It also investigates how Information and Communication Technology (ICT) supports innovation by connecting theoretical knowledge with real-world applications. Preliminary findings from a 12-week enrichment program indicate that while creative problem-solving activities did not directly enhance creativity, they significantly fostered students' interest and community engagement. The study offers actionable insights for educators and policymakers to develop culturally responsive, creativity-driven curricula that support diverse global contexts.

## Motivation, curiosity, and reativity

Wallas (1926) posits that curiosity is fundamental to creativity, influencing the four stages of creat ive activity: preparation, incubation, illumination, and verifica tion. Csikszentmihalyi (1988b, 1990) supports this idea, confirming the positive correlation between curiosi ty and creativity. In educational contexts, curiosi ty not only drives engagement but also strengthens creative pro blem-solving abilities. As Ladson-Billings (2021) notes, “students’ own level of enthusiasm and genuine interest was contagious,” emphasizing the importance of curiosity in creating a dynamic and engaging learning environment. Information and Communication Technology (ICT) and creativity Information and Communication Technology (ICT) fost ers creativity by providing resources and platforms  for exploration, analysis, and creation, encouraging students to deeply engage with content and develop in novative solutions. ICT also bridges the gap be tween theoretical knowledge and real-world application, allowing students to address real-world problems and enhance their ability to connect academic concepts with practical experiences. Cultural influence on creativity Cultural norms significantly shape motivation towar d creativity and innovative achievement, with varyi ng emphasis on individualistic versus collective creat ivity across cultures. Gardner’s (2008) theory of multiple intelligences highlights the importance of recognizing and nurtur ing diverse talents within different cultural contexts. Csikszentmihalyi’s Flow Theory (199 0) further explains how cultural norms impact indivi duals’ engagement in challenging tasks, emphasizing the ro le of cultural contexts in skill development and cr eative expression.

## Problem statement

A complex interplay of personal traits, educational  culture, timely curriculum, motivation, mentorship, and environmental factors shapes the cultivation of creativity. Despite its global importance, research on creativity is disproportionately focused on North American contex ts, often neglecting the diverse trajectories and c ultural nuances that influence creative development worldwi de. This study aims to address this gap by examinin g how educational environments, curricula, mentorship, and cultural influences collectively shape students' creative growth. Framed by the research question, what combinations of pedagogical strategies and curricul um designs most effectively integrate student interests to fos ter creativity, innovation, and sustained engagement in learning processes? This study employs a mixed-methods approach, incorporating art-based and place-based curriculum analysis. It seeks to deepen our understanding of c reativity’s vital role in the 21st century, particu larly in an era shaped by rapid advancements in artificial intellig ence. The study’s findings will provide actionable insights for educators, policymakers, and parents, offering evid ence-based strategies to nurture creativity and innovati on in diverse, inclusive, and globally relevant educational contexts.

## Research questions

1. How do students' interests shape their engagement and effectiveness in community-oriented problem-solving initiatives?

2. What combinations of pedagogical strategies and curriculum designs most effectively integrate student

interests to foster creativity, innovation, and sustained engagement in learning processes?

3. What is the impact of a thinking skills curriculum on the development of students' creativity,

particularly in addressing complex, future-oriented challenges?

## Research design: Mixed methods – design-based design

This study employed a mixed-methods intervention design (Creswell, 2015; Creswell & Clark, 2018) to examine creativity in educational settings. The study invol ves a comprehensive data collection process, includ ing a 12-week out-of-school enrichment curriculum, the Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking (TTCT) assessment, artifact analysis, and observational data. The research sample consists of two different middle school classes participating in an enrichment program (see Figure 1).

## Results

The i nitial findings suggest that while creative problem-solving activities did not directly enhance student s’ creativity, they significantly influenced students' interests and community engagement. The development of these skills was shaped by the interplay between students ' interests, available resources, and ICT tools. Ke y factors, such as hands-on experiences, peer interactions, and teacher faci litation, were crucial in supporting this development. These findings deepen our understandin g of how the combination of students' interests, re sources, and ICT tools fosters 21st-century skills.

## Conclusions, discussion, and implications

This study demonstrates that integrating mixed methods into curriculum development offers a nuanced, culturally responsive approach that accommodates diverse stude nt backgrounds while maintaining rigorous research standards. Teachers are pivotal in facilitating connections between students' interests and community challenges, including those related to future global issues. Education in creative problem-solving must emphasize integrative thinking that addresses a broad spectrum of politic al, environmental, social, and economic concerns  (Plucker & Peters, 2020). This approach not only equips students to tackle future challenges but also fosters a deep connection with their local and global communities, encouraging active participation and contribution to the broader world.
