# Considering, Recognizing, and Valuing Culture in Afrikan Contexts

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Afrikan countries and cultures create solutions for the needs and conditions of their people, yet their contributions to technological innovations continue to be overlooked. In this paper, we argue that app developers on the Afrikan continent have been accepting educational responsibility by creating mobile applications that specifically meet the needs of the people. From a qualitative research perspective, we conduct a systematic review and cultural studies analyses to explore Afrikan innovations in mobile applications. The findings reveal that mobile developers are 1) building apps that are specific to the Afrikan context and 2) making use of existing apps and appropriating them to the Afrikan context.

## Introduction

Promoting diversity, equity, and justice in educati onal settings cannot be accomplished without consid ering, recognizing, and valuing culture. Although acknowle dged as a crucial component of everything about bei ng human, the concept of culture is often abstract. Th is difficulty is perhaps what leads to culture, at best being understudied, misunderstood, or at worst being igno red. UNESCO defines culture as "the whole complex o f distinctive spiritual, material, intellectual and emotional features that characterize a society or so cial group, not limited to the arts and letters, and including mode s of life, the fundamental rights of the human bein g, value systems, traditions and beliefs" (UNESCO, 1982). For this paper, considering culture means taking into account the ways of knowing and experiencing of different l earners, based on who they are and where they origi nate. Recognizing culture means being aware of the whole complexities of individuals, where they come from, and how their communities and experience influence thei r being. Valuing is consideration and recognition p ut into practice. In the educational context, this can mean valuing different knowledge systems in the way they want to be understood and incorporating the complexities of  realities in the design of learning experiences and environments.

Afrikan countries and cultures create solutions for  the needs and conditions of their people, yet thei r contributions to technological innovations continue  to be overlooked. Worldwide 2.7 billion people use smartphones (Blair, 2020). Afrika ranks highly as one of the fastest-growing developer communities, as evidenced by contributors to GitHub, a cloud-based repository  platform (Kazeem, 2019). From a qualitative resear ch perspective, we conducted a systematic review and c ultural studies analyses to explore Afrikan innovat ions in mobile applications.

At the core of our submission is the notion that culture is a crucial component of one's identity. Our paper explores the ways in which culture manifests in the design of mobile devices, which are common learning devices across educational settings.

Purpose We explore mobile learning literature to demonstrat e how people on the Afrikan continent are consideri ng, recognizing, and valuing culture as they take responsibility for their localized needs by building apps within the African context or appropriating apps to fit the ed ucational needs of their communities. Localization,  in this context, means adapting content or a product to a specific locale. In this study, 'environment' is defined as extended reality (XR), which includes virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), mixed reality (MR), and oth er XR technologies that support and enhance learning and human performance, and to provide users a better immersive experience (Truman, 2019). We refer to standalone p roducts as those things made by man's hand, tools, or systems; products are created manifestations of human thought and ingenuity.

## Theoretical framework

One must make a clear distinction between a theoret ical framework and the particular case being analyzed within it. A theoretical frame presents a general viewpoint and is applicable to a number of cases, whereas the object of a study is a particula r case, whose details can only be given a local explanation (Alasuutari, 1996, 376-377).” ICLS2022 Proceedings 1285 © ISLS Given Alasuutari’s explanation of a theory as a ge neral viewpoint applicable to a number of cases, this research situates itself with The Culture Based Mod el as a theoretical framework for analyzing culture-based information and communication technologies (ICTs). Specifically, Young's Culture Based Model (2009) proposes that all ICTs are culture-based, but some are more culture-neutral (internationalized) and others more  culturespecific (localized). Further, there is a much broa der space in which to design. Young situates the de sign and analysis of technologies from a culture-based context to better understand target audiences and build technologies (Young, 2009, 2008). Similarly, this research seeks  to understand the mlearning applications and the c ulturebased contexts in which they were built, used, or appropriated.

## Results

Worldwide 2.7 billion people use smartphones (Blair, 2020). Afrika ranks highly as one of the fastest-growing developer communities, as evidenced by contributors  to GitHub, a cloud-based repository platform (Kaze em, 2019). Mobile devices, specifically mobile phones, remain the most widely used technology on the Afrik an continent. Preliminary findings of the research indicate that scholars and app developers are accepting educational responsibility through building technologies in Afrikan cultures in two ways: 1) developing mobile apps that are specific to the context and 2) making use of already existing apps and appropriating them to the Afrikan context. There are a growing number of mobile applications that have been designed by and for Afrikan contexts. Some of these in-house mobile applications remain local to the country and the contexts (e.g., Mixit). Others continue to use existing Apps such as Whatsa pp to fit their educational context. This use is consistent with early thoughts on mLearning that re cognised the importance of the mobile phone as a pe rsonal device. The importance was not on what was being created by educators for learning, but rather on the apps that were already on the device, which people were using  in their everyday life that was not being repurpos ed for learning.

While there are multiple ongoing software and mobil e app development projects around the Afrikan continent, much of these data are not published in academic journals. When it is published, it is thro ugh online news outlets, articles, and software development fo rums. Hence, it is impossible to entirely capture l ow-key outlets describing how products are produced, utilised, or appropriated.

## Significance of the study

Hence if learning sciences research desires to addr ess and disrupt such inequities that are interconne cted with diversity, equity, and social justice, as noted in the call, perhaps one of the components is to become more aware of how culture is considered, recognized, and valued in educational settings and in tools for learning. The research in Afrikan contexts is growing, and there is much to learn. Afrikan mobile developers and their localized mobile applications are on the rise. This is significant a s the contributions of indigenous cultures to educa tional technology and beyond is history in the making. As a society with limited human resources, the United States, in particular, will be poised to seek Afrikans to fulfill the growing technological needs and conditions of the present and future generations.
