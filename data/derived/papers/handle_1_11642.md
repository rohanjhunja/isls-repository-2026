# The Impact of Narrative and Feedback on Cognitive Load and Learning in Educational AR Games

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Narratives and feedback are crucial in educational AR games, yet their interplay remains underexplored. A laboratory study (N = 123) investigating narrative and process-oriented feedback (i.e., presence vs. absence) showed no significant relationship between them on cognitive load and learning, but process-oriented feedback improved transfer. Immersion mediated by germane cognitive load, extraneous and germane cognitive load effect recall. Further disentangling the relationship between both factors in AR learning games is essential for deeper understanding.

## Introduction and research questions

Game-based learning and augmented reality (AR) as an immersive technology have gained interest as promising approaches to learning  as both have the potential to motivate learners to engage with learning content (Plass et al., 2020; Tobar-Muñoz et al., 2017). However, although motivation is an important factor for lear ning, positive learning outcomes are the superior goal of education. Taking this goal into account, game design should not only focus on how game design elements can enhance motivation but also on how to manage cognitive load as human’s working memory is only limited to ensure effective learning (see e.g., Sweller et al., 1998).  F or designing educational (AR) games, there are a huge number of possible game design elements whose effectivenes s for learning has not been systematically investigated. Narrative and feedback are two of those game design elements that may particularly influence learning outcomes as well as cognitive load. In games, feedback can be defined as the system’s response to learners’ actions while playing, guiding them toward instruct ional goals and supporting their learning processes (Lester et al., 2020). Johnson et al. (2017) distinguish – among others – between processoriented and outcome-oriented feedback. While outcome-oriented feedba ck informs learners just about the correctness of their answers, process-oriented feedback focuses on the actual learning pr ocesses, providing explanatory information to support learners. Previous research showed that process-oriented feedback can lead to improved learning and less cognitive load than outcome-oriented feedback (e.g., Billings, 2012; Moreno & Mayer, 2005). Narratives can be described as a storyline that provides conte xtual information, connects rules of play, characters, and events (Plass et  al., 2020), and can also facilitate cognitive processes and therefore promot e learning (Dickey, 2020). Through providing a specific context, learning i s situated in a way that may facilitate knowledge retention (Lave & Wenger, 1991). Narratives may have an even higher impact on learning when they are integrated into AR games due to the capability of AR to immerse learners into a game (i.e., learners perceive themselves as enveloped, integrated, and capable of action through interaction possibili ties in the AR game, Witmer & Singer, 1998). Furthermore, narratives can be more authentic as they are integrated and combined  with the real environment with AR embedding the game into the real world (contextuality, Krüger et al., 2019). Despite the potential of narratives for learning, there is a concern that narratives may increase the cognitive load that prevents successful learning. Due to the additional information, learners may b e distracted, which hinders the processing of relevant information. These potential s as well as concerns regarding narratives in game-based learning are supported by empirical research that has shown mixed results (see Breien & Wasson, 2021; Dickey, 2020). Although previous studies have examined the effects of different types of feedback and the use of narratives independently, there remains a significant gap in r esearch regarding the interplay between these two g ame elements and their combined impact on learning in educational AR games. Our investigation aims to examine the effects of narrative and feedback – particularly their relationship – in educational AR games on learning and learning-related variables. In general, we assumed that the presence of a na rrative and process-oriented feedback would enhance learning. The assumptions were pre-registered on AsPredicted.

## Method

A laboratory study with a 2×2 between-subjects design was conducted to investigate the re lationship between narratives and feedback and their combined impact o n learning and learning-related variables in an educational AR game. While Palzer et al. (2025) focus on the dependent variables motivation a nd immersion, this study explores cognitive load and learning in terms of recall and transfer performance as well as the impact of cognitive load and immersion on learning performance. The narrative factor deter mined either the presence (N+) or the absence (N–) of a fictional narrative in the AR game, whereas the feedback factor varied the presence (PF+) or absence (PF–) of process-oriented feedback. PF – simply indicated whether a learner’s answer was cor rect (i.e., outcome-oriented feedback). PF+ provided additional information to support lear ners in their process by either referencing the game objects and mechanics that can lead to the correct answer or detailed learning content-related explanations. N = 123 participants (age: M = 22.3, SD = 3.4) played an educational AR game about pedology (i.e., development of soil and soil characteristics) on a HoloLens 2. They had to deal with two levels in which they explored the game environment, interacted with virt ual objects to obtain further information about the  subject matter, and had to draw conclusions to achieve the learning goals of the game.  After the game, participants answered questionnaires concerning cognitive load  subdivided into extraneous (ECL), intrinsic (ICL), and germane cognitive load  (GCL; Klepsch et al., 2017) and immersion (Georgiou & Kyz a, 2017), as well as a knowledge test to measure recall and transfer perfo rmance. A subsample of n = 40 participants was interviewed after they completed t he knowledge test to obtain further qualitative inf ormation, including participants’ perceptions of the AR game’s usability, narrative, and feedback.

## Results, discussion and conclusion

Our study investigated the impact of narrative and feedback in educational AR games on cognitive load and learning. Results showed no relationship between bo th game design elements, and neither had an impact on cognitive load and recall performance  (p ≥.05). However, process-oriented feedback  (M = 15.3, SD = 3.5) enhanced transfer performance in contrast to outcome-oriented feedback (M = 13.7, SD = 4.3), F(1, 119) = 5.11, p =.026, ω² = 0.03. These results are basically not consistent with previous research and might be explained by the design of the implemented narrative and feedback. Regarding the narrative, interview statements indicate that the narrative was not present in the participants’ minds during the game, which suggests that the narrative was only loosely linked to the gameplay. Fisch (2000) s tated that these so-called extrinsic narratives do not have the same beneficial effect on learning as narratives that are strongly entwined with the game ’s content  (intrinsic narratives). This provides an explanation for why  the narrative did not show an impact on cognitive load and learning. Process-oriented feedback providing general support could not reduce extraneous cognitive load that has been shown by previous studies (e.g., Moreno & Maye r, 2005). However, studies showed that adaptive sup port can result in a significantly greater reduction of extra neous cognitive load and an increase of germane cognitive load than non-adaptive support (Johnson & Marraffino, 2021). Ther efore, providing detailed information in feedback regardless of their actual need s may not be beneficial for minimizing extraneous c ognitive load or increasing germane cognitive load. It may even have had a negative impact  on affective-motivational aspects, which is discussed in Palzer et al. (2025). The unexpected positive impact of process-oriented feedback on transfer and the non-existent impact on recall at the same time may be c aused by process-oriented feedback promoting rather problem-solving skills and flexible thinking which are more important for knowledge transfer (Spiro et al.,

2013) than knowledge retrieval.  Our investigations regarding the impact of cognitiv e load on learning are

consistent with previous research and the definition of all three kinds of cognitive load (Sweller et al., 1998). ECL predicted 5% of variance in reduced recall performance, F(1, 121) = 7.01, p =.009, with, β = –0.23, t(121) = – 2.659, p =.009. GCL predicted 4 % of variance in enhanced recall performance, F(1, 121) = 5.35, p =.022, with β = 0.21, t(121) = 2.31, p =.022. Moreover, the current results suggest, also consistent with p revious research (Huang et al., 2020), that immersion is an importan t factor for enhancing germane cognitive load and t hus cognitively engaging with learning materials, which  in turn has a positive impact on learning in terms  of recall performance (β =.08, z = 2.10, p =.035). As there was no impact of cognitive load or immersion on transfe r performance, a closer look at transfer in AR  game-based learning in the future could offer valuable insights into the processes underlying knowledge transfer. Furthe r research would help to better understand the rela tionship between differential variables in learning scenarios (e.g., cognitive load and immersion) and the application of knowledge in other tasks and situations.  To sum up, w e conclude that the design of narratives is essenti al for a positive impact on learning and cognitive load. Fee dback should be adaptive and consider individual di fferences and the needs of learners to support them. In this study, we have shown that an extrinsic narrative and non-adaptive feedback, which provide varying amounts of informat ion to support learning, do not have an interre lationship in terms of cognitive load and learning. Therefore, ex ploring the relationship between intrinsic narrativ es and different types of feedback and investigating the n ature of that relationship as a next step in resear ch would be beneficial because intrinsic narratives can facilitate learning signif icantly better than extrinsic narratives. These insights can help to make recommendations for the design of educational AR games.
