# The Relationship between Teachers’ Dialogic Questioning Strategies and Students’ Creative Thinking in Elementary School Language Classrooms

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigates the utilization of dialogic questioning strategies by elementary school teachers to foster students’ creative thinking during Chinese language lessons. The sample comprised five teachers and their 224 students. Employing a mixed-method analysis, we found substantial variation in the efficacy of different dialogic strategies in provoking creative thinking. Notably, the dialogic strategy of inviting students to build on ideas exhibited the strongest association with creative thinking, while persistently questioning the same student proved less effective. The analysis contributes to a deeper understanding of how teachers implement discursive moves to facilitate creative thinking in the language classroom.

## Introduction

Preparing students to become creative thinkers, who can adapt to an unpredictable society and enable to navigate their future, is a paramount educational goal in today’s classroom. Teachers bear the responsibility of cultivating student creativity (Soh, 2017).  Students who exhibit creative thinking possess the ability to associate unrelated knowledge, explore  different perspectives, and synthesize ideas into novel concepts  (Sun et al., 2020). In the process of creative learning, students require opportunities to express their ideas, receive feedback on their insights, and engage with others’ viewpoints and evaluations (Beghetto, 2016). However, in traditional classrooms, teachers often hold the floor, assume t he role of knowledge dispensers, and ask fewer high er-order thinking questions, inadvertently suppressing students’ ideas. Dialogic teaching has emerged as a promising pedagogy to orchestrate productive classroom interactions that stimulate student thinking (Alexander, 2018). While previous studies have focused on the effectiv eness of teacher-student dialogue in enhancing students’ classroom e ngagement and subject knowledge attainment (e.g., Chen et al., 2020; Howe et al., 2019), the relationship between dialogic teaching and creative thinking has received limited attention. Few studies have explored the specific dialogic teac hing strategies that promote students’ creative thinking in the Chinese language subject. Hence, this promoted the investigation of this study into dialogic questioning strategies that shape students’ creative thinking in language classrooms. Accordingly, the aim of the present study was to investigate the relationship between dialogic teaching and students’ creative thinking in Chinese language lessons. Three research questions were formulated: (1) what are the questioning strategies commonly employed by teachers to facilitate dialogic teaching? (2) what types of student discourse related to students’ creative thinking  are typically elicited by teachers’ dialogic teaching ? (3) what specific dialogic questioning strategies are more effective in stimulating students’ creative thinking?

## Participants

This study is a part of a larger project that aims to explore the relationship between teachers’ dialogic practice and students’ creative thinking in elementary language classrooms. Five focal teachers were selected from four schools in two districts of an economically developed city in the easte rn region of China. They were chosen for analysis due to the same curriculum content taught by them. A total of 244 fourth-grade students participated in the classes taught by these five teachers, with 219 students completing the student questionnaire. The gender distribution was 49% females and 51% males, and the students’ ages ranged from 9 to 10 years old. Data source and analysis Two main data sources were  analyzed in this study: videos and transcripts of the whole-class instruction and a student self-report questionnaire on creative thinking. For the first research question, the coding scheme developed by Tao and Chen (2023) was employed to codify the teachers’ discursive moves. This coding protocol included three clusters of dialogic teaching moves: Sharing, Articulating, and Building on, with twelve specific dialogic strategies (see Table 1).

## Results

Teachers’ questioning strategies to implement dialogic teaching As shown in Table 3, the results indicated that Cluster 2 (Articulating) was frequently utilized during elementary language lessons, followed by Cluster 3 (Building on) and Cluster 1 (Sharing). In terms of specific talk moves, two dialogic teaching moves,  namely A1 (ask the same student to say more) and BO2 (ask students to extend others’ ideas), were frequently observed in the classroom. Teachers also frequently employed talk moves such as A2 (revoice and require the same student to verify their ideas) and S1 (pose open-ended questions) to facilitate classroom dialogue. These were followed by BO4 (ask students to evaluate others’ i deas), S2 (pose cognitively demanding questions), and A3 (provide challenges and request the same student to elaborate on it). Students’ verbal performance of creative thinking Regarding creative thinking, the analysis revealed that student discourse related to the fluency indicator were the most frequent in the classroom (n = 42). This was followed by indicators of elaboration (n = 18) and flexibility (n = 13). However, there was no evidence of students’ original thinking observed. Relationship between dialogic teaching and creative thinking The results demonstrated that Cluster 1 (Sharing) and Cluster 3 (Building on) exhibited a  high percentage in facilitating creative thinking. Regarding specific talk moves, some tactics  had a greater likelihood of stimulating students’ creative thinking, including BO3 (ask students to explain others’ ideas), S2 (pose cognitively demanding questions), BO2 (ask students to extend others’ ideas), S1 (pose open-ended questions), and S4 (invite students to explain ideas). Conversely, S3 (invite students to share ideas), BO1 (ask stude nts to restate others’ ideas), and BO5 (ask students to compare different ideas) did not effectively promote creative thinking. It is noteworthy that Cluster 2 (Articulating) and its specific talk move A1 (ask the same student to say more) were frequently employed by teachers in the language classroom, but they had limited success in triggering students’ creative thinking. The correlation analysis showed that Cluster 3 (Building on) had a significant and positive correlation with creative thinking (r =.73, p <.05). Although Cluster 1 (Sharing) did not show a significant correlation with creative thinking, the correlation coefficient indicated a nearly moderate positive correlation (r =.41, p >.05). Concerning Cluster 2 (Articulating), no significant correlation was found with creative thinking (r = -.35, p >.05). ## Conclusion

In sum mary, this study represents in  an important step in the research on dialogic teaching by uncovering its untapped potential in promoting students’ creative thinking and identifying effecti ve questioning strategies. The findings are  pivotal for bolstering our understanding of the relationship between language, interaction, and learning and can inform future educational practices aimed at facilitating creative learning.
