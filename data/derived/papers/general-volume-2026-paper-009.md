# Making Collaborative Creativity Visible: The Viacocrea Dashboard

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Addressing complex global challenges requires learners to engage in cocreative processes and regulate their contributions at both individual and group levels. However, supporting and visualizing such multifaceted collaboration is difficult. Building on our previous work and the Viacocrea platform, which guides students through cocreation, we have developed the Viacocrea Dashboard—an interactive tool that visualizes group cocreation dynamics and computes creativity indicators using Natural Language Processing and learning analytics. Key metrics are developed that include fluency (idea quantity), flexibility (topic diversity), elaboration (idea richness), and originality (novelty). The dashboard helps students reflect on and regulate their work, draw inspiration from peers, and enhance cocreation strategies, while providing teachers with actionable insights for feedback and support. In this demo, we will present the Viacocrea dashboard, invite the audience to engage in interactive demonstrations, and discuss how Viacocrea can advance the orchestration of collaborative creativity in education.

Creativity in education is a complex and multifaceted phenomenon that emerges through social interaction and situational contexts. It often involves iterative, flexible, and collaborative thinking, particularly when students work together to address open-ended problems (Sawyer, 2021). The creative process is frequently described as encompassing two main stages: divergent thinking, where individuals generate a wide range of ideas, and convergent thinking, where ideas are refined, evaluated, and selected for implementation (Heinbucher & Bucher, 2022; Sawyer, 2021).

We ground the concept of collaborative creativity in dialogic theory, and it emphasises the emergence of new perspectives through interaction among group members. Within these dialogic spaces, shared dialogue, intersubjective understanding, and reflective discussion foster the development of creative ideas (Wegerif, 2019). Central to this process is the cultivation of constructive tension, where diverse viewpoints encourage students to explore alternatives and refine their contributions.

Sawyer (2022) emphasizes the central role of artifacts in creative collaboration. Artifacts act as tangible representations of evolving ideas, mediating dialogue and enabling students and teachers to track progress, reflect on emerging solutions, and sustain a continuous exchange around creative work. By visualizing the evolution of ideas, artifacts support metacognition, reflection, and iterative improvement. Recent advances in interactive technologies have expanded opportunities to nurture collaborative creativity in classrooms. Digital tools now enable students to co-construct physical or virtual artifacts that capture group reasoning, dialogue, and actions (Sun et al., 2022; Richardson, 2020). These tools deepen engagement with idea generation, elaboration, and refinement processes, while providing teachers with insights into group dynamics and participation.

The Viacocrea platform offers an interactive, multi-user environment designed to scaffold structured cocreative processes in educational contexts. It organizes creativity into six orchestrated phases—Start Up, Define, Design, Build Up, Sum Up, and Communicate—guiding students through ideation, discussion, elaboration, and refinement while fostering collaboration and creativity. Students typically work in groups of four to five within a synchronous, shared workspace. Each group tackles a specific challenge, progressing through the six phases using targeted techniques that support cocreation—approximately three per phase (Pifarré, 2019). For instance, nursing students might design an educational campaign to promote public health, while others might develop an educational game to tackle a specific learning need. In a typical classroom, multiple groups work on the same challenge, enabling comparison of approaches and promoting a richer cocreative environment. Building on this foundation, the Viacocrea Dashboard was developed to make cocreative processes visible and actionable. It visualizes key indicators of group creativity—fluency, flexibility, originality, and elaboration—allowing students to reflect on and regulate their contributions, understand group dynamics, and enhance collaborative strategies. Simultaneously, it provides teachers with insights to monitor progress, guide performance, and deliver targeted feedback and support. The Viacocrea Dashboard builds on the existing Viacocrea platform where students work collaboratively in groups using a structured, multiuser and synchronic space for solving creatively a challenge. Their contributions are systematically captured in a database and exported to spreadsheets, where each row corresponds to an individual student contribution. The dataset is organized with columns that include Group, Phase of Activity, Creative Technique Used, and Idea Text, providing a structured format for subsequent analysis of participation, idea generation, and collaborative dynamics.

The dashboard processes these contributions to provide real-time visualization of each group’s cocreative process, while simultaneously allowing groups to observe and compare their contributions with those of other groups working on the same challenge. This feature encourages reflection on one’s own strategies, identification of strengths and gaps, and adaptation of collaborative practices. By making group-level processes visible, the dashboard fosters sustainable, evidence-based reflection, supporting iterative improvement and higher-quality cocreation processes and outcomes.

The Viacocrea Dashboard computes creativity indicators based on students’ contributions within the platform’s shared workspace, using a combination of Natural Language Processing (NLP) and machine learning techniques, the dashboard provide actionable learning analytics for both students and teachers. These indicators capture core dimensions of creativity identified in research as fundamental to collaborative idea generation— namely fluency, flexibility, elaboration, and originality (Guilford, 1950). Fluency reflects the quantity of ideas generated by the group across different phases of the creative process. By visualizing idea counts per phase and per creative technique used by the group, the dashboard provides insights into students’ cognitive productivity and engagement at each stage, supporting reflection and selfregulation. Flexibility reflects the diversity of topics or conceptual categories addressed by the group. To capture this dimension, topic modelling is applied to identify thematic variation, and ChatGPT is used to generate topic clusters from students’ contributions. As a key indicator of creativity, flexibility highlights learners’ ability to shift perspectives, explore alternatives, and combine ideas in novel ways (Guilford, 1950). Elaboration of ideas captures the depth and richness of students’ contributions. NLP techniques analyze the richness and coherence of group ideas, considering factors such as average sentence length, sentence structure, and semantic connections between contributions.

Originality (Novelty score) is assessed using the Universal Sentence Encoder, which converts ideas into embeddings. Semantic similarity between embeddings is calculated to measure how distinct each idea is relative to others in the group, allowing identification of the most innovative contributions (Haq, et al., 2024). Together, these indicators provide an integrated set of analytics that make the cocreative process visible and interpretable for both students and teachers. Students can monitor their progress and adjust their strategies to improve idea generation and collaboration, while teachers obtain insights into group dynamics and cognitive diversity that support targeted feedback and scaffolding. This integration of creativity metrics and learning analytics strengthens the dashboard’s capacity to support and guide cocreative work. Main features of the Viacocrea dashboard Idea generation: Fluency and elaboration The quantitative analysis focuses on measuring student contributions in terms of the number of ideas, the total number of words, and the number of questions posed. Specifically, the number of ideas represents the count of ideas contributed by students (fluency), while the number of words captures the total words (elaboration) used by each student or group across different phases. The number of questions reflects student engagement and curiosity within the collaborative activity. To effectively visualize these metrics, pie charts (See Figure 1) are used to depict overall distributions.

The comparative analysis examines the distribution of ideas, words, and questions across different student groups, phases, and techniques (see Figure 2). This analysis enables the identification of group progress over time, highlighting how student contributions evolve throughout the collaborative activity. It also reveals the techniques most employed by students and the phases in which they are most active, providing insights into engagement patterns and collaborative dynamics. To effectively convey these trends, multi-bar charts are used, allowing for a clear visualization of variations across groups, phases, and techniques, and facilitating an intuitive understanding of student performance and participation in different phases and techniques. Figure 2 Visualization of Cocreative Indicators Across Groups Topic modelling: Flexibility Topic modelling (flexibility) is performed using the ChatGPT API to extract and cluster the main topics present in students’ responses. This analysis allows for the examination of how ideas are distributed across different themes, providing insight into the flexibility and diversity of each group’s contributions. By identifying the breadth of topics addressed, students and educators can reflect on cognitive diversity and how ideas are explored in different areas of discussion within collaborative activities. We visualize the group topics using a collapsible tree structure, where each group is displayed at the top level, followed by its main themes, with individual topics nested under each theme. This hierarchical representation allows for an intuitive exploration of how ideas are organized, showing both the breadth and depth of each group’s contributions. Semantic novelty score: Originality The novelty score quantifies the originality of ideas generated by student groups and provides a multi-level perspective on creative contributions. It is calculated using semantic similarity analysis, each student contribution is transformed into a high-dimensional embedding using the Universal Sentence Encoder (USE) (Authors, 2023, 2024), and the similarity between contributions is measured using cosine similarity. Contributions that are semantically distant from others receive higher novelty scores, reflecting their uniqueness within the group context. Scores are calculated at three hierarchical levels:

1. Overall group novelty: the total novelty contributed by each group across the entire project.

2. Phase-wise novelty: the novelty contributed by each group within different project phases.

3. Technique-wise novelty: the novelty contributed by each group based on the techniques they employed.

To visualize these dimensions, stacked grouped bar charts are employed. The X-axis represents the different groups, while the Y-axis depicts novelty scores. Similarly to Figure 2, for each group, bars are grouped by phase or technique, and stacks within the bars represent contributions from different phases or techniques. This visualization strategy allows a comprehensive understanding of group creativity, enabling easy comparison between groups, identification of the phases and techniques that contributed most to originality, and assessment of the diversity and uniqueness of contributions across the project. Implementation and evaluation of the dashboard The Viacocrea Dashboard has been implemented in real-classroom settings, students work collaboratively in multi-user, synchronous spaces, generating textual contributions that are automatically processed and visualized by the dashboard in real time. Both students and teachers can access group-level and cross-group metrics, supporting reflection, self-regulation, and monitoring of cocreative processes. To evaluate its usability and pedagogical effectiveness, we adopted a mixed-methods approach, combining qualitative and quantitative data. Qualitative data are gathered through classroom observations, screen recordings, and transcriptions of teacher-student interactions to capture how participants engaged with the dashboard. Quantitative data included click logs, heatmaps, and task-based performance measures to analyze navigation patterns, areas of engagement, and efficiency in completing predefined tasks. Additionally, students and teachers completed standardized evaluation questionnaires, including the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the System Usability Scale (SUS), to assess perceived usefulness, ease of use, and overall user satisfaction.

By triangulating behavioral, performance, and perceptual data, we obtained a comprehensive picture of how both teachers and students interact with and benefit from the Viacocrea Dashboard. The findings from these pilot implementations provide evidence of its potential to enhance collaborative creativity, support self-regulation, and offer actionable insights for teachers to guide cocreative learning processes more effectively. The Viacocrea dashboard is intended to serve a dual purpose: supporting student self-regulation and reflection, as well as providing teachers with insights to guide and scaffold cocreative activities. By making cocreative processes visible, the dashboard aims to enhance collaborative knowledge building and foster higher-quality cocreation outcomes.

In our conference session, we will present the development of the Viacocrea dashboard, demonstrate how it functions in practice, and share insights from its evaluation by both teachers and students. Participants will also engage directly with the Viacocrea environment, allowing them to experience how the tool supports cocreation through hands-on interaction. Through collaborative exploration, attendees will assess its affordances and limitations and consider its potential to serve as a transformative resource for fostering cocreative practices in authentic classroom contexts.
