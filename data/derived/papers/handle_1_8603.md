# Strategies to Manage Scientific Uncertainties for Productive Sensemaking

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study aims to explore instructional strategies to manage students’ scientific uncertainties in sensemaking activities in the science classroom. Sensemaking activities in four science teachers’ classrooms were analyzed for this study, two from Korea and two from the USA. The analyzed data include video-recordings of classroom discourses. Six themes about features of teachers’ instructional strategies that supported students’ productive management of the uncertainties were identified through analysis.

## Introduction and Background of the Study: Sensemaking and Uncertainty

This study aimed to explore features of instruction al strategies to support students’ management of sc ientific uncertainties they encounter in sensemaking activit ies in the science classroom. With a critique on te achercentered type of classroom, science education studies have widely agreed on enacting practice-focused curriculum in the science classroom (e.g., National Research Council, 2012). The fundamental goal of epistemic practices of scientific communities can be described as sensemak ing, which refers to “a dynamic process of building  or revising an explanation in order to “figure something out” –to ascertain the mechanism underlying a phenomenon in order to resolve a gap or inconsistency in one’s  understanding” (Odden & Russ, 2018, pp. 191–192). Researchers have designed curricula and pedagogical strategies to help students make sense of scientific concepts (Schwarz et al., 2021); however, it is not clear wh ose sense do students make: Is it the scientists’, the teachers’, or the students’? We notice that students encounter  epistemic and content uncertainties entangled toge ther in sensemaking processes. If our goal is to help students develop their own knowledge that makes sense to them so they are capable of explaining a phenomenon, students’ scientific uncertainty should be considered as a significant resource in the sensemaking process.

With the view of scientific uncertainty as an umbre lla term which includes content and epistemic uncertainties (Kampourakis & McCain, 2020), this st udy aimed to examine teachers’ supports to manage t hese uncertainties. Content uncertainty refers to uncert ainty regarding what knowledge to interpret a phenomenon. Epistemic uncertainty refers to uncertainty regardi ng how to use and generate knowledge to interpret a phenomenon on and why the interpretation is valid and reliable (Chen & Qiao, 2020). Building on the definition of scientific uncertainties, we explored the follow ing research question: How can teachers support stu dents to productively manage these uncertainties, which refers to managing uncertainties through meaningful practices in the science classroom? The current study intends to contribute to dealing with this issue by examining scientific uncertainties that emerge in students’ sensemaking processes and teachers’ instructional strategies to effectively use students’ scientific uncertainty as a resource to foster them to make sense of phenomenon, data, and argument.

## Research design and methods

This study reflectively analyzed four science classrooms, two in Korea and two in U.S., that adopted sensemaking activities. While two Korean science teachers taugh t at middle school level, two U.S science teachers were at upper level elementary school. The main data sources of this study were 23 video recordings of the cla sses and transcripts of discourse in the classes: elven lessons collected from Korean classrooms, twelve lessons collected from U.S. classrooms. In the data analysis, to grasp features of each class, we first iteratively watched the video recordings and read the transcripts. Then, we divided the data into events with the cues of changes in a classroom sessions and discussion topics. The next step dedic ated to initial coding of events. Starting from the  sign of uncertainty in each event, core concepts, function of uncertainties and teachers’ responses were coded. Then, we interpreted processes of managing uncertainties and teachers’ responsive supports on these processes. Previous literature and codes were adapted in the coding pro cess (e.g., Chen, 2022; Jordan & McDaniel Jr, 2014). Categories and definitions of the management of uncertainty as a pedagogical resource are shown in Table 1. Last step of the data analysis was to compare the coded events and identify across our database patterns co ncerning teachers’ supports for students’ management of scientific uncertainties. We described these patterns as potential themes concerning how science teachers mange scientific uncertainties in sensemaking in the classroom. ## Findings

Six themes of teachers’ instructional strategies to productively managing scientific uncertainties for sensemaking were identified through our analysis. (a) problematize problems regarding not only what students know (content uncertainty) but also how to explain (epistemic uncertainty), (b) use scientific uncertainty to encourage students to re-collect evidence, (c) use scientific uncertai nty to clarify what students learned and construct more sophisticated explanation, (d) encourage students to utilize scientific practices to resolve scientific uncertainties,

(e) use scientific uncertainties to reflect on students’ reasoning, and (f) use scientific uncertainties to plan further

## Discussion

This study explored features of instructional strat egies to support students managing scientific uncer tainties in their sensemaking processes. In the practice-focuse d curriculum, students are expected to position as agents in sensemaking of the natural world (NRC, 2012). As scientists do, it is evident for students to encounter numerous uncertainties in their sensemaking processes. Instr uctional strategies are needed to support students to identify, manage and resolve uncertainties. By presenting features of these instructional strategies, this study contributes to developing teaching for students’ engagement in sensemaking in the science classroom. Further, this  study focused on management of scientific uncertainties, which encompass content uncertainties as well as ep istemic uncertainties. This allowed to identify more diverse uncertainties that students struggle to resolve in the science classroom, and add more cases and expand the existi ng discussions of developing instructional strategi es to support uncertainty management in the science classroom.
