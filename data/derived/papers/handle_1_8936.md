# Design of Personalized Instruction Using Learner Data

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
With the development of advanced technologies, teachers can overcome the limitations of traditional teaching and learning practices by giving personalized instruction. Although personalized instruction can be enhanced with online dashboards showing learning progress and achievements, teachers should develop competencies in how to use the dashboards to design personalized instruction. This study explored how teachers (n = 20) designed personalized instruction using learner data. The think-aloud method is used to explore how teachers create a lesson plan using learner data such as prior knowledge, learning motivation, participation, and so forth. This study found that in-depth learner diagnosis and repetitive design activities play crucial roles in personalized instructional design. Furthermore, the instructional design process had a significant effect on the design outputs and lesson plans. This study has significant implications for teacher education, revealing challenges that teachers encounter in designing personalized instruction with learner data.

## Introduction

With the development of information and communicati ons technology, data on learner characteristics, th e learning process, and results are collected and ana lyzed quickly, which creates an environment for des igning personalized instruction. Personalized instruction refers to a class that provides an optimized learning experience by tailoring learning activities and resources to t he characteristics and needs of individual learners or groups of learners. Previous studies have reported that personalized instruction has a positive effect on learners’ academic achievements (Smale-Jacobes et al., 2019; Tomlinson et al., 2003) and makes learning more attractive to learners (Subban, 2006). It was also reported that it could effectively support the learning of underachievers who have been excluded from traditional instruction (Patrick et al., 2013). However, providing teachers with learner data does not make them better at designing personalized instruction. (Lai & Schildkamp, 2013). The reasons why teachers might avoid using given data include workload, ethi cal issues, lack of competency, and difficulties in fin ding meaningful information from the data. To effec tively support personalized instructional design using learner data, it is necessary to deeply understand how teachers use data for instructional design. In this study, we explore the personalized instructional design process using learner data by applying the think-aloud method and examine  the influence of the process on the personalized instructional design output (i.e., lesson plans). The research questions are: First, how do teachers design personalized instruction using learner data? Second, how does the instructional design using learner’s data influence the quality of lesson plans? Personalized instructional design using learner data It is important to understand individual learners’ characteristics and their needs in order to design personalized instruction more effectively. The personalized instructional design uses the learner’s data to accurately diagnose the educational needs of learners. The starting point for learner diagnosis is generating questions in order to answer with data, such as whether the students are well-un derstood and whether there are students who have no t completed learning activities (Wise & Jung, 2019). Next, it is necessary to identify the data related to the questions and to understand the structure and content of the data (Mandinach & Gummer, 2016). The final step for diagnosis is interpreting the data. The process of finding meaning in data is a process of discovering educational value by understanding data in an integrated way and connecting it with context (Herodotou et al., 2017). Diagnostic results from learner data can be used to design activities for the entire class, specific learner clusters, and individual learners. First, teachers candesign the whole class activity by modifying the contents and tasks of the textbook according to the characteristics of the entire class. Next, the activity for a specific group of learners can be specifically designed for those whose charac teristics are very low or high and who are diagnose d as requiring differentiated treatment. Wise and Jung (2019) referred to this as “scaffolding” for the tar get cluster. Finally, learning activities or instructional strategies can be designed for individual learners by utilizing adaptive digital resources (Pane et al., 2015; Patrick et al., 2013). This strategy will allow individual learners to learn along a personalized learning path (Pane et al., 2015; Patrick et al., 2013). ICLS2022 Proceedings 1269 © ISLS

## Participants

A total of 20 elementary school teachers (17 females, 3 males) participated in the study. They had worked for over five years and were interested in using data a nd personalized instruction. The average teaching experience of participants was 9.6 years (SD = 4.37); three teachers (15%) had a master’s degree, and 15 teachers (75%) had education and research experiences related to data use or personalized instruction. Research process To explore how teachers use learner data to design personalized instruction, the think-aloud method was used in a laboratory context. First, participants practi ced the think-aloud method and the research tools.  Second, participants were asked to design a personalized instruction session using learner data for 60 minutes and write a lesson plan. Last, an interview was conducted to investigate their perceptions of the instructional design process and outputs.

## Data collection

This study collected data using the video conferenc e tool, Zoom, because of the COVID-19 pandemic. All tasks were performed on an online webpage, and the whole process was recorded. Participants were asked  to create a lesson plan by designing a personalized instruction that teaches “the division of fractions” in a 6th grade math class using the given learner data and considering the design context (e.g., school characteristics, classroom environment). Participants carried out the think-aloud method for 60 minutes while designing the instruction. For personalized instructional design, the learner data, including learner characteristics, prior knowledg e level, mathematics learning motivation, comprehension, participation, and learner cluster information, were provided to the participants. The learner data were presented i n raw data, graph, and text formats throughout the class page and individual learner pages (See Figure 1). The go al of designing a lesson that reflects the characte ristics and needs of the learner was also presented, along with the context. Furthermore, we collected the lesson plans written by participants. Teachers were asked to fill out a lesson plan form that could describe the lesson’s objectives and instructional procedures, and they could modify the  form if necessary. They were asked about what type s of learner data were mainly referenced in the intervie w, what was considered in order to design a persona lized instruction, and whether there were any difficulties in the design process. ## Data analysis

A total of 1,056 analysis units were generated by s egmenting the video of the instructional design pro cess video into 1-minute increments. The video was coded according to the coding scheme: (1) learner diagnosis; (2) instructional design (personalized design, traditio nal design, monitoring and revision); and (3) searc hing for information related to the lesson. When it was difficult to code with video, interview data was used to supplement it. The inter-rater reliability (Cohen’s Kappa) ranged from 0.80 to 0.99. We calculated the ratio of time for each category of the coding scheme and then used it for statistical analysis. The instructional design outputs were evaluated wit h a rubric developed based on previous research (Sh in, 2021). To understand and evaluate the lesson plan a ccurately, we conducted an interview with a teacher. The ICLS2022 Proceedings 1270 © ISLS rubric consisted of (1) personalized learning plans, (2) competency-based learning, (3) continuous assessment, (4) learner-directed activities, and (5) using technolo gies for personalization. Using the rubric, two res earchers independently evaluated the lesson plans, scoring 1 to 3 points for each category. The possible total score ranged from 1 to 3. The inter-rater reliability (Cohen’s Kappa) ranged from 0.88 to 0.95. To analyze the process of instructional design, we conducted a descriptive statistical analysis and a chronologically ordered representations of discours e and tool-related activity (CORDTRA) analysis. Two researchers classified the teachers’ design patterns into two types based on learner diagnosis-instructional design activity. Furthermore, an independent sample t-test was performed to compare the differences between p rocess types. An independent sample t-test was conducted t o check whether there was a significant difference in the quality of design outputs between process types.

## Results

The process of personalized instructional design using learner data The learner diagnosis activity occurred for 31.54% (SD = 11.58%) of the total design time on average among the personalized instructional design activities us ing learner data. Instructional design activities a ccounted for 55.91% (SD = 16.26%) of design time on average. Among the inst ructional design activities, the most frequent activity was the traditional design activity (M = 59.37%, SD = 12.27%)—followed by the personalized design activity (M = 17.07%, SD = 10.73%) and the monitoring and revision activity (M = 9.49%, SD = 8.36%). The search for information related to the lesson activi ty took up 31.59% (SD = 13.63%) of the total design time on average.

Using the CORDTRA diagram, learner diagnosis and in structional design patterns were explored to derive design process types (See Figure 2). According to h ow often learner diagnosis and instructional design patterns were observed, the process types of personalized in structional design using learner data were divided into two types. The first type was the iterative design type. In this type, learner diagnosis and instructional design patterns occurred repeatedly throughout the design process. The second type was the linear design type, in whic h the pattern of repeating learner diagnosis and instructional design seldom occurred. This pattern was found less in the linear type (M = 2.10, SD =.99) and in the iterative design type (M = 6.20, SD = 1.87). Figure 2 a. iterative design type, b. linear design type An independent sample t-test was carried out to che ck whether there is a difference in the ratio of de sign activities according to the process types. First, a significant difference was found between the iterative design and the linear design type in the learner diagnosis act ivity (t(18) = 2.30, p =.034). Teachers of the iterative design type (M = 36.91%, SD = 9.83%) spent more time diagnosing learners than the teachers of the linear design type (M = 26.16%, SD = 11.06%). Next, a significant difference was found in the personalized design activities, which is a sub-activity of the instructional design activ ity (t (15.56) = 3.19, p =.006). The iterative design type (M = 23.36%, SD = 10.41%) had a higher ratio of personalized design activity than the linear design type (M = 10.79%, SD = 6.84%). No statistically significant difference was found in other design activities. The influence of instructional design process on the quality of lesson plans An independent sample t-test was conducted to investigate whether there was a difference in the quality of the lesson plan between the iterative design type and t he linear design type. Consequently, there was a si gnificant difference according to the process types (t (18) = 2.72, p =.014). The lesson plans designed by teachers of the iterative design type (M = 13.10, SD = 2.64) were qualitatively better than those designed by teachers of the linear design type (M = 10.00, SD = 2.45).

## Discussion

This study explored the process of personalized ins tructional design using learner data. It was desira ble to repeatedly conduct learner diagnosis and instructional design in the design process for personalized i nstruction. The teachers of the iterative design type created higher quality lesson plans than those of the linear design type. The former tended to interpret more learner data and design more personalized activities than the latt er. This means that the checking and interpretation of learner data must be continued in the instructional desi gn process to develop a personalized lesson plan.

Even if teachers use data to diagnose learners well, they will not be able to successfully design pers onalized instruction without appropriate educational support. Therefore, it means that abilities beyond data li teracy are required for teachers (Mandinach & Gummer, 2015). However, many teachers do not adapt instructions according to the learners’ abilities and have difficulties in designing personalized instructions (Al-Lawati & Hunsaker 2007; Latz et al., 2009); time and experience do not natu rally increase the ability to design personalized i nstruction (Tomlinson, 1995). It is necessary to support teachers so that the learner’s diagnosis results can be well-connected to the personalized instructional design. For examp le, prompts can be inserted in the learning analyti cs-based dashboard so that teachers can interpret data well in considering pedagogy or context, or data requiring attention for interpretation can be displayed with data from various sources. In future research, it is necessary to specifically develop a tool or instructional model that supports  the teacher’s personalized instructional design us ing learner data.
