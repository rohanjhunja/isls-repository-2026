# Fostering Preservice Teachers’ Design Thinking by Integrating Generative AI and Analytics-Supported Reflective Assessment in a Collaborative Inquiry Environment

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examined how integrating generative AI (GenAI) and analytics-supported reflective assessment (ASRA) within a Knowledge Building (KB) environment supports preservice teachers’ (PSTs) design thinking (DT). Participants were 110 PSTs in a semester-long instructional design course. We implemented a design that combined collaborative inquiry, delayed GenAI use, and reflective analytics, positioning GenAI as a design partner. Data from a DT scale and instructional design artifacts were analyzed. Results showed gains in problem discovery and definition and a shift toward more iterative design practices. The study offers a learning sciences account of integrating GenAI and ASRA to support DT in teacher education.

## Participants and instructional design

This study was conducted at a top-tier teacher education university in central China, whose core institutional mission is to prepare qualified K–12 teachers. The participants were 110 third-year PSTs enrolled in a compulsory undergraduate course. All participan ts were English education majors, a discipline that is highly gender-skewed in the Chinese teacher education context. Guided by the four theoretically grounded principles—epistemic agency, collective responsibility for idea improvement, human –AI co-agency and co-design, and embedded and transformative assessment, we developed an integrated pedagogical design that coordinated KB inquiry, GenAI-supported co-design, and ASRA.  The design comprises three interrelated perspectives: (1) a design-oriented collaborative inquiry cycle structured around the DDIPT model (Discover –Define–Ideate–Prototype–Test); (2) GenAI-supported co-design to expand, critique, and refine instructional ideas; and (3) ASRA, including KBDeXbased discourse visualizations and metacognitive scaffolds to guide monitoring and regulation.

## Data sources, analysis and results

To examine changes in PSTs’ DT, we used two complementary data sources: self-reported DT and successive versions of group-based instructional design artifacts. Self-reported DT was measured using the 29-item DT Competency Scale (Cai & Yang, 2023). The questionnaire was administered online at the beginning and end of the course. Of the 110 participants, seven cases were excluded due to incomplete or inattentive responses, resulting in 103 matched cases. Reliability was high (overall Cronbach’s α = 0.931). After confirming normality, paired-sample t-tests and Cohen’s d were used to assess pre–post changes. Table 1 presents the means (M) and standard deviations (SD) of PSTs’ DT before and after the combined use of GenAI and ASRA, respectively, and the paired-sample t-test results. Table 1 shows a statistically significant improvement of PSTs’ DT from pre-test (M = 5.15, SD = 0.60) to post-test (M = 5.25, SD 773 = 0.58), t(102) = 2.151, p =.034, with a small effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.21). This indicates that the observed change was modest in magnitude and should be interpreted as gradual developmental movement rather than a large shift in self-perceived competency. Overall, these results indicate incremental growth in PSTs’ early-stage DT processes, particularly problem exploration and definition, while later phases such as ideation and testing may require longer-term scaffolding or additional design cycles to become salient in self-report measures. In the human–GenAI co-designed condition, mean scores increased steadily from 14.6 at T1 to 18.2 at T3. By contrast, in the human-designed condition, mean scores rose more slowly, from 7.9 to 9.2 over the same period. Because both design conditions were experienced by the same PST groups as part of a sequential design process, these trends reflect within-design developmental contrasts rather thandifferences between independent groups. This descriptive pattern suggests that the human– GenAI co-designed condition demonstrated stronger growth in instructional design performance, consequently, their DT competencies across the semester.
