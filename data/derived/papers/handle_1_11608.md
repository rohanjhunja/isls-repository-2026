# If The Field of Black Digital Humanities (Black DH) Speaks to the Learning Sciences

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The field of learning sciences is increasingly concerned about issues of power, equity, and justice. Though researchers candraw on theoretical frameworks such as critical race theory, (CRT), this paper argues that the emerging field of Black Digital Humanities (Black DH) can also provide the field with additional perspectives that can further help the field understand and develop approaches that continue to address issues of race, power, equity, and justice in the learning sciences. I write to seed a meaningful conversation among scholars in these fields as we collectively aim to center the learner in our research,design, and practices.

Issues of race and power in the learning sciences The learning sciences is increasingly showing inter est in the issues of power, identity, and culture. Scholars are exploring the power dynamic in learning environments (Lee, 2022), and more researchers are recognizing that the learning sciences should focus on issues of justice  and entitlement in and through learning environmen ts (Esmonde & Booker, 2017). For instance, Leibovitz e t al. (2024) call for further commitment to racial justice, urgent and vigorous participation in the racial justice initiative. For the authors, this includes learning about social justice through the framework of pedagogies of witn essing (Leibovitz et al., 2024).  However, “the lea rning sciences possesses limited theoretical underpinning s that make the relationship between power and learning visible, even as the field strives to speak across both a situated, sociocultural tradition and a more  individualistic psychological tradition.” (Esmonde & Booker, 2017, p. 1). Learning scientists have over the years worked on culture, race, power, politics, and ideology  to challenge the assumed neutrality of learning and research (Archer et al., 2016; Conner, 2014; Esmonde, 2014; Jurow & Shea, 2015; Nasir  et al., 2013). Indeed, the integration of different theories and methodologies has always been conceived as a critical for the development of more relevant approaches to examining and designing for learning scientists (Esmonde & Booker, 2017). Hence, the exploration of emerging disciplines, or engagement with  scholars of color (Asino & Bayeck, 20 22) is not foreign to the learning sciences.  The inclusion of new perspectives, theories, and me thodologies has often led the field to explore new dimensions of learning; to challenge indifference toward the experiences of people who have been racially discriminated and historically erased (Politics of Learning Writing Collective, 2017). Learning scientists are increasingly examining various spaces, methods, theories, and tools to critically study power in learning; they are calling for scholarship that explicitly defends and  advances the rights and comfort of marginalized groups (Politics of Learning Writing Collective, 2017). This paper aligns with this call to critically ex amine power in learning and stand for the rights and well-being of minoritized groups in any learning setting by arguing that Black DH can provide an additional theoretical underpinning for learning scientists to contend with issues of race. Black Digital Humanities (Black DH) Black DH is an emerging field conceived as the process or practice of engaging with the experiences and history of Black people through digital humanities methodol ogies (Bayeck, 2024). Black DH addresses the intersection of Black lives, Black cultural and social experienc es, and Black humanity with digital technologies. A s a field negotiating both Blackness and the digital, Black DH is an endeavor to help restore the humanity of Black people using digital platforms and tools (Gallon, 2016). As Kim Gallon writes, in advocating a group whose identity has historically been questioned and denied, Black DH “troubles the very core of what we have come to know  as the humanities by recovering alternate constructions of  humanity that have been historically excluded from  that concept” (p.45). Scholars of Black DH think critica lly about the issue of power; consider seriously th e digital processes and tools used to study humanity; and com bine histor ical and contemporary perspectives of Black experiences to inform research and shape practices in the field (Brown et al., 2016; Gallon, 2016). Black DH is also the process or practice of recognizing that Black history and experience are defined by the intersection of policy, economics, race, techno logy, and gender. By using technologies and other e merging tools to empower and enact respect for Black lives,  practitioners simultaneously transform, translate,  and speak about Black experiences in society. In transforming the experiences of Black people, Black DH can in turn inform other fields, such as instructional design and exhi bition design, in their shared pursuit of creating inclusive learning environments for all students, as well as the field of learning sciences, which is about unde rstanding the complexities of learning across contexts to devise new approaches (Lee, 2018). Hence, Black DH lends itself well to the work of learning sciences by providing historical context for the complexities of learning for Black learners The relevance of Black DH for the learning sciences Black DH brings to the fore questions of race and r acialized systems that have affected and still affe ct Black people. The field tells the story of people working  to restore their humanity by using digital tools. Give n their interest in organizing information to reach a diver se audience, the field of learning sciences can be informed by Black DH. In Making a Case for Black Digital Humani ties, Kim Gallon (2016), drawing  from Kirschenbaum (2014), states the following: The field of Black DH reflects less an actual “thing” and more of a constructed space to consider the intersections between the digital and blackness …  Like race, gender, class, and sexuality— all social constructs, if you will —the digital humanities incre asingly hold real meaning and significance in the academic universe (p. 43).

There is no question that race, gender, class, sexuality, and all social constructs increasingly hold real meaning in the learning sciences. As such, the learning scienc es can benefit from Black DH scholars’ insights int o the intersection of technology and blackness. The lens of Black DH is critical in examining, analyzing, and providing perspectives on technology and Blackness. Being abl e to communicate, inform, and even learn about thes e complex interactions and intersections requires sch olars in the field to think about learning and its intersection with Black experiences and blackness. Furthermore, Black DH is also about creating spaces to learn, talk, and tell stories about Black experiences in a digital world with digital tools. The strategies, technologies, spaces, and ways employed to tell or teach these Black experiences v ary in this emerging field. The expansion of Black DH also resides in its conversations and interactions with other disciplines such as those discussed in this paper. The “more critical [Black DH] practices segue into these spaces [e.g. learning sciences] the greater the ability to shape [the learning sciences] into places of encounter for [le arning], critique, intellectual growth, creative in spiration, and even activism.” (Clover et al., 2016, p.124).

According to Weheliye (2014), Black DH interrogates  the sociopolitical relations that have racialized and informed the literature, philosophy, and texts often used. Gallon (2016) goes further to add that Black DH reveals how methodological approaches for studying and thinking about the category of blackness may co me to bear on and transform the digital processes and tools used to study humanity…. [Black DH] helps to unm ask the racialized systems of power at work in how we understand the digital humanities as a field and utilize its associated techniques (pp. 43-46).

Black DH can  inform on the methodological approaches used to design, investiga te, and understand learning as it relates to Black people, black exper iences, and blackness. It may also transform the pr ocesses and tools used to design and analyze learning. Embedded in Black DH are critical approaches or practices which could add to the understanding of the interplay of race and learning, technology and learning. For instance, the critical lens of Black DH can inform how scholars  engage with technologies employed to design  and learn. With its historical lens, Black DH can bring a new dimension to the questions of technology use and power dynamic by providing a historical insight into current experiences of black learners. The intersection of Black DH with the learning sciences can create new avenues for resear ch, shaping what research is conducted; how research is conducted; who is involved in research, and what qu estions are asked. Intersecting Black DH with the learning sciences can highlight voices that have often been left out or ignored, and forms of knowledge, practices, and ways of learning that may still be overlooked. Given that the emerging field of Black DH is also about improving black experiences in different contexts, Black DH can transform the study, design of learning settings, and as such learning experience of black people in any setting. As an interdisciplinary field interested in inclusion, de sign, and learning, learning sciences can build upon Black DH knowledge to make greater c ollective progress to understand, support, and design learning for all. With Markauskaite et al.’s (2024) I contend that Black DH community has a body of empirical research, prior knowledge, and expertise to contribute, to advance research on learning in the learning sciences. Simultaneousl y, Black DH will benefit from these interactions and make progress in its endeavor to positively affect the experiences of Black learners in any context or situation.

## Conclusion

Scholars in the fields of learning sciences have explored issues of race, power, equity, decolonization, and justice. This paper aligns with work and agues for the inclu sion of Black DH perspectives in the learning sciences scholarship on learning, build on Black DH to understand blackness, and reflect on  how blackness is shaped, portrayed, submerged, or even erased through the to ols or methodological approaches employed in the field’s study and design of learning.
