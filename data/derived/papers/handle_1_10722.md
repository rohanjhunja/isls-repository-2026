# A Study on AI-Augmented Concept Learning: Impact on Learner Perceptions and Outcomes in STEM Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores the efficacy of AI-enhanced concept learning among adult learners, aiming to bolster their comprehension and facilitate the transition to embracing technology, refining metacognitive reading strategies, and improving subsequent knowledge test scores. Leveraging an AI-driven formative assessment feedback system, named SMART, AI integration was implemented in pre-class activities within a Biology course. Learners demonstrated enhanced mental models of STEM readings, and while the levels of technology acceptance were not statistically significant, we observed numerical increases in perceived AI usefulness. However, no significant relations were found with perceived ease of use and metacognitive awareness. The impact of concept learning through SMART on knowledge test scores demonstrated partial visibility. This research underscores the holistic integration of AI tools, highlighting the importance of educators to align instructional methods such as AI with learning objectives, content, assessment tests, and learners’ AI literacy levels, particularly within the domain of online STEM education.

## Introduction

Active participation in pre-classroom learning in an introductory STEM course is essential, especially considering the challenges faced in comprehending concepts from  reading materials (Gasiewski et al., 2012; Lytle & Shin, 2020; Meaders et al., 2020). The difficulty lies in helping students construc t strong mental models, vital for interactive in-classroom learning, particularly in online settings  where unsupervised pre-classroom learning is common. This deficiency often leads to insufficient  prepare dness, lower academic achievements, and unfortunately, students leaving STEM fields.

Our research focuses on using AI to enhance concept learning, which is crucial for understanding STEM content through reading or online video lectures.  This cognitive process is integral in fostering a d eep understanding of the subject matter and is essentia l to cultivating higher-order thinking, including problem-solving and critical thinking skills (Kitsantas et al., 2019; Nazari et al., 2021). Addressing these challenges, our study investigates the effectiveness of AI technology in enhancing concept learning among adult learners. Our fundamental premise revolves around the integration of AI applications, enabling online learners to partake in personalized, self-paced learning. The Student Mental Model Analyzer f or Research and Teaching (SMART), is an AI-based formative assessment and feedback platform wh ere learners write summaries of readings based on the AI feedback, especially addre ssing missing concepts identified through compariso n to expert models.  This approach not only fosters positive attitudes t owards AI in education  but also enhances performance across various tasks. We aim to investigate whether this integration offers students an advantage, not only in comprehending course content but also in tr ansitioning to technology acceptance, developing metacognitive reading strategies, and gaining knowledge. The following re search questions guide the current study:

• To what extent does concept learning on SMART assist learners in building robust mental models?

• How do adult learners’ technology acceptance and me tacognitive awareness change through their

experience with SMART?

• How does concept learning on SMART impact the variation in adult learners’ exam scores?

## Context

This study utilized data from 2 2 undergraduate students who consented to research participation am ong those enrolled in two sections of a Biology course at a t echnical college. Over six distinct weeks in the semester, for pre-classroom activities, learners were assigned specific chapters to read and submit summaries using SMART. They revised their summaries, receiving AI-generated formative feedback as much as they wanted until they were satisfied with it. As a result, they submitted 246 revisions across six reading assignments on SMART w ith an average of three revisions per assignment. After studying every three chapters, students underwent evaluation through an exam, including multiple-choice and short-answer formats. Throughout the semester, students completed four exams, with three of them linked to summary assignments through the SMART system.

## Data collection and analysis

SMART analytics generates 10 similarity indices, sp anning from 0 (indicating complete dissimilarity) t o 1 (indicating absolute similarity), evaluating the pr oximity of a learner's mental model to an expert mo del in three dimensions (see Table 1). For analysis, this study used the initial and final versions of the summarie s. Additionally, we administered pre-and post-surveys, including the Technology Acceptance Model instrument (Davis, 1989) and the Metacognitive Awareness of Reading Strategies Inventory (MARSI) (Mokhtari & Reichard, 2002). The MARSI instrument identifies three factors: glob al reading strategies, problem-solving strategies, and support reading strategies. We only considered problem-solving-related questions as they align better with the requirements of summary writing. Furthermore, we collected exam score data after the semester concluded. We conducted data analysis using Multivariate Analy sis of Variance (MANOVA) and employed Bayesian statistics, which included t-tests and regressions. The utilization of Bayesian statistics  was chosen for its ability to enhance result interpretability and provide more accurate inference, particularly in sc enarios with limited sample sizes or variables.

## Discussion

The study's results indicate that the utilization of SMART had a positive impact on Biology students' mental model development regarding their understanding of the re adings. Although the differences observed were not statistically significant, the descript ive statistics revealed that learner perceptions re garding the usefulness of AI technology increased over the semester, displaying a wider range of opinions. However, there was a min or decrease in the perceived ease of use, suggesting t hat learners encount ered challenges, prompting further investigation into specific obstacles hindering the seamless use of this tool. Expanding on the previous query, we investigated ho w much concept learning via SMART contributes to changes in adult learners' perceived usefulness of AI technology. We identified a positive correlation between perceived usefulness and two surface-level similarity measures: the Number of Concepts and Density. A subsequent multivariate regression test offered moderate evidence for these two similarity measures as predictive factors. It appears that learners' endeavor to inte grate concepts into their summaries, mirroring an e xpert's approach on SMART, acts as a predictor of their positive perception of AI's utility. Contrastingly, our analysis revealed no significant  change in metacognitive awareness of reading strategies between pre-and post-measures. Furthermore, no SMART similarity index wa s found to be significantly associated with learners’ levels of metacognitive awareness. Initially, we hypothesized that learners who contemplate content to produce a better summary  would exhibit improved recognition of reading strategies. However, it seems that SMART feedback, which primar ily focuses on the key ideas of the read ings, may not directly influence learners’ awareness of reading s trategies. These findings highlight the need for fu rther investigation into the relationship between concept learning and reading strategies as metacognitive activities. In terms of the influence of SMART on exam scores, only Exam 4 demonstrated predictability based on several SMART similarity indices. One potential exp lanation is the distribution of the chapters provid ed to the students through SMART: Exam 4 covered the highest portion of the chapters (25%), while Exam 2 and 3 comprised 14% and 10% of the chapters, respectively. Another factor could be the interconnectedness of  the subject material in Exam 4, which predominantly revolves around the products of gene expression. The ability to articulate the complete process of gene expression,  encompassing both transcription and translation as  required earlier on (chapter 9.3 & 9.4), may have facilitate d their understanding of subsequent chapters. In essence, it’s possible that by comprehensively understanding gene  expression and protein synthesis, they could more readily connect related ideas, thus forming a stronger foundation of knowledge. Further exploration across various content areas is warranted.

In summary, this study highlights the potential int erplay between AI-augmented concept learning, learners' perceived value of AI technology, and kno wledge acquisition. It is crucial for educators to seamlessly integrate AI technology with learning objectives, content, and associated teaching methods. Achieving success in AI-empowered learning can enhance learners' acceptance  of AI and engagement in AI-supported learning activities, ultimately maximizing the potential benefits of AI tools.
