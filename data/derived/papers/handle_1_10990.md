# Investigating the Influence of AI-Augmented Summarization on Concept Learning, Summarization Skills, Argumentative Essay Writing, and Course Outcomes in Online Adult Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study aims to explore the influence of concept learning facilitated by an AI-augmented summarization feedback tool, the Student Mental Model Analyzer for Research and Teaching (SMART), on various learning outcomes within an undergraduate English course using linear mixed-effects (LME) modeling and Bayesian correlations with data from 22 participants. Significant improvements in learners’ mental models and associations of concept learning with subsequent learning activities suggest the potential of such tools in improving learning performance.

Pre-class summarization activities in higher education is a common practice aimed to foster deep comprehension and critical thinking skills (He et al., 2016). Despite instructional support, many learners struggle to fully engage in summary writing for learning, impacting comprehension, class participation, and learning outcomes (Park & Kim, 2012). Formative feedback, whether it be automatically generated or from instructors, can be utilized to rectify learners’ incorrect understandings and facilitate their mental model development (Cavalcanti et al., 2021). Utilizing technology for automatically generated feedback can allow for clear and timely delivery  (Zou et al.,

2023) while also providing individualized feedback to pinpoint crucial points for revisions (Zhu et al., 2020).

The Student Mental Model Analyzer for Research and Teaching (SMART)  is an AI-augmented formative assessment and feedback tool developed to assist quality summary writing through feedback such as concept maps which highlight correctly identified key concept and relations (Kim & McCarthy, 2021).  While previous research mainly focused on SMART's impact  with conceptual learning, there is the need to investigate its multidimensional impact, including its relationship with other in-class activities and course outcomes. With the hypothesis that SMART-facilitated concept learning could influence the preparedness for subsequent in-class assignments, the research questions that guided our study are as follows: (1) To what extent does summary writing on SMART enable learners to develop a solid mental model of the readings? (2) To what extent is adult learners’ concept learning on SMART related to summarization skills, argumentative essays, and overall course scores ? Participants were recruited from an undergraduate-level English course in a technical college across two semesters, resulting in 22 consenting participants after excluding those with missing data. Throughout the semester, learners took part in pre-class reading and summarization activities using SMART on two separate occasions. Subsequently, they participated in online discussion forums where they shared summaries of readings without the use of SMART and submitted an argumentative essay. Concept learning was measured through 10 indices calculated automatically by the SMART system, through comparison between submitted summaries and expert models. These indices were based on the 3 dimensions of knowledge structures – surface (number of concepts, number of relations, density), structural (average degree, meandistance, diameter), and semantic (matching concepts, matching propositions, Recall-C and Recall-P) (Kim et al., 2023). Recall-C and Recall-P refers to the proportion of correctly identified key concepts and relations, respectively. Summary writing discussion posts were assessed on its comprehensiveness of content and quality of argument in a scale between zero to five base d Gao et al.’s (2019) rubric. The argumentative essay and overall course scores were calculated and reported to the researchers by the instructor. We used linear mixed-effects (LME) models to track quality changes occurring during learner summary revisions. Also, Bayesian statistics are utilized to explore the associations between variables. The LME analyses controlling for random-effects variables (learner, assignment) revealed that there are statistically significant changes of summary quality during revisions in terms of Recall-C (t = 3.131, p = 0.002) 2309 and R ecall-P (t = 4.089, p < 0.001). The Bayesian correlation results showed that there are significant positive correlations between SMART indices with subsequent learning activities. Specifically, using a two-sided alternative hypothesis there was a significant correlation for Recall-C and Recall-P with respect to the content coverage index of summarization in discussion forums (r =.636 and r =.614, respectively). This was accompanied by a Bayes factor (BF10 = 30.522 and BF10 = 20.215, respectively) indicating extreme and strong likelihood of this occurring under the H1 than the H0. For the argumentative essay, diameter and Recall-C showed significant correlations (r =.585 and r =.592, respectively) of a strong occurrence likelihood (BF10 = 12.296 and BF10 = 13.694, respectively). Finally, the overall course score was significantly correlated with Recall-C and Recall-P (r =.716 and r =.591, respectively) with an extreme and strong likelihood of this occurring (BF10 > 100 and BF10 = 13.446, respectively).

The purpose of this study was to examine concept learning progression facilitated by the AI tool, SMART, and its impact on subsequent activities and course scores. Results of the LME analysis exhibited notable improvement in better understandings of reading articles through revisions in the semantic dimension (Recall-C, Recall-P). The absence of significant changes in the surface and structural dimensions may be attributed to the assignment guidelines limiting variation, as demonstrated in previous investigations (Kim et al., 2023). Despite limitations such as small sample size and lack of control group, this shows AI-augmented summarization feedback tools hold substantial promise in enhancing learners’ academic progress as backed up by vocabulary into their subsequent assignments. Consequently, integrating AI-augmented learning support tools, such as SMART, into instructional design stands to significantly benefit and advance proficiency in writing education.
