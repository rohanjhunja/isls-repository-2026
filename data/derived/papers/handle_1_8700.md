# Exploring the Interaction Between L2 Learners and AI Translator in English Writing

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigated how L2 learners interact with an artificial intelligence (AI) translator for English writing and explored the effects of the English writing skills on interaction patterns. L2 learners (n=21) wrote an essay in English using an AI translator. As a result, three clusters were found: English writing, Korean writing, and Interactive writing. The interaction pattern was different according to the expression scores. The results provide implications in designing interactions between learners and AI.

## Introduction

In the era of digital transformation, learners should develop literacy in terms of how to interact with new technologies like artificial intelligence (AI) tool s. Learners who effectively interact with AI tools may exhibit higher performance and learning gains than those who overly depend on or ignore AI tools. AI literacy education is necessary to help learners to develop competenci es to well collaborate with AI tools for an authent ic task. Despite the importance of interacting with such too ls, there is a lack of in-depth research of explori ng the interaction between learners and AI tools, which ca n provide implications not only for the development  of AI tools but also AI literacy education.

Based on activity theory, this study explores how L2 learners interact with an AI translator while writing an English essay. Although the AI translator is easy to use, learners may interact with it in different ways according to their English writing skills. Learners with low English writing skills are likely to have higher ne eds of using the AI translator. This study explores the activity system of interaction between an L2 learner and an AI translator, consisting of multiple actions and operations (Kuutti, 1996). The following research questions are addressed: (1) How do L2 learners interact with the AI translator in writing an English essay? (2) How are the interaction patterns related to L2 learners' English writing skills?

## Participants

A total of 21 undergraduate and graduate students (13 female, 8 male) participated in this study. All participants were studying different majors and als o had different competency in English writing. Part icipants exhibited differences in content (M=2.81, SD=.981) and expression (M=3.05, SD=.865) scores of English writing competency.

## Procedure

First, we explained how to use the AI translator, P apago (https://papago.naver.com), and allowed participants to sufficiently explore the tool. Seco nd, for the assessment of English writing skills, p articipants individually wrote an English essay without the AI translator. Finally, they wrote another English essay, whose topic is different from the previous one, with the AI translator. We recorded the second writing activities on video to analyze how learners interacted with the AI translator.

## Data collection and analysis

The video was analyzed by applying Jordan and Hende rson’s (1995) human-computer interaction analysis framework for analyzing the interaction patterns of learners. We developed a coding scheme consisting of actions and operations to analyze video data. The coding scheme includes planning, composing, and revising actions including sub-actions. The video data were independently analyzed by two researchers and then discussed to resolve differences in coding results (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.792). To identify interaction patterns of L2 learners and the AI translator, we analyzed the percentage o f time spent on each action and then carried out K-means cluster analysis. We found three interaction patterns and then conducted the Kruskal-Wallis test to ex amine the differences in the actions according to the interac tion patterns. In addition, two researchers evaluat ed English writing with a rubric consisting of content (Cohen’ s Kappa = 0.712) and expression (Cohen’s Kappa = 0. 734). ## Results

Interaction patterns between L2 learners and AI translator The cluster analysis results revealed three different interaction patterns between L2 learners and the AI translator: (1) Korean writing with limited use of the AI translator, (2) English writing with limited use of the AI translator, and (3) interactive writing with the AI  translator. The Korean writing cluster (n=8, 40%) wrote the entire text in Korean and then entered it into a AI translator. The English writing cluster (n=7, 35%) wrote most of the text in English by themselves, but if necessary, Korean at the level of words and phrases was entered into the AI translator. Lastly, the Interactive writing cluster (n=5, 25%) displayed the high proportion of  time spent revising translation results. Kruskal-Wallis tests indicated that the Korean writing cluster had significantly more “writing in Korean” action (H=17.449, p<.000), and the English writing cluster had significantly more “writing in English” action (H=17.166, p<.000) than other clusters. The Interactive writing  cluster exhibited the highest proportion of actions for “revise and use” (H=9.316, p<.009) than other clusters. Other actions were found not to be statistically significant.

Differences in interaction patterns according to learners' English writing skills The results of the Kruskal-Wallis test to analyze E nglish writing competency by interaction patterns revealed the difference in the content score was no t statistically significant (H=1.803, p=.406). However, the expression score (H=6.113, p=.047) was statistically significant between the cl usters. In particular, the English writing cluster displayed the highest expression score, while the Korean writing cluster showed the lowest. Both the learners with high and those with low English writing scores had a limited percentage of interaction with the AI translator. It was found that learners with both  high and low scores had low interaction with the t ranslator; however, there was a difference in the number of inputting words in their writing task. ## Discussion

This study found that L2 learners interacted with t he AI translator in three different ways. While som e learners interacted with the AI translator well, others spent a lot of time on writing an English essay without the AI translator. Learners with low English writing skills tended to overly depend on the AI translator, so they seldom revised its outputs. In contrast, learners with hig h English writing skills were not easily satisfied with the translation outputs, so they might consider how much they would use the AI translator for English writing. Some learners actively interacted with the AI translator, whereas others seldom used it due to the lack of usefulness. This result is consistent with Chartterjee and Bhattacharjee (2020), who argue that attitude toward AI tool has a significant impact on behavioral intention of the individuals to adopt AI in education. Future research is necessary to investigate how the interaction patterns influence the development of L2 learners’ English writing skills. This study implies that AI literacy education should focus on the interaction between learners and AI-based tools.
