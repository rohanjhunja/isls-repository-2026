# Putting Theory into Practice: The Complex Experiences of Veterinary Students in a Constructivist Writing Assignment

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study implements design-based research (DBR) to enhance authentic advocacy in a veterinary course. The study analyzed learner experiences through surveys and network analysis, focusing on alignment with constructivist and constructionist learning theories. Results identified varying degrees of alignment with these theories across different student clusters. Some students exhibited strong alignment with constructivist principles like knowledge construction, while others showcased elements of constructionist learning, such as real-world application and problem-solving. The findings offer insights into how instructional design influences learning experiences and informs pedagogical practices in higher education. The study concludes that authentic advocacy, when grounded in educational theories, can significantly improve learning outcomes, critical thinking, and professional development. It also prepares learners for the challenges of the 21st century. The study shows how real-world scenarios can help students learn skills that are useful for future challenges and suggests that this approach may be beneficial for other higher education courses.

## Introduction and background

We seek to enhance an authentic advocacy project fo r improving communication skills in a veterinary co urse through the implementation of design-based research (DBR). Students were asked to research a controversial veterinary issue and write a letter to a public off icial advocating on the issue of unwanted horses in the U nited States. Our goals through the iterations were to enhance the practice of authentic advocacy in ot her courses, generate discourse for its widespread adoption, and  contribute to the community's knowledge-building efforts. We hope to demonstrate the potential of this approac h for preparing learners for needed 21st century sk ills, such as communication, critical thinking, and creativity.

Authentic advocacy projects in higher education sho w that there are multiple benefits of them for students. Alimen (2022) suggests that advocacy-based teaching strategies in higher education can a lign with the concept of open-mindedness and contribute to a university's triple function of instruction, research, and community service. McCan (2015) highlights that an advocacy project supports student learning and engagement, particularly in challenging social and economic tim es. Alderton (2017) discusses the benefits of engag ing undergraduate students in authentic research experi ences, including improved communication, or ganizational skills, self-efficacy, and collaboration. The current study expands upon these findings. The theoretical framework of this study draws upon two theories to support its foundation. First, cognitive constructivist theory emphasizes knowledge consolidation (Piaget, 1952). Second, social constructivist theory includes principles such as audience engagem ent, co-construction of knowledge, negotiation of meaning, and scaffolding (Vygotsky, 1978), and  emphasizes that learning is a dynamic process invol ving individual, collaborative, and collective construction of knowl edge (Papert, 1993). Additionally, c onstructionist theory, encompasses elements such as authentic audience eng agement, practical application, learner agency, and experimental exploration (Papert & Harel, 1991).

The objective of this study is to explore the inter dependent relationships within a constructivist and constructionist learning activity in a veterinary m edicine course. The aim was to enhance our understanding of the learner experience. The research question was: How do the interdependent elements of student struggles, what worked, and theoretical alignment influence the ite rative design process and semantic network analysis  of an authentic audience project?

## Methods and results

The current study implemented DBR in a professional veterinary medical school course to investigate the design of an authentic audience project. Students in the f irst year of the Doctor of Veterinary Medicine prog ram were assigned an individual project in which they were directed to research the relevant i ssue of unwanted horses in the United States. Each student then chose a positi on on the issue. In the present study, the third it eration of a broader DBR project, we aimed to evaluate the effec tiveness of an authentic audience project. To achie ve this, we implemented a Learning Experience Network Analys is (Donaldson, 20204).  By analyzing the learning experience network map of correlations at p<.01 with three clusters at Q=0.454, we were able t o identify the complex relationships between what worked for stude nts during the assignment, their struggles, and pri nciples from constructivist and constructionist theories.

For instances, the students encompassed within the large red circles cluster showcased academic growth due to a range of effective practices and transformative experiences. They excelled in developing critical thinking skills, which empowered them to engag e deeply with the subject matter and fostered their  ability to approach complex problems from multiple angles. Additionally, these students refined their professional writing  skills by producing thought-provoking and well-articulated arguments. Their experiences led to a realization of the broader impact of experts in their field. Also, these stude nts actively developed their own opinions and recog nized the relevance of their personal identities within the context of their studies. Their success can be attributed to several key factors, such as clear and well-structured instructions, easily accessible resources, and guidance that facilitated their identity development. Their success aligned w ith the constructivist theory principles of embraci ng multiple pathways, approaches, and perspectives, construction of knowledge through argumentation, and the development of new perspectives. Furthermore, their experiences  were consistent with the constructionist theory pr inciples of constructing tangible artifacts, engaging in authentic purposes, and fostering a spirit of tinkering and exploration. These students used reflection and metacognition and they recognized the value of productive failure a s a means of enhancing their learning.

## Discussion and conclusion

Though our findings support those in the articles r eviewed above, we found it interesting that the con structivist and constructionist principles were not equally dis tributed between the two clusters. This suggests that some students had experiences aligning more with constru ctivist principles, and other students had experien ces more aligned with constructionist principles. This raise s questions about whether certain educational practices might favor one theoretical framework over the other.

In this research study, we aimed to evaluate the efficacy of authentic advocacy in a veterinary course and to demonstrate the potential of this approach for enhancing learning and preparing learners for 21st century skills.

Our findings suggest that authentic advocacy can be  an effective way to teach students how to think cr itically, communicate effectively, and collaborate with other s, all of which are essential 21st century skills. By incorporating authentic advocacy into their courses, educators can help  prepare students for the challenges they will face in their future careers. The study's find ings could inform instructional design and pedagogical practices in higher education and professional programs in sc ience, technology, engineering, and mathematics  (STEM) courses, ensuring that learners are well-equipped to meet future challenges. The study also could have implications for instructional design and pedagogical practices in higher education more broadly, as it highlights the potential benefits of using real-world scenarios to teach students important skills.
