# Barriers and Facilitators to K-12 Teachers’ Engagement with Open Educational Resources (OER): A Systematic Review

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Despite their learning potential, Open Educational Resources (OER) are rarely used. This poster presents preliminary results from a systematic review of the barriers and facilitators that influence K-12 teachers’ engagement with OER. A thematic analysis of 63 studies identified barriers and facilitators related to five key factors. The findings underscore the need for support to enhance teachers' engagement with OER by providing high-quality, adaptable, and contextually relevant OER, sufficient school support, and professional development.

## Background

Open Educational Resources (OER) advance learning i n K-12 classrooms. As freely accessible resources, OER reduce costs in education (Blomgren, 2018; Wiley et  al., 2012). OER also provide teachers with opportunities to adapt teaching, for example, by addressing heterogeneity  (Blomgren, 2018), or for supporting contextualizing learning materials to the curriculum (Fernandes et al., 2013). Furthermore, they foster  OER-enabled pedagogy (e.g., allowing students to create and adapt materials independently; Wiley & Hilton, 2018). However, transforming the educational landscape and improving the quality of learning depends less on the availability of OER and more on teachers’ engagemen t with these resources (Mishra, 2017). K-12 teachers can engage with OER in various ways: According to the 5R permissions (Wiley & Hilton, 2018), the open licenses of OER allow teachers to retain (make, own, and control a copy of OER), revise (adapt, adjust, or modify content), remix (combine with other materials), reuse (implem ent in pedagogical settings), and redistribute (sha re copies with others) OER.

Understanding the barriers and facilitators that hi nder and foster K-12 teachers’ engagement with OER is crucial for stakeholders, including  policymakers, OER authors, and professional develop ment facilitators. Previous literature reviews related to barriers and facilita tors focused on higher education (e.g., Luo et al., 2020; Mullens & Hoffman, 2023)  and are thus not easily transferable into K-12 education due to differences between both educational systems  (e.g., greater emphasis on differentia tion and lo cal curricula in K-12 education; Blomgren, 2018). Using a systematic review approach, we aimed to ide ntify the barriers and facilitators that influenced K-12 teachers' decisions to retain, revise, remix, reuse, and redistribute OER.

## Method

For the reporting of the pre-registered systematic review, we followed the recommendations of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Statement (PRISMA; Page et al., 2021). Studies were eligible if they were published in peer-reviewed journals between 2002 and 2023, and involved either a sample of K-12 teachers who engaged with OER or users of  an OER repository for K-12 teachers. Eligible studies were identified through a comprehensive literature search conducted in ERIC, PsycINFO, ScienceDirect, Scopus, and the Web of Science  Core Collection. Additionally, the 100 most relevant results from  Google Scholar were included to ensure comprehensive coverage (Martín-Martín et al., 2021). The final search term was (teacher OR educator) AND (OER OR “open educational resources” OR “open education resources” OR “open educational practices” OR “open textbook”). Additionally, we conducted a backward and forward s earch (Booth, 2008) to identify studies that were not included in the databases or were not found using our search term (e.g., due to the absence of OER-related terms in the abstract).

To identify, synthesize, and analyze studies, we first screened the titles and abstracts using the web-based tool Rayyan (Ouzzani et al., 2016). Studies that met the inclusion criteria after the full-text screening were subsequently coded (e.g., country, type of OER), and their quality was assessed using the Mixed Method s Appraisal Tool (Hong et al., 2018c). Given the diversity of study designs and contexts, a thematic analysis  (Braun & Clarke, 2006) was appropriate for synthesizing both qualitative and quantitative data. With a thematic analysis, we identified codes from the qualitative and quantitative results of the studies. Subsequently, we derived themes related to barriers and facilitators of K-12 teachers’ engagement with OER.

## Results

Fifty-nine reports, including a total of 63 studies,  met the inclusion criteria. The research designs comprised 25 quantitative studies, 21 qualitative studies, and 17 mixed methods studies. Besides 10 studies involving more than one country, samples were located in North America (k = 27), Africa (k = 14), Europe (k = 6), Asia (k = 5), and South America (k = 1). ## Discussion

With a systematic review, we synthesized and analyzed the research on barriers and facilitators of K-12 teachers’ engagement with OER. The barriers and facilitators identified in this study were similar to those found in research syntheses on  OER in higher education  (e.g., Luo et al., 2020; Mullens & Hoffman, 2023). A particularly noteworthy finding is the prominence of professiona l development programs (e.g., OER4Schools), which confirms their importance in equipping teachers with the skills to find, modify, use, and share OER. There are some limitations  related to the methodological approach and the included studie s. Regarding the methodological approach, we only included studies published in peer-reviewed English-language journals. Therefore, despite an extensive literature search, we are unable to capture the entire research landscape. Th is limited coverage  could also explain the scarcity of studies involving teacher samples in ce rtain regions. Limitations also arise from the included studies, as many studies em ployed small sam ple sizes and focused on descriptive analyses of survey data. Due to the heterogeneous contexts of the studies, the gene ralizability of the findings is limited.

Practical implications include the need for more rigorous methodological approaches (e.g., experimental designs to analyze design elements of effective OER-related teacher professional development). Following the focus on teachers in this systematic review, a subsequent analysis of students using OER could yield new insights into OER implementation in K-12 classrooms, such as a meta-analysis on the efficacy of OER in K-12 classrooms. The most important practical implications are  that teachers need  (1) discoverable, high-quality, adaptable OER that align with teach ing practices and the curriculum, (2) external supp ort in schools, including a sufficient infrastructure, and (3) professional development.
