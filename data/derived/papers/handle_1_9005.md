## Introduction

The New London Group multiliteracies theory has reframed and advanced our understanding of literacy in various contexts. Multiliteracies theory explains that there are multiple ways of communicating and brings to light cultural and linguistic (Perry, 2012; Sang, 2017). Game rese arch has used multiliteracies as a theory and frame work to investigate, explain, and demonstrate the literacy practices players engage in during gameplay (Ellison & Solomon, 2018). Yet, this research leaves out board games, and specifically African board games such as Songo. This study engages the literature from a multiliteracies perspective to start uncovering literacy practices in Songo (Figure 1) and shows the cultural situatedness of these literacies. ## Theoretical perspectives and Methods

Multiliteracies serves as a framework to examine literacies in Songo board gameplay. Using video recordings of gameplay, this microethnography employs interaction analysis as discussed by Erickson (2006) to investigate the different modes of communication employed by players during gameplay. Therefore, this analysis will include language and other forms of expression (e.g., body movements, or gestures) occurring during gameplay in Yaoundé, Cameroon. Participants were adult players.

## Study Context

On the cultural spectrum, Cameroon identifies more with collectivist cultures (Legare &Nielsen, 2015). Cameroon has two official languages, French and English, as a result of the colonial past. However, these official languages interact with local languages. Different languages such as pidgin-English or Camerounism, peculiar to Cameroon have emerged from this interaction (Nkwain, 2014). Camerounism draws on local languages and distance itself from standard French (Biloa, 2012). The communal approach to life and ways of doing facilitates interactions among the multiple ethnic groups that make up the country (Bayeck, 2019). The city of Yaound, where the study was conducted, is multiethnic, located in the French speaking part of the country, and has the Ewondo ethnic group as first dwellers. ## Findings

Findings from this study indicate that meaning making is shaped and informed by the cultural context. The following paragraphs highlight the interplay of language use and collaboration with the cultural context. Language Use. The language used during Songo gameplay in Yaoundé, Cameroon was informed by the cultural context. As shown in the excerpt below, in a game opposing Caulet and Abi, language was a combination of local and former colonizers' language, that is, French. Excerpt 1.

ICLS2022 Proceedings 1974 © ISLS Caulet: tu auras les pourrissons (plays the second hole on his right) [You will have pourrissons] Abi: qui pourrissons ?

[Who pourrissons?] Caulet: tu auras les pourrissons (plays the third hole on his right) [you will have the pourrissons] The exchange between both players exemplifies the use of a language that is peculiar to Cameroon. Words like “pourrissons” is the combination of the French verb “pourrir [to rot] and the suffix “ssons” which is a form for French verbs conjugated in the first plural person of the present tense. “Pourrissons” is used here as a noun to mean seeds with no strategic importance because they cannot lead to victory. Similarly, “bricole and bricoler [tinkering]” is a camerounism that means not doing well or succeeding in the activity one is engaged in. Culturally-informed collaboration: The cultural practices explain audience participation and collaboration with players during the game. The excerpt below illustrates interaction between players and the audience in a game opposing Nti to Ngibi. Nti, in difficulty, benefits from his collaboration with Pierro an audience member.

## Conclusion

Findings in this study show that literacy practices in board gameplay are culturally situated. The field of New Literacies Studies has contributed to our understanding of the cultural situatedness of literacy. However, it only focuses on communities' peculiar use of reading and writing skills (Mills, 2010). This study contributes to research on board games by focusing on literacies. It highlights the influence of culture on literacy practices in games. Game researchers are yet to extensively explored the role of culture in shaping literacies in gameplay. These findings will inform game studies and advance our understanding of meaning making in various cultural contexts.
