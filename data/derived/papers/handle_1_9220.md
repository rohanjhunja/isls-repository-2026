# Leveraging CSCL to Support Parent Involvement and Increase Student Learning Outcomes in Communities That are Historically Excluded From STEM

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Parental involvement is a critical lever that can increase positive academic outcomes for K-12 students. Technology can increase access to parental involvement opportunities to broaden the participation of parents who are from backgrounds that have historically been excluded from STEM fields. Increasing their involvement has the possibility of increasing students’ STEM learning outcomes. This study focuses on middle school students and parents from groups that have been historically underrepresented in STEM. We work with teachers to leverage a technology enhanced social learning environment (SLE) to invite parents and students to share familial knowledge and everyday lived experiences that are relevant to the scientific content being covered in their classes. Our research findings show that on classroom based scientific content measures, students who participate in the SLE, outperform students who do not. In addition, students who participate in the SLE with a parent or family member outperform students who participate individually. These findings advance our understanding of the impacts of parent involvement and emphasize the need to create innovative ways to broaden the participation of parents from diverse backgrounds.

## Introduction & theoretical background

Increased parent involvement has been highlighted as a way to support positive academic self-efficacy, reinforce positive homework behaviors, and increase students’ intrinsic motivation (Fan &  Williams, 2010). Although it has been emphasized as a key factor to support student learning outcomes, there is a scarcity of research focusing on directly linking parent involvement with students’ academic achievement. In th is project, we address the question of whether parental involvement increases students’ scienc e learning outcomes. Our work focuses on middle school students and parents who are from communities that have been historically excluded from STEM. We work with teachers to create culturally responsive ways to invite pare nts and students to share their lived experiences and familial knowledge in connection with scientific content that is bein g covered in their science classes. Because the work incorporates an online social learning environment, we build on frameworks from the literature on computer supported collaborative learning and strive to advance knowledge in multiple areas. First, in recognition that parental involvement is a key lever for improving students’ learning outcomes (Jeynes, 2007), we aim to refine and expand the work on using technology-rich learning environments to increase the science educational involvement of parents from racial and ethnic backgrounds that are under represented in STEM. In addition, we build on prior research on supporting science teachers in greater technology integration (Author,

2008) and leveraging funds of knowledge (Moll, et al., 2013) in their classrooms by providing prof essional

development opportunities that help them to translate cultural knowledge into rigorous science instruction. Prior to the current study, we conducted focus groups with parents during which the y shared concerns about low parental involvement in classroom and school-based activities for f amilies of color. They also mentioned challenges they face as parents of older children. While some mentioned that they have given students assignments that ask for parental involvement, they had not considered ensurin g that they were culturally responsive. They described technologies they use to support parental involvement such as an online parent portal where parents can access students’ academic info (grades, homework assignments, etc.). Some were familiar with the social learning environment used in our work, and all were willing to design and participate in activities designed to be culturally responsive and aligned with their classroom instruc tion. Affordances of technology & computer supported collaborative learning We view the social-media learning environment as one that supports collaborative  learning.  We have known about the many affordances of technology in educational contexts for decades.  Multiple reviews have summarized the many ways that it can support learners to engage in Computer-Supported Collabora tive Learning (CSCL) (Dillenbourg, Jarvela, & Fischer, 2009).  Simply defined, collaborative learning occurs when two or more learners work together to accomplish a particular learning goal.  We use principles from CSCL research to inform our approach to technology integration.  For example, during the professional development meetings we discuss how teachers can provide guidance for students and parents to engage in productive social interactions without over scripting their participation. Students and their parents collaborate to complete activities in order to achieve particular learning goals.  Our project is especially well-situated to examine  the rich affordances of CSCL experiences since we will be investigating how teachers, students, and parents  form a knowledge building community via interactions with the SLE activities (Zhang, Scardamalia, Reeve, & Messina, 2009).  This project responds to the call to take into account social and cultural contexts and pe dagogical strategies as we study the role that CSCL experiences (Jeong & Hmelo-Silver, 2016).  Parents and students establish a joint task and engage in productive processes. Our findings contribute to knowledge of how to support teachers, students and parents to use technologies to enhance learning outcomes.

We hypothesize that a technology-enhanced SLE will support parents’ participation in students’ science learning activities, explicitly validate diverse cultural backgrounds in science instruction, and, ultimately, broaden STEM participation for students of color. Teachers will have an innovative method of instruction and a new tool for cultivating home-school partnerships centered on academics. Students wil l have a method of engaging in scientific practices that are relevant to their lived experiences. Parents will have a new avenue for contributing to their child’s science learning. As part of a larger project, the main research question in this study is, “Does parent involvement increase student learning outcomes?”

## Participants

Sixty-six middle school students from an urban middle school in the Midwestern US in grade 7 participated in the study activities. The students were from two classrooms taught by science tea chers who shared the same curriculum. The school’s Black, Indigenous, People of Color (BIPOC) student enrollment is 71%.

## Procedure & measures

This project leverages a technology-enhanced social learning environment (SLE) called Flipgrid™ (flipgrid.com) to engage science teachers and student-parent pairs in discussions and activi ties designed to create connected learning experiences (Varma et al., 2008) that prompt middle school students to connect their personal interests and cultural knowledge with their academic science learning experiences. The F lipgrid social learning environment is available via an app that works on laptops, tablets, and smartphones. In a typical sequence of events, teachers create a video or text-based question or assign a brief ac tivity within the SLE and share the question via a secure link sent to students and parents. Then parents and stude nts create a video-based response between 90 seconds and 5 minutes long. Teachers can show the video responses during class, respond to them individually, or ask students to engage in an online discussion by responding to each other’s videos. This project is well-situated to examine the rich affordances of computer supported collabor ative learning experiences as we focus on how teachers, students, and parents form a dynamic knowledge building community via interactions with the SLE activities  (Varma et al., 2008).

## Discussion

In this project, we have observed how technology can help parents from diverse  backgrounds to engage in academic activities with their students and have an impact on their students’ classroom experiences and learning outcomes. Parents share their knowledge in their own voice and in their first langua ge. In some instances, the teacher shows the video response in class and the student translates. This sends a powerful message that the students’ funds of knowledge are valued in their classrooms. Students see t hat their parents have valuable knowledge to share, and parents feel validated as key advocates in their children’ s academic experiences. The roles that parents play in the SLE activities illustrate that technology-e nhanced interventions can increase and sustain parent involvement so that parents are able to engage in productive parent involvement. The SLE is flexible enough that teachers can invite parents to participate in multiple roles across a wide range of content areas. Parents and students can participate in academic activities that are culturally rich and personally meaningful.
