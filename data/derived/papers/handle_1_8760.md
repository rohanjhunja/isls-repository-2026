# Establishing a Teacher Community for Innovation in Chinese Classrooms

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
To help Chinese teachers understand and implement competency-based curriculum design, we established a teacher innovation community in China. During the summers of 2020 and 2021, K-12 teachers and researchers jointly designed STEM courses on climate change during 6-week workshops. Researchers use social platforms to share teaching experience to help teachers better understand and implement innovative courses.

Research background Countries all over the world are vigorously promoting competency-based curriculum teaching reform. Since 2017, the Ministry of Education of China has begun to rev ise the syllabus of K12 schools, and put forward re levant concepts such as core literacy of Chinese students.  But at present, most teachers still only focus on teaching content in the classroom, and don't really understa nd the competency-based curriculum design.  This wo rk presents a new study of a professional development community for K12 interdisciplinary teachers to design and enact new competency-based inquiry lessons.

## Research questions

How can we best help teachers to complete a good design that they will actually use in their own teaching? How can we help teachers make progress in changing their practice?  How can we help them to help each other?

## Methods

Design-Based Research is a method used to improve design practice and enhance the scientific understanding of how design affects learning (Barab & Squire, 2004). Design-based research is suitable as the methodology of this research, because it will help generate new understandings, theories, products and strategies, explain  and potentially influence knowledge construction and le arning in the professional community of teachers.  We engaged in two cycles of design, in the summers of 2020 and 2021 respectively, with overlapping groups  of teachers. We made revisions in 2021 based on the outcomes of 2020.  Below we describe these methods 2020 Workshop: The pretest revealed that almost 70% teachers have no project-based learning experience. The main objective of the 2020 summer workshop was how to design STEM inquiry units, and teachers met for six weeks to engage in design activities, working in teams. Teachers made use of the WISE (Web-based Inquiry Science Environment) platform to create interdisciplinary climate education projects. Participants: There were 45 K12 teachers participating in six weeks of online workshops, from a range of disciplines, including science, math, language, social studies, geography and art. Outcomes: ten teachers from ten schools developed lessons and tried them in classrooms (Figure 1). ## Conclusions

It was an engaging experience for these teachers to design an inquiry unit (often in STEM topics), but still another challenge in how to use those designs effectively with students. Teachers have no experience with these forms of teaching, and did not learn in this way when they were students. So, these new inquiry approaches are unfamiliar, and teachers found themselves somewhat paralyzed wh en they tried to engage these methods.  We found th at teachers need more guidance, particularly at the outset, and are now developing methods for support. This research has established a teacher community for innovation in China. The 2020 and 2021 summer workshops enabled K12 teachers and researchers to jointly design climate change-themed STEM curricula. The workshops and subsequent school trials also allow u s to discover the problems of teachers, conduct tar geted discussions, and help them to design competency-centered courses according to their school conditions.  Members of the research team visit these classrooms and capture videos of teachers to share with other teachers, in order to help them understand better.

From a learning sciences perspective, this effort c an help us investigate the specific challenges experienced by teachers, account for their difficulties, and develop support. This project is significant because it is part of a wider effort called the Critical Action Learning Exchange (CALE) that includes teachers from many countries, and tries to develop a critical action curriculum that is appropriate to cultural context.
