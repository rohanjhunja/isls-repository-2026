# Challenging Inequality: A Proposal to Raise Conscientização about Financialization with Colombian Rural Communities

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In this poster, we explore the relationship of Freire’s notion of critical consciousness with critical and sociocultural theories for learning. Specifically, we use conscientização as a construct to co-design an intervention to raise awareness about the impact of financialization on small farmers’ everyday lives. We draw upon qualitative data gathered with rural Colombian farmers to articulate strategies for engaging in co-design of financial literacy strategies for underserved and marginalized communities.

## Introduction

In this poster, we explore how  Paulo Freire' s notion of  conscientização undergirds a framework centered on dialogue to support people in collectively challeng ing and changing historical practices of oppression implemented by the financial system.  Financial literacy, focusing on at-risk or vulnerable populations, has emerged as a response by governments and multilateral organization s to minimize negative effects of financialization (Haiven, 2017). However, critical social sciences scholars have portrayed financial literacy as an instrument to normalize oppressive practices of global financial systems (Lazarus, 2020), making it more difficult for individuals to recognize the structural failures of a system responsible for financial and economic inequality. Our research focuses on how Freire’s liberatory approach  could support marginalized communities to respond to the challenges of financialization (Friedline et al., 2022). Conscientização is the transformative process to develop critical consciousness (Freire, 2005), enabling individuals to understand the impact of socio-economic contradictions in society, recognize their external  (social structure) and internal (beliefs) nature, and actively assume their role as agents of change.  According to Freire  (2021), individuals can either integrate with  their context, developing the critical ability to make choices and transform their reality, or adapt to it, being subject to the choices of others and usually adopt the externa l prescriptions formulated as a form of self-defense. Drawing on the lived experiences of Colombian rural communities and supported by  document analysis and the examination of statistical information, we aim to respond to these research questions: How adapted or integrated are Colombian rural communities with the increasing  presence of finance in their everyday lives? What are the challenges of implementing a conscientização methodology using online and mobile technologies? An exploration of critical consciousness theory Fighting inequality  is difficult if individuals are unable to see  or are  manipulated to ignore  the political and ideological structures underlying oppressive system s. Challenging the status quo requires going beyond  the exploration of the causes and consequences of inequality and focusing on the transformative power of learning to promote solidarity, collective thinking, and critical consciousness (Freire, 2005) and adopting new and alternative educational approaches (Lee et al., 2020). Freire’s approach, based on dialectical thinking and dialogical action (Holst, 2019), aims for a  vision of knowledge in “constant motion and change.” Following Freire’s view of pedagogy as “a process of continuous coinvestigation of the world” (p.560), and his proposition that the researcher role is “to dialogue with the people about their view and my (our) view” (p.96), in this research we aim to integrate his approach to conscientização as a liberatory process as summarized in the thematic investigation process. This process is constituted of four stages: 1) investigate the meaningful thematic, 2) codification, 3) decodification, and 4) action (Freire, 2005). In this paper, we describe preliminary findings of the first stage and outline design of future steps aligned with Freire’s proposal for raising critical consciousness.

## Identifying meaningful thematic: Research design and findings

In line with Freire’s emphasis on discovering and stimulating the people’s awareness of generative themes, during 2022 and 2023, we visited 4 different communities in Colombia to gain insight i nto the "contradictions which involve the inhabitants of the area" (Freire, 2005, p. 112). Using qualitative methods and ethnographic techniques, we conducted workshops and interviews with community participants and gathered data in the form of audio transcriptions, images, videos, documents, and field notes.  Employing a grounded theory approach, two researchers analyzed the data looking for insights about how these communities cope with financialization and inequality in their lives and how they engage with discourses regarding financial literacy. Through th is process, we identified th ree overarching themes: 1) Financial literacy is defined and shaped by personal contexts. Participants highlighted the critical role of personal and famil y practices in shaping understandings of financial literacy. 2) The traditional financial system does not facilitate fi nancial inclusion. We identified how financial inclusion should not be only about providing access to financial products but ensuring that people understand the affordances of the financial system. Data evidenced how financial exclusion is perpetuated by unequal conte xts and opportunities and how historical negative perceptions of the financial system impede action. 3) Technological practices limit fintech solutions. Technology, particularly Internet access, plays a determining role in the potential of fintech-based financial inclusion strategies. Technological  practices, including risk adversity, and gaps generated by the digital divide, impact the frequency and depth of transactions us ing these alternative methods. These themes constitute the starting point for the subsequent stages. Future steps: Codification, decodification, and action Drawing on co-design principles and following the small-circles methodology (McKercher, 2020), we will invite participants within Colombian farmers ’ learning ecolog ies, including farmers, extension agents, scholars, and policy makers, to validate and unpack these themes in the codification stage. Based on the idea of designing "consequential learning that is future-oriented for learners from nondominant communities" (Cortez & Lizárraga, 2023, p. 52)  the co-design team will develop a learning experience to f acilitate dialogue and critical reflection with Colombian rural communities about the impact o f finance in their everyday lives. All interactions are expected to be remote, supported by online platform s like Zoom and WhatsApp, which offer the opportuni ty to examine how this process unfolds in such an environment. Following the DBR tradition, we expect to collect data including individual interviews, group sessions, chat content, artifacts, and field notes.  We also intend to follow the decodification stage by implementing the co-design learning experience and assessing how the experience supports participants to make explicit their real consciousness of the world  (Freire, 2005). In the final stage of “action accompanied by reflection” we will assess the incorporation of new elements in the individual behaviors and the real consciousness transformation at the individual and collective levels. Transformative social change and the development of critical consciousness are e ssential to support this process from an educationa l perspective. Combining conscientização, DBR, and co-design seems to be an appropriate way to ensure tha t the change will be  sustainable over time and address these communities ' needs. Our research project ex amines the benefits and challenges of this combination with historically oppressed and marginalized communities, incorporating a liberatory approach to financial literacy.
