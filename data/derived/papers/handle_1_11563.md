# Towards Possibility Discourse: Teacher-Facilitator Interactions in Professional Development Focused on Ambitious Instruction

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Teachers' assumptions about learning can limit their openness to ambitious instructional practices. Yet professional development research lacks a focus on how these assumptions are challenged through interaction. This study addresses that gap by examining how teacher-facilitator interactions navigate resistance to support possibility discourse—where assumptions are reexamined. Using micro-ethnographic analysis of sessions on controversial classroom issues, we identify two discursive devices: "manage and solve", which closes examination, and "proactive re-authoring", which opens space for rethinking.

## Introduction

The increasing complexity of contemporary life highlights the need for ambitious instruction—teaching practices that foster problem-solving skills. Such practices require teachers to engage in  complex learning processes that may create conflicts with their existing assumptions (Kolikant et al., 2020), which can bring about resistance. Professional development  can support teachers in developing their knowledge and examin ing assumptions (Lefstein et al., 2020). Yet research often overlooks how teacher-facilitator interactions  might leverage resistance as a learning resource  (Gitlin & Margonis, 1995), fostering shifts in discussion toward possibility discourse (Rainio & Hofmann, 2021). Through analysis of the discursive devices teachers use, (a lso termed “micro-linguistic tools”, Mueller & Whittle, 2011, p. 5), it may reveal teachers' self-view as agentic or unagentic regarding the difficulties experienced by s tudents. Teachers might stabilize limited views about students' difficulties, restabilize them by closing new opportunities, or destabilize them—articulating ruptures, and opening opportunities for possibility discourse (Rainio & Hofmann, 2021). This study investigates how teacher-facilitator interaction can support possibility discourse by identifying opportunities that support or hinder this process. We propose that successful  teacher-facilitator interaction in professional development with a focus on ambitious instruction includes navigating toward possibility discourse.  We view  facilitation as an interactive activity where facilitators not merely design curriculum, but also support teachers to expand their ways of thinking and acting in unfamiliar areas. Teacher-facilitator interactions bring diverse perspectives that encourage teachers to question assumptions and engage with resistance as a constructive part of the learning process.

## Research design

This study draws from two professional development programs (August 2024 –June 2025), involving 50 Israeli teachers and four experienced facilitators in 18 sessions overall. We focus on two early sessions of one program, in which teachers planned lessons, and reflected on their classroom experiences related to controversial social and political issues—key to addressing teachers’ discomfort and exposing systemic challenges. Data include video and audio recordings of each thr ee-hour session, transcribed and analyzed using a micro-ethnographic approach to discourse analysis (Bloome et al., 2005). We first identified and examined issues that appeared particularly consequential—such as how participants navigated tensions between perspectives. We then located rupture opportunities, such as the neg otiation of tensions, and identified acts of resistance. Next, we compared these rupture opportunities across sessions to explore how they unfold in the interaction. Finally, we identified discursive devices by comparing data examples across sessions (Rainio & Hofmann, 2021).

## Findings: The use of discursive devices by teachers and facilitators

In Session 2, teachers engaged in a “playback” acti vity exploring students' and teachers’ perspectives  on controversial issues. Shira shared a classroom event: “I told [the students] it was hard for me to hear  a student's statement…and that I can understand where you’re coming from”. She acknowledged her discomfort, expressing understanding without revealing her stance on the issue. Next, facilitator Bill proposed a “toolkit” of pred efined strategies to manage such emotional situations, perhaps aiming  to offer structure in moments of teacher uncertainty. Teacher Hadassa openly resisted this approach, arguing that prepackaged solutions c annot capture the “trenches” of classroom life, which demand ethi cal considerations. She called for a values-driven approach, emphasizing how public education should “dare to say something meaningful about the world”. Hadassa’s critique surfaced a tension: Should teachers acknowledge emotion while withholdi ng their stance, or engage more directly with the ethical and political dimensions of controversial issues ? This framing challenged systemic assumptions and advocated great er decision-making latitude. Bill’s response, while acknowledging concerns, focused on prepackaged tools—what we term to manage and solve—which restabilized the discussion rather than leverag ing the resistance to engage ethical complexity. Hadassa’s stance —proactive re-authoring—redirected the group's focus to the broader purpose of teaching, an opportunity for destabilization. Another teacher, June, commented to Hadassa “I avoid engaging with certain issues—it feels dangerous or I don’t know how to respond…I really need tools for dealing with that”, reflecting teachers’ uncertainty about navigating politically sensitive contexts. June recognized the need for concrete tools and sought m ethods that addressed complexity. Hadassa’s resistance had opened a space for collective reflection on the teacher’s ethical role. However, a second facilitator, Nir, shifted focus  by prioritizing session objectives, missing an opportu nity to deepen the collective examination of how addressing such tensions might challenge existing assumptions.

## Discussion and implications

Our analysis shows how micro-analysis of language use reveals  “the smallest openings…create explicit spaces” (Rainio & Hofmann, 2021, p. 742) within the subtext that can strengthen teachers professionally and support their active engagement with change. Missed opportunities to address resistance risk lea ving current assumptions unexamined. Future research should explore how these strategies can navigate interactions toward (or away from) possibility discourse, leading to more deliberate ways to support teachers.
