## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Natural phenomena and scientific issues are intrinsically interdisciplinary. A variety of academic disciplines of natural science are essential for students to explain a phenomenon or related issues along with their reasonings for the phenomenon. The primary goal of the current study is to examine how students’ interdisciplinary understanding of carbon cycling manifests itself using the latent Dirichlet allocation model. This study provides important insight into the potential impact on diverse curricular and instructional approaches in science.

## Introduction

Natural phenomena and scientific issues are intrins ically interdisciplinary (ID). ID understanding in a variety of disciplines of natural science is essent ial for students to explain a phenomenon or related social scientific issues. The current science educa tion system focuses mainly on a discipline-based curriculum, but recent national documents and standards for science education in many countries have demonstrated the value and essentiality of ID approaches to learning. Despite a growing emphasis on ID learning and teaching in K–16 education, few researchers have identified the ID connections within student science knowledge. This study involves stud ents’ answers to constructed-response (CR) questions in the Interdisciplinary Science Assessment for Carbon Cycling (ISACC, You et al., 2021) to interpret what core science concepts and corresponding disciplines are evident in their answers using topic modeling. We examined the relationship betwee n the proportions of science disciplines and students’ scores. The guiding research questions for this project were as follows:

a) How is students’ ID knowledge about carbon cycling represented in their written answers?

b) How is the relationship between the proportion o f the ID represented in the students’ answers and

scores?

## Theoretical Framework

Interdisciplinary understanding ID understanding has been defined as “the capacity to integrate knowledge in two or more disciplines to produce a cognitive advancement in ways that would have been impossible or unlikely through single disciplinary means” (Boix Mansilla & Duraisingh, 20 07, p. 219). You et al. (2021) argued that the combination of knowledge from different individual disciplines could be a prerequisite for demonstrating ID understanding.

Topic modeling Interest in the use of topic modeling techniques to discover abstract “topics” in the collection of documents has increased (Nagwani, 2015). The students’ answers to the CR items provide an example of the texts educational scholars are interested in. The most popular model of the topic modeling technique is latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA; Blei et al., 2003). It is commonly used to discover the scope of topics and the underlying patterns contained in text documents.

## Participants and ISACC

The ISACC consists of 454 students’ responses to 19 items, including 11 multiple-choice (MC) items and eight CR items, collected at a public high school and a research university in Texas (You et al., 2018, 2021). From eight CR items, we excluded three items based on a single discipline’s knowledge and the lack of information to extract topic structures. Thus, we analyzed students’ written answers for five CR items (i.e., Item 8, Item 12, Item 14, Item 15, Item 19) in the ISACC.

## Data analysis

The first step of the topic modeling is data cleaning, which eliminates the meaningless texts, “don’t know” texts, and empty texts. In the second step, we preprocessed the data through a) tokenization (i.e., breaking the sentence into multiple words), (b) lemmatization (i.e., converting the words into their ICLS2022 Proceedings 1832 © ISLS word stems), and c) stop-word removal. We extracted topic structures using the LDA model and then fitted a regression model to reveal the relationship between topics and students’ scores.

## Discussion

ID understanding is a key desired outcome in science education. The analysis of the CR items to measure ID understanding revealed how the students integrated their disciplinary knowledge and to what extent disciplinary knowledge was used to build ID knowledge. The significance of this study lies in its potential ability to guide how science could be taught to foster ID knowledge that would explain a natural phenomenon. A metaperspective on multiple scientific disciplines and sufficient knowledge could eventually lead to successful ID teaching, even though the science courses would remain as separate disciplines. The results of the study have encouraged additional development of ID-focused curricula.
