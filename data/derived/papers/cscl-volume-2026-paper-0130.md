# Hierarchical Multi-Agent System for Instructional Design in Music Knowledge Building

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study presents a hierarchical multi-agent system (MAS) to support music teachers in designing Knowledge Building (KB) -oriented lessons. The system integrates four expert agents (KB, musician, music pedagogy and music psychology) to generate holistic instructional design that contain embodied and creative music activities. A preliminary qualitative interview with five primary school teachers shows positive feedback of creativity and applicability. Further work will validate the lesson plan quality, compare with single-agent systems, and examine classroom impact through longitudinal studies. Knowledge Building (KB) in music education Constructivist in music education emphasises active participation and bodily engagement through authentic, social, and reflective experiences (Shively, 2015). M usic education also values culture, affective emotions, and embodied experience (Garnett, 2013; Vander Schyff et al., 2018). Compared to traditional constructivist education, Knowledge Building (KB) focuses on the continuous and dynamic improvement of ideas through collective learning (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2010). KB pedagogy encourages deeper learning through artifact production, such as composition, improvisation, and performance in music (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2010). Collaborative learning, however, has also been applied widely in music teaching and practice (Forbes, 2020), suggesting a potential link between music education and KB pedagogy. Despite th ese alignments, the direct application of KB pedagogy in music education remains limited. This gap indicates the need for pedagogical and technological innovation to conduct KB principles with creative and affective music learning. Current issue and exploration in KB music lessons A primary school in China implementing KB across subjects provide the context for this exploratory study. Five music teachers (4 female, 1 male; 2-20 years of teaching experience; Grades 2, 4, 5 & 6) joined a semi-structured interview addressing theoretical and practical challenges in applying KB and AI tools in music lessons. After thematic analysis, teachers reported difficulty aligning open-ended KB inquiries with curriculum standards (Tan et al., 2021). Some confuse the fixed-group discussion with the collaborative knowledge-sharing groups. Teachers also ask for strategies that connect deeper music understanding and psychological knowledge in pedagogy. Interdisciplinary skills are needed in KB pedagogy, leading to the use of AI to solve the complex problems in teaching. Existing AI agents often generate too advanced activities for the targeted students’ age. For instance, suggested singing tasks exceeded student’s vocal abilities, and some embodied activities were impractical with the large students amount in Chinese schools. Teachers further requested clearer inquiry prompts and adaptable activities that support creativity and collaboration. These challenges and requirements highlight the need for greater control through a more flexible AI system that better integrates music practice with KB pedagogy. Holistic perspective in music teaching and tool use Teachers prefer an AI system that perform as co-creators, generating synthesised ideas rather than giving concepts and decision. As a complex system, MAS offers such potential by emerge new and unreducible feature through the interaction between individual agents beyond their initial attr ibute (Chen et al., 2024; Waldrop, 1993). Compared to Large Language Model (LLM) and single agent, MAS can not only reduce produce less hallucination (Huang et al., 2025), but also support complex problems, provide collective intelligence, iterative feedback and flexible subtask management (Li et al., 2024). Accordingly, four experts were designed to reflect the complex and interdisciplinary nature in effective teaching (Mishra & Koehler, 2006), integrating perspectives from music theories, psychology, classroom dynamics, and ethical considerations of AI (Celik, 2023). In detail, KB expert ensures collaborative knowledge creation, while the music educator focuses on music pedagogy and curriculum alignment. The musician contributes to expertise in music theory, history, culture, and performances. Finally, the music psychologist deals with motivational, emotional, and cognitive developmental issues. The hierarchical Multi-Agent System (MAS) 710 The hierarchical structure of the Multi-Agent System (MAS) enables modular design and iterative refinement across domains (Talebirad & Nadiri, 2023; Tran et al., 2025). This design aims to generate instructional design (ID) that integrate KB principles with practical music pedagogy, addressing both cognitive and affective aspects, and supports the sophisticated knowledge and teacher control. A supervisor agent manages the overall system goal (𝒪𝒪𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐), assigns individual goals (𝒪𝒪𝑖𝑖) to sub-agents through Standardised Operating Procedures (SOPs). Teachers input teaching objectives or prepared ID through a chat interface. The supervisor agent synthesises a new ID according to the other agents (see Figure 1). Users can then entre requirements to iteratively refine outputs. The knowledge base contains KB lesson templates, which were validated and adapted by KB experts and music teachers, inquiry prompts, and activity examples supported by musical and psychological literature. Curriculum standards and textbooks ensure practical feasibility. Figure 1 The Hierarchical MAS for the Generation of Music KB Lesson Plan Feedback and future plan System performance was evaluated using Multiagentbench (Zhu et al., 2025). Participants included the 5 music teachers from the previous interview. In a group interview, participants were show about how to input and revise ideas. Three instructional design s about singing and music appreciation for grade s 2, 4 and 5 were generated discussed in terms of KB alignment, pedagogical coherence, applicability, developmental appropriateness. The ID contains KB principles such as collective idea improvement, co-developed artifacts and suggest for student wellbeing. Overall, the new MAS was viewed positively. Teachers found the activities “very creative compared to simple singing activities” and applicable for grade 5 students to move, listen and feel the music (Teacher 2, group interview, September 2025). They also valued the integration of psychological perspectives which support student motivation and engagement. Different from simply a full lesson plan provided by another MAS developed by Xin et al. (2025), this hierarchical MAS can more adequately capture the affective dimensions. However, teachers requested clearer output structures: “The content and activities were basically feasible, but can you make the output clearer—follow the template we always use?” (Teachers 3, group interview, September 2025). Specifically, a novice teacher asked, “I want more inquiries in the lesson plan, better to cover every sentence I need to say in class” (Teacher 5, group interview, Septermber 2025). Experienced teachers agreed this value, especially for preparing demonstration lessons (Teachers 2 and 4, group interview, September 2025). The development of this system and verification of teaching effectiveness have not been complete. More lesson plans revised by experts will be added to the knowledge base to improve comprehensiveness. A validated evaluation scale based on the ADDIE model (Branch, 2009) will be created for experts in KB and music psychologist to scoring. The usability, teacher satisfaction, and learning outcomes will be examined through longitudinal studies. Technical improvement will address reaction speed, stream output, and interface design. Conclusion This study presents a preliminary design of a complex hierarchical MAS for KB informed music instructional design. By integrating KB theory, music pedagogy and psychology, the system offers a more interactive and flexible approach which supports creative, collaborative, and student-centred learning. While further validation is required, findings suggest that MAS can enhance ID while teachers are encouraged to adapt AI-generating lesson plans to actual classroom and cultural contexts to maintain subjectivity (Hesse & Helm, 2025; Pikhart & Al-Obaydi, 2025). The system integrates four expert agents  (KB, musician, music pedagogy and music psychology) to generate holistic instructional design  that contain  embodied and creative music activities. Knowledge Building (KB) in music education Constructivist in music education emphasises  active participation and bodily engagement through authentic, social, and reflective experiences (Shively, 2015). M usic education also values culture, affective emotions, and embodied experience (Garnett, 2013; Vander Schyff et al., 2018). Compared to traditional constructivist education, Knowledge Building (KB) focuses on the continuous and dynamic improvement of ideas through collective learning (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2010). KB pedagogy encourages deeper learning through artifact production, such as composition, improvisation, and performance in music (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2010). Collaborative learning, however, has also been applied widely in music teaching and practice (Forbes, 2020), suggesting a potential link between music education and KB pedagogy. Despite th ese alignments, the direct application of KB pedagogy in music education remains limited. This gap indicates the need for pedagogical and technological innovation to conduct KB principles with creative and affective music learning. Current issue and exploration in KB music lessons A primary school in China implementing KB across subjects provide the context for this exploratory study.  Five music teachers (4 female, 1 male; 2-20 years of teaching experience; Grades 2, 4, 5 & 6) joined a semi-structured interview addressing theoretical and practical challenges in applying KB and AI tools  in music lessons. After thematic analysis, teachers reported difficulty  aligning open-ended KB inquiries with curriculum standards (Tan et al., 2021). Some confuse the fixed-group discussion with the collaborative knowledge-sharing groups. Teachers also ask for strategies that connect deeper music understanding and psychological knowledge in pedagogy. Interdisciplinary skills are needed in KB pedagogy, leading to the use of AI to solve the complex problems in teaching. Existing AI agents often generate too advanced activities for the targeted students’ age. For instance, suggested singing tasks exceeded student’s vocal abilities, and some embodied activities were impractical with the large students amount in Chinese schools. Teachers further requested clearer inquiry prompts and adaptable activities that support creativity and collaboration. These challenges and requirements highlight the need for greater control through a more flexible AI system that better integrates music practice with KB pedagogy. Holistic perspective in music teaching and tool use Teachers prefer an AI system that perform as co-creators, generating synthesised ideas rather than giving concepts and decision. As a complex system, MAS  offers such potential by emerge new and unreducible  feature through the interaction between individual agents beyond their initial attr ibute (Chen et al., 2024; Waldrop, 1993). Compared to Large Language Model (LLM) and single agent, MAS can not only reduce produce less hallucination (Huang et al., 2025), but also support complex problems, provide collective intelligence, iterative feedback and flexible subtask management (Li et al., 2024). Accordingly, four experts were designed to reflect the complex and interdisciplinary nature in effective teaching (Mishra & Koehler, 2006), integrating perspectives from  music theories, psychology, classroom dynamics, and ethical considerations of AI (Celik, 2023). In detail, KB expert ensures collaborative knowledge creation, while the music educator focuses on music pedagogy and curriculum alignment. The musician contributes to expertise in music theory, history, culture, and performances. Finally, the music psychologist deals with motivational, emotional, and cognitive developmental issues. The hierarchical Multi-Agent System (MAS) 710 The hierarchical structure of the Multi-Agent System (MAS) enables modular design and iterative refinement across domains (Talebirad & Nadiri, 2023; Tran et al., 2025).  This design aims to generate instructional design (ID) that integrate KB principles with practical music pedagogy, addressing both cognitive and affective aspects, and supports the sophisticated knowledge and teacher control. A supervisor agent manages the overall system goal (𝒪𝒪𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐𝑐), assigns individual goals (𝒪𝒪𝑖𝑖) to sub-agents through Standardised Operating Procedures (SOPs). Teachers input teaching objectives or prepared ID through a chat interface. The supervisor agent synthesises a new ID according to the other agents (see Figure 1). Users can then entre requirements to iteratively refine outputs. The knowledge base contains KB lesson templates, which were validated  and adapted by KB experts and music teachers, inquiry prompts, and activity examples supported by musical and psychological literature. Curriculum standards and textbooks ensure practical feasibility.
