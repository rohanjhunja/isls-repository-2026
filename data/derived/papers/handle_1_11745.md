# Teacher SEL Matters: How Interventions Shape Teachers’ Well-Being and Professional Growth

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This systematic review analyzed 15 empirical studies on Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) interventions for teachers. Results indicate a focus on emotional regulation, stress management, and resilience for in-service teachers, while pre-service programs emphasize emotional intelligence, interpersonal skills, and decision-making. Mindfulness-based programs are most common. In-service teachers benefit from classroom strategies, whereas pre-service teachers gain foundational competencies. Gaps in cultural awareness and theoretical grounding suggest the need for more inclusive and theory informed SEL program design.

## Introduction

SEL is defined as the process of developing compete ncies in emotional regulation, relationship-building, and responsible decision-making (CASEL, 2023). While SEL research often focuses on K-12 students, a growing body of evidence suggests that teachers' own SEL co mpetencies are critical to their well-being and professional effectiveness (Jennings & Greenberg, 2009; Gu et al., 2022; Lang et al., 2020). However, limited research addresses the specific SEL needs of teachers, and few studies differentiated betw een pre-service and in-service teachers. Pre-service teachers face identity formation and classr oom readiness challenges, whereas in-service teachers often deal with high stress and teaching a nxiety, especially in an AI-driven environment (Hopcan et al, 2024). This review addresses the following research questi ons: (1) What are the core components of SEL programs for teachers? (2) What theoretical frameworks support these programs? (3) What types of interventions are used? (4) What are the impacts on teacher well-being and professional development?

## Method

This review followed the PRISMA guidelines (Moher et al. 2009). Searches were conducted in ProQuest and ERIC databases using terms such as "social-emotional learning," "teacher education," and "intervention." Inclusion criteria: (a) published between 2014-2024; (b) peer-reviewed English-language articles; (c) participants were pre-or in-service teachers; (d) focused on SEL interventions.  A total of 15 studies were included. Two trained reviewers independently screened and coded the studies. The coding process combined inductive qualitative coding and quantitative frequency analysis to extract patterns in components, theories, and impacts.

Among the 15 studies reviewed, 9 focused on in-service teachers, 5 on pre-service teachers, and 1 on both groups. While emotional regulation, stress management, and resilience are more frequently targeted in in-service teacher programs, pre-service programs emphasize interpersonal skills, decision-making, and emotional intelligence. Cultural awareness and self-reflection are less frequently addressed in both gr oups (Figure 1a). Figure 1b shows that 40% of studies did not specify any theoretical foun dation. Among those that did, the Prosocial Classro om Model and the CASEL framework were the most commonl y used. Other frameworks such as Situated Learning Theory, Social Cognitive Theory, and Critical Race Theory appeared infrequently.  A mong the interventions, mindfulness-based experiential programs dominate (47%), followed by in-person training (27%), while informal support networks and online/blended courses are less common, each representing 13% of interventions. ## Discussion

This review provides evidence that SEL interventions can significantly support teachers' personal and professional development. While in-service teachers benefit from stress-and resilience-focused programs, pre-service teachers gain foundational competencies. The dominance of mindfulness-based approaches shows promise, but the lack of cultural responsiveness and clear theoretical framing limits program depth.
