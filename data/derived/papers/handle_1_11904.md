# Enhancing Interdisciplinary Perspectives and Professional Agency in STEAM Teacher Communities through Knowledge-Building Collaborative Discourse

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores the impact of collaborative discourse in STEAM teacher communities on interdisciplinary perspectives and professional agency. Using a mixed-methods approach, 69 teachers participated in discussions on the Knowledge Forum. Discourse data and quantitative analysis were collected through pre-and post-tests. The results indicated (1) collaborative discourse evolved from sharing to argumentative and innovative modes. (2) Knowledge-Building collaboration enhanced interdisciplinary perspectives, especially in integrated STEAM practices. (3) professional agency improved, particularly in positive interdependence.

## Introduction

Current global challenges necessitate interdiscipli nary integration and technological applications to address complex problems (Casado & Checa, 2020). Against th is backdrop, STEAM education is widely recognized as an effective model that facilitates learners' understanding of integrated practices across disciplines, enabling them to manage diverse topics, internalize knowledge, and apply learning outcomes to real-life situations (Mejias et al., 2021). However, studies have shown that existing te acher training programs are insufficient to fully s upport the needs of STEAM education (Zhang & Zhu, 2023), making it imperative to advance STEAM teachers' professional development to translate acquired knowledge into classroom practices. The goal of STEAM education is to cultivate high-level technological talent, thereby driving social and scientific progress (Kennedy & Odell, 2014). According to Kim and Kim (2016), STEAM teachers need not only subject-specific knowledge and higher-order thinking skills but also community communication abilities and the capacity to contribute to the community. Additionally, Ebby et al. (2024) found that collaborative discourse within teacher learning communities not only enhance teach ers' agency but also promote mutual learning and pr actical engagement. These perspectives highlight the importance of studying how STEAM teachers exhibit collaborative discourse, interdisciplinary thinking, and professi onal agency within communities. However, related st udies in the field of learning science remain limited.

This study aims to investigate the critical role of collaborative discourse in STEAM teacher training. To deeply analyze the process of collective knowledge work, the research adopts Scardamalia and Bereiter's concept of "knowledge building," viewing knowledge creation  as a social process of continuously generating and improving valuable ideas (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2 006). The Knowledge Forum (KF), a computer supporti ve learning environment and designed based on knowledg e building principle, is employed as an environment  for collaborative discourse, aiming to visualize divers e perspectives and assign responsibility to the com munity for advancing knowledge (Bereiter & Scardamalia, 20 21), thereby enhancing teachers' confidence toward ST EAM lesson design and practice.

The study addresses two core questions: (1) How do teacher communities develop their discourse moves, interdisciplinary perspectives, and agency in STEAM  lesson design in Knowledge Building environment? (2) How does collaborative discourse during the process  of STEAM lesson design promote the transformation of interdisciplinary perspectives and the enhancement of professional agency?

## Method

The participants were 69 graduate students (N=69) f rom the Master’s Program in Curriculum and Instruct ion, primarily in-service teachers from elementary to high school, ag ed 22 to 47 years.  This study employs a mixed-methods approach, incorporating theoretical learnin g, reflection, community collaboration and discussi on to conduct STEAM lesson design, aiming to explore the transformation of teachers' interdisciplinary persp ectives and professional agency within a knowledge-building environment. The research  utilized two key principles of knowledge building: "community knowledge, collectiv e responsibility" and "knowledge building discourse " as the core framework for pedagogical design.

The pedagogical design process includes the followi ng stages: First, teachers share their prior experi ences and understanding related to STEAM education and ag ency. Second, through reading and information shari ng, they delve into the theories and research related to Knowledge Building and STEAM. Next, teac hers engage in STEAM lesson design and participate in discourse wi thin the community, exploring various design possib ilities, gathering peer feedback, and refining their designs. Finally, after a semester of Knowledge Building activities, teachers reflect on their understanding of STEAM education and agency. Throughout the process, the Knowledge Forum serves as the learning platform, where teache rs autonomously read, share, and integrate ideas, w hile continuously engaging in reflection and collective participation in the knowledge-building process. The research data of discourse were notes, collecte d from teachers creating on the Knowledge Forum. Discourse were categorized into three types —sharing, argumentative, and innovative —based on the literature, with each note and response (e.g., A creats a no te, B responds to A's idea) considered a discourse round, serving as the unit of analysis. Two researchers collaboratively analyzed the discourse and determined a Kappa reliability of 0.82. Descriptive statistics and paired t-tests were used to examine the evolution of the discourse. The measurement of STEAM interdisciplinary perspectives was conducted using the scale developed by Lin et al. (2022), which showed good fit indices (χ²/df = 1.03, RMSE A = 0.009, SRMR = 0.04, CFI = 0.99, TLI = 0.99). Teachers' professional agency was assessed using the scale by Pyhältö et al. (2015), demonstrating good construct validity with fit indices: χ² (non-significant), CFI/TLI >.95, RMSEA <.06, and SRMR <.08. Pre-and post-training surveys were administered to assess the development of teachers' interdisciplinary perspectives and professional agency. To evaluate the impact of collaborative discourse on STEAM interdisciplinary perspectives and professional agency, SmartPLS software was used  to conduct structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM), performing reliability and validity tests, as well as path coefficient analysis.

## Result

First, in the development of discourse, t-test analysis revealed that initially, sharing disc ourse accounted for the highest proportion at 58%, followed by argumentative discourse at 23%, and innovative discourse at 19%. In the later stages, the proportion of innovative discourse increased to 47%. The t-test results indicated that although the proportion of sharing discourse decreased in later stage, it did not reach a significant level; the nu mber of argumentative discourse increased significantly from 59 to 121 (t = -2.757, p < 0.01); and the number of innovative discourse grew significantly from 47 to 220 (t = -4.847, p < 0.001). Second, the analysis of STEAM interdisciplinary per spectives showed significant growth across six dimensions: teaching effectiveness, interdisciplina ry communication, engagement, cognitive fluency, technological literacy, and integrated STEAM. The m ost notable change occurred in integrated STEAM (t = - 9.066, p < 0.001), indicating significant progress in teachers' interdis ciplinary perspectives after a semester of community collaboration within a knowledge-building environment. Third, professional agency analysis revealed signif icant improvements across six dimensions: collaborative decision-making, positive interdependence, collective effica cy, proactive help-seeking, self-regulation, and co-regulation. The most prominent improvement was in positive interdependence (t = -7.458, p < 0.001), demonstrating that teachers increasingly vi ewed learning as a shared resource relationship wit hin the community, rather than an individual endeavor, thus promoting the development of professional agency. Finally, the analysis of the impact of Knowledge Building discourse on interdisciplinary perspectives and professional agency was conducted using PLS to explore potential relationships between latent variables. The reliability and validity tests indicated good inter nal consistency and discriminant validit y among the constructs. Path coefficient analysis in the PLS structural mod el showed that the discourse's effect on interdisci plinary perspectives had a path coefficient of 0.392, with a t-value of 3.734 (p  < 0.001), indicating a highly significant positive effect. Although the discourse's effect on professional agency had a path coefficient of 0.307 and was not statistically significant, the positive coefficient suggests a favorable impact. Based on the research findings, four conclusions ca n be drawn: First, discourse evolved from inquiry-based to innovative modes. Second, the transformati on of interdisciplinary perspectives emerged from t he collective cognitive responsibility. Third, there was significant enhancement in professional agency, especially in positive interdependence. Fourth, collaborative dis course, particularly innovative ones, had a positiv e impact on interdisciplinary perspectives. These results suggest that knowledge-building discourse supports teachers' professional development, with future efforts focusing on enhancing participation and exploring long-term effects on professional agency within communities.
