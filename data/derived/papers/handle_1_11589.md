# Challenges of Integrating ChatGPT into EFL Writing: An Activity Theory Framework

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores EFL students' challenges with ChatGPT in English writing tasks through Activity Theory analysis. Thirteen university students completed two writing tasks and interviews. Findings reveal primary contradictions (need for additional tools, ChatGPT version inconsistencies); secondary contradictions (feedback discrepancies, low proficiency limiting feedback utilization, trust issues, ethical concerns, ChatGPT dependence, culturally insensitive feedback, and limitations compared to human feedback); and a quaternary contradiction regarding structured AI tool education. Implications include balancing AI use with independent practice, establishing educational frameworks for AI tools, and maintaining human instruction alongside AI implementation.

## Introduction

As AI tools like ChatGPT become increasingly integr ated into language education, understanding student s' experiences with these tools is essential. For EFL learners, academic writing requires linguistic prof iciency alongside rhetorical and critical thinkin g skills (Hyland, 2009). However, traditional instr uction often lacks personalized feedback and immediate support. ChatGPT offers potential assistance throughout the writing process through interactive feedback (Su et al., 2023). Yet, its integration raises concerns about trust, ethics, over-reliance, and discrepancies between AI-generated output and students' actual abilities (Im, 2023; Bok & Cho, 2023). The pedagogical implications of these concerns remain u nderexplored, particularly in non-Western educat ional contexts where cultural and institutional factors m ay influence AI adoption. Furthermore, the rapid ev olution of AI capabilities necessitates continuous examination  of how these tools reshape writing instruction and  student development. Existing research often overlooks stud ents' lived experiences and structural tensions tha t arise during tool use. To address this gap, this study ad opts Activity Theory (Engeström, 1987) to examine s ocial, cultural, and tool-mediated dimensions of EFL writing with AI. By anal yzing contradictions within the activity system, we explore how technology integration impac ts writing development and classroom practices. Thi s theoretical framework allows for a nuanced understa nding of the complex interplay between technologica l affordances and educational objectives in contempor ary writing instruction. Our research question is: What contradictions do EFL students experience when using ChatGPT in academic writing tasks?

## Methods

Thirteen undergraduate students from a South Korean  university, with varied majors and English profici ency levels, participated in the study. Following a background questionnaire, participants completed two argumentative writing tasks. The first allowed fr ee use of tools, including ChatGPT and other resour ces. The second limited them to ChatGPT alone and required at least five in teractions with the tool. After the writing tasks, participants joined one-on-one interviews in Korean, reflecting on their exper iences, challenges with feedback, tool limitations, and ethical concerns. Data sources inc luded questionnaire responses, writing samples, Cha tGPT interaction logs, and interview transcripts. We analyzed the data using Engeström's (1987) activity system model, identifying contradictions across elements such as tools, rules, subjects, and outcomes. Coding was conducted in multiple rounds by three researchers, with audit tr ail for reliability. Activity Theory's concept of p rimary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary contradictions guided the categorization of emerging tensions.

## Results

Figure 1 illustrates the contradictions within the activity system. The student (subject) engages in English essay writing (activity) with the aim of improving writin g skills (object), resulting in the production of a n essay (outcome). This activity is mediated by tools such as ChatGPT and translation aids (tools), and is sha ped by university regulations and ethical guidelines (rules)  within the broader academic community (community).  The division of labor involves shared contributions fro m both the student and ChatGPT, reflecting the coll aborative nature of human–AI interaction. The analysis revealed multiple levels of contradictions within this system. Figure 1 Contradictions within the Activity Systems of EFL Students Using ChatGPT Primary contradictions  (a) are tensions within a single element of an activity  system, involving ChatGPT's role as a writing tool. A key primary contradiction emerged in version-based inconsistencies: students noted that newer versions such as GPT-4o were significantly more accurate and responsive than older ones like GPT-3.5, leading to unequal user experiences.

Secondary contradictions (b), (c), (d), (e) and (f)  emerge between elements of an activity system and disrupt the intended process or outcome. A subject –tool contradiction (b) was prominent, as students reported misalignments between their writing needs and ChatG PT’s feedback—such as issues with formatting, rhetorical coherence, culturally irrelevant examples, difficul ty adopting suggestions due to low English proficie ncy, and lack of trust in the tool. A subject –rules contradiction (c) surfaced due to unclear guidelines on plagiarism and appropriate AI use. The subject –division of labor contradiction (d) involved students’ growing reliance on ChatGPT, which reduced active engag ement in writing. Tool –object tensions (e) were noted due to ChatGPT’s limitations as a writing tool, which hindered skill development, reduced self-efficacy, and led students to rely on additional tools for specific tasks. Lastly, tool –community contradictions (f) reflected students’ preference for human feedback, which they viewed as more personalized and pedagogically meaningful than ChatGPT’s generic responses.

Tertiary Contradictions  are tensions between different developmental stages  of the activity. In the current study, no tertiary contradictions were iden tified, suggesting that ChatGPT has not yet replace d existing writing practices to a degree that would produce systemic conflicts. A quaternary contradiction (g) emerged between the writing activity and the broade r academic system. Students described a lack of structured education o n how to use ChatGPT effectively and ethically.  They noted inconsistent policies across courses, unclear rules on acceptable use, and limited instruction on prompt crafting or responsible AI engagement. This mismatch between ra pid AI adoption and limited institutional support l ed to confusion and diminished the tool’s educational potential.

## Conclusion

This study explored how EFL learners integrate Chat GPT into their writing processes, revealing multipl e contradictions in Human-AI interaction. While ChatGPT improved writing flue ncy and structure, it also led to challenges—such as over-reliance, reduced self-efficacy, and unclear ethical boundaries—that hindered students' independent skill development. These contradictions —within the tool, between users and tools, and betwe en the activity and the broader system—highlight the complexity of AI integration in education. Although ChatGPT can enhance writing quality, it may impede growth without critical guidance (Im, 2023; Bok & Cho, 2023). Educators should embed AI tools within structured pedagogical frameworks that foster critical engagement and ethical use, including instruction on citation, plagiarism, and feedback evaluation (Rahman & Watanobe, 2023). The tool’s limitations also underscore the need for human feed back, particularly for culturally sensitive guidanc e (Barrot, 2023). This study's scope is limited by a small, ho mogeneous sample and a single writing genre. Future  research should address diverse learners, contexts, and long-term effects. Effective AI integration ultimately r equires thoughtful design, institutional support, and ongoing human involvement.
