# Developing an Approach for Evaluating Designs of Generative AI-Supported Learning

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
While literature observes a surge in Generative AI (GenAI)-supported learning designs, these designs are often evaluated narrowly based on learning outcomes, offering limited insight into how GenAI contributes to pedagogical designs and supports learning processes. This paper addresses this gap by propos ing a holistic evaluation framework that encompasses three interrelated interactions: (1) interactions between learning designers and GenAI in shaping design embodiment, (2) interactions between GenAI and learners in mediating learning processes, and (3) interactions facilitating Explainable AI, focusing on how learning designers and learners understand, validate and improve the responses of GenAI. Grounded in C onjecture Mapping (Sandoval, 2014), the proposed framework organizes the evaluation of these interactions to inform how learning designs lead to intended learning outcomes. The framework provides a systematic means for evaluating and improving GenAI– supported learning designs beyond learning outcomes alone.

Recent systematic reviews (Belkina et al., 2025; Law, 2024; Mittal et al., 2024)  reported a worldwide surge in research on Gen erative AI (GenAI)-supported learning across higher education and K –12 settings. Despite the critical alerts in literature on pitfalls, challenges and issues concerning the use of GenAI in education, critical examination of how GenAI shapes teaching and learning remains  lacking. Schulz-Schaeffer (2025) argues that GenAI is different from traditional education technologies regarding task-relatedness, user interaction and agency, and urges the field to have greater criticality in utiliz ing GenAI. Yet, most studies narrowly examined GenAIsupported teaching and learning in terms of teaching performance (Mulyani et al., 2025) and learning outcomes (Zhu et al., 2025). Few studies (Jurenka et al., 2024; Tang & Putra, 2025) delved into evaluating learning processes enabled by GenAI  and they are often specific (e.g., on dialogical learning) and context bonded (e.g., for STEM education).  A more systematic approach is therefore needed to examine how GenAI contributes to learning designs to harness the potential of GenAI in education. This paper addresses this gap by proposing an evaluation framework that is  adaptive and capable of accounting for the distinctive affordances of GenAI in assessing and supporting learning.

Conjecture Mapping as the evaluation framework To address this literature gap, this paper refers to Conjecture Mapping (Sandoval, 2014)  as a framework to improve the argumentative grammar for examining GenAI learning designs. We map three aspects of interrelated interactions related to GenAI to the conjecture map and foreground them for evaluation. Conjecture mapping is an approach proposed by Sandoval (2014)  for systematic education design research. It serves as a methodological framework to respond to the critics on Design-Based Research (DBR) for the lack of methodological clarity and rigor (Easterday et al., 2018; Kelly, 2004). According to Sandoval (2014), Conjecture Mapping operationally involves the articulation of two types of conjectures: (1) design conjectures, outlining how certain mediating processes in learning will occur given the designed learning environment, and (2) theoretical conjectures, linking the enacted mediating processes with the anticipated learning outcomes, to understand how the implemented learning experiences translate into actual learning and development. Articulating these conjectures facilitates the exploration and refinement of hypotheses or conjectures about how learning occurs.

A contribution of Conjecture Mapping is the systematic representation of design and theoretical conjectures in visual representation. The graphic portrayal of conjectures enhances transparency in illustrating relationships between the high-level conjecture on learning, learning designs, mediating processes, and learning outcomes, contributing to understanding how learning designs lead to specific outcomes.  The developed conjecture maps serve as a basis for examin ing and validating the proposed design and theoretical hypotheses. It also supports evaluation, feedback, and refinement of learning designs in iterations. Three aspects of GenAI-related interactions for evaluation 2554 In this section, we highlight three key aspects of GenAI-related interactions that should be explicitly articulated and evaluated. These interactions are situated within the conjecture map articulating how a high-level conjecture is embodied in the design of GenAI-supported learning, how the learning design leads to mediat ing processes (i.e., design conjectures), and how the mediating processes lead to learning outcomes (i.e., theoretical conjecture). Figure 1 Three Aspects of GenAI-Related Interactions for Evaluation The first aspect of interactions concerns the interactions between learning designers (including teachers and researchers as designers) and GenAI in embodying GenAI as a learning tool to enact the  intended pedagogy (i.e., high-level conjecture about learning). This embodiment is mainly achieved through prompt engineering and/or large language model fine-turning. This corresponds to the translation of high-level conjectures into design embodiments as materialized through GenAI as the overall design embodiment. Evaluation thus focuses on the extent to which the  intended pedagogy is embodied in GenAI as a learning tool, for example, the evaluation of prompting strategies based on goal fidelity (Ali et al., 2023). The second concerns  interactions between Gen AI and learners. Th is interaction  relates to the links between the embodied design and mediated learning processes in Sandoval’s generalized conjecture map. We highlight four sub-categories of interactions for evaluation.

• Sub-category 1: the extent to which the interactions between GenAI and learner actualize the intended

learning design, and how well it aligned with the intended pedagogy.

• Sub-category 2: the usability of the interface, examining the efficacy of H uman-Computer Interaction

(HCI) designs (Ho & Vuong, 2025) that facilitates learning processes.

• Sub-category 3: the quality of GenAI outputs. This includes the accuracy of disciplinary knowledge, the

quality of language and dialogue (such as the level of coherency  and depth of dialogue, the degree of natural communication, etc), and the detection of AI hallucination (e.g., AI bias).

• Sub-category 4: the quality of teacher-GenAI augmentation in supporting the processes of learning. This

sub-category concerns how teachers respond to GenAI’s limitations in learning processes. The third focuses on human-in-the-loop interactions that support Explainable AI (Bellas et al., 2025). These interactions allow users (e.g., learning designers, teachers and learners) to understand, validate and improve the responses of GenAI for ethical, inclusive and trustworthy use. Human-in-the-loop (Mosqueira-Rey et al.,

2023) is an essential design of human-centered AI systems and is unique to the use of AI tool  in learning. The

quality of the interaction s in this aspect is influenced by both the technical design that allows GenAI to provide explanations for human understanding and also the t eachers’ and learners’ competency, AI literacy, and agency to utilize and understand the explanations generated.

## Process tracing as an evaluation methodology

To evaluate the three aspects of GenAI-related interactions embedded in conjecture map s, we propose adopting process tracing (Bennett & Checkel, 2015) as an evaluation methodology. Process tracing is a qualitative research an observed outcome (e.g., learning outcome) within a specific case (Bennett & Checkel, 2015). It establishes the "how" and "why" of a causal relationship  by empirical tracing and examining the specific chains of sequence of activities and interactions in a hypothesized causal process (e.g., learning process). The three aspects of interrelated interactions can be evaluated using process tracing. The  causal relationships between learning design (cause) and the learning outcomes (outcome) are hypothesized in the form of design and theoretical conjectures  (learning process). Tracing and examining the chains of evidence on how and why the embodied learning design leads to mediated processes with observable interactions and artifacts (i.e., design conjectures) and how the mediated interactions lead to learning outcomes (theoretical conjectures) allows us to establish the conjecture map as the causal processes in learning.
