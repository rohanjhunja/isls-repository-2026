# Students’ Inter-brain Coupling in Real-World Classroom Reflects Learning Outcome in Middle School

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The classroom is the fundamental unit for the facilitation of learning. While existing research has highlighted the correlation relationship between students’ inter-brain coupling and learning performance within real classroom, there remains a gap in understanding how this varies among different disciplines and educational stages. This study employs a large dataset to calculate the correlation between student-student inter-brain coupling during middle school Chinese and Math classes and learning outcomes. The results reveals a significant positive correlation between student-student coupling and academic performance in both Chinese and Math among middle school students, with a notably stronger correlation in Math. This study validates the potential of student inter-brain coupling in exploring middle school classroom learning processes, revealing subject variations, and highlights the research's ecological validity and effectiveness.

## Introduction

The classroom is an essential setting for education, playing a critical role in students' learning and  attracting extensive attention in the fields of education, psychology, and neuroscience. A deep understanding of the dynamic process of classroom teachin g is crucial for improving student learning. Tradit ional research on classroom teaching primarily relies on students ’ self-reports or teachers ’ observations or third-party observations. These approaches are subjective and fail to capture the dynamic aspects of the learning process. To further comprehend the dynamics of "effective learning", the perspective of neuroscience is increasingly coming into focus (Varma et al., 2006).

The classroom serves as a collective learning environment, where the "interactions" among students and their impact on learning outcomes are significant t opics of discussion. Previous studies have shown th at neural synchronization between students, known a s inter-brain coupling, may be key to understanding the pro cess of collective learning (Reeve et al., 2004). Inter-brain coupling is regarded as an indicator of infor mation exchange and resonance, demonstrating similarity in cognitiv e processing among students engaged in identical ta sks (Nastase et al., 2019). It has been argued that similar brain activities among students may be indicative of mutual influence, shared attention, and shared understandi ng during the learning process (Dikker et al., 2017; Cohen et al., 2017; Ki et al., 2016). Research has explored inter-brain coupling in typical learning scenarios such as lectures, video viewing, group collaboration, and virtual rea lity, finding significant correlations between inte r-brain coupling and learning outcomes (Bevilacqua et al., 2019; Gumilar et al., 2021; Meshulam et al., 2021). However, most of these studies are limited to laboratory settings with low ecological validity (Matusz et al., 2019). And the small sample sizes also leaves the effectiveness and reliability of their results to be further verified. Additionally, most research subjects have been from single-classroom high school and university groups, with i nsufficient exploration of differences across subjects and educatio nal stages. Studies have shown that there are subje ctspecific differences in student interaction pattern s in high school Chinese and Math classes, making i t crucial to investigate the differences in brain coupling across various subjects (Chen et al., 2023). Therefore, this study conducted a large-scale data collection over three semesters in real Chinese and Math classes at a middle school, using wearable EEG  devices to explore the relationship between classr oom student-class brain coupling and academic perfo rmance. The inter-brain coupling values in different frequency bands were calculated for students of various educa tional stages and explore the correlation with lear ning outcomes. Our aim is to reveal the potential of bra in coupling in predicting learning efficacy and to explore how this relationship varies across different education al stages. Our study hope to provide more neurophys iological evidence for the dynamic process of student learning in classrooms, emphasizing the high ecological validity and effectiveness of the research and addressing the exist ing gap in discussions on subject-specific and educational stage differences.

## Methods

Paticipants Forty-three junior high school students from two separate classes in Beijing participated in this st udy. These participants were instructed to wear electroencephalogram (EEG) headring during their regular Chinese and math classes in real classroom settings over three semesters (including the first and second years of junior high school), as shown in Figure 1. However, due to the EEG data not meeting the quality requirements, only the data from 41 students were retained, and these students were inv olved in both the Chinese and math classes.  A total of 40 classroom sessions of student data were collected. E EG data were acquired using portable two-channel wearable headsets manufactured by Brainno company. Additiona lly, midterm and final exam scores of the students were recorded and provided by the school.

## Data analysis

This study conducted a five-step preprocessing of the EEG data, which included detrending, artifact marking, removal of artifacts and eye movement, and segmenting each individual's 40-minute EEG data from each class session into 80 EEG segments, each lasting 30 secon ds. Finally, EEG segments with peak amplitudes exce eding a predefined threshold were discarded (Xu et al., 2022). After preprocessing,  the total interdependence val ue (TI) of each class were calculated for student-student inter-brain coupling. This method is a coupling algorithm  based on signal frequency domain coherence, which has demonstrated superior performance compare d to other coupling algorithms in preveious studies  and various contexts, including classrooms, music conce rts and  mixed reality environments (Chabin et al.,  2022; Chen et al., 2023; Dikker et al., 2017). The TI va lues for all pairwise combinations  of students in each class session were calculated, in the frequency bands of wide band (1-40Hz), delta (1-4 Hz), theta (4-8 Hz), alpha (813 Hz), low beta (13-18 Hz), high beta (18-30 Hz), and gamma (30-40 Hz) frequency bands (Teplan, 2002). The student-class coupling value for student i was computed by averaging their TI values with all other students. For the performance data, the students' midterm and  final exam scores were first ranked in ascending order and then mapped them to a range of 0 to 1. Th e standardized scores represent the learning outcom es of the students, with higher values indicating better performance. Then, the average student-class TI value for each student was computed for each half-semester, and each average inter-brain coupling data corresponded to a performance s core. Furthermore, Pearson's correlations separately for the student-class inter-brain coupling values and their respective correlations with student academic performance were calculated for Chinese and mathematics classes.

## Discussion

This study investigates the correlation between stu dent-class inter-brain coupling values and learning outcomes in real Chinese and Math classes at the junior high school level. The results indicate a strong correlation between student-class inter-brain co upling and mathematical learning outcomes across al l frequency bands, while for Chinese classes, significant relationships are observed only in the alpha and highbeta frequency bands. The results further validates the significant value of inter-brain coupling in predicting classroom student lear ning outcomes and extends the discussion to encompass different subjects. Our study amplifies the predictive capacity of student-class inter-brain coupling in mathematics learning. Extending beyond previous work (Chen et al., 2023), our research explores various frequency bands, uncovering noteworthy results across these spectru ms, and first identifies a positive correlation bet ween student-class couplingand Chinese learning outcomes in the alpha and highbeta bands. This could be due to our study' s larger sample size, highlighting complex cognitive processes like attention, memory, and cognitive control in real-world classroom learning across diverse brain regions. The alpha band, mainly linked with attention, episodic memory, semantic memory, thinking, and perceptual learning,  may play a crucial role in the context of Chinese classes (Bays et al., 2015; Klimesch et al., 1998; Williams  et al., 2019). The beta band, as the predominant f requency in EEG signals during open-eye periods, is primarily associated with actively processing information, engaged attention and cognitive processes (Segalowitz et al., 2010; Engel & Fries, 2010; Teplan, 2002). The di scovery of the beta frequency band may be related to its incre ased activity during open-eye periods and its involvement in other complex cognitive processes.

The difference between junior and senior high school may be attributed to variations in classroom content, teaching styles, as well as inherent disparities in  brain neurodevelopment at different ages. From ear ly to late adolescence, there is a continuous d ecline in the total power of the EEG, especially in  the slow wave frequency band (Lüchinger et al., 2011). Additionally, slow w ave power is replaced by faster activities during t his period. Therefore, compared to high school students, middle  school student s exhibit more active EEG activity, particularly in slow wave activities such as highbeta and gamma bands. As a result, inter-brain coupling activities among middle school students may be more pronounced. This explains the significant findings across all frequency bands in middle school math classes. Additionally, significant results were observed in Chinese classes not only in the alpha frequency band but also in th e highbeta frequency band, which may be attributed to the increased activity of slow waves in middle school students.

In conclusion, our study utilized wearable EEG devi ces to longitudinally track students' EEG signals i n real classrooms, investigating subjects differences  in brain activity during real classroom learning. The results revealed the significant potential of student-class inter-brain coupling in assessing the learning process of middle school students in both Chinese and Math classes, e nhancing our understanding of the neurophysiologica l perspective of classroom learning processes.
