# Digital Literacy in Physical Education: A Systematic Review of Frameworks and Development Areas

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This review examines digital literacy in physical education (PE), an underexplored area despite increasing technological integration. We address three key questions: (1) What theoretical frameworks are used? (2) How is digital literacy reported in PE? (3) What areas need further development? A systematic review of 12 studies shows digital literacy in PE is mainly framed through digital literacy and digital competence models. While overall levels were moderate to high, disparities emerged by gender, grade level, and experience. Key barriers include inadequate professional development and a lack of standardized assessment tools. This review highlights the need for targeted training and objective evaluation methods to better capture digital literacy in PE.

## Introduction

The rise of digital technologies has transformed ed ucation, enhancing motivation, collaboration, and c ritical thinking (Hughes et al., 2018; Lin et al., 2017). H owever, their integration into physical education (PE) has been slower due to its ‘hands-on’ nature and reliance on in-person interaction (Varea et al., 2021). Additional ly, low digital literacy among PE teachers poses challenges to effective implementation (ISTE, 2024; Krause et al., 2020). The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated the adoption of digital too ls in PE, including fitness trackers, visual representation tools, and exergames (Krause et al.,  2020). Despite this progress, concerns remain rega rding infrastructure, training, and teacher preparation (Jastrow et al., 2022). Digital literacy, defined by Paul Glister, plays a key role in utilizing technology effectively in PE.  Beyond basic knowledge, it involves accessing, managing, and app lying digital tools (Sivrikaya, 2020). While digita l literacy has gained attention in education (Casey et al., 2017; U.S. De partment of Education, 2024), research on its practical application in PE is limited.  Most studies measure literacy levels among teachers  (Belisario, 2023)  or students (Rodniam, 2020) rather than examining its implementation. This review aims to synthesize exis ting research, explore theoretical frameworks, and ident ify areas for improvement. It addresses the followi ng questions. RQ1: What theoretical frameworks are app lied? RQ2: How is digital literacy reported in PE? RQ3: What areas require further development based on identified barriers, challenges, and suggestions?

## Methods

A comprehensive protocol was established to ensure consistency and minimize bias. This included predefined search strategies, quality assessment methods, and outcomes synthesis procedures. Search strategies We conducted a thorough search across 19 databases via EBSCOhost, without restrictions on language or publication type, to capture a wide range of releva nt studies on digital literacy in PE. Inclusion cri teria required studies to (a) p resent primary data, (b) address both digital literacy and PE, while exclusion criteria filtered out conceptual papers and studies unrelated to PE or digital literacy. The search strategy used a Boolean combination of keywords: AB (digital literacy OR digital competence OR digital competency) AND AB (physical education) Quality assessment and outcome synthesis The Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) criteria (2024) w ere applied to evaluate study quality, using specific checklists for quantitative (Moola et al., 2020) and qualitati ve (Lockwood et al., 2015) research. No studies wer e excluded solely based on quality. Instead, assessment results informed data interpretation. Data extraction followed the JBI Mixed Methods Data Extraction Form with a Convergent Integrated Approach (Lizarondo et al., 2020), recording key study details such as author, year, study design, population, theoretical framework, and major findings. Study selection Following PRISMA guidelines, 30 studies were initia lly identified. After removing duplicates, 28 remai ned for screening, leading to the exclusion of 7 based on predefined criteria. One additional report was excluded as it was not accessible. A full-text review of 20 studies resulted in 12 being included in the final analysis.

## Results

RQ1: What theoretical frameworks are applied?

Studies applied various models, including digital literacy framework of Ng (2012), the TPACK model (Wohlfart et al., 2024), and digital competence frameworks (Bernate et al., 2021, Rojo-Ramos et al., 2020). However, there is no universal definition of digital literacy in PE.

Some studies used the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and Teacher Self-Efficacy (TSE) (Belisaro, 2023), or explored the relational nature of technology in education (Wallace et al., 2023). RQ2: How is digital literacy reported in physical education? Descriptive studies Seven studies assessed digital literacy levels in P E, reporting moderate to high scores (from 3.18 to 5.03). Differences emerged based on gender, grade level, a nd experience. Some studies found higher digital li teracy among females (Rodafinos et al., 2024; Rodniam, 2020), while others reported no significant differences (Dizlek & Uzun, 2022; Sivrikaya, 2020). Teachers with 10+ y ears of experience tended to have lower digital lit eracy (Dizlek & Uzun, 2022).

## Qualitative approaches and inferential statistical methods

Five studies employed qualitative or inferential me thods. Chiecher & Riccetti (2023) found that digita l competence improved post-pandemic. Other studies examined digital literacy as a predictor of technology use in PE instruction (Belisario, 2023; Wohlfart et al., 2024). However, research on digital literacy in PE activities (e.g., tracking monitors, wearables) remains scarce.

RQ3: What areas require further development based on identified barriers, challenges, suggestions?

A key barrier was low digital literacy among teache rs and inadequate professional development (Wallace  et al., 2023), despite self-reported moderate-to-high proficiency levels. Studies suggested integrating digital literacy into  curricula (Sivrikaya, 2020), creating targeted tra ining for PE teachers (Wallace et al., 2023), and fostering positive attitudes toward digital competence (Rodafinos et al., 2024). Future research should incorporate objective measures to minimize self-report biases.

## Discussion

This review is the first to focus exclusively on digital literacy in PE, examining theoretical frameworks, reporting trends, and areas for development. The findings hig hlight a growing interest in this field  (publications between 2020 and 2024) but reveal gaps, particularly among high school and post-secondary educators, as well as elementary and middle school students.

Regarding theoretical frameworks (RQ1), most studie s applied digital literacy or digital competence mo dels, through inconsistencies in conceptualization persis t. Some also incorporated TAM, TSE, and Entangled Pedagogy, reflecting trends in broader educational research. For digital literacy levels in PE (RQ2), findings s uggest moderate to high proficiency among teachers and students. However, gender differences emerged incon sistently, particularly in collaboration skills. Yo unger individuals often displayed higher digital literacy, possibly due to greater exposure to technology. Addressing developmental needs (RQ3), some teachers perceived their digital literacy as low despite moderate to high reported levels, underscoring the need for targeted  professional development in PE. Additionally, most studies relied on self-reported data, highlighting the necessity for objective assessments.

## Implication

In the digital era, strengthening digital literacy is essential. This review suggests:

• A unified framework: Standardizing definitions, components, and measurem ents will enhance research

coherence.

• Objective measurement: Complementing self-reports with external assessments will improve accu racy

and comparability.

• Greater digital exposure: Training programs and cur ricular integration can better prepare teachers and

students for digital advancements in PE.
