# Incorporating Critical Data Literacy into a STEM Summer Camp

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores middle-grade girls' understanding of Critical Data Literacy (CDL) after participating in a week-long residential STEM camp. This research examines how informal learning settings can foster CDL among predominantly underrepresented minoritized (URM) girls. Through qualitative interviews, the study reveals how participants engaged with CDL components: formulating questions, gathering relevant data, employing analytical tools, and interpreting findings. The insights aim to inform strategies for enhancing CDL in informal environments, contributing to broader educational goals in data science and supporting the development of critical thinking skills for addressing societal issues.

## Introduction and theoretical framework

Data Science Education (DSE) is crucial in today’s data-driven world, equipping students with the skills ne eded to navigate an increasingly complex digital landsca pe (NASEM, 2022). Critical Data Literacy (CDL) exte nds beyond basic data comprehension to include an under standing of data’s societal impact, ethical conside rations, and role in decision-making (Louie, 2022; Stornaiuolo, 2020). CDL enable s learners to critically assess and interpret data, recognizing its potential as a tool for justice and social good (Pangrazio & Selwyn, 2023). This study examines how middle-grade girls develop CDL in a week-long residential STEM camp, addressing gaps in research on informal learning environments and their role in fostering data literacy (; Sager al., under review). The goal is to explore how these set tings support CDL development and to inform effecti ve curriculum design. Specifically, the study asks: How do middle school girls demonstrate critical dat a literacy skills in informal learning environments, such as summer camps? CDL involves key competencies, including formulatin g structured questions, collecting and assessing reliable data, and employing analytical tools for i nterpretation (Bargagliotti et al., 2020; Bowler et  al., 2019). Learners engage in activities that highli ght how data visualizations shape conclusions (Leto urneau et al., 2019) and develop awareness of data privacy and ethical considerations (Hautea et al., 2017). By integrating these skills, CDL prepares learners to engage with data responsibly and meaningfully in various contexts.

## Methodology

This study utilized an exploratory case study approach (Yin, 2014) to examine changes in interests in STEM and learning of CDL among predominantly underrepresente d minoritized (URM)  girls. The project involved an established one-week residential STEM camp hosted by a local nonpro fit (Milton et al., 2023). The camp took place at a small public university in the southwestern U.S. during the summer of 2024. The program's topics were structured around daily themes to enhance the learning experience and ensure cultural relevance. The camp emphasized data literacy through collabora tive projects where campers created infographics addressing real-world issues. Using CODAP (The Concord Consortium, n.d.), campers analyzed data, developed research questions, and visualized findings. Grou ps (3–5 members) worked by grade level, covering topics s uch as food access, air pollution, and soil contaminati on. The study included 34 participants: 70% Black/A frican American, 12% Asian/Pacific Islander, 12% Hispanic/ Latina, and 5% White/Caucasian. Gra de distribution was 32% in 7th, 50% in 8th, and 18% in 9th grade.

We conducted 11 semi-structured interviews (one per group) to explore ca mpers’ experiences and engagement with data science. Transcribed data were analyzed using deductive coding (Creswell & Guetterman, 2019), guided by the CDL framework. The first two a uthors collaboratively coded the data, refining def initions through consensus-building (Wasser & Bressler, 1996) to ensure reliability.

## Discussion, implications, and conclusion

The findings highlight how CDL  skills—formulating questions, gathering data, analyzing da ta, and interpreting findings—prepare students to tackle global challenges and embody the conference theme, “Educating for WorldMaking: Envisioning and enacting sustainable solutions to g lobal crises”. In formulating questions, students like Zuri connect personal experiences with larger issue s, like pollution’s impact on health, demonstrating  a crucial step in CDL: identifying problems and asking impactful questions. This reflects the theme by fostering awareness and curiosity, essential to envisioning solutions f or global crises (i.e., Bargagliotti et al., 2020). In gathering and compiling data, Anna’s systematic approach to resea rching soil pollution shows students engaging respo nsibly with data, which underpins sustainable, evidence-based decision-making. This phase highlights how CDL promotes rigorous analysis, empowering students to envision and act on sustainable practices (Bargagliotti et al., 2020). The skill of using analytical tools, as seen in Alexis’s use of CODAP, equips students with  the ability to transform data into accessible visualizations. This  step fosters data-driven insights, aligning with the theme by preparing students to engage with and address real-world issues thoughtfully (i.e., Bowler et al., 2019; Letourneau et al., 2019; Sager, 2024). Finally, interpreting findings, as demonstrated by Brielle’s infographic and Zuri’s reflections, showcases how students use design and narrative to communicate complex issues in engaging  ways (Bowler et al., 2019; Hautea et al., 2017; Sager, 2024; Sager et al., under review). These communication skills promote an optimistic narrative, underscoring the s tudents' role as future world-makers who see both challenges and avenues for improvements (Louie, 2022; Stornaiuolo, 2020). This study has the potential to impact both practical and theoretical aspects of DSE. On a practical level, the findings could guide the development of curricu la that effectively engage middle school learners i n informal settings. Theoretically, it could provide new insig hts into how informal learning environments facilit ate the acquisition of CDL. The study may also inform futur e research, policy, and practice in DSE, particular ly in promoting inclusion and access in underrepresented groups.

In conclusion, CDL skills empower students to critically engage with pressing issues and advocate for positive change, positioning them as agents ready t o build sustainable solutions. Integrating CDL into  education not only cultivates technical proficiency but also instills a sense of responsibility, enabling studen ts to confront global crises as informed and active participants.
