# Examining College Students’ Psychological Dynamics in ChatGPT Assisted Programming

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In this study, we explored differences in students’ psychological dynamics among three programming support groups. The control group completed programming tasks exclusively with error hints provided by the Jupyter platform. The ChatGPT hints group provided students with more comprehensive and detailed explanations when errors occurred, while the ChatGPT enhanced hints group received not only detailed explanations but also code examples. We examined how various psychological factors, including students’ intrinsic goal orientation, extrinsic goal orientation, task value, task anxiety, critical thinking, cognitive engagement, feeling of knowing, and judgment of learning, interacted within each group and how these psychological dynamics differed across the three groups. While we found substantial similarities across the three groups, we also observed notable distinctions, particularly the negative connections between task anxiety and specific variables. This study holds theoretical and methodological importance by employing a complex dynamical system (CDS) perspective to understand the interplay of students’ psychological factors.

## Introduction

The advent of ChatGPT has ignited substantial inter est among researchers in the realm of AI in educati on. A particularly prominent area of focus centers around the utilization of ChatGPT in programming assistance. Yet, it is important to note that research in this domain is still in its early stages, with a noticeable gap in the investigation of how students ’ psychological factors interact within the context o f ChatGPT assisted programming and how these experiences diverge from traditional programm ing parad igms. This study aims to explore the dynamic interplay between students ’ psychological factors in ChatGPT assisted programming.  More specifically, we investigate the impact of different levels of suppo rt on students ’ psychological dynamics.  The psychological factors of interest include students’ intrinsic goal orientation, extrinsic goal orientat ion, task value, task anxiety, critical thinking, cognitive engagement, feeling of  knowing  (FOK), and judgment of learning  (JOL). These selected variables encompass motivation, emotiona l aspects (anxiety), cognitive skills (critical thi nking), metacognition (FOK and JOL), and engagement, all of which are crucial factors influencing learning and problemsolving in the context of ChatGPT assisted programming.

The study’s significance lies in several key areas. First, it  contributes to the burgeoning field of AI in education by shedding light on how students ’ psychological dynamics are influenced by AI interve ntions during programming tasks. This insight is essential for ed ucators, curriculum designers, and policymakers see king to optimize AI integration in education. Moreover, we rely on a complex dynamical system (CDS) perspective  to understand the intricate relationships among psychological factors (Koopmans, 2020). By examining these factors within a network framework, we can unravel the comp lexity of student learning in AI-enhanced environments. Lastly, the findings have practical implications fo r educators and institutions as they design ChatGPT assisted programming tools. Understanding how AI assistance impacts stud ents’ motivation, engagement, and metacognition can inform the development of more effective and personalized educational technologies.

## Participant

This study comprised three cohorts of college stude nts majoring in educational technology, totaling 85 participants. The average age of these participants was 19.91 years (SD = 1.11). All three cohorts of students had previously learned the module ‘Matplotlib for data visualization and analysis ’ within the ‘Python Programming’ course, taught by the same instructor.  Written consent was obtained from each participant.  The control group, consisting of 27 students, completed programming ta sks exclusively with support from the Jupyter platf orm, which will be further  explained below. This group serves as the baseline against which the  other groups are compared, representing the typical student experience with existing programming tools. The ChatGPT hints group comprises 32 students who receive assistance from ChatGPT, a natural language processing AI model. ChatGPT provides them with hints that are designed to be more comprehensive and explanatory than the hints given to the control group. The third group, ChatGPT enhanced hints group, is similar to the ChatGPT hints group, but with an additional feature. In addition to comprehensive  hints, students in this group also receive code ex amples that illustrate the hints provided by ChatGPT.  The inclusion of code examples aims to help  students understand not only the concepts but also the practical implementation of programming solutions. There were 26 students in the ChatGPT enhanced hints group.

Programming environment and task All participants were required to complete a data visualization task independently in the JupyterLab environment. JupyterLab is a versatile and extensible programming environment primarily used for data sci ence and scientific computing. It is an evolution of the classic Jupyter Notebook and offers a more flexible environment for working with data and code. We redesigned JupyterLab to offer various levels of code error hints generated by ChatGPT.

## Procedure and measures

Before the experiment, the same instructor introduced the programming task to all three groups of students. Afterward, the participants were asked to complete a questionnaire adapted from the MSLQ (Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire)  (Pintrich, 1991). The questionnaire  was refined to  measure students’ intrinsic goal orientation (IGO), extrinsic goal orientation (EGO), task value (TV), task anxiety (TA), and critical thinking (CT) in this context. Specifically, the questionnaire included 4 items for IGO, 4 items for EGO, 6 items for TV, 4 items for TA, and 5 items for CT. We used a 7-point Likert scale, where 1 indicates ‘not at all true of me’ and 7 indicates ‘very true of me’.

After the experiment, we measured students’ cognitive engagement while they completed the task and their metacognitive judgements. Cognitive engagement was assessed by the Situational Cognitive Engagement Instrument (Rotgans & Schmidt, 2011), which consists of four items that measure three facts of engageme nt: (1) engagement with the task at hand, (2) effort and pe rsistence, and (3) experience of flow. A sample ite m was “I kept thinking deeply about the task ”. Moreover, we assessed two types of metacognitive judgments:  Feeling of Knowing (FOK) and Judgment of Learning (JOL). FOK w as evaluated using the question, ‘Please predict your confidence in your future ability to recall or reco gnize the learned information in this task. ’ JOL, on the other hand, was measured with the question, ‘Please predict your future performance in completin g a similar programming task.’ In assessing both FOK and JOL, participants rated t heir predictions on a scale from 1 to 10, where 1 indicated very low likelihood, and 10 indicated very high likelihood. Analysis We used a network estimation technique, EBICglasso (Extended Bayesian Information Criterion Graphical Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator), to construct a network of the studying variables for each group (Friedman et al., 2008; Isvoranu & Epskamp, 2023). Specifically, the EBICglasso estimator incorporates a model selection criterion (EBIC) and a form of regulariza tion (LASSO) to estimate a sparse graphical model t hat best represents the relationships between variables (Friedman et al., 2008). It aims to find the most relevant connections in the network while also accounting for noise and potential false positives in the correlation matrix. Additionally, we assessed the edge stability of the networks using 1,000 bootstraps.

## Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we observed strong positive connecti ons between FOK (Feeling of Knowing) and JOL (Judgment of Learning) and between IGO (Intrinsic Goal Orient ation) and TV (Task Value) across all groups.  The strong positive connection between FOK and JOL suggests that students with greater confidence in their knowledge and understanding (FOK) exhibited increased confidence in their ability to tackle similar tasks in the fut ure (JOL). This phenomenon reflects a strong alignment between students’ subjective sense of knowing and their predictions about their future learning success.  Moreover, it is plausible that students who had a strong intrinsic motivation to learn the material (IGO) also perceived the tasks as more valuable or meaningful (TV). These findings suggest that the two types of metacognitive judgement, as w ell as IGO and TV had consistent relationships, irrespectiv e of the different groups of participants.

Additionally, a positive connection existed between EGO and CT in the control group, which was absent in the other two groups. In the control group, stud ents with EGO may be driven by a desire for improved grades or the demonstration of their programming abilities to friends and family. Consequently, they may place a higher value on critical thinking skills. In contrast, stu dents in the ChatGPT  assisted groups  may not attribute their success to their critical thinking skills due to the detailed hints and code examples provided by ChatGPT. Another significant contrast among the three groups pertained to the negative associations of task anxiety (TA) with other variables. Specifically, students in the control group revealed a negative connection between TA and CE, while the ChatGPT hints group displayed a negativ e association between TA and FOK. In the ChatGPT enhanced hints group, negative connections were observed between TA and JOL, as well as between TA and IGO. In the control group, where students received less detailed support, the experience of higher levels of task anxiety was associated with decreased cognitive engagement. In contrast, ChatGPT assisted programming appears to help alleviate the negative impact of task anxiety on co gnitive engagement. However, the presence of more detailed support from ChatGPT may reduce the need for the st rong feeling of knowing, as students could rely on the provided assistance.  Similarly, in the ChatGPT enhanced hints group, whe re students received even more extensive support including code examples, students might not develop strong JOL or intrinsic motivation if they still experience high levels of task anxiety. These findings have significant practical implications.
