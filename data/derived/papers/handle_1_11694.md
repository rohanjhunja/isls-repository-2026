# Assessing Student Attitudes and Understanding of Interdisciplinarity: Introducing the SATIS Survey

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Educational systems still struggle to institutionalize cross-disciplinary collaboration among educators in post-secondary institutions. Furthermore, there is limited understanding of how exposure to interdisciplinary learning might influence students’ epistemic beliefs. To address this gap, our team developed and piloted the Student Attitude Toward Interdisciplinarity Survey (SATIS). Preliminary findings reveal that while students acknowledge the importance of integrating multiple disciplines to tackle complex problems, it remains uncertain whether they independently apply knowledge from diverse courses to real-world challenges.

## Introduction

“Interdisciplinary studies is a process of answering a question, solving a problem, or addressing a topic that is too broad or complex to be dealt with adequately by a single discipline, and draws on the disciplines with the goal of integrating their insig hts to construct a more comprehensive understanding.” (Repko & Szostak, 2021, p. 9). Although interdisciplinary skills are generally viewed as advantageous in professional environments, introducing students to interdisciplinary competencies within educational settings is a significant challe nge (Knechtel, 2020; Samson et al., 2012). Institutional contexts often fail to provide the ne cessary support for cross-disciplinary collaboration among faculty aiming to co-create interdisciplinary materials and design probl ems that require integrated, real-world solutions (LeVasseur & Giuliani, 2022). As a result, such initiatives are scarce, and limited research exists on their impact on students. In the  context of this research, over a dozen of these initiatives have been documented in Quebec’s (Canada) college network (Cégep, see below). Many of these ad hoc efforts began with key faculty members, leaving little empirical evidence of their impact o n students and their ability to foster transfer of learning (Lobato, 2012; Lobato & Hohensee, 2021). A better comprehension of the conditions leading to sustainable cross-disciplinary education and their impact on students  is crucial to foster institutionalization of these individual efforts. Recent research by Lepage and Toutloff (2021) identified specific epistemological beliefs that promote interdisciplinary initiatives among Qu ebec college teachers. The research demonstrates the impact of teachers’ beliefs on the emergence of these init iatives. However, little is known about the impact of student s’ beliefs on the acquisition of interdisciplinary knowledge and knowledge transfer (Horn et al., 2022).

To address this gap, it is crucial to develop and c onsistently utilize tools that evaluate the effects  of students’ exposure to interdisciplinar y initiatives (Chalmers et al., 2017). Our study investigates the change in epistemological beliefs of students participating in an interdisciplinary initiative, seeking to answer the following research questions: Does participation in interdisciplinary educational  initiatives help the learner a)  recognize the need to explain or solve complex problems by re cruiting knowledge from different disciplinary pers pectives and b) shift their attitude toward the importance of interdisciplinarity?

## Methods

Through the lens of the Common Ground Theory as des cribed by Repko (2007), the tool used in this study aims to assess how the development of a common cognitive frame of reference, the role of collaborative language, and the integration of disciplinary insights can affect students’ epistemological beliefs. Our study takes place at Cégep level, a post-secondary institution where students in Quebec typically enroll  in a two-year program  prior to pursuing university studies. To address our researc h questions, our group adapted and piloted a survey, The Student Attitude Toward Interdisciplinarity Survey  (SATIS), to a group of 42 students exposed to an interdisciplinary initiative. The project paired two courses —Behavioural Biology  and Introduction to Psychology—in a co-designed format for Cégep students registered in a psychology profile of the social science program. By integrating the two courses, instructors aligned objectives, assignments, and ac tivities to help students contextualize and connect complex concepts  across both disciplines. This approach aimed to fo ster interdisciplinary thinking during a critical stage of students’ epistemological d evelopment. The development of SATIS was inspired by several key sources: the Interdisciplinary Teaching Readiness tool (Wang et al., 2020) the report by Lepage and Toutloff (2021), and the InterCLASS questionnaire (Charbonneau et al., 2015). These tools were adapted to specifically assess the evolution of students ’ attitudes in a two-discipline interdisciplinary intervention. The adapted survey  consists of 13 Likert-scale questions, alongside an open-ended question prompting students to propose interdisciplinary solutions to a real-life problem. The addition of this question was intended to evaluate how effectively students apply  and transfer interdisciplinary knowledge to practi cal challenges. The real-life problem was created by teachers to specifically address the co-designed course outcomes. Real-Life Problem Question: A student diagnosed with Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) struggles to concentrate while studying. What recommendations would you provide for this student’s wellbeing? Answers were coded by the two teachers independentl y in the following categories: Biological Approach, Psychological Approach, Combined Approach, or None. This poster introduces the SATIS survey and presents initial findings from an online questionnaire completed by 19 students enrolled in the paired courses. The survey was conducted at the start of the semester  and again after a 15-week semester, with an intention  of conducting it again over their two years of Cégep. This longit udinal design seeks to track  changes in students’ attitudes toward interdisciplinarity over time.

## Results

Survey results show that most students (79%) agreed  that solving problems in biology and psychology of ten necessitates contributions from both disciplines. Specifically, in response to the statement, concepts from biology and psychology are both necessary to fully understa nd either discipline individually, 10% of students strongly agreed, 37% agreed, and 10% somewhat agreed. However, an asymmetry in perceived relevance emerged: while students widely viewed biology as beneficial to und erstanding psychology (82%), fewer students believe d that psychology could aid in addressing biological issue s (63%). Similarly, with  problems purely in the Social Sciences, most students believe that interdisciplinary solutions a re esse ntial, only 37% agree ing with the statement: In Social Sciences, complex or complicated question s can often be answered using knowledge from a single discipline.

Although responses to the  Likert-scale SATIS questions indicated that students recognized the importance of incorporating knowledge from various disciplines, most students suggested a single-discipline approach when asked to address a real-life scenario. Indeed, an analysis of the language used in their a nswers to the open-ended question reveals that most students did not s uggest a solution that integrated elements from bot h disciplines. Of the students surveyed, 8 students recommended a psychological approach, for example “Study in a structured environment and take a lot of breaks to keep you concentrated. Maybe do some sports before studying to help the mind be more concentrated.” Only 2 students suggested a biological approach, such as ‘Get Adderall Drugs’. The two students who suggested a combined approach drew on personal experience, as illustrated in the following response: ‘Not every person with ADHD is the same. I have ADHD, and some days I concentrate better with soothing music, while on others, complete sile nce helps more. Medication has also been beneficial  for me, so it could help the student in question.’ Preliminary results indicate that their attitudes toward interdisciplinarity did not change significantly throughout the course of the semester.

## Conclusions and implications

Our findings indicate that although students recogn ize the theoretical value of interdisciplinary appr oaches, they often struggle to apply cross-disciplinary knowledge effectively to real-world problems. This gap underscores the need to create learning exp eriences and assessments that promote not only inte rdisciplinary awareness but also the ability to transfer knowledge even without expl icit prompts. Purposefully designed activities can encourage students to integrate and apply knowledge across di sciplines, moving beyond reliance solely on persona l life experiences. Future research will track these stude nts throughout their college years to examine wheth er their epistemological beliefs and problem-solving skills develop through exposure to interdisciplinary interventions.
