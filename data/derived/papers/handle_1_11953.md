# Leveraging Architectural Visualization and AI Design Tools to Enhance the design of Active Learning Curriculum

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Higher education is experiencing an increase in the use of Active Learning Classrooms (ALCs), which present educators with the challenge of utilizing the spatial and technological resources of these spaces effectively to foster dynamic, student-centered learning. Traditional tools often fail to provide practical and flexible strategies to optimize the use of ALC spaces. To fill this gap, this paper presents the Intelligent Design Environment for Active Learning (IDEAL) project, an AI-assisted design environment leveraging two architectural visualization approaches. IDEAL enables educators to rethink and redesign curriculum tailored for ALCs by managing hands-on spatial understanding through physical models or interactive scenario planning using a 3D virtual environment. The project not only inspires innovative curriculum design but also deepens educators’ engagement with ALC resources, fostering collaborative communities for future curriculum development.

Active learning classrooms: Challenges and focus Research has consistently demonstrated the value of  active learning approaches, such as flipped classr ooms and inquiry-based learning (Krajcik et al., 1998; Linn & Hsi, 2000). The number of active learning classrooms (ALCs) at universities continues to grow as colleges and u niversities discuss investing in flexible, technolo gy-enhanced learning spaces, such as student-centered learning environment s with upside-down pedagogies (SCALE-UP) classrooms, to support these approaches.  With the growing popularity of ALCs, educators also  face a new challenge: How can they adapt their course designs to these dynamic environments, and what support do they need to do so?

This paper will introduce the Intelligent Design Environment for Active Learning (IDEAL) project, which aims to transcend the traditional text-based course design method. The IDEAL project employs two visualization approaches commonly used in architecture with Intelligent to create an interactive platform that is deeply participatory and fosters community collaboration. Challenges and current support in ALCs Rooted in constructivism, active learning is a broad concept that generally refers to student-centered, cognitively engaged and collaborative activities  (Bonwell. Charles C, 1991; Prince, 2004). Chi (2009; 2014) introduced the ICAP framework, emphasizing the learner’s outputs t o accurately classify the activity and assess its c ognitive engagement. Active learning classrooms provide a supportive env ironment for constructivist educational experiences, fostering social interaction and colla boration (Holec & Marynowski, 2020). Nevertheless, teachers face multiple challenges in designing courses, including insufficient design expertise, difficulty adapting teaching methods to ALCs, and unfamiliarity with ALC technol ogies (Najafi et al., 2024). Also studies for the effects of ALCs on instructors are still very limited  (Leijon et al., 2024). To implement ALCs and foster collaborative teacher communities, teacher training remains the primary supportive method (Knaub et al., 2016). Research focus To bridge the gap between ALCs’ potential and actua l usage, this study provides structured, community-based support for curriculum design by (1) identifying te achers’ design pain points, (2) developing targeted  solutions, and (3) examining how IDEAL helps educators optimize ALCs curriculum design.

## Methodology: Understanding educators’ needs

The study drew on the need for technology training,  reference cases, and community support, and it use d case studies with two Canadian teacher educators. Each taught a teacher credentialing program course in newly piloted active learning classrooms, bringing distinct disci plinary perspectives. Across two semi-structured interviews— one on general design practices and one on a specif ic course—participants used physical classroom models to envision spatial layouts. Verbatim transcripts were then thematically analyzed (Braun & Clarke, 2006), revealing curriculum design strategies, challenges, and supports that inform the development of IDEAL.

## Key findings

Both educators saw potential in using physical clas sroom space —particularly vertical surfaces —for active learning yet lacked concrete examples of effective spatial design. They also struggled to integrate ad vanced technologies (e.g., AI chatbots, simulations) due to unfamiliarity and insufficient training. Emphasizing co-design and community, they proposed sharing a “library” of teaching ideas and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration, especially in the teacher credentialing program. Fi nally, physical m odels of the classroom and 3D-printed furniture enable teachers to grasp the size and res ources of the room intuitively. Participants indica ted the area where activities are expected to take place by moving furniture and rearranging space manually and the dynamics of the class when envisioning activities (see Figure 1). Collective and collaborative community IDEAL’s community features include (1) a collaborat ive “community library” of shared resources, (2) a communication platform for exchanging layouts and strategies, and (3) real-time multi-user course design for broad collaboration among educators, designers, and researchers.

## Discussion

After completing its preliminary design in early 2025, IDEAL will pilot a prototype inspired by teacher interviews. Faculty from teacher credentialing programs who hav e piloted TEAL classrooms will provide feedback on IDEAL's features. Early stages of the project will primarily focus on  high-tech-equipped teaching spaces, given the specific “TEAL” classroom model chosen. Future versions will accommodate lower-tech classrooms, further broadening active learning’s reach and impact.
