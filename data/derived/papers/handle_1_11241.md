# The Future Landscape of Education: Constructing an Analytical and Application Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence in Higher Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) has infused new vitality into education, making the regulation of its application in higher education a current research focus. This study conducts a systematic literature review to comprehensively assess the application landscape and challenges of GenAI in higher education, constructing an action framework encompassing four key elements: Formulate policy and regulation, Enhance academic integrity and assessment, Advance preparation and training, and Develop teaching and learning strategies. The framework aims to provide structured pathways for universities to ensure the ethical and compliant application of GenAI, uphold academic standards, foster skill development, and promote instructional innovation. Specifically, by delineating action pathways according to educational entities (governmental bodies, educational institutions, educators and students), this study provides clear execution directions for each role, facilitating collaborative efforts. The framework serves as a bridge between policy and practice, offering guidance for integrating GenAI into higher education systems effectively.

## Introduction

The rapid emergence of Generative Artificial Intelli gence (GenAI) is transforming higher education. Universities have responded more actively than prim ary or secondary institutions (Wang et al., 2024), especially after the launch of ChatGPT, which has improved teaching efficiency and quality (Jing et al., 2024). As adoption accelerates, institutions seek w ays to integrate GenAI, manage associated risks, and support pedagogical innovation.

Existing literature addresses specific issues. Chan  (2023) proposes an AI ecological policy framework; Wang et al. (2025a) examines GenAI’s impact on education through qual itative analysis. However, these approaches are fragmented and lack a holistic model for application.

To address this gap, this study systematically revi ews existing research and employs grounded theory to construct a comprehensive framework for G enAI integration in higher education. The framework offers practical guidance for effective a doption, enabling institutions to harness GenAI for more personalized and innovative learning environments.

## Methodology

To investigate application norms of GenAI in higher  education, this study first conducted a systematic literature review (PROSPERO Protocol: CRD4202459877 4) to define research questions, ensure comprehensive coverage, and integrate existing find ings using standardized assessment tools (Diekemper et al., 2015; Page et al., 2021). This p rovided a solid foundation for identifying core iss ues and building the framework.

Given the qualitative nature and complexity of disc ussions on GenAI norms, traditional quantitative methods proved insufficient. Thus, gro unded theory was adopted to build theory  from empirical data. Originating from Glaser and Strauss, grounded theory enables the extraction of key concepts and relationships through iterative analysis (Dey,2004). It offers distinct advantages in handling complex, uncertain research contexts (Chen et al., 2024b), supporting the construction of a practical GenAI framework tailored to higher education needs.

Search strategy In this study, the PRISMA screening process was str ictly followed for literature selection (Page et al., 2021; Dai et al., 2024). Literature searches were conducted in three databases: Scopus, Web of Science, and EBSCO. Referring to related research (Chen et a l., 2024a), the final search query used for topic selection was as follows (using Web of Science as a n example):TS = (("higher education" OR "university" OR "college") AND ("GPT*" OR "ChatGPT"  OR "generative artificial intelligence" OR "generative AI" OR "Large Language Model*" OR "GAI"  OR "GenAI") AND (" suggest*" OR "guideline" OR "guidance" OR "framework")).Since OpenAI launched ChatGPT in November 2022, the search period was set from 2022 onwards, with the final retrieval date being July 11, 2024. This process resulted in 572 articles being identified.

Manual screening To ensure the selected literature aligns with the r esearch topic and maintains quality standards, the research team established screening criteria, which include focusing on studies that substantively analyze regulated GenAI use, are available in full text, contain complete information elements (such as abstracts and references), and have undergone standard peer review. The manual screening process was conducted in two s tages. In the first stage, articles were excluded based on a review of titles and abstracts by two team members over three weeks, leaving 49 articles. In the second stage, a full-text review was conducted by three team members over two weeks to identify articles most relevant to the study, ultim ately retaining 22 articles from three databases. Open coding Open coding entails systematically reviewing and re fining literature to extract key concepts and categorize them into preliminary domains. Verbatim expressions are preferred to preserve authenticity, while similar concepts are compared and merged until saturation is reached. In this study, text segments relevant to the research theme were selected, and 7 2 initial concepts were identified through conceptu al analysis. Redundant or overlapping ideas were conso lidated—for example, concepts such as “evaluate the output” and “double-checking ChatGPT’s responses” were grouped under th e category “Cautiousness about large model outputs.” Ultimately, 18 preliminary categories were formed, reflecting core concerns regarding GenAI’s regulated application in higher education. Axial coding Axial coding builds connections between initial con cepts to form higher-level categories. In this study, the 18 preliminary categories derived from open cod ing were further analyzed to identify underlying relationships within the educational system. This p rocess resulted in four main categories: Formulate Policy and Standard, Enhance Academic Integrity and  Assessment Practice, Develop Teaching and Learning Strategy, and Advance Preparation and Trai ning. These reflect key dimensions for guiding GenAI integration in higher education.

Selective coding Selective coding refines the relationships among primary categories, presenting them in a "storyline" to identify a central category that reflects the overall framework. After analyzing the categories and their interrelationships, an action framework for applying generative AI in higher education was developed, as shown in Figure 2.

## Discussion

Analysis of the action framework for generative artificial intelligence in the

## context of higher education

This study constructs an action framework for the a ppropriate application of GenAI in higher education through a three-level grounded theory coding approach. The core ele ment, Formulate Policy and Standard, provides the foundation for GenAI regulat ion. Policies and standards are developed by authoritative institutions, ensuring the ethical and educational alignment of GenAI use. For instance, the French Ministry of Education emphasizes AI literacy  and critical thinking (A cadémie de Paris, 2023), while UNESCO advocates for regulations that ensure human values and educational missions are upheld (UNESCO, 2023).

The second element, Advance Preparation and Trainin g, emphasizes the importance of pilot testing and AI training for educators and students. Pilot programs enable universities to assess risks and optimize GenAI implementation. This step is exempli fied by Japan’s national pilot program, which successfully demonstrated the significance of prepa ration in schools (MEXT, 2023). Training ensures that both educators and students are equipped with the necessary skills to use GenAI responsibly and effectively in teaching and learning.

Develop Teaching and Learning Strategy, the third e lement, focuses on maximizing GenAI’s potential to enhance personalized instruction and collaborative learning. GenAI can tailor course content to meet individual student needs, improving teachin g precision and effectiveness (Wang et al., 2025b). It fosters collaborative, inquiry-based learning, promoting critical thinking through  multi-perspective discussions. However, to fully integrate GenAI, educational institutions need to adapt assessment methods to match the capabilities of AI tools.

The final element, Enhance Academic Integrity and A ssessment Practice, addresses the challenges GenAI poses to academic integrity. This involves rethinking traditional assessment techniques and adopting diverse evaluation strategi es, such as oral presentations and group projects, to ensure authentic academic performance. Research indicates that educating students about plagiarism and using detection tools are crucial in maintaining academic fairness (Cotton et al., 2023).

In conclusion, this action framework provides a str uctured approach to integrating GenAI in higher education, balancing innovation and regulati on. By ensuring policy development, preparation, innovative teaching strategies, and safeguarding academic integrity, universities can enhance educational outcomes while minimizing risks, contributing to the sustainable evolution of the educational system. Analysis of insights and action pathways for different educational entities in the generative artificial intelligence action framework Educational entities in this study refer to organiz ations or individuals within the educational system responsible for supporting, implementing, or advanc ing educational goals, including governmental bodies, educational institutions, educators, and students. Each entity plays a key role in the generat ive artificial intelligence application framework. This  section analyzes the insights and action pathways provided by the generative AI framework for these entities (as shown in Figure 3). The government is responsible for formulating polic ies and standards, ensuring ethical guidelines and safety measures for GenAI’s educatio nal use. By establishing national policies and providing funding, governments support higher educa tion institutions in  implementing operational standards and maintaining academic integrity.

Educational institutions serve as the core agents for policy implementation, refining operational standards to align with institutional needs. They a lso provide training and resources for faculty and students to ensure effective and responsible GenAI use. Moreover, institutions develop academic integrity standards and assessment models to ensure equitable access and positive outcomes (Chen et al., 2025).

Educators translate policies into practical teachin g by incorporating GenAI into curriculum design, fostering critical thinking, and encouraging collaborative learning. They also maintain academic integrity through assessments that prevent over-reliance on  GenAI-generated content (Maheshwari, 2024).

Students, as the primary beneficiaries, must follow ethical standards and use GenAI responsibly. They acquire AI literacy and ethical awareness, uti lizing GenAI as a supplementary learning tool while engaging in personalized and collaborative learning under educators' guidance.

In conclusion, each entity—government, institutions, educators, and students—has distinct yet interconnected roles in ensuring the ethical and in novative application of GenAI, contributing to the advancement and quality of higher education.

## Conclusion

This study conducts a systematic literature review to evaluate the current applications and challenges of GenAI in higher education. Based on these findings,  an action framework is developed with four key components: policy and regulation, academic integrity, preparation and training, and teaching strategies.

The study uses grounded coding and analysis to iden tify critical factors influencing GenAI’s ethical compliance, academic standards, skill development, and instructional innovation. The framework divides action pathways for educational entities, offering clear and targeted execution strategies for each ro le, ensuring effective and collaborative GenAI applications in higher education. While providing structured guidelines, the framework must be adapted to each i nstitution’s unique resources and technological capabilities. Future research could focus on develo ping context-specific models and refining practical applications to fully leverage GenAI’s potential, f ostering digital transformation and advancing educational practices.
