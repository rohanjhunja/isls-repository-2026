# Reducing Academic Gaps Through AI: Personalized Learning Pathways in Mathematics

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines the effects of an AI-powered educational platform on personalized learning and academic performance in mathematics. It focuses on individual effectiveness and learning patterns over time. The findings indicate that AI-based personalized learning significantly improves mathematics performance, particularly among struggling students. This research underscores the potential of AI tools to bridge academic gaps by delivering tailored learning experiences and influencing students' learning trajectories.

## Background and research gap

There are several factors affecting achievement gaps in student learning. such as socioeconomic status, ethnicity, gender, and individual student characteristics (Bonal & González, 2020). Personalized learning is essential to address these disparities in the classroom. Personalized learning refers to instruction in which the pace of learning and the instructional approach are optimized for the needs of each learner (United States Department of Education, 2017). Personalized instruction is crucial for stud ents to fully engage with learning and achieve acad emic goals (Reigeluth, 2016; Reigeluth et al., 2015; Watson & Watson, 2016). However, since teachers have limited classroom time, it is hard to provide personalized instruction for each student (Holmes et al., 2018).  Given this challenge, the deployment of technology is regarded  as a viable approach to offering tailored instruct ion to individual students in a classroom environment with  time constraints and a large number of students (R eigeluth et al., 2015). Particularly, AI plays a vital role in facilitating personalized learning experiences f or students (Holmes et al., 2018). However, research on its impact on learning outcome s remains limited, particularly concerning achievement levels and longitudinal learning trajectories. This study aims to address these gaps by investigating the effects of an AI-powered educational platform on personalized learning and academic performance in mathematics over time. The research focuses on analyzing individual effectiveness and learning patterns, wit h particular attention to struggling students. It s eeks to explore how AI tools can help bridge academic gaps in mathe matics by delivering tailored learning experiences.  The central research question guiding this study is: What are the students’ learning trajectories while using the AI-based platform over time?

## Participants and context

Twenty-three students and their homeroom teacher in South Korea participated in this study. The participating school was affiliated with a group having predominantly low-socioeconomic status and underrepresented students, including immigrants. The intervention was conducted within a third-grade mathematics class and lasted for one semester.

## Procedure and platform used

The AI mathematics platform used in this study was called “Tok-Tok Math Expedition”. It was first launched in 2020 and developed by the Korean Ministry of Educat ion for elementary students in mathematics. The pla tform consists of several functions and activities, including a digital textbook, AI-recommended math problems tailored to each student’s performed activities, and math games (see Figure 1). While this paper focuses on math outcome trajectories, the broader and more in-depth implementation context is thoroughly described in Lee (2024). Data sources and analysis The study utilized data from three repeated mathema tics assessments administered at pre -, mid-, and post-test intervals. The assessments were developed through a  collaborative process involving a researcher and t wo mathematics education experts, incorporating iterat ive refinement and pilot testing to ensure validity. Each assessment included 20 questions, with a maximum possible score of 100 points per test. A quantitative approach was employed to analyze students' learning outcomes. Descriptive statistics were used to provide an overview of the data, while infe rential methods, including repeated measures (RM) A NOVA and linear mixed-effects modeling, were appl ied to examine changes in students' performance ove r time and to identify patterns in their learning trajectories.

## Results and discussion

To examine the effectiveness of personalized learning through AI, students' learning outcomes of three tests were analyzed. First, we compared the first test and the last test shown in Figure 2 on the left. The math scores for lowachieving students improved from 32.5 to 63.44, wit h an increase of 30.94, which indicates that they i ncreased their learning the most. The math scores for intermediate students improved from 56.79 to 82.31, with an increase of 25.52. The math scores for high-achieving students improved from 75.0 to 87.19, wit h an increase of 12.19. An RM ANOVA was conducted to compare the difference s between test 1 and test 3 across academic groups. The factor of time showed a statistically significa nt effect (F=93.72, p<.001), and academic levels a lso revealed a statistically significant effect (F=42.8, p<.001). Moreover, the interaction between time and acade mic levels was statistically significant (F=5.79, p=0.01). Thi s indicates that the changes between pre-and post-test scores differ across academic levels and reduced gaps among the low-performing, intermediate, and high-performing students. Our findings revealed that the gaps among  the three groups in the pre-test were large, but these gaps became smaller in the post-test.

Next, we compared test 1, test 2, and test 3. Figure 2 on the right shows academic performance trajectories over time by level. The majority of students increa sed their scores steadily from the beginning to the  end of the intervention whereas a few students dropped in test  2. There can be several reasons for this including  external issues, one possible reason might be it took some t ime to be beneficial from the system and needed tim e to get familiar with the system. The time that impacted students varied from student to student. Nonetheless, as indicated in the thick black line in Figure 2, the overall score increased over time. In addition to the descriptive statistics, a linear mixed-effects model was fitted, while allowing the effect  of time to vary acros s students. The model included a random intercept and a random slope for time nested within each student. The analysis revea led a significant effect of time on the score (estimate =  11.522, t(22.006) = 8.048, p <.001), indicating t hat as time increases, the score also tends to increase. The model accounted for a significant portion of the variance in score. The random effects analysis revealed substantial va riability among participants, with a variance of 50 3.8 for the intercepts and 29.8 for the slopes. These findings provide evidence for individual differences in the baseline scores and the rate of change over time among students. It  implies that educators need to consider the indivi dual differences in their learning.

## Implications and conclusion

This research holds significance for both practitioners and researchers as it aims to elucidate effective approaches for implementing personalized learning through an AI interface to enhance student engagement and achievement. It is important for teachers to utilize AI-based tools or intelligent tutors to deliver personalized instruction for their students based on their prior knowledge and achieve ment levels. Additionally, this study highlights ho w t o mitigate academic gaps by analyzing the repeated da ta in mathematics over time, particularly for strug gling students. By providing tailored pedagogical approaches using AI, this research endeavors to address the pervasive disparity in academic achievement.
