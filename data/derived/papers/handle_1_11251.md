# Teacher Candidates’ Identity Construction as Collective Movement along Zones of Sense

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In this manuscript, we explore how collective sensemaking takes may take place during teacher preparation programs and how this might influence the development of professional teaching identities. Drawing from a cultural historical view of learning, we situate this conceptual manuscript in identity development processes of teacher candidates during graduate courses while engaged in student teaching experiences. We suggest that teacher candidates construct and develop their professional teacher identities as an active social process through sensemaking. Understanding teacher identity development has implications for their professional development, and for teacher preparation programs.

## Introduction

Teacher identity is defined as the awareness of being teachers (Hanna et al., 2020) or as developing a self-image as a teacher (Beijaard et al., 2004). Such identity evolves  as teachers gain experiences from courses and during student teaching experiences (Beauchamp & Thomas, 2009). It is important to understand how teacher identity develops because teachers’ learning and actions  is connected to their identity (Roth, 2007). Teacher identity development is a process that begins as one decides to become a teacher (Virta, 2002). Prioritizing the inherent dependency between learning about teaching and developing a teacher identity. Such perspective emphasizes that learning  is culturally mediated  through external artifacts, and takes place in social activity (Vygotsky, 1987). In these processes, individuals expand their perspectives about who they are and consider new ideas about who they are in the world (Holland et al., 19 98). Learning plays a role therefore in identity development (Esteban-Guitart, 2021, p. 171).

There is a connection between  “actual identities”  that are accepted and realized; and “designated identities” that develop while in collective activity (Sfard & Prusak, 2005). Reflection has a role in this experience by creating a sense of one's abilities as a teacher (Kelchtermans, 2009). While preparing to become a teacher, students participate in student teaching experiences and take classes at their institution. They are active participants as they  reinterpret their own experiences through new lenses, leading to enhanced professional identities as teacher  (Beijaard et al, 2004). Teacher candidates might be taking classes together, but their learning experience, and identity development will differ. The reason for this is rooted in the ways they engage in sensemaking of collective experiences through a process of inte rdependence between the subjective and the experiential experiences (Esteban-Guitart, 2021). In other words, identity involves a volitional process where people make decisions as to what to endorse and what to reject (Esteban-Guitart, 2021). We strive to connect this to the conceptualization of sense or “inner speech” or a complex activity involving initial thinking that can then evolve toward language in written or oral forms (Vygotsky, 1987, p. 272). Vygotsky makes a difference between “ sense”, which is more experiential in nature, and when the individual brings forth one of these different zones of the sense into what he calls, “meaning” (Vygotsky, 1987, p. 276). In thinking about  teacher preparation courses and the connection to  the teachers’ student teaching, we invite teacher educators to invite teacher candidates into multimodal experiences to assist candidates in reflecting upon ideas about teaching and learning. We proposed that  multimodal activities can invite teacher candidates to generate external expressions about teaching and learning and have the potential to act as semiotic tools (Vygotsky, 1978) mediating sensemaking for identity development. Using multimodal data during courses can provide opportunities to explore questions such as: How can teacher candidates evolve toward identifying as teacher while in a course and in student teaching experiences? From this lens, it will be interesting to explore how the future teachers engage in a sensemaking process in relation to teaching and learning with multimodal tools.

## Theoretical background

While identity has traditionally been viewed as a more individually developed phenomenon, new definitions have now been proposed, including more diversity in terms of  fluidity from individual to collective aspects (Vignoles et al., 2011). A more dynamic and complex concept is increasingly called for (Beijaard et al., 2004; Patterson, et al., 2022).

In this study, we wish to advance a  perspective of teachers’ identity that is integrative of both personal and individual  dimensions and considers contextual and material aspects. Inspired by dialogical approaches centering sociocultural processes, and cultural aspects (Penuel & Wertsch, 1995). From this perspective, identity is fluidly transforming in collective spaces. We support the idea that teacher candidates  will have simultaneous various I-positions while engage in courses and student teaching (Akkerman & Meijer, 2011). In other words, becoming teachers requires turns forward in identity as teacher candidates evolve in their understandings of who they are as educators in a social context (Esteban-Guitart, 2021; Holland et al., 1998). Understanding this identity development process  is important because learning, actions, and identity  are closely connected (Roth, 2007).

As we introduced earlier, l earning to feel as  teachers involves connecting  “actual identities”  with “designated identities” (Sfard & Prusak, 2005). Teacher candidates engage i n sensemaking as they move across this continuum. Besides these fluid  identities, teacher candidates carry dispositions and beliefs and ways of defining knowledge or ways of behaving, which contribute to form professional identity (Leeferink et al., 2019). Sensemaking is connected to identity development and shapes the learning about identity (Graus et al., 2022). Sensemaking, ownership, and agency are all indicators of identity development. Sensemaking is important to connect the theoretical and practical aspects of the teaching profession (Beijaard & Meijer, 2017). From Vygotsky’s (1987) lens, sense is a fluid  and complex and involves  various zones with evolving steadiness. Meaning can be seen as one of these zones of the sense (Vygotsky, 1987, p. 276). In this manuscript, we assume that teacher identity development requires sensemaking and movement along  zones of sense  which takes place at the collective level. Meaning is the most consistent of these zones. The actual meaning of the word does not change rapidly (Vygotsky, 1987, p. 275).

We propose to pay special attention in research to the interaction between language  and collective moments in action involving human beings  (see Penuel & Wertsch, 1995)  in the student teachers' identity development. Alongside language, in this manuscript, we see multimodal artifacts has having an important role in sensemaking and therefore in the development of identity. We propose that we can use what has been referred to as identity artifacts (Leander, 2002), or “documents created by the learners about themselves, in which they try to capture all the things that make sense and are m eaningful to them and which, subsequently, can be u sed by teachers to work on curricular and pedagogical cont ent” (Subero et al., 2018, p. 156), to learn about  teacher candidates professional identity development (Leander, 2002). We propose that to understand language and material tools and their relationship, we must trace various social, discursive, and material means involved in the student teachers ' sensemaking. These different material means can hence help us in understanding identity learning Our interest lies also in identity development depicted in teacher candidates’ conversations about teaching  and activities that might move beyond the standard curriculum.

Alongside we propose to study how metaphors around teaching a nd learning can be used as tools for sensemaking about curriculum.

## Conclusions and future possibilities

The collective learning process that connects actua l and designated identities as teacher candidates a re in their program of studies, is not very well understood. This manuscript proposed to illuminate the process of integration of various knowledges from courses and student teaching experiences that teacher candidates experience as they progressively identify as teachers. This context meaningfully con tributes to the 2025 ICLS’ meeting theme that invites us to “explore learning across diverse cont exts” and “deepen our understanding of the processe s, tools, contexts, and outcomes associated with learning in all its forms”. The mansucript  suggest the potential of embodied and multimodal identity texts and of prese nt experiences while highlighting the need to empha size the role of past experiences in one’s teaching, an aspe ct that enhances our understanding on how to prepar e teachers for complex classroom contexts.
