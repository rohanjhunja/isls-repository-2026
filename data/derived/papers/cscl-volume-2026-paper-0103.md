# The Relationship between Argumentative Discourse Sequences with Generative AI and Students’ Argumentative Writing Structure

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster examines how sixth-grade students' argumentative discourse with generative AI relates to the structure of their subsequent argumentative writing. Using Pragma-dialectical stages for discourse and Toulmin elements for essays, the study found that AI-mediated dialogue was dominated by confrontation-oriented claim and rebuttal sequences, while students' essays showed stronger transfer of data and backing than rebuttal, warrant, or qualifier use. The findings suggest that generative AI can support evidence-based argument writing when classroom prompts explicitly scaffold warrants, qualifiers, and reflection on opposing positions. Key words: Generative AI, argumentative discourse, AI-learner interaction, sequence analysis, writing transfer

Generative AI may function as a dialogic partner that prompts learners to state positions, respond to counterarguments, and elaborate evidence. This study asks: (1) What Pragma-dialectical sequence patterns appear in students' argumentative discourse with generative AI? (2) How are Toulmin elements distributed in their argumentative writing? (3) How are discourse sequence patterns related to writing structure?

## Literature review

Pragma-dialectics Theory for Argumentative Discourse Analysis Pragma-dialectical theory treats argumentation as an interactive process moving through confrontation, opening, argumentation, and concluding stages. This lens is useful for analyzing AI-mediated dialogue because it captures how learners initiate disagreement, negotiate standpoints, and respond to challenges. Toulmin's Argumentative Model for Analyzing Writing Structure Toulmin's model identifies claim, data, warrant, backing, qualifier, and rebuttal as core elements of argumentative structure. Prior writing research shows that students commonly produce claims and evidence but need support to articulate warrants, qualifications, and counterargument responses. Generative AI in argumentative discourse and writing Recent studies suggest that generative AI can promote higher-order thinking by asking students to justify, compare, and revise ideas. However, less is known about whether discourse patterns formed with AI transfer into students' independent argumentative writing, particularly in elementary classrooms.

## Method

Twenty sixth-grade students in South Korea participated in six sessions using ChatGPT to discuss controversial issues. Students acted as discussants, while ChatGPT provided prompts, counterarguments, and follow-up questions. This poster reports preliminary analysis of four students' six-session logs and their argumentative essays.

Discourse was coded at the utterance level using Pragma-dialectical stages, and essays were coded at the sentence level using Toulmin's six elements. Two trained coders independently coded the data and resolved disagreements through discussion.

## Preliminary results

Across 95 utterances, discourse was dominated by Confrontation (84 instances, 88.4%), followed by Argumentation (9, 9.5%) and Opening (2, 2.1%). The most frequent 3-gram sequence was ConfrontationConfrontation-Confrontation, indicating repeated cycles of position-taking and rebuttal rather than progression through all stages of critical discussion.
