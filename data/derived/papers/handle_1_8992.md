# UTeach/NYC: A Research Practice Partnership to Expand and Improve Computer Science Education for All

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This 3-year Design-Based Implementation Research (DBIR) study focuses on a Research Practice Partnership (RPP) that encourages broad student participation in computer science (CS), looking at underrepresented urban youth in New York City. In particular, the research examines what organizational conditions, processes, and supports are associated with improved participation by Black and Latino/a students in CS, as measured by the diversity of students enrolling in CS courses. Results indicate an increase in trust has been reliant on the strides made from the partners involved, as well as building upon the RPP framework. Improved communication has resulted from continuity of meetings, regular exchanges of email, and having detailed agendas and expectations for the partners involved.

## Introduction

Key to this work is imagining new ways of conductin g inquiry; cultivating partnerships, working with communities as authentic partners and engaging in l arger-scale research practices, such as multi-sited ethnographies, accessing population level data, uti lizing new forms of data and data analytics, integr ating data sources that intersect with education and developmental stages to inform the design of classrooms, schools, and systems in the service of equity. We report on a Research Practice Partnership (RPP) between The University of Texas Austin (UT Austin) and New York City (NYC) public schools to examine the degree to which co-design of curriculum, instructional practices, and teacher supports can build organizational capacity to support equitable computer science (CS) teaching and learning as called for by the CSforAll national movement. This study focuses on remaining questions about the organizational con ditions and instructional practices that encourage broad student participation in CS, looking at underrepres ented urban youth. This paper will focus on the fir st of the research questions from the grant: What organizatio nal conditions, processes, and supports are associa ted with improved participation by Black and Latino/a students in CS, as measured by the diversity of students enrolling in CS courses?

## Perspectives/Theoretical Framework

Education researchers have long attempted to bridge  the divide between research and ongoing challenges  of practice faced by educators through pedagogy, techn ology, and methodology innovations (Anderson 2012; Koehler et al., 2007). Impediments to the successfu l development and implementation of innovations hav e hampered and stifled innovations from being adopted to support students (Potvin et al., 2018). Most ob viously, educators and administrators in K-12 schools and di stricts may lack the expertise to properly implemen t technological innovations to the rationale that stems from research for improving teaching and learning (Louie & Buffington, 2018).

## Methods

Methodologically, a Design-Based Implementation Research (DBIR) approach was employed to address the three overarching research questions. In addition, using Penuel and Gallagher’s (2017) design template to ef fectively design curriculum adaptively across levels and sett ings, partners developed curriculum using an iterat ive and collaborative design process from joint feedback between UTeach CS teachers, NYC DOE, UTeach curriculum designers, and researchers.

The project implementation and impact section aimed to capture significant accomplishments over the last academic year, including a success story and m ajor activities completed within the year. The next  question addressed whether or not the professional development and/or the curriculum was changed for the past academic school year, including changes made to address diversity and inclusion. The third question in this section asked to describe the relationships among RPP members, sp ecifically, a sense of trust, open communication, a nd working together to build the capacity of educators and researchers. The fourth question in this section aimed to answer whether any other outcomes have resulted fro m the project to date. The fifth question in this s ection addressed primary barriers and challenges in the project, and how they were addressed-both outside of COVIDICLS2022 Proceedings 1898 © ISLS 19 and related to COVID-19. And lastly, the evaluators aim to understand the plans and goals for the next year of the project.

The Starting Strong unit is used to introduce the course to students, embed non-cognitive strategies, activities, and supports in the curriculum, provide for more student choice in course activities and projects (more differentiation) and add career content to course materials. Starting Strong teacher materials prepare them for the course, classroom set up, strengthen how culturally-relevant pedagogy is addressed, provide more examples, howto videos, and student work examples spanning range of performance, improve pedagogical support content for teachers, especially Project-Based Learning (PBL).  Embedding CS background content in teacher materia ls strengthen opportunities for students to address pe rsonally relevant problems, and improves access to course content for English Language Learners (ELL) students (specifically in Big Data and Internet units). Additionally, teachers participate in an intensive, in-person, su mmer PD, and are provided in-person support session s throughout school year, designed to foster community-building, create opportunities to collaborate, and provide on-demand supports. Researchers are collecting data on student interest, engagement, and mindset and d ata are immediately returned to teachers so that they can a dapt instruction accordingly. Researchers also facilitate unit debrief sessions with teachers to review and discuss student data and outcomes.

## Results

Three years of Advanced Placement (AP) CS exam results, provided by the College Board, show that students in UTeach CS Principles courses nationwide include mor e females and other underrepresented students and t hese students performed slightly better than national av erages. In total, 75% of UTeach students passed the  exam compared with 72% nationally, and female students passed at a rate of 75% compared with 69%. Pass rate s for African American and Hispanic students in UTeach we re 43% and 57%, compared with national pass rates o f 41% and 55%. Finally, 62% of underrepresented students overall in UTeach courses passed compared with 55% nationally. Both females and underrepresented students comprised a higher proportion of the total number of exam takers in the UTeach CS principles (CSP) group compared with national participation rates. The progress made over the course of the RPP is ref lected in the interview/survey responses from the third-year evaluation. Respondents noted that there  is an increase in trust from all key stakeholders,  overall positive responses, improved communication, and “more of a solid team.” An increase in trust has been reliant on the strides made from the partners involved, as well as building upon the RPP framework (Penuel & Gallagher, 2017). The improved communication of has resulted f rom continuity of meetings, regular exchanges of em ail, and having detailed agendas and expectations for the partners involved. An increase in flexibility throughout the 2020-2021 academic year was outlined by the respondents in their role of supporting teachers during this unique year. Additionally, respondents provided recommendations for the upcoming academic year: to elevate th e role of the practitioner, continue to receive and enact PD feedback, and for the practitioners and researchers to continue to work closely together.

## Significance of the Study

This project will contribute to knowledge about eff ective organizational conditions, as well as instru ctional interventions to promote participation, engagement,  and learning across diverse groups of students in AP CS Principles, the professional learning and support necessary to prepare teachers with diverse content backgrounds to teach CS, and the strategies employed to address problems of practice through collaborative, iterative design.
