# Taking the Lead: Exploring Collaborative Initiative in CSCL Contexts

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL) environments, students' ability to engage proactively is crucial for effective collaboration and problem-solving. This study reexamines the concept of initiative in CSCL contexts, redefining it as collaborative initiative, which refers to students' spontaneous and voluntary contributions that are intended to enhance peer interaction and learning experiences. Building upon existing frameworks, we propose a refined model that emphasizes unprompted, proactive student actions categorized into task-related and dialogue-based initiatives. Using the HoloOrbits simulation, designed for augmented reality headsets to support student learning about planetary motion, we analyzed interactions among 12 university students. Our findings delineate six sub-facets of collaborative initiative, highlighting how students propose strategies, share divergent ideas, and invite peer contributions without external prompting. The study highlights the potential of fostering collaborative initiative to advance collaborative problem-solving and enhance the effectiveness of CSCL environments.

In computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL) environments, active student engagement is critical for effective problem solving. A key component of this engagement is initiative—the spontaneous and unprompted contributions that drive dialogue forward beyond scripted interactions (Nickerson, 1976). Although prior research has highlighted the importance of initiative (Yang & Heeman, 2010; Nouri & Traum, 2014, Howard et al., 2017), existing studies often overlook the unprompted nature of what we term collaborative initiative. This research reexamines initiative as proactive student actions that enhance both task progression and dialogue during collaborative problem solving (CPS).

A review of the literature on collaborative dialogues reveals two primary facets of initiative: task initiative, which involves guiding collaborative activities through the formulation of strategies and plans, and dialogue initiative, which focuses on managing conversation dynamics and soliciting peer input (Chu-Carroll & Brown, 1997). However, existing frameworks, such as those offered by the OECD’s PISA (2017), Hesse et al. (2015), and Sun et al. (2020), primarily emphasize structured task performance, often overlooking the spontaneous, interactive aspects of collaboration. In response, our research seeks to refine the concept of initiative in CSCL by categorizing the forms of initiative students demonstrate during CPS into task-related and dialoguebased contributions. This study examined collaborative initiative in an immersive CSCL environment using HoloOrbits—a simulation designed to teach planetary motion through augmented reality on Microsoft HoloLens2. Twelve participants (six dyads) from a Midwestern university took part in one-hour sessions conducted in a laboratory setting. Within this controlled laboratory setting, the tasks were designed to elicit unprompted collaborative behaviors. Participants engaged in a series of integrated activities—beginning with an introductory exercise on elliptical orbits, followed by an immersive AR simulation, and culminating in collaborative planning, data collection, and analysis. Audio recordings of the CPS tasks were transcribed and analyzed using an iterative, phronetic coding approach (Tracy, 2013). We began by identifying key terms and phrases, which were then grouped into categories reflecting task-related and dialogue-based initiatives. The coding process achieved an inter-rater reliability (kappa > 0.76), ensuring the consistency and reliability of our findings.
