# Analyzing Teacher Learning in a Research Practice Partnership

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Teachers are increasingly participating in RPPs with the goal of improving their schools and districts. This context provides teachers with unique learning opportunities as they directly take part in school improvement work, yet we know little about what teachers learn from their involvement in RPPs. This study explores teacher learning over the course of the first year of a multiyear RPP with a team of researchers from a local university.

## Introduction

Research Practice Partnerships (RPPs) are long-term  collaborations between practitioners and researche rs in which the collective engages in school improvement work by investigating persistent problems of practi ce and solutions (Coburn & Penuel, 2016). RPPs are increasingly utilized for school improvement purposes because they address local problems of practice and enable greater use of research in decision making (Coburn & Penuel, 2016). Despite a growing body of literature and rise in popularity of RPPs, there is a need for more detailed research on the processes and outcomes of these partnerships (K lar et al., 2018). What teachers learn from partici pating in RPPs is one such outcome that requires more research. Previous studies have focused on the learning of district leaders (e.g., Rigby et al, 2018), but few previous  studies have focused on teacher learning within RP Ps. Since RPPs offer teachers the opportunity to engage in di strict-wide instructional improvement work, this ra ises the question of teacher learning in such a context. In this study, we investigate what teachers learn from  these partnerships by examining the following research qu estion: How does teacher’s reasoning about improvem ent work shift over time as they engage in RPP work?

## Conceptual Framework

This study is grounded in a situated theory of learning which identifies learning as shifts in participation within a community of practice (Lave & Wenger, 1991). A community of practice is a group that collectively engages in a shared repertoire of routines as they work toward s a common goal (Wenger, 1998). In this study, we e xplore how teachers’ participation patterns shift over time as they engage in a routine of school improvement meetings. Through these regular meetings, a community of practice is formed between the participants. We define the teachers’ participation in this community of practice as their contributions to the collective discussions. More specifically, we conceptualize these contributions as episodes of improvement work reasoning (EIWRs), an adaptation of Horn’s (2007) episodes of pedagogical reasoning (EPRs). Horn (2007) defined EPRs as detailed instances of teacher reasoning, focused  on issues or questions about teaching practice. We  define EIWRs as episodes of elaborate reasoning about the purposes, obstacles, and methods of school improvem ent work. We make this distinction as teachers are part icipating in discussions about district-wide instru ctional improvement and not teaching practice.

## Methodology

This study draws on a case study approach (Yin, 201 4) and employs an embedded multiple-case study desi gn. The data for this study comes from a larger multiye ar RPP project in which a team of researchers from a large Midwestern university collaborates with several local K-8 school districts with the aim of investigating issues and solutions surrounding math education within each di strict. A subgroup of members from each of these te ams meets regularly to plan the work of the respective whole RPP team. This study centers on these subgroup meetings because they prompt teachers to engage in rich disc ussions as they engage in meaning making (Horn, 200 7) surrounding instructional improvement work. The four teachers on the Bay School District subgroup are the focus of this study. The district’s subgroup also include s a district administrator, one university research er, and one university-affiliated math coach, all of whom also engage in the subgroup’s discussions. Data Sources and Analysis This study utilizes four data sources: (a) 26 hours of video recordings of 15 subgroup meetings, (b) transcripts of subgroup meetings, (c) transcripts of initial inter views, (d) transcripts of end-of-year interviews. T he unit of analysis for this study is individual EIWRs shared by teachers during subgroup meetings and interviews. First, EIWRs were identified in each data source. Then, EI WRs were coded by topical content so that similar t hemes ICLS2022 Proceedings 1978 © ISLS can be compared over time (Horn, 2007). Several categories of EIWRs emerged, such as problem identification, norms, roles, data use, and solution formulation, amongst others. Due to space limitations, only a small subset of EIWRs concerning problem identification is presented here.

## Findings

Preliminary analysis indicates teachers’ reasoning about relevant problems of practice shifted over time (see Table 1). In initial meetings, as the subgroup laid the f oundation for improvement work, they discussed prob lems associated with the district’s math program. During  this phase, each of the teachers pointed to a diff erent issue they thought contributed to unsatisfactory outcomes of the math program. In the first meeting, Lenny brought up the lack of cross-grade level collaboration opportunities for teachers. During the third meeting, Nicole spoke about low test scores, while Frankie highlighted the lack  of math interventionists and coaches. During subse quent meetings, as they continued to engage in discussion surrounding problem identification, all three teachers zeroed in on the fact that the district’s teachers lacked a uniform understanding of high-quality math tasks. Thus, as the teachers engaged in RPP work, their reasoning about  issues associated with the quality of their math p rogram shifted, becoming more unified and clearly defined.
