# Black Prompts not Included: AI Bias Impeding Real-Time Co-Creative Ideation in Black Students' Conceptual Design

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This research paper investigates using artificial intelligence (AI) and image generators to foster rapid ideation among Black design students. It centers on Devonte, a Black undergraduate game design student, and his experiences using image generators as a co-creative tool for designing game characters. Pilot study finds racial and cultural biases in image generators, hindering culturally relevant designs. These biases not only challenge Devonte's creative process but also underscore the broader issue of inclusivity in technological tools. The paper advocates for further discussion and research on addressing these biases, emphasizing the need for image generators that support the diverse cultural backgrounds of users. This is crucial for unlocking the full creative potential of Black students in STEM spaces and ensuring equitable access to technological advancements.

## Introduction

This pilot study examines the use of image generators as tools in collaborative design, particularly for enhancing creative ideation. It also addresses the significant challenges Black students face,  where racial and cultural biases in image generators outputs create friction in the creative process, a consequence of the image generators' training datasets (Field et al., 2021; Navigli et al., 2023). This pilot explores the challenges faced by Devon te, a Black undergraduate design student, in creating character  designs. Devonte frequently has to modify his prom pts to counteract the biased outputs from Midjourney, which often fail to reflect his cultural aesthetics. By documenting Devonte's continuous prompting for race, the pilot study asks the question: how do the inherent biases in image generator outputs affect the creative processes and satisfaction levels of B lack students in their design projects? Addressing this question we explore the influence of AI biases on the creative experiences of Black students and identify productive approaches for enhancing the in clusivity and effectiveness of AI tools in diverse educational settings.

## Theoretical framework

Image generators, serving as innovative tools for e xperimentation and enhancing rapid creativity and d esign processes, come with inherent biases that can impede creativity (Fraser et al., n.d.; Gallegos et al., 2023; Gupta et al., 2022), a concept further explored through the case study of Devonte. Image generators are advance d AI algorithms that process and generate human-like images, video, and other media by analyzing va st datasets (Navigli et al., 2023). Unfortunately, they often reflect the biases pres ent in their training data, raising concerns about equitable representation and cultural sensiti vity in educational applications (Bender et al., 2021). These biases in image generators can lead to skewed content generation and interpretation. Their potential is to overcome initial creative roadblocks, as well as help users develop their computational thinking and creativity  skills in connecting them to practical applications (Cave et al., 2020; Kim et al., n.d.) by using co-creative AI tools allowing users to collaborate with machines in rapid cycles of creative generative design (EL-Zanfaly et al., 2022; Kim et al., n.d.; Lopes et al., n.d.; Rezwana & Maher, 202 2; Urbandavis et al., 2021). At their core, many toolkits designed for novices using image generators focus on providing learners with the tools to "tinker" with AI through the process of co-creative AI. This process allows users to collabora te with a machine on generative design by contributing a set of parameters (Kim et al., n.d.; Urbandavis et al., 2021). This pilot project demonstrates how image generators can be used for collaborative AI e ngagement. In our scenario, students are utilizing the image generator Midjourney, where the visual models, prim arily trained on Western art and photo sets, influe nce the outputs for their input prompts (Murphy, 2023). These categories emphasize the use of computers i n the creative design process. However, a limitation of this approach is that the cultural bias embedded (Fraser et al., n.d.; Jo & Gebru, 2020) in these models makes it difficult to advocate for their use as equitable co-creative collaborators. Centering collaborative human-computer interaction in the ideation and design pro cess for Black students necessitates a deeper understanding of co-creative partnership. There is a significant advocacy for the retraining of language models, both linguistic and visual (Ben der et al., 2021; Gallegos et al., 2023; Jo & Gebru, 2020). Academics and creative advocates emphasize that bui lding these models without removing bias can exacer bate long-term issues, making it challenging to continually i ncorporate impartiality in informal STEM learning environments, thus limiting their potential.

## Methods: Context, participants, and data

Devonte, an undergraduate design student, used AI s oftware to ideate and improve his original video ga me characters over two design sessions that lasted thr ee days in the spring of 2023. Our pilot study eval uated the impact of an AI-integrated co-creative arts program on students like Devonte. We used arti fact and content analysis to evaluate Devonte's skill development, problem-solving strategies, and artistic expression by examining his design portfolio, digital creations, and specific projects. We took screenshots of his original hand-drawn digtla sketches and recorded a screencast of him entering the projects into the system and recording the output. Content analysis of his written reflections, artist stateme nts, and digital interactions provided insight s into his thought processes and learning experiences. We also analyze d his interactions with image generators through hi s prompt logs, gaining a deeper understanding of his engagement with AI in creative design.

## Discussion and future research

The insights gained from Devonte's experience in AI-assisted design highlight crucial implications for future research, particularly in how we approach the devel opment of co-creative AI models for Black students. This research underscores the importance of creating AI tools that not only facilitate creativity but also ensure cultural connectivity in their outputs, resonating with the users' identities and experiences. Future studies should focus on understanding the unique cultural contexts and experience s of Black students, a process requiring fieldwork into their home and peer environments to gather rich, cu lturally relevant data that can be integrated into AI models (with associated privacy safeguards).  Such an approach ensures that the AI tools develope d are not only technologically advanced but are also deeply rooted in the cultural nuances and identities of the students they are designed to assist.

In our upcoming research, we are committed to devel oping AI models that are both co-creative and culturally sensitive. Our goal is to enhance the cr eativity of Black students in STEM fields, increasi ng their motivation and involvement in professional activ ities. We recognize that current systems are not wi thout flaws, especially concerning harmful social and racial bia ses. Therefore, we will focus on addressing these b iases to ensure that our AI models produce outputs that are not only innovative but also culturally resonant. T his work is essential for establishing a more equitable and inclusive educational technology landscape.
