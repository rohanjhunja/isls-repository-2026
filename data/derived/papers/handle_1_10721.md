# A Comparison of Deep Learning and Supervised Machine Learning Methods in RF tracking for Neurodivergent Learners

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Representational Flexibility (RF) is an individual's cognitive ability to select, coordinate and create alternative representations during information processing. Students with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) typically lack representational flexibility and therefore are underrepresented in the fields of STEM. Research has used virtual reality-based (VR) 3D simulations to promote RF development of ASD adolescent learners. However, to promote RF development, the need to be able to track RF throughout the intervention is critical. In this paper we report RF tracking in a VR-based learning program intended for RF development. We studied the performance of state-of-the-art deep neural network models to track RF subsets throughout the intervention. We trained these models, along with multiple classical machine-learning classifiers, using multimodal data collected from 209 sessions with ten ASD adolescents. Our empirical analysis shows promising evidence that deep learning can be used to track the cognitive-affective states of learners with ASD in real time.

## Introduction

Individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) often experience developmental delays leading to cognitive and social skill challenges that cause difficulty in ST EM education. Consequently, students with ASD are a n underrepresented group in STEM education (Ehsan et al, 2018). Representational flexibility, which incl udes the facility to use multiple external representations of information and the ability to switch flexibly between them, is a critical index for the practices of computational thinking and problem solving in the STEM domains. However, the development and enactment of representational f lexibility is effortful and demanding for most lear ners, but particularly so for students with ASD. Prior research observed that compared to their non-autistic peers, students with autism and a high heterogeneity, experienced d ifficulty in cognitive shifting, holistic processin g, and categorical induction (e.g., encoding general simil arity and thematic relations). Yet cognitive traini ng that aims to promote RF among heterogeneous learners with special needs is limited. Recent research has introduced virtual reality (VR) -based training to foster ASD students’ RF developme nt (Ke et al, 2020, Moon  et al, 2022). VR enables students to perform various 3D design tasks, which can foster RF practices. Given that ASD stu dents’ cognitive-affective states are dynamic, an essential component of VR-based training is the ability to track the RF state of learners in order to provide them with timely and adaptive learning s upport. However, there is a lack of empirical research tracking ASD students’ RF or oth er cognitive skills development during a longitudin al intervention. This study is part of a longitudinal project aimed at designing and studying individuali zed training of RF for computational and scientific problem solving among adolescents with ASD. Specifically, this training constitutes an adaptive learning program in a Collaborative Virtual Environment (CVE), called Force and Motion

– Adaptive Representation (FM-AR). To address individual learners’ needs rapidly and adaptively, we aim to

develop prediction models that track students’ RF d evelopment using deep neural network driven classif iers, trained on the different modalities of data that can be extracted from the VR Sessions.

## Literature review

Latent knowledge estimation using educational data Several machine learning techniques, such as clustering, classification, text mining, social network analysis, have been used in educational data mining to extract sal ient relationships (or knowledge discovery) from th e educational dataset (Khan, et al, 2021). Recently, deep learning (DL) techniques have been used for knowledge tracing or predicting students’ knowledge or skill development based on their learning activity (Piech et al, 2015). For example, Sharada et al. modeled students’ knowl edge retention using deep learning and random forests with a dataset collected from a web-based mathematics tutor (Sharada et al, 2018).  However, research is lacking on using deep learning for latent knowledge estimation in a highly interactive learning environment, which is distinct from the type of educational measurement with tests  or performance data from a learning management sys tem. The distinction is in that latent knowledge is situ ated in learner actions/interactions and is dynamic ally changing while it is being measured (Slater et al, 2017). The few studies examining the DL techniques for latent knowledge estimation reported mixed results of the performance of DL models in comparison with tra ditional machine learning techniques (Wang et al, 2017).

Representational flexibility in STEM education Integrated STEM education requires students to gath er and analyze data to understand the characteristi cs of a design challenge and its solution, as well as coord inate multiple representations of the solution thro ugh iterative testing and prototype revisions. Research suggests that RF is related to general STE M competences (Pande et al,

2017) and the struggle of ASD students in integrated STEM education has been attributed to their low RF (Moon,

2020). ASD adolescents may experience learning-related stress due to low levels of inhibition, attention switching as well as disengagement and distraction when encou ntering overwhelming obstacles. Using VR to support inquiry-based, design problem-solving tasks can address these difficulties as wel l as foster a deep understanding of integral STEM concepts, as studies have shown that immersive virtual environments are generally effective for enhancing the social, emotional, and cognitive skills of the individuals with ASD (Li et al, 2023).

## Method

FM-AR data In this paper, we present the data collected from t raining sessions with ten adolescents with ASD in t he FM-AR program. Building on previous work, we designed this CVE and its inquiry-based cognitive skills training program to engage ASD learners in a range of 3D-represented engineering design quests. Utilizing pe rsonalized learning and constructionism, FM-AR trains participants how to design and program motion and logic-based simulations, ranging from interactive objects to computerized ch aracters, in an o pen-source, Open Simulator-supported, 3D CVE. FM-AR is aimed to facilitate participants’ RF, or thei r ability to select, connect, convert, and construc t multimodal representations of concepts from physics and math, while practicing computational thinking (CT, e.g., sequencing, iteration, events, and conditionals). FM-AR offers a variety of scenario-based modules with specific design tasks. In this paper we will discuss the res ults derived from training sessions from two specif ic design quests or modules, Bridge Construction and NPC Desi gn. Examples of the activities performed in both modules can be seen on Figure 1. All ten of the program participants completed the  first module, Bridge Construction, within a minimum of 8 sessions (1–1.5 hour per session), with some participants completing it within 20 sessions. Three of the 10 participants expressed interest in completing a second module and chose NPC Design, completing it within a minimum of 15 sessions. All these activities resulted in 209 completed sessions from all 10 participants. From each one of the 209 session, audio and screen recordings as well as logged computational behavior s were non-intrusively collected and archived using the Opensi m server. The resulting texts were discretized into  fiveminute intervals, forming the text section of our dataset.  The behavioral logs were transformed to quantify t he frequency by which each behavioral feature occurs w ithin each 5 min interval while an audio sentiment analysis quantified the emotional sentiment of the participa nt within the same intervals. Together they formed the tabular section of our dataset. The resulting dataset contained 2203 data instances of text and tabular data. Figure 1 Examples of Module activities Constructing the bridge in the Bridge Construction module (left), using a 3D flowchart to visualize the NPC's “thought process” in the NPC Design Module (right) Labels and classifiers Based on a recent critical review of the literature, we defined four RF-oriented subset skills that we aim to track during these CVE-based learning sessions (Ke et al, 2020, Moon  et al, 2022): a ttention-switching/cognitive shifting (ACS), alternative representation (AR), pattern development (PD)  and pattern contextualization (PC).

Using both speech and log-data-mining techniques with supervised machine learning,  we tracked and collected study participants’ positive and negative enactment s of the aforementioned RF subsets throughout their CVE sessions. This expert labeling produced 8 labels ov erall, with 2 labels independently tracking the pos itive and negative enactments of each of the four RF subsets (e.g. ACS_N and ACS_P as negative and positive enactments of ACS). Building on the authors’ previous work with similar data (Sokolikj et al, 2023), multiple neural networkbased classifiers were developed for each type of data gathered, with the goal of being able to independently label each enactment of the fou r RF subsets using binary classification, given a p articular set of features, specifically the tabular and text data described in the previous  section. A multi-layered perceptron (MLP) consisting of multiple dense layers was developed for RF classifi cation using the tabular data. A deep neural networ k was developed for classification using the text data, u sing an initial pre-trained text embedding layer, specifically a token-based text embedding layer trained on English Google News  (Bengio et al, 2003). Add itionally, a deep neural network was developed using the preprocessor  and encoder from BERT, one of the more successful deep learning architectures in text mining (Devlin et al, 2019). Moreover, an array of classical machine learning methods was used for processing both types of data.  TFIDF was used to vectorize the text data for the classical machine learning algorithms.

## Results

In this paper, we report the results acquired from some of the classifiers outlined earlier. Specifically, among the classical machine learning methods, the two methods  with the highest AUC score will be considered. For  the tabular data analysis, the Naïve Bayes and Decision Tree methods performed the best among the classical machine learning methods while for the text data analysis, the K-Nearest Neighbors and  the Decision Tree method. The reported results displayed on Figure 2, show certain patterns in th e performance of the classifiers. Overall, the neural network-based classifiers perform better than the classical ones. In particular, the MLP perform better than the other classifiers used on the tabular data. Similarly for the text data, BERT has the best performance for all the positive enactments of the RF subsets while the Google Text classifier tends to perform best with the negative enactments. Overall, the higher AUC achieved by the neural netw ork-based classifiers again suggests that the neural network methods  are better able to handle  the inherent properties present  in the different modalities of extracted data, than any variation of the classical machine learning methods. ## Conclusion and future work

In this paper we present our work on developing an online virtual environment though which we would pr omote positive representational flexibility development in adolescent students with ASD. As the goal of the current CVE learning program is to promote pos itive RF development for our participating students, the ability to track their dynamic cognitive-affective states in real-time is essential.  We outline our efforts in devel oping state-of-the-art deep learning and machine learning models to achieve the ability to non-intrusively track the dynamic cognitiveaffective states of our par ticipating students in real-time. Our presented data is extracted from the 209 1-1.5hr sessions from participants who differed in age, education level as well as proficiency with CT and phy sics. The repeated voluntary attendance of these distinct ind ividuals shows that a CVE-based learning program can be adapted to be engaging for a variety of personaliti es. Our classifiers’ performance also shows a few d istinct patterns that can be observed when transitioning fr om different types of data modalities as well  as the varying types of classifiers that are used in their analysi s. Our presented work is just the initial steppingstone of a novel approach of latent knowledge estimation, and we bel ieve it shows promising evidence that latent knowle dge estimation can be achieved in real time in an online virtual environment. In our future work, we plan to explore and develop a deep learning architecture that can combine the different modalities of data extracted from our CVE in order to better track the dynamic cognitive-affective states of our session participants.
