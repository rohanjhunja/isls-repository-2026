# Learning Efficacy and Effect of Scaffolding in Online Engineering Education During COVID-19 Pandemic

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We explore the challenges of teaching "hardware-embedded Robotic Operating System (ROS) skills" online in a lockdown situation to participants through a hands-on competition. Teamwork is a vital skill in solving modern engineering problems. During Covid19 lockdown situations, the interaction between teammates significantly reduced compared to regular times. This prompted us to design an engineering problem statement with scaffolding that improves engineering education learning efficacy. A total of 465 teams (1860 participants) were introduced to a problem statement comprising of a autonomous robot navigation, manipulation and a perception task for an indoor unmanned ground vehicle. We study the performance of teams based on several factors such as participant's score, task's difficulty level, number of resources given and the nature of online sessions by instructors. We found the scaffolding approach helped teams in implementing a solution by exploring alternative methods and thus their knowledge.

## Introduction

e-Yantra Robotics Competition (eYRC) is an initiati ve hosted at Indian Institute of Technology Bombay to bring the experience of project-based learning to engineering participants using a competition to deliver handson training (Krithivasan, Arya, et al., 2014). In e YRC, participants are given a problem statement cal led a “theme” to be solved using a robot as a team of 4 undergraduate engineering students. In the past eight years of eYRC, teams collaborated on a hardware-based projec t physically coming together on their college campu s. This collaboration and other aspects of eYRC such a s hardware prototyping and fabrication have been challenged by the lockdown caused by the Covid-19 pandemic. The theme called “Sahayak Bot (SB)” was introduced to 1860 participants (465 teams) in 2020. The probl em statement was to program a simulated mobile cobot (a collaborative robotic arm mounted on an autonomou s ground vehicle) to assist in inventory packaging ac tivities (Bejarano, 2019). The mobile cobot assiste d in packaging by finding objects and moving them to the required location. The theme problem is divided into multiple tasks spread across six to eight months. The tasks are designed to train novice participants to program a simulated mo bile robot to find, identify, and move objects to d esired locations autonomously. The theme contains six task s which focus on getting familiar with Robot Operat ing System (ROS) (Quigley et al., 2009), Gazebo (Koenig  & Howard, 2004), control of mobile robots, mapping and navigation (Filipenko & Afanasyev, 2018), robotic arm manipulation (Chitta et al., 2012), and perception. The mobile cobot consisting of a Universal Robot, UR5 robotic arm mounted on a mobile base was modeled in a simulated environment.

Throughout six tasks, the participants were provide d with reading resources, live sessions, and discus sion forums to solve a problem statement. In the initial tasks, detailed support was given to ensure participants could gain momentum to tackle the challenges. As the participants progressed in tasks, support was gradually reduced to encourage independent exploration and learning. This approach was inspired by the widely used techn ique called “scaffolding” (Hammond & Gibbons, 2005) whic h refers to providing successive levels of temporar y support. This helps participants reach higher levels of comprehension and skill acquisition that they might not be able to achieve without assistance.

The challenge is to design a method for teaching sk ills required to work on industry grade mobile cobo t in a simulated environment. Our study analyses the perfo rmance of teams on factors such as interaction betw een teammates, scaffolding in a task's difficulty level, resources given and online interaction by instructors. The significant contributions of this paper are as follows: ¥ Designing a scalable methodology to teach the skills required to work on industrial grade mobile cobot. ¥ Investigation of learning efficacy in the proposed methodology. ¥ The effect of scaffolding in the proposed methodology is explored. The competition integrated hands-on engineering top ics as part of the learning activity, inspired by q uotidian activities in indoor environments. It was designed in a simulated environment and carefully broken dow n into several tasks to better understand the problem stat ement, as shown in Figure 2. After successful task implementation, the participants learned different robotics concepts inspired by this theme, and its e fficacy was analyzed based on the gathered data and insights.

The teaching resources in this theme were mdBook (K linke & Witzel, 2002), interactive live sessions, s urveys, and discussion. The mdBook consisted of resources, learning material, and references for each task. Participants post their queries on the Piazza forum for asynchronous discussion (Andresen, 2009). In surveys, questions related to the task were asked based on participants' exper ience and time invested in theme. Interactive sessi ons were conducted to understand participants' problems and provide hints to solve them. Quantitative analysis is based on data collected through surveys, tasks, forums, and learning materials. In contrast, the qualitative analysis relies on resource statistics, performance in each task, and interactive live sessions. ## Conclusion and Future Scope

The scaffolding approach encourages teams to work more efficiently by assigning roles and responsibilities that helps teams to find the best solution for a given problem. Based on the surveys and feedbacks, we analyzed that a step-by-step approach combined with temporary sup port that is eventually removed helps the teams gai n confidence in the skills developed through the task s in the competition. The effectiveness of teaching  such concepts can be tested further by designing a theme that will take the students from simulation to hardware. The challenges in converting the software in simulation  to code for hardware will thoroughly test how well  the participants have absorbed the concepts being taught through the tasks.
