## Introduction

The Knowledge Building Teacher Training Program in this study aims to equip pre-service and former educators with the skills and mindset required proficient in knowledge building education. Scardamalia and Bereiter (2014) believe that in knowledge building environment, learners are expected to take the responsibility for recognizing conceptual inadequacies, organizing activities that  induce cognitive conflict, and assessing results, with the support from teacher, technology, and peers. Recognizing that students in this program, including pre-service and former teachers, have unique teaching beliefs  and background, this study  emphasizes understanding students' learning process. A s an adaptive knowledge building program designer, providing a better and sustainable learning environment is very essential. By understanding students' initial comprehension of  knowledge building, designer can redesign the program to support the students’ needs. This allows for more customized and flexible improvements in the program’s activities and intera ctions to address different students' levels of comprehension, fosters and supports a sustainable knowledge building environment. Based on the research background, this study aims to understand students' comprehension of the c ommunity aspects of knowledge building (democratizi ng knowledge, symmetric knowledge advancement, knowled ge building discourse and community knowledge, collective responsibility) from the dimensions of knowledge telling, knowledge sharing and knowledge creation.

## Method

A qualitative method has been adopted to analyze the participants’ understanding of the four knowledge building principles (community aspect): democratizing knowledge, symmetric knowledge advanc ement, knowledge building discourse and community knowledge, collect ive responsibility. These are examined from the perspectives of knowledge telling, knowledge sharin g, and knowledge creation. Data were collected before the program started. A total of 55 participants answered the survey questions, which included a 7-points Likert scale and open ended queries about their understanding of  the principles. According to the research question, a coding scheme was used to categorize code participants’ understanding of knowledge building. The ranged from “not understand/irrelevant” to “knowledge telling ”, “knowledge sharing ” and finally “ knowledge creation ”. In this study, participants’ responses coded as “knowledge creation” indicate an understanding that encompasses both “knowledge telling” and “knowledge sharing” dimensions. Conversely, if a participant’s understanding is coded as aligning with the  “knowledge sharing ” dimension, it implies that the ir comprehension includes “knowledge telling” and “knowledge sharing” aspects. ## Conclusion

This study shows that understanding participants' views on the principles helps designer enhance the program. By analyzing these insights, designer can improve knowled ge building community dynamics for better discussio ns and allocate more time to less understood principle s. This approach enhances learning experiences and also ensures the program evolves in response to learners' needs, leading to a sustainable and engaging learning journey.
