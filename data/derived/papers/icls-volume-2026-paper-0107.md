# Developing Agency using Analytics-Supported Collective Reflective Assessment among PSTs

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This quasi-experimental study examined the impact of Analytics-Supported Collective Reflective Assessment (AsCRA) on pre-service teachers’ (PSTs) agency. The AsCRA tool was adapted with layered information visualization and step-by-step reflective scaffolds to address PSTs’ operational challenges and reduce information overload. Forty-seven PSTs in the experimental class completed knowledge building (KB) inquiry with AsCRA, while 40 PSTs in the comparison class used KB-based independent/collaborative reflec tive notes. Analyses of Knowledge Forum notes and group discussion data revealed the experimental class exhibited higher agency (participation, internal cohesion, responsiveness). AsCRA fostered agency via reflective inquiry, theory co-construction and sustained collaboration, with agency positively correlated to participation, novel contributions and communication efficiency. This study validates that adapted AsCRA enhances PSTs’ agentic, epistemic and disciplinary agency, providing practical implications for teacher education through structured collective reflection and collaborative KB environments.

The development of pre-service teachers’ (PSTs’) agency is vital for effective teaching amid ongoing educational reform. Modern education emphasizes critical thinking, collaboration, and self-directed learning, and PSTs with strong agency can better adapt to diverse classrooms and build inclusive learning environments (DarlingHammond, 2005; Hattie, 2009). Reflective assessment is a key driver of agency, as it enhances metacognition by supporting self-evaluation and learning goal-setting (Boud, 1995; Gibbs & Simpson, 2004). Analytics-supported collective reflective assessment (AsCRA) further improves traditional reflection with data-driven, precise feedback to guide proactive learning (Yang, 2019; Yang et al., 2016). Agency includes three interrelated dimensions: agentic engagement, epistemic agency, and disciplinary agency (Bae et al., 2024). Agentic engagement enriches learning meaning and predicts better academic outcomes (Reeve & Tseng, 2011; Jang et al., 2024), shaped by intrinsic motivation (Ryan & Deci, 2017) and classroom opportunities, while sociocultural research on this topic remains limited (DeCuir-Gunby & Schutz, 2014). Epistemic agency relies on collective knowledge co-construction (Damşa et al., 2010), and disciplinary agency refers to domain-specific cognitive practice (Lombardi et al., 2022), both rooted in social learning contexts. Knowledge-building (KB) design, which focuses on shared epistemic responsibility and collective idea improvement, effectively fosters agency (Scardamalia, 2002; Zhang et al., 2011), though challenges remain in clarifying epistemic agency (Oshima et al., 20 18). AsCRA integrates KB principles with tools such as PIT and KBDeX to visualize collaborative discourse, boosting content understanding and self/co-regulation skills (Lee et al., 2006; Yang, 2019).

This quasi-experimental study examines AsCRA’s effects on PSTs’ three dimensions of agency and the underlying mechanisms. Using data from Knowledge Forum notes and classroom discussions, it addresses three research questions:

(1) To what extent does AsCRA enhance PSTs’ collective knowledge building for agency?

(2) To what extent does AsCRA promote PSTs’ agency?

(3) How are PSTs’ collective knowledge building and agency related?

## Research context and participants

The study was conducted in the compulsory “Learning Sciences ” course at a Chinese university focused on teacher education. This course aims to develop undergraduates’ skills in scientific inquiry, argumentation, critical thinking, metacognition, and self-regulation through theory and hands-on activities. Eighty-seven PSTs 954 participated: 47 in the experimental class and 40 in the control class. The 19-week course met twice weekly (45 minutes per session).  A quasi-experimental design was used. The experimental class engaged in collective reflective assessment using data-driven forms in a knowledge construction environment, while the control class conducted individual or collaborative summary reflections based on KB principles. In both classes, students worked in groups of 5– 7 on semester-long inquiry projects. Consent was obtained, and participants could withdraw at any time.

Design of the intervention To apply the knowledge building (KB) model in practice, Chen (2011) proposed a four-stage implementation model, and Yang (2018) further developed a three-stage KB model integrated with reflective assessment. Guided by the 12 core KB principles and Yang’s (2018) model, this study designed a targeted intervention. The AnalyticsSupported Collective Reflective Assessment (AsCRA) tool was adapted for pre-service teachers: KBDeX visual data were layered into core and secondary indicators for clear presentation; reflective assessment was structured into three step-by-step scaffolds with instructor guidance to reduce operational complexity and information overload.

Instruction in the comparison class Under the guidance of the same teacher as the experimental class, PSTs in the control class explored the same topics and participated in cooperative inquiry under the knowledge construction teaching model. In Phase 1 and Phase 2, both classes performed the same tasks; it was not until Phase 3 that their work began to differ.  At this stage, the control class was encouraged to develop agency by creating summary reflection notes individually or collaboratively using knowledge construction principles. ## The Three-Component Pedagogical Design

Data sources and analysis Preprocessing knowledge forum notes To examine differences in knowledge-building (KB) actions between the experimental and comparison classes, we conducted a qualitative content analysis of discussion notes. We developed a coding framework based on prior studies of agency characteristics (Yang, 2019; Yang, Chen, et al., 2020; Yang, Chan, et al., 2024). The coding framework was revised and expanded for pre-service teachers’ agency, adding two specific indicators (reflective regulation of inquiry, collective theory co-construction) based on the characteristics of teacher education, which enriches the measurement of agency in teacher training contexts. Two researchers independently coded over 1,000 notes from the experimental class (representing >30% of all notes), achieving a Cohen’s kappa of 0.85, indicating high inter-rater reliability. Based on this coding, we calculated the proportion of higher-level KB actions and performed Chi-square analyses were conducted using SPSS 21.0 to identify significant differences between classes.

955 Analyzing agency actions and KB practices Building on the content analysis, we examined participants’ agency by comparing higher-level KB actions and sequential patterns of agency behaviors across classes. Lag sequential analysis (Bakeman & Gottman, 1997) was performed using the Sequences package in R, calculating z-scores to capture transitions between actions. A zscore above 1.96 indicated a significant sequential relationship. Changes in collective knowledge building (RQ1) Role-taking is central to understanding group cognitive behavior, as different role combinations can influence both group and individual learning outcomes. To explore micro-level social and cognitive patterns, we applied Group Communication Analysis (GCA; Dowell et al.,2020) to the discussion notes. An independent samples t-test was conducted to compare GCA metrics between the experimental and comparison classes, highlighting differences in collective knowledge building.

Effects of Analytics-supported Collective Reflective Assessment (AsCRA) on Agency (RQ2) Agency Differences Between Classes. We compared higher-level KB actions, actions of agency, and their sequential dependencies between the experimental and comparison classes. Chi-square tests assessed differences in action proportions, while lag sequential analysis identified significant tra nsitions between agency behaviors. Changes in Agency Over Time in the Experimental Class. To investigate the impact of AsCRA, we compared the experimental class’s agency actions before (Phase 1) and during (Phase 2) implementation, capturing temporal changes in engagement and reflective practices.

Relationships between collective knowledge building and agency (RQ3) To examine the link between PSTs’ collective KB and individual agency, Pearson correlation analyses were performed using SPSS 21.0. Each student’s agency contributions were quantified across dimensions, and correlations were assessed between individual agency scores and GCA metrics. Relationships between PSTs’ collective knowledge building and agency (RQ3) Correlation analysis revealed class-specific association patterns between GCA metrics and agency (Table 3). In the experimental class, agentic engagement correlated with participation (r=0.292) and internal cohesion (r=0.298); disciplinary and epistemic agency were strongly correlated with newness (r=0.412/r=0.478) and communication density (r=0.353/r=0.488), indicating higher-order agency relies on high-quality, efficient knowledge contribution. In the comparison class, epistemic agency correlated with participation (r=0.368), internal cohesion (r=0.463) and social impact (r=0.487), reflecting dependence on basic group interaction rather than contribution quality.

Overall, different agency dimensions are supported by distinct group communication features, and adapted AsCRA reshapes the association between collective knowledge building and high-order agency by prioritizing contribution quality and efficiency.

## Key findings interpretation

This study verifies that adapted AsCRA  (layered visualization + step-by-step scaffolds) significantly enhances PSTs’ collective knowledge building (participation, cohesion, responsivity, social impact) and three dimensions of agency, extending prior AsCRA research (Yang, 2019) by validating it s effectiveness in PST education with learner-specific adaptation. The results align with KB theory (Scardamalia, 2002) and Damşa et al. (2010), confirming structured, adaptive collaborative interaction fosters collective epistemic agency —with the adapted design solving the original AsCRA’s problems of high operational difficulty and information overload for PSTs. Mechanisms of AsCRA on PSTs’ agency Adapted AsCRA promotes agency through three PST-tailored mechanisms: 1) Layered information visualization reduces cognitive load, increasing reflective assessment participation; 2) Step-by-step reflective scaffolds (data reading→gap identification→action planning) guide active inquiry regulation, enhancing agentic engagement per Self-Determination Theory (Ryan & Deci, 2017); 3) Data-driven objective feedback targets collective reflection, facilitating theory co-construction and improving epistemic/disciplinary agency (Bae et al., 2024).

## Comparison with prior studies and contextual significance

This study advances existing research with two key innovations: 1) It fills the gap of AsCRA adaptive design for PSTs (most prior research focuses on in-service teachers or general higher education); 2) It combines GCA and lag sequential analysis to explore static correlations + dynamic transitions between collective knowledge building and agency, moving beyond static impact studies (Damşa et al., 2010). ## Implications for educational practice

First, integrate adapted AsCRA into PST core courses (e.g., Learning Sciences), combining online KB Forum reflection with weekly offline group discussions to foster collaborative reflective culture. Second, provide targeted scaffolds for PSTs (operation manuals, tool training) to reduce the learning curve of educational analytics tools, with step-by-step guidance for reflective assessment. Third, implement phased reflective assessment: focus on basic reflective behaviors in the early stage, and high-level agency coherence (via cognitive network analytics) in the middle/late stage, combining online and offline reflection.  At last, optimize group communication structures by aligning with agency-specific needs: boost participation/cohesion for agentic engagement, and encourage novel, efficient contributions for epistemic/disciplinary agency.

## Limitations and future research

This study has limitations: it relies solely on Knowledge Forum data, lacks personalized scaffolds for PSTs of varying operational proficiency, and has a small sample from a single Chinese normal university, limiting generalizability.

Future research will: 1) Conduct longitudinal studies to track PSTs’ agency development from pre-service to in-service teaching; 2) Test adapted AsCRA across different PST disciplines (Chinese, Math, Science) with discipline-specific scaffolds; 3) Expand the sample to multiple universities/regions in China (and cross-culturally) to improve generalizability; 4) Combine automatic and manual coding for more objective data analysis;

5) Design personalized adaptive scaffolds for PSTs with different cognitive and operational characteristics.
