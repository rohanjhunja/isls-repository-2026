# “Raise Up Radio: Creating a Field Guide for Libraries Serving Rural Communities.”

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The Raise Up Radio in Libraries (RURL) project was created to provide a learning platform for youth and families living in rural areas. In partnership with local libraries, the program works with youth in the community to produce STEM-related podcasts that are distributed via radio. To support and expand these efforts, team members will conduct a needs assessment and task analysis to develop a field guide for library professionals seeking to design radio programming in partnership with their local communities.

The raise up Radio program The Raise Up Radio in Libraries (RURL) program was created to provide a learning platform for youth living in rural areas. In partnership with local libraries, the program works with youth in the community to produce STEM-related podcasts that are distributed via radio. Radio is used as a means of distribution because of its low barrier of access for families in rural communities. In addition, the project will build infrastructure for participating libraries through collaboration and mentorship with family learning scholars at the University of North Texas and the University of Alabama. The program began in 2021 and is scheduled  to continue through August of 2024, with six libraries participating. To support and expand these efforts, team members will develop a field guide for library professionals seeking to design radio programming in partnership with their local communities. Following the application of the ADDIE instructional design framework, researchers will analyze the benefits and challenges the first cohort librarians encountered during the program  to discern what information and resources should be included in the field guide. ADDIE instructional design framework The ADDIE instructional design framework promotes the use of effective learning interactions with measurable learning goals (Hess & Greer, 2016). The model is often considered to be the most widely utilized framework within the field of instructional design and consists of five phases: • Analyze the identified problem or learning situation. • Design measurable objectives to address the problem or learning situation. • Develop resources that allow learners to meet objectives. • Implement the resources. • Evaluate how the resources address the needs of the learners (Hess & Greer, 2016). The ADDIE model was selected for this project to allow researchers to utilize an iterative design process, allowing RURL participants to provide feedback throughout the program. According to Hess and Greer, the “model’s wide applicability and recursive nature provides for a wide variety of uses for the library, especially in projects requiring ongoing assessment and evaluation to demonstrate progression on instructional goals” (2016, p. 268). Needs analysis The objective of the needs analysis is to determine the success and challenges that the librarians encountered during the program, identify the processes that were successful for program planning and design, and identify any challenges that need to be addr essed for future cohorts. To determine these objectives researchers will utilize several data collection methods, including a literature review of successful informal learning environments, interviews with program participants, and field notes of ongoing programs. A qualitative analysis of the data will 2339 dete rmine the resources and training sessions participants considered beneficial during the program, identify aspects of the program that need improvement, and provide insight into additional resources that need to be developed. Task analysis The task analysis will be used to identify previous instruction and resources that have been developed for topics such as community outreach, podcasting, and program planning. For example, the RURL program is based on the connected learning framework, which “advocates for broadened access to learning that is socially embedded, interest-driven, and oriented toward educational, economic, or political opportunity “ (Ito et al., 2013, p. 3). Connected learning is a relatively new learning theory and may require  instruction for librarians who are participating in the Raise Up Radio program. The Connected Learning Alliance (https://clalliance.org/) provides links to several open educational resource courses. The courses cover information concerning the principles of connected learning, strategies for creating connected learning programs, and suggestions to support creative learning. In addition, the task analysis should also identify gaps in available resources that need to be developed. For this program, new content should focus on applying connected learning concepts to programs specifically in libraries in rural areas. Until recently, the connected learning framework has been implemented in larger libraries in urban areas. Learner analysis The learner analysis will be used to identify the characteristics, competencies, and learning preferences of the participants of the RURL program. For this analysis, researchers will utilize the components of a learner analysis outlined in Design for Learning: Principles, Processes, and Praxis.   Fulgencio and Asino stated that the “key factors of the learner analysis to consider are learner characteristics, prior knowledge, demographics, and access to technology” (2021, p. 2).
