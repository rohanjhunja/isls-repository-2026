# Design of XR Technology to Prompt Deeper Psychomotor Skills Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Advanced extended reality (XR) technologies in psychomotor skill learning (PSL) offer promising implications to accelerate the learning process and enhance learning outcomes. The proposed prototype explores the intricate PSL mechanisms focusing on mental processing and motor body movements to achieve deeper PSL. The design of XR technology aligns with generative learning theory driving both mental processing and motor processing in PSL, with a goal to foster more effective and efficient PSL.

Psychomotor skills learning Psychomotor skills are physical behaviors requiring mental and motor processing (Miller et al., 1960; Schmidt,

1975) learned in hierarchical stages (Dave, 1970). Each psychomotor skill stage builds on the previous to achieve

mastery (Gagne, 1962; Romiszowski, 2009). Historically psychomotor skill learning (PSL) approaches have emphasized physical body movement while neglecting mental processing.  Mental processes are about learners engaging in a thinking process about how they are m oving and performing behaviors. Several motor learning theories have attempted to explain the mechanisms o f PSL, such as Fitts and Posner’s (1967) three-stage model, closed-loop theory (Adams et al., 1971), schema theory (Schmidt, 1975), dynamic systems theory (Kamm et al., 1990), and ecological theory (Turvey et al., 1995). However, these theories enlighten the motor processing but are limited in unpacking the relationships among mental  and motor processes which is key to the effective and efficient development of PSL. Understanding the mechanisms undertaken during mental and motor proce ssing for PSL can be informative to designing effective strategies that optimize practice and learning outcomes. Extended (XR) technology for psychomotor skills learning XR technology is rapidly accelerating to transform the physical fitness industry. XR technology is an umbrella term for any technology that alters reality by addi ng digital elements to the physical or real-world environment (Rauschnabel et al., 2022). XR technology includes Virtual Reality (VR), Augmented Reality (AR), and M ixed Reality (MR), each offering distinctive advantages for improving physical fitness training. By constructively using the sophisticated capabilities of XR technologies, learners can have the opportunity to deeply immerse and interact with the content (i.e., psychomotor task) (Jonassen, 199 5) through HMD headsets or handheld devices. Theoretically, XR technology can provide real-time instruction al prompts and immediate feedback regarding performance. XR features hold promise for PSL by providing a mechanism to prompt mental engagement and providing learners with enhanced visualization s, auditory, and haptic prompts and feedback, safe training environments, and real-time performance feedback. Despite the efforts made to motivate and immerse le arners using XR technology (i.e., VR exergames) to develop psychomotor skills, these programs often fail to engage the learners in the important mental and motor processing crucial for achieving d eeper levels of PSL. Principles of instruction can support the design of more effective XR-enhanced learning environments. Design considerations for effective PSL Generative learning theory (GLT) delves into the intricate processes of mental functioning and learning. Wittrock

(1992) suggested that c entral conceptualization of behavior patterns associate d with muscle activity, guided by

external sensory cues, can enhance motor performance. When learners continuously monitor and regulate their thinking process during learning they achieve more effective learning outcomes (Fiorella & Mayer, 2022). By actively selecting, organizing, integrating, and el aborating new information about their body movement  during psychomotor task learning, learners become more fully immersed in the content and construct complex knowledge structures (Jonassen, 1995), leading to the achievement of deeper PSL. GLT recommends that technology can aid in knowledge construction by guiding learners in interpreting, organiz ing, and integrat ing (generating) new information into existing mental structures (Duffy & Jonassen, 2013). Instruction integrating these technologies may be very effective and efficient in prompting learners to both physically interact with content while consistently engaged in mental processing of new information and skills during PSL (Koszalka et al., 2019). In an Augmented reality (AR) prototype,  for example,  the learner will see themselves overlaying a shadow image portraying the correct body movements  for say a  bicep strengthening exercise. The shadow demonstrates the desired body movement to guide actual body movement. As the learner moves correctly their image will move within the shadow and receive audio, visual, and/or haptic cues about the performance and whether it is within guidelines. If they move incorrectly or outside the shadow, cues will describe the error and provide feedback. These feedback mechanisms will pr ompt learners to construct and correct mental representations of the correct (and incorrect) body movement while they are performing and developing the skills. By supporting mental processing through audio, visual, or haptic means, this shadow or translucent figure shared via XR technology (i.e., AR mobile application) candemonstrate, monitor, prompt, affirm, and correct execution during PSL.

While several technologies claim to offer real-time analysis of a learner’s physical body movement with feedback mechanisms (visual, audio, or haptic), a knowledge gap exists regarding how feedback is provided, and which is most helpful to learners. Uncertainties re main concerning the nature of feedback provided (kn owledge of results or knowledge o f performance), timing of delivery, sensory modalitie s used (visual, audio, or haptic), and its effectiveness in guiding the learner during stages of PSL. The multi-sensory feedback may best be delivered by layering feedback through various modalities (vi sual, auditory, haptic). Tailoring the timing and type of feedback may prevent overreliance and cognitive overload seen in some immersive learning environments.

## Implication for instructional design in psychomotor skill learning with XR

Emerging XR technologies have the potential to revo lutionize psychomotor skills learning (PSL) and to help learners achieve deeper levels of PSL. To achieve this potential, it is important to create and te st XR-based PSL prototype environments that can focus learners, attention on PSL tasks by prompting them to both interact correctly in desired behaviors and consciously enga ge in mental processes  to support PSL. Such environments can guide learners in decomposing complex skills, providing visual guidance, and layering multiple forms of feedback to foster a deeper PSL. These features can facilitate the development of motor skills and mental processing. GLT can inform the design of XR technology to create an effective and efficient PSL environment, however, research is necessary to validate these th eories. Nonetheless, this proposed XR technology prototype represents a significant step forward in PSL instruction, leveraging emerging technologies to prompt deeper PSL.
