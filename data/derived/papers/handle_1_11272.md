# The Configuration Effect of Learner Characteristics on Learning Outcomes in Generative Artificial Intelligence Supported Environments

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
At present, there is a research gap on which learner characteristics may lead to high and low learning outcomes supported by generative AI. To this end, this study used Qualitative Comparative Analysis (QCA) to explore the configuration effects of learners' motivation, technological dispositions, and prior experiences on learning outcomes. This study involved 29 college students as participants, who were required to use ChatGPT to assist in the revision of English essays. The research identified 3 configurations leading to high learning outcomes and 2 configurations associated with low learning outcomes, shedding light on the complex effects of learners' characteristics in GAI-supported learning. The findings indicate that learners' perceived usefulness of GAI is a critical factor in determining learning outcomes, and strong learning motivation can mitigate the negative effects of a lack of prior experience.

## Introduction

Many studies indicated that incorporating GAI into e ducation was beneficial in terms of improving learn ing performance, enhancing creative problem-solving abilities, and cultivating students' critical and reflective thinking skills (Essel et al., 2024). Nevertheless, some studies also found that in som e cases, the utilization of GAI tools did not lead to better academic performance or high-quality learning outputs  (Abbas et al., 2024). In response to this contradiction, some researchers po inted out that the effectiveness of using new techn ologies depends on not only the technology itself, but also learners’ internal factors, such as technology acceptance, prior experience, and motivation, which have important im pacts on the learning outcomes in technology-supported learning (Huang et al., 2023). Hence, exploring which characteristics of learner s influence the outcomes in a generative AI-supported learning environment is of crucial signific ance for us to gain deep insights into the underlying mechanisms of how generative AI technology facilitates instruction and learning.

To address this research gap, this study investigat ed the performance of 29 university students in an English writing task by the Qualitative Comparative Analysis method. Specifically, we sought to investigate the following two research questions. RQ1: Which configurations of learner characteristics affect learning outcomes in generative AI-supported environments?  RQ2: Which learner characteristics play a more impo rtant role in enabling effective learning? How do these characteristics interact with each other?

## Literature Review

Factors influencing GAI-supported learning In technology-enhanced learning environments, learning outcomes a re not solely mediated by the technology itself. Learners' individual characteristics can significantly impact their learning behaviors, leading to differences in final outcomes (McGreevy & Church, 2020). Yang e t al. (2018) have pointed out that learners’ motiva tion, technological dispositions, and prior experiences i nfluence their behavioral, cognitive, and emotio nal engagement, which in turn affects their development  of knowledge, skills, and higher-level abilities. This study aims to explore the individual factors influencing GAI-supported learning from these three aspects. From the perspective of motivational characteristics, learning motivation not only plays a crucial role in improving academic performance but also serves as a dynamic factor influencing learners' perceptions, attitudes, and actual use of specific technologies (Kazu & Kuvvetli, 2023). Self-efficacy, control beliefs, and task value are three key components of motivation (Pintrich et al., 1993). Studies have shown that these motivation factors have a significant impact on learning outcomes  (Wang et al., 2023; Qu & Wu, 2024; Joo et al., 2013). From the perspective of technological dispositions, learners ' technology acceptance also plays an important rol e in influencing learning behaviors (Rajabalee & Santall y, 2021). Numerous studies have confirmed the posit ive impact of technological dispositio ns, especially perceived usefulness, on AI-supported learning. These studies suggested that perceived usefulness positively affe cts students' willingness to continue using the tec hnology (Rajabalee & Santally, 2021), leading to improved l earning outcomes (Chou et al., 2022). From the pers pective of prior experience, learners' existing experiences can also have a moderating effect on learning outcomes. Conole et al. (2017) pointed out that learning outcomes in technology-supported environments are not solely determined by the technology itself; learners' digital literacy also plays a crucial role. In summary, few studies have discussed the individual factors th at influence learning outcomes in GAI supported environments. A review of the literature suggests that learners' motivational characteristics (e.g., self-efficacy, task value, control beliefs), technological dispositions (e.g., perceived usefulness), and prior experiences (e.g., experience with generative AI) may all influ ence learning outcome s. Qualitative Comparative Analysis (Chen et al., 2022), which integrates the strengths  of both qualitative and quantitative research, can  explore potential combinations of antecedent conditions that explain specific phenomena, focusing on how things happen. Therefore, this study attempts to use fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) to exp lore the configurational effects of learner characteristics on the outcomes of GAI-supported learning.

## Participants and contexts

The participants of this study were 29 Chinese univ ersity students who were not majoring in English, i ncluding 11 males and 18 females. The learning context of this study focused on an English writing task with a total duration of three hours. After drafting their initial essays, participants used the inte grated ChatGPT tool within the online learning platform to optimize and revise their essays.

Research framework The antecedent variables in this study contain self-efficacy, task value, control belief, perceived use fulness, and GAI use experience (Figure 1). Among them, self-efficacy, task value, and control belief belong to the motivation dimension; perceived usefulness belongs to the tech nological dispositions dimension; and generative AI  use experience belongs to the prior experience dimension.

## Data collection and calibration

As this study is featured with small samples, continuous variables, and multiple factors affectin g the same outcome, fuzzy set QCA (fsQCA) was chosen as the an alytical method, and fsQCA 3.0 software was selecte d to analyze the data.  This study collected data on antecedent variables t hrough a questionnaire. The motivational characteristics section utilized three components f rom the Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionn aire (MSLQ): self-efficacy, task value, and control beliefs (Pintrich et al., 1993). The technology dispositions section adapted the scale developed by Davis (1989) based on the Technology Acceptance Model and designed a 6-item scale to evaluate perceived usefulness. In the prio r experience section, learners rated th eir frequency and proficiency of technology use based on their person al experiences.  Learning outcomes were defined as the outcome variable, measured by the scores assigned by teachers to the English essays written by the learners. Since the antecedent variables and outcome variable s in this study are continuous, this study employs direct calibration to determine three main anchor p oints, using the 95%, 50%, and 5% percentiles as th e full membership point, crossover point, and full non-membership point, respectively (Yan & Li, 2023).

Specifically, cond itions presented in parsimonious solutions are considered core conditions, while those found only in the intermediate solutions are treated as peripheral conditions. Configuration H1 (~SE*~CB*PU*UE):  This configuration consists of the combination of P U, UE, and the absence of SE and CB, all of which are core conditi ons. It indicates that regardless of learners' perc eption of the value of English writing tasks, those with prior ex perience of GAI tools and a positive attitude towar ds its usefulness can achieve ideal learning outcomes. Furthermore, even if without high self-efficacy or a belief in their control over learning achievements, learners can st ill achieve good results supported by their past GA I use experiences and technological inclination. Configuration H2 (SE*~TV*CB*PU*~UE): The core condition of this configuration is SE and UE, with TV, CB, and PU act ing as peripheral conditions that contribute to hig h-level learning. Unlike Configuration H1, this configurati on demonstrates that learners with high self-efficacy can overcome the limitations of lack of prior technical  experience. Their confidence in controlling learni ng performance and positive perceptions of technology' s usefulness can mitigate the negative effects of i nsufficient GAI use experience and task value. Configuration H3 (~SE*CB*PU*~UE): The core condition of Configuration H3 is PU and UE, with SE and CB serving as a periph eral condition. Similar to the previous two configu rations, Configuration H3 highlights the significance of per ceived usefulness for learning outcomes. It suggest s that regardless of whether students value the task or not, a high-level technology perceived usefulness combined with strong control beliefs over task outcomes, can lead  to successful learning, even though students have not previously engaged with GAI techniques. Moreover, the influence of perceived usefulness can somewhat mitigate the impact of low self-efficacy.

For low learning outcomes, the results identified two configurations, with an overall consistency of 0.901 (greater than 0.8) and a coverage of 0.440, explain ing 44.0% of the cases. In Configuration L1 (SE*~CB*~UE), the presence of SE, which is a peripheral condition, along with the absence of the core conditions CB and UE, collectively leads to low learning outcomes. It indicates that a weak sense of control over learning achievements, coupled with a lack of relevant GAI use experience,  can result in poor learning outcomes. In Configuration L2 (TV*~PU*UE), PU and TV are core conditions, while UE is a peri pheral condition. It involves the combination of TV, UE, and the absence of PU. The findings furt her emphasize the importance of perceived usefulnes s, suggesting that if learners fail to recognize the a dvantages of GAI techniques, they are unlikely to a chieve good learning outcomes in a GAI-supported learning task, despite having a high level of task value.

## Discussion and conclusion

This study employs the fsQCA method to analyze the s ynergistic effects of 5 antecedent variables (self-efficacy, task value, control beliefs, perceived usefulness, and GAI use experience) on learning outcomes suppor ted by GAI. The main findings of the study are as follows: Finding 1: GAI-supported learning can be influenced by multiple in dividual factors. The results of the necessary condition analysis indicate that both high and low learning outcomes supported by GAI are not caused by a specific variable. Learners' motivation, technological dispositions, and prior experience synergistically influence the final learning outcomes. The findings  of this study indicate that no single factor can f ully account for learners' outcomes in GAI-supported learning. Inste ad, learners' mot ivation, technological dispositions, and prior experience intertwine, producing either positive or negative effects on the final outcomes. Finding 2: Perceived usefulness serves as a key role in GAI-supported learning. The results of the configuration analysis indicate that in the pathway s leading to high learning outcomes, perceived usef ulness is a core condition in Configurations H1 and H3, while i t serves as a peripheral condition in Configuration  H2. Conversely, in the pathways leading to low learning outcomes, perceived usefulness is absent as a core condition in Configuration L2. For students achieving high le arning outcomes, perceived usefulness compensates f or any lack of motivation through supportive technology ex perience, while also mitigating deficiencies in pri or experiences under the influence of control beliefs or self-efficacy. In contrast, for students achieving low learning outcomes, perceived usefulness is a absent core variable. This indicates that if students fail to recognize the value brought by the technology, they may struggle to fully engage in technology-supported learning environments. Finding 3: Motivation can mitigate deficiencies caused by lack  of technological experience. Configurations H2 and H3, which lead to high learning outcomes, highlight the importance of motivational characteristics. In contrast, Configuration L1, associated with low learning outcomes, identifies control beliefs— one of the motivational characteristics —as a missing core variable. Unlike past research th at primarily focused on the impact of learners' motivation on learning p erformance (Rashid & Asghar, 2016), this study expa nds our understanding of how the interaction of self-efficacy, usage experience, and other factors influ ences learning outcomes. The findings suggest that even without technological experience, learners can overcome initial obstacles by leveraging strong self-efficacy and control beliefs, ultimately leading to positive learning outcomes. In summary, t his study employs fsQCA to explore the configurational effects of learner c haracteristics on learning outcomes in environments supported by GAI. The results of this study also suggest that the v ariation in learners' learning outcomes under GAI support is no t caused by a single factor. Instead, the combinati on of motivation, technological dispositions, and prior experience influenced the final learning outcomes, revealing the complex mechanisms underlying technology-supported learning.
