# Bridging Confidence and Practice: Effects of Data Literacy Professional Development on Teachers’ Knowledge, Self-Efficacy, and Value Beliefs

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Teachers are increasingly expected to use data for instructional decision-making, yet many report limited preparation. This study evaluated a professional development (PD) program on teacher data literacy in Singapore using a mixed-methods design. Baseline surveys and tests were completed by N = 144 teachers; matched pre– post data were obtained from 21. Quantitative analyses showed small, non-significant gains in data knowledge, stable self-efficacy, and stable perceived value of data use. Attrition limited statistical power, though post-test completers scored higher than the baseline sample. Qualitative interviews with n = 8 teachers indicated that hands-on analysis of authentic datasets supported confidence, but translation of patterns into pedagogy was hindered by time constraints and limited mentoring. Findings suggest that the PD improved knowledge more readily than beliefs, highlighting the need for explicit scaffolds and organizational support to sustain data use.

In an increasingly data-driven educational landscape, teachers are expected to engage in data-driven decisionmaking (DDDM), using student data to guide instruction and monitor progress (Mandinach, 2012). This relies on data literacy; the ability to interp ret, evaluate, and use data in practice, which includes both technical skills and beliefs such as self-efficacy (SE) and perceived value of data use (PVOD) (Mandinach & Gummer, 2016). Yet many teachers report limited preparation, conceptual ambiguity about  “data,” and difficulty translating patterns into instruction (Lee et al., 2014). To strengthen the key competencies that enable DDDM, this study examines a professional development (PD) program integrating DDDM-oriented activities with data literacy training and its effects on teachers’ data literacy knowledge, SE, and PVOD in Singapore.

## Literature review

Data-driven decision making Data-driven decision-making (DDDM) is promoted to improve teaching and student outcomes through iterative cycles of locating, analyzing, and interpreting data to guide instruction (Mandinach, 2012). However, DDDM is also closely tied to performance accountability regimes, which can narrow what counts as data by prioritizing academic and test-based indicators, and place compliance demands that could conflict with authentic school improvement and teaching quality. Teachers tend to blend formal assessments with informal noticing and contextual knowledge, in ways that diverge from policy expectations of data use (Gummer & Mandinach, 2015). Teacher professional development Within Desimone’s (2009) PD framework, effective data-focused PD should first shift knowledge and beliefs, which then influence practice and student outcomes. Meta-analyses and intervention studies show that PD with strong content focus, active learning using authentic datasets, coherence with systems, and collaboration can improve teachers’ data-related knowledge and SE, though sustained changes in practice are less consistently documented (Filderman et al., 2021). Recent scholarship notes two gaps; (a) overemphasis on technical skills over beliefs under accountability pressures and (b) sparse evidence from Asian contexts on how organizational conditions shape the enactment of DDDM-oriented PD (Lee et al., 2024). The present study addresses these gaps by examining a Singapore PD that integrates DDDM-oriented activities with data literacy training and focuses on immediate outcomes of the PD intervention which are teachers’ knowledge and beliefs.

## Research methods

Our study addressed: To what extent does PD participation relate to changes in (a) DKQ, (b) SE, and (c) PVOD? We used a mixed-methods design combining explanatory sequential and convergent parallel strands (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2018). High post-test attrition (matched n = 21) and modest intervention dosage made it a quasi-2599 experiment. Participants (N = 144) were practicing teachers from Singapore primary and secondary schools, who completed baseline surveys. PD featured collaborative workshops, hands-on tasks with datasets (1-hr online module, 6-hr workshop), and discussions, aligned with Desimone’s (2009) principles of content focus, active learning, coherence, collective participation. Post-test (n = 21 matched) and interviews (n = 8) followed. At baseline (N = 144), SE  was moderately high (M = 45.12, SD = 5.55, Range = 25 to 60; α =.90, ω =.90). PVOD was high (M = 61.70, SD = 7.27, Range = 43 to 80; α =.92, ω =.92). Data knowledge was moderate (M = 13.41, SD = 6.77). DKQ multiple-choice items showed acceptable functioning (difficulty p =.17 to.96 with 5 easy items p >.90 and 1 difficult p <.20; discrimination r =.19 to.49 with Items 13 and 17 weak r <.15). Openended items (n = 5) had fair inter-rater reliability (ICC =.30 to.55, M =.43; Items 1 and 5 strongest at.55). Matched pre-post analyses (n = 21 pairs; Shapiro-Wilk p >.05 confirming normality) yielded small nonsignificant gains via paired t-tests. SE increased by Δ = 0.67 (d = 0.14, p =.52). PVOD changed by Δ = -0.24 (d = -0.04, p =.85). DKQ increased by Δ = 1.85 (d = 0.31, p =.17). All remained non-significant after BH correction. Attrition analysis (85% loss) compared completers to baseline via Welch's t-tests (BH-adjusted). Completers scored higher on SE (g = 0.49, p =.01), PVOD (g = 0.42, p =.01), and DKQ (g = 0.65, p <.001), corroborated by Mann-Whitney U (p ≤.066). This suggests selection bias rather than intervention effects. Semi-structured interviews (n = 8) explored participants’ perceptions of DKQ, SE and PVOD. Transcripts were analyzed  using Braun and Clarke’s (2006) thematic analysis. Four themes emerged from the data. First, teachers viewed data as essential for diagnosing issues and tracking progress yet varied in practice from advanced heat maps/error coding to surface-level grades. Second, hands-on practice such as mock datasets, and peer discussions built confidence in analysis and interpretation (aligning with SE gains). Abstract statistics like standard deviation felt overwhelming and untransferable. Third, a translation gap persisted. Teachers struggled to link data patterns to instructional actions (e.g., reteaching, grouping) and requested worked examples or heuristics. Fourth, structural barriers like time pressures, competing duties, and lack of mentoring constrained sustainment. Teachers advocated for school data specialists, blended formats, and follow-ups. routines and humanities teachers’ systemic barriers (Khor & Chee, 2025). Interviews complemented quantitative patterns, suggesting the PD supported proximal knowledge uptake but struggled with DKQ-to-SE translation due to missing pattern-to-pedagogy exemplars, mentoring, and follow-up (Khor et al., 2025). High/stable PVOD acted as motivator, constrained by time/duties. This echoes PD literature on concr ete/contextual tasks outperforming abstract training (Filderman et al., 2021) and DDDM needing organizational scaffolds (Gummer & Mandinach, 2015), though causal mechanisms remain suggestive given non-significant results.

## Implications for PD design and policy

First, although hands-on dataset analysis and peer discussion built confidence (Theme 2), DKQ gains did not reliably translate to SE. PDs should sequence concrete tasks like mock dataset analysis and visualizations  before statistical terminology, embedding concepts within applied problem-solving (Sinha, 2025). Second, the translation gap (Theme 3) where teachers requested worked examples linking patterns to instructional actions explains why knowledge gains fell short of belief shifts. PDs should include st ructured resources such as case libraries mapping error patterns to reteaching strategies, grouping protocols, exemplar feedback and rehearsal cycles. Third, universal structural barriers (Theme 4) e.g. time constraints, competing duties, and absent mentoring, limited the dispositional change despite knowledge uptake. Schools should establish data mentors, collaborative review time, and lightweight PLCs focused on instructional problem-solving. Fourth, high baseline PVOD alongside heterogeneous DKQ and SE suggests path differentiation: scaffolded skill-building labs for lower-DKQ teachers, pedagogy clinics emphasizing  translation for higher-DKQ groups. Fifth, DKQ psychometrics showed ceiling effects (5 items p >.90), weak discrimination (2 items r <.15), and fair inter-rater reliability (ICC =.43), limiting sensitivity. Future measures should incorporate harder item s, performance-based tasks analyzing authentic data, and refined rubrics with anchor examples. Measurement limitations (ceiling effects, weak discrimination, ICC =.43) and the modest-dose design without mentoring follow-up prevented assessment of sustained effects. Future work could test higher-dose PD with mentoring and a long-term follow-up.
