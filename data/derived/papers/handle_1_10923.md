# Supporting collaborative learning and/or knowledge building in initial teacher education using video: a systematic review with PRISMA

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper reviews the use of video technologies in teacher education, focusing on how they enhance collaborative learning and knowledge building. The review follows the PRISMA methodology and analyzes 35 peer-reviewed papers published in English between 2000 and 2023. Three main video modalities of use were identified: "Video Recording," "Video-Based Content," and "Live Video Conferencing." The findings show that video technologies play a crucial role in collaborative learning and knowledge building in teacher education. "Video Recording" supports reflective practices and collaborative self-improvement among educators. "Video-Based Content" provides access to high-quality resources, fostering collaborative learning and knowledge building. Although less studied, "Live Video Conferencing" offers real-time interaction and collaboration opportunities. This review highlights the growing importance of video technologies in shaping teacher education and emphasizes the need for further research to fully unleash their potential.

## Introduction

The use of video technologies in teacher education has gained attention in recent years due to their p otential to improve collaborative learning and knowledge buildi ng (Darling-Hammond & Bransford, 2005; Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2006). This paper comprehensively reviews and analyzes l iterature on this topic, aiming to understand how video technologies are used in initial teacher education for collaborative learning and knowledge building. Several key questions guide this study. Firstly, it identifies the main research topics covered, e xploring themes and their alignment with broader goals of teacher e ducation. The review also examines the diverse appl ications of video, such as reflective practice and content d elivery, and their contribution to collaboration and knowledge building.

## Review methodology: Data collection

The PRISMA technique, a systematic procedure for sy stematic reviews and meta-analyses, was followed in this study. It excluded other document kinds and chose 35 peer-reviewed English publications from academic journals. The publication in English, complete text accessibility, dates of release between January 2000 and June 2023, and emphasis on video in initial teacher education were the requirements. We eliminated a number of non-peer-reviewed sources in order to uphold scientific inte grity. Then, we thematically analyzed the remaining papers, yieldin g three sub-themes that are crucial to answering our study objectives.

## Results

A search on B-On databases (ERIC and ISI Web of Science) yielded 1055 journal articles related to "initial teacher training", "initial teacher education", "student teacher", "video", "knowledge building", "collaborative learning", "cooperative learning" and "higher education". 35 pertinent papers were found for an alysis after being screened and duplicates were eliminated and those papers can be found by  following the URL: https:// drive.google.com/ file/d/1fq8tXLyc9xBqSLUmRBGLv9yaWhS-q0xB/view?usp=sharing. RQ1.: What are the main topics in the selected papers?

Diverse topics can be found  in educational research on the incorporation of tec hnology into teacher education. Four categories emerge from our analysis: 1) "Techn ological Innovation in Education," which examines h ow technology can change education (18 papers); 2) "Video Technology in Teacher Education," which evaluates how it affects instruction and learning outcomes  (15 papers); 3) "Collaborative Learning and Knowled ge Building," which highlights interactive learning techniques  (1 paper); and 4) "Benefits and Challenges of Video Technology," which talks about its real-world applications  (1 paper). Key facets of technology's impact on education are covered in each category, including i nstructional strategies, digital literacy, and coll aborative learning. Even though there aren't many publication s in some categories, they nonetheless provide insi ghtful information about improving learning environments. These results highlight the significance of continuing study in this dynamic subject and add to a more nuanced u nderstanding of the changing link between technolog y and education.

RQ2.: What are the video modalities of use?

Based on the frequency of these three categories  - "Video Recording" (21 papers), "Video-Based Content", (12 papers) and "Live Video Conferencing"  (2 papers) - in a subset of selected papers, this research class ifies video modalities. "Video Recording" is the most popular m ethod, encouraging peer participation and self-observation. "Video-Based Content" facilitates group learning by providing pre-made instructional materials. It is rarest to find "live video conferencing" to be useful for project management and remote learning in real time. These sections highlight the various ways that video is used in ed ucation and show the different educational goals th at each modality serves.

## Findings

RQ3.: Are video technologies being used to support collaborative learning and/or knowledge building?

Video technologies are becoming a basic part of education, providing opportunities for group learning and information construction. By classifying video moda lities into "Video Recording," "Video-Based Content," and "Live Video Conferencing," this study sheds light o n their respective functions. The most popular tech nique, "video recording," encourages educators and student s to share information and engage in self-observation and reflection. "Video-Based Content" facilitates cooperative learning by providing ready-made instructional resources. Less research has been done on "live video conferencing," but it has potential for in-person interactions during meetings and remote learning. These modaliti es enable teachers to improve their methods, spark conversations, and include students in group projects. Live co nferencing offers chances for instantaneous peer connections and collaborative participation, despit e the paucity of study on the subject. In general, video technologies are still developing, influencing teac hing methods and encouraging group projects and kno wledge acquisition.

## Discussion and conclusion

In order to improve collaborative learning and knowledge building, there has been a noticeable emphasis in recent years on using video technologies into initial teacher education. Three main uses of videos are id entified in this review using the PRISMA method: "Video Recording," "Video-Based Content," and "Live Video Conferencing." These results highlight the critical role that video technologies play in supporting educators' collaborative learning experiences. While video-based content provides easily available resources f or knowledge construction, video recording encourages introspection and self-improvement. Live video conferencing, while yet und erstudied, allows for real-time communication and teamwork. All things conside red, video technologies are reshaping pre-service teacher education and providing opportuniti es for joint learning and knowledge acquisition. Ho wever, more investigation is necessary to completely grasp their potential influence on efficient teacher preparation.
