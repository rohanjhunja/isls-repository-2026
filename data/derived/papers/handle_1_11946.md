## Introduction

AI-based personalized learning has the potential to transform education by tailoring instruction to the unique AIbased personalized learning can transform education  by meeting individual student needs, but it must a ddress equity to benefit all learners eq ually. Khan and Ghasempour (2024) emphasize that wh ile AI can revolutionize education, it requires careful design to ensure fai rness and inclusivity, preventing the widening of e xisting disparities. The Education for All movement undersc ores access to quality education as a human right, essential for empowerment and sustainable development (UNESCO  & Right to Education Initiative, 2019). Personaliz ed learning supports this vision but raises concerns about fairness and accessibility. Using Sustainable Development Vision 4 (SDV4), Dimension 4: Practices, we examine AI’s alignment with equitable education. This framework highlights actionable strategies: encouraging inclusion, supporting at-risk learners, providing diversity-responsive training, and ensuring ong oing professional development (UNESCO, 2017). Throu gh this lens, we explore AI’s potential to foster inclusive practices and bridge equity gaps. Purpose This review critically examines AI-based personalized learning, focusing on its alignm ent with equitable education practices. It aims to provide educators, researchers, and policymakers with insights to addr ess challenges, anticipate barriers, and build inclusive systems rooted in humanistic values. Guided by this objective, our research question is: How can AI shape personalized learning to promote equitable and inclusive education?

## Methods

This study followed PRISMA guidelines (Page et al.,  2021) for a systematic review of AI-based personalized learning and equitable education, analyzing literat ure from 2019 –2024. We searched ERIC, IEEE Xplore, and gray literature using keywords like “AI in Educatio n,” “Personalized Learning,” and “Equity in Educati on,” identifying 65 papers. After excluding duplicates, non-English articles, and irrelevant sources, 34 studie s were selected. Screening occurred in two stages: two rev iewers assessed abstracts (L evel 1), resolving disagreements via discussion, and one reviewer conducted full-text reviews (Level 2), consulting a second if needed. This process ensured a reliable, focused analysis of equity challenges and opportunities.

## Results and discussion

This study presents findings in response to three s ub-questions that explore how AI can shape personalize d learning to promote equity and inclusion. Q1 examin es effective design and implementation strategies, Q2 identifies unintended consequences that may hinder equity, and Q3 focuses on minimizing bias in  AI systems. Together, these questions provide a comprehensive f ramework for leveraging AI while addressing risks a nd challenges.

Q1: How can AI-powered personalized learning be designed for equity? AI-driven learning optimizes education by tailoring in struction to individual needs, preferences, and kno wledge levels (Pataranutaporn et al., 2021; Murtaza et al., 2022). Adaptive AI enhances engagement through personalized feedback, multimodal content de livery, and digital twin approaches that create inc lusive learning experiences (Vander Vorst & Jelicic, 2019; Furini, 2022). Cloud-based AI tools bridge the digital divide by extending access to remote and underserved students (Luca, 2023). Ad ditionally, AI automates administrative tasks, allowing teachers to focus on individualized instruction and  support in diverse learning environments (Vasudeva n, 2021; Ayeni et al., 2024).

Q2: What unintended effects could AI-powered learning have on equity? While AI has transformative potential, it also risk s reinforcing biases. Deep learning models trained on biased data may result in unfair outcomes for marginalized groups (Mansur et al., 2019; Ayeni et al., 2024). Algorithms such as Naive Bayes and SVMs ma y introduce additional biases due to their underlyi ng assumptions (Mansur et al., 2019). Personalized learning can sometimes pri oritize technology over student needs, reducing soc ial interaction and limiting soft skill development (Priscilla et al., 2019; Bernacki et al., 2021). Over-personalization may restrict students’ exposure to diverse perspect ives (Tatineni, 2020). Moreover, the digital divide  remains a major barrier, as access to technology and reliable internet is not universally available (Vasudevan, 2021). Q3: How can AI-powered learning systems minimize bias and ensure equity? To prevent bias, AI models must be trained on diverse datasets and undergo continuous oversight (Pataranutaporn et al., 2021; Luca, 2023). Fairness-aware models, ethical data governance, and transpar ency in AI decision-making are essential for equitable ou tcomes (Tatineni, 2020; Dumont & Ready, 2023). User-centered design ensures that AI adapts to students’ cognitive and e motional needs, rather than reinforcing biases (Shi n, 2023). Human oversight remains crucial—teacher guidance and student feedback must inform AI development to ensure equitable learning experiences (Feruni, 2022; Elam,  2024). A "human-in-the-loop" approach can refine AI systems and ensure they align with diverse student needs (Maghsudi et al., 2021). Finally, bridging th e digital divide requires robust technological infrastructure, inclusive online and offline learning environment s, and tailored resources for students with disabilities (Feruni, 2022). By integrating technology with human-centered practices, AI-powered personalized learning can become a tool for equity rather than exclusion.

## Conclusion

AI-powered personalized learning: Equity and inclusion AI-driven personalized learning has the potential to t ransform education by tailoring instruction to indi vidual needs, enhancing engagement and autonomy. However, its implementation must be carefully designed to prevent reinforcing inequalities. This review, framed by Dimension 4: Practices fro m the SDV4 framework, underscores the need for inclusive, ethical AI systems that promote equity and accessibility. Encouraging inclusion and supporting at-risk learners To foster inclusive learning environments, AI systems must be trained on diverse datasets and continuousl y monitored for bias. This is especially critical for  at-risk learners, including those with disabilities or  from marginalized communities. Human oversight remains essential in ensuring AI effectively supports educators and meets diverse student needs.

Diversity-responsive training and ethical AI design AI techniques such as normalization and outlier handling significantly impact inclusivity. If not care fully designed, these methods can overlook unique student  needs, reinforcing bias. Transparency in data anal ysis and machine learning processes is crucial. Experts must critically evaluate AI models to ens ure fairness, prevent overfitting, and address ethical concerns.

The role of educators and ongoing professional development AI should complement, not replace, human educators.  By automating administrative tasks, AI allows teac hers more time for professional development, enabling th em to apply inclusive strategies effectively. Howev er, equitable AI implementation requires addressing the digital divide, ensuring all students have access tonecessary technology and infrastructure. Collaboration among educators, technologists, and researchers is key to developing AI-powered learning systems that truly support equity.  By prioritizing  ethical design, transparency, and human oversight, we can create more accessible and personalized learning opportunities for all students.
