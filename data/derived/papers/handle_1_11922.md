# Visualized Course Design Analytics - a Case Study for Blended Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This case study examines the use of learning design and analytics in higher education course development. The focus is on a course with pre-defined structure and two teachers separately developing and implementing the course in two consecutive years. The teacher created learning designs, automatically produced analytics visualizations and teacher reflections were analyzed. Preliminary results reflect how visualized learning design analytics function as tangible support for insights into teachers’ design thinking and can support learning design reflection. SWOT analysis reveals helpful as well as hindering factors for teachers’ practices.

Learning design (LD) has been emphasized as one of the key elements of successful online learning (Martin et al., 2019). LD documents design and sequencing of teaching practice, for the purposes of improving understanding and evaluating teaching and learner activities (Lockyer et al., 2013). LD focuses on determining learning conditions, activities and their sequences with conceptual and technological support (Divjak et al., 2022). LD combined with learning analytics provide possibilities to analyze and visualize learning activities (Toetenel & Rienties, 2016) and such synergies have been suggested to benefit both teachers and their design work (Macfadyen et al., 2020). Learning analytics has been suggested to enhance LD and generate innovations to higher education curricula, by focusing on learning outcomes and student workload, aligning study program and course level learning outcomes as well as ensuring constructive alignment and assessment validity (Toetenel & Rienties, 2016). While the benefits of systematic learning design have been recognized as important for course success, the adoption of technology support for learning design is low (Prieto et al., 2014) and research on teacher experiences with learning design and analytics is still lacking. To address this gap, we have investigated how LD and analytics can serve teachers’ design thinking within a higher education course and how teachers describe strength and weaknesses of visual design analytics support.

## Materials and methods

This case study focuses on two higher education teachers (A & B) developing and implementing the same blended 5 ECTS bachelor level program course in the social sciences field. Mode of delivery, learning outcomes, student workload, subject content and division of lecture and group work time were pre-defined for the course. As teacher B took over course responsibility from teacher A, the changes in the course design were reflected in the visual design analytics tool. Analytics visualizations and teachers’ reflections were collected, and reflections were analyzed using SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) framework which provides a useful way to examine technology designs and their implications (Farrokhnia et al., 2023).

This case study examined the experiences of two teachers utilizing visual design analytics tool to create design for a same blended higher university course. Visualized course design analytics revealed teachers’ unique focus on student workload structure distribution and made teachers’ design thinking more explicit and accessible for individual and group reflection. SWOT analysis revealed several supportive and hindering factors for teachers’ work, highlighting the importance of balance between the required resources and benefits for teachers in development and uptake of learning design and analytics.
