# Collaborative Authentic Professional Development on AI Uses: Reducing Teacher Workload and Fostering Communities of Practice

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines the impact of a collaborative authentic professional development (CAPD) program on reducing teachers' workload, improving self-efficacy, and fostering collaborative learning through Communities of Practice (CoP). The eight-session program led to significant reductions in workload, improved teachers’ self-efficacy, and statistically significant increases in AI usage across eight key areas of professional tasks. Teachers noted the benefits of AI in automating administrative duties, creating innovative lesson ideas, and offering emotional support while also addressing challenges such as reliability, ethical concerns, and adoption barriers.

## Introduction

The increasing workload of teachers has become a cr itical global issue, significantly affecting job sa tisfaction, stress levels, and professional burnout (Wang, 2024). Research shows that teachers spend a substantial portion of their weekly hours on admini strative and non-instructional tasks (Bryant et al., 2020), which re duces time available for lesson preparation and meaningful int eraction with students. This study explores the eff ectiveness of a Collaborative Authentic Professional Developme nt (CAPD) program, focusing on generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, as a solution to alleviate excessive te acher workload and support professional growth. Whi le prior studies have highlighted AI’s potential in automati ng administrative tasks and enhancing teacher self-efficacy, few have proposed structured PD models that integrate AI within actual school contexts to systematically develop teachers' competencies. Drawing on the theoretical frameworks of Collaborative Professional Developmen t (CPD), Authentic Professional Development (APD), situated learning (Greeno et al., 1998), and Communities of Practice (CoP; Wenger, 1998), this study designed a nd implemented the CAPD program. The program engage d teachers in hands-on exploration of AI-based workload reduction strategies across eight ke y domains of educational work —including teaching-learning activities & lesson planning, student guid ance & counseling services, measurement & grading work, student recor d work, paper administration work, school event preparation—through c ollaborative activities that culminated in the co-creation of a "Prompt Collection Handbook." Through this process, teachers not only developed practical AI integration skills but also organically formed a Community of Practice. This study investig ates the comprehensive impact of the CAPD program on teacher workload, self-efficacy, AI usage, and collaborative learning.

## Methods

This study employed a sequential mixed-methods explanatory design (Creswell, 2015), combin ing quantitative and qualitative approaches to comprehensively examine the impact of a generative AI-based workload reduction CAPD program on teachers. This study involved 31 elementary school teachers in South Korea, recruited through an online forum for elementary school teachers. The first phase began with a pre-survey, measuring participants’ workload, intention to use AI, self-efficacy, and AI usage. These baseline measures wer e used for comparison with post-survey results to assess the effects of the CAPD pr ogram. The two-week CAPD program consisted of eight sessions totaling eight hours. It was led by the first author of the study, who has 12 years of teaching experience and extensive expertise in using AI to red uce teachers’ workload. The program aimed to provid e practical in situ strategies for workload reduction through generative AI, specifically ChatGPT. After completing the CAPD program, participants took a post-survey to measure changes in the same variables. Quantitative data were gathered through pre-and post-surveys to measure teachers' workload, intention to use AI, self-efficacy, and AI usage. Survey instruments included: a workload scale adapted from Edwards et al. (2008), an intention-to-useAI scale based on Davis’s TAM model (1989), and a self-efficacy scale derived from Tschannen-Moran & Hoy (2001). Instrument validity was reviewed by two exp erts, and reliability was confirmed with Cronbach’s  alpha coefficients ranging from 0.880 to 0.957. For the quantitative analysis, IBM SPSS 27 was used. The second phase involved qualitative semi-structured interviews with eight teachers, selected  through purposive sampling based on the largest pre-post changes in AI usage. Participants were chosen from both initially low and high AI users to ensure the exploration of diverse perspectives.. The data were analyzed using inductive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006), with the codebook developed by the  first author and reviewed by the second author. To  ensure trustworthiness, participant anonymity, neutral que stioning, and member checking with five participant s were employed to reduce potential bias. All research was conducted with prior approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB).

## Results

Reducing Workload: The AI-based CAPD program effectively reduced teachers’ workload. Quantitative analysis revealed a statistically significant reduction in workload after the training (t=2.644, p=.013), and qualitative data confirmed that teachers used AI to automate repetit ive administrative tasks, particularly those involv ing text generation. Teacher B noted that this automation si gnificantly eased their burden. Teachers also exper ienced emotional support by expressing their work-related concerns to AI, showing that the benefits extended beyond functionality.

Intention to Use AI:  While there were observable changes in classroom pr actice, the program did not lead to a statistically significant change in teach ers’ intention to use AI (t=-1.905, p=.066). Qualitative findings revealed hesitation due to concerns over AI reliability and accuracy, along with ethical issues such as data privacy. For instance, Teacher E stated that “AI-generated content may look accurate on the surface but can often be misleading,” highlighting the need for more practical, in-depth training and resources. Enhancing Self-Efficacy: The CAPD program significantly enhanced teachers’ s elf-efficacy (t=-4.447, p=.000). In the post in-depth interview, teachers reported that AI supporte d various homeroom tasks, such as classroom management and parent communication, making them feel more capable and confident. Increasing AI Usage: Moreover, AI usage frequency significantly increased across all eight professional work domains covered by the program (p<.01). ‘Lesson planning and learning support’ and ‘student record writing’ were the most frequently used domains both before a nd after the program. Notably, previously underutil ized areas—such as ‘family cooperation,’ ‘student guidance and  counseling,’ and  ‘school event preparation’ —also showed substantial improvement. Teachers shared tha t CAPD helped them realize AI could be applied beyond lesson design, extending to a wide range of educational responsibilities. Collaborative Learning and Formation of Communities  of Practice (CoP): Lastly, CAPD went beyond simple knowledge delivery by encouraging teachers t o learn together and helping to form a teacher-led Community of Practice (CoP). The program actively i ncorporated small-group activities where teachers shared their AI experiences, explored strategies for classroom application, and collaborated through tools like Google Jamboard, as well as participated in prompt-sharing and peer feedback sessions. Through these activities, trust and collaboration developed among participants, and even after the training, teachers voluntarily created a group chat to continue sharing AI strategies and prompts in real time. Teacher D said that “Personally, I found the smallgroup activities very beneficial. Even if the small group sessions were a bit longer, I think learning would be more meaningful if we could share challenges and go through trial and error together.”

## Discussion

This study empirically confirmed that the AI-based CAPD program effectively reduces teachers’ wo rkload, increases AI usage frequency, enhances self-efficacy, and fosters Communities of Practice (CoP) among teachers. These findings align with previous research suggest ing that AI reduces teachers’ workload and prevents  burnout (Goldman et al., 2024), as well as studies showing that AI-based professional development grounded in social cognitive theory improves self-efficacy (Yang et al., 2024). However, teachers’ intention to use AI did not increase after the CAPD program. This may be explained by a ceiling effect, as participants voluntarily joined the program and already had high AI adoption intentions. Additi onally, concerns over AI reliability, data privacy, and the evolving role of teachers may have limited further increases in adoption intention. These concerns are consistent with prior research addressing the ethical risks of AI, data security issues (Goldman et al., 2024), a nd bias in AIdriven decision-making (Celik et al., 2022). Theoretically, this study demonstrated the effectiv eness of integrating collaborative and authentic professional development into AI-related teacher training and applied Wenger’s (1998) CoP theory and Greeno et al.’s (1998) situated learning theory to explore AI’s role in fostering teacher learning communities. Practically, this study addresses the imbalance between educatio nal context and teacher PD in AI research (Tan et a l., 2025) and offers a practical model of AI-based online training applicable to rea l school settings. Rather than focusing solely on technical adoption, this study highlights  the importance of authentic, context-connected, and collaborative PD programs, providing a foundation f or the future expansion of practice-based AI training for teachers.
