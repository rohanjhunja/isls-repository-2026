# A Framework for Inclusive Game-Based Learning Design, Illustrated Through the Development of a Media Literacy Game

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Game-based learning (GBL) provides opportunities to support diverse learning goals but GBL can support diverse learners with different cognitive, motivational, and gaming backgrounds remains relatively unclear. A conceptual framework to guide the design and research of inclusive GBL is introduced.

The need for inclusive game-based learning Game-based learning (GBL) emerged as an effective pedago gical strategy for experiential problem-solving that mirrors real-world scenarios. Despite its successes, there remains a notable discrepancy in its effectiveness among learners with multifaceted and intricate identities (Cope & Kalantziz, 2015; Lukosch et al., 2017) and background in the subject matter or gaming overall (Cezarotto et al., 2022; Lukosch et al., 2017). Age, previous educ ational attainment, and digital fluency, among others, are pivotal determinants of their openness and confidence to engage and learn with GBL (Charlier et al., 2012). The ext ent to which GBL can be universally beneficial is a  question that has yet to be thoroughly investigated in the r esearch field (Harteveld et al., 2020), by understa nding how game design elements can engage individual learners  through an individual-centric approach rather than target a specific demographic (Fulmore, 2015). While Design for All and Universal Design seek to create inclusi ve experiences for a broad user base, Inclusive Design adds a layer of pragmatism by aiming to serve as many people as “reasonably possible” (Persson et al., 2014). The objective is to address the spectrum of user needs rather than merely increase user numbers (Joyce, 2022). To addr ess such a gap in conceptual and practical guidelines to support inclusive GBL, this study asks “how can we design and evaluate inclusive game-based learning?”

## Conceptual framework: Inclusive Game-Based Learning (IGBL)

The inclusive game-based learning (IGBL) framework is designed to guid e the analysis, design, and evaluation of GBL that considers diverse and multifaceted iden tities and characteristics of participants who inte rsect as learners and gamers. While existing  frameworks helped guide the (interaction and game m echanics) design and study of GBL in alignment with the learning goals  (Fu & Yu, 2008; Mitgutsch & Alvarado, 2012; Plass et al., 2014; Yusoff & Kasmin, 2015), our aim with IGBL was to focus on the diverse learner/gamer identity and characteristics and their implications for game design. We identified this construct as learner/gamer readiness for GBL.

Figure 1 Inclusive Game-Based Learning (IGBL) Framework Readiness for GBL is defined as the learner/gamer’s  willingness and capacity to participate in, remain engaged, and gain meaningful learning outcomes from GBL experiences. Research on readiness for online digital learning identified a spectrum of contributing fact ors, including information technology proficiency, positive attitudes, cognitive abilities, and self-efficacy (Bovermann et al. 2018; Wei & Chou, 2020). Digital proficiency is defined as learner/gamers' proficiency with gaming in digital environments; capacity as their proficiency in  the subject matter;  self-efficacy and motivation as their perception of capacity, intrinsic motivati on and recognition of importance and usefulness (Bovermann et al., 2018).

Effective GBL design begins with strategically incorporating gameplay elements and subject matter in a manner that is engaging and relevant to diverse aud iences, in consideration of their motivation and se lf-efficacy for continued commitment and progression within the  game (Gopalan et al., 2017). This tailored approac h to understanding and supporting readiness aims to prov ide a focused lens through which the learner/gamer' s varied proficiency and motivation can be accounted for during the design and observation of GBL, specifically its game mechanics, learning mechanics, and UX/UI design.
