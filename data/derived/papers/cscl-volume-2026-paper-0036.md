# Using Multimodal Analytics-Supported Reflective Assessment to Promote Undergraduates’ Productive Social-emotional Interaction

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This quasi-experimental study investigated the effects of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment (MA SRA) on undergraduates' collaborative knowledge advancement and socio-emotional interactions. The experimental group (n = 48), utilizing MASRA with visualized analytics and reflective prompts, was compared to a comparison class (n = 38) using portfolio assessment. Analysis of 4034 discussion notes revealed the MA SRA group significantly outperformed the comparison class across six colla boration dimensions, achieving nearly double the collective knowledge advancement. The experimental class demonstrated more positive and fewer negative socio-emotional interactions, alongside more frequent “negative → positive” transitions. Furthermore, MA SRA strengthened the co-occurrence of metacognitive discourse and positive socio-emotional dynamics, suggesting it enhances students' ability to regulate emotions and maintain group cohesion. These findings indicate that MA SRA is a scalable model that effectively fosters both cognitive-level knowledge innovation and productive socio-emotional dynamics in collaborative learning environments, offering evidence-based strategies for higher education. Knowledge building (KB) is a deep constructivist teaching model emphasizing collective responsibility for idea improvement and community development. Technology-supported KB has shown promise in fostering higher-order skills among university students, incl uding critical thinking, metacognition, and disciplinary literacy (Bereiter & Scardamalia, 2003; Moss & Beatty, 2006; Scardamalia, 2006; Yang, van Aalst, et al., 2020). However, realizing this potential depends heavily on students’ social interactions, particularly socio-emotional interactions, which involve behaviors such as expressing care, developing trust, fostering belonging, and promoting a sense of community (Bakhtiar et al., 2018; Kreijns et al., 2003). Effective socio-emotional interactions are critical in collaborative inquiry, as they support team functioning, motivation, and cognitive engagement (Näykki et al., 2014; Pekrun & Perry, 2013; Pekrun, Vogl, et al., 2017). Positive socio-emotional interactions facilitate conflict resolution, help-seeking, collective planning, and behavioral engagement, whereas negative interactions—arising from cognitive challenges, motivational issues, or interpersonal dynamics —can impede learning and reduce satisfaction (Blumenfeld et al., 1996; Kempler & Linnenbrink, 2013; Lajoie et al., 2015; Näykki et al., 2014). Despite this, research on promoting socio-emotional interactions using technology remains limited, especially in complex, open-ended learning contexts. Reflective assessment involves monitoring, reflecting on, and adjusting one’s learning using evidence from rubrics or analytics, fostering metacognitive awareness and self-regulation (Yang, Zhu, et al., 2022). Multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment (MASRA) extends this approach by providing learners with real-time, multimodal data — behavioral and self-report— on their cognitive, metacognitive, and emotional processes (Blikstein & Worsley, 2016; Tao & Zhang, 2021; Yang, Chen, et al., 2020; Yang, Zhu, et al., 2022;). MASRA integrates planning, monitoring, reflecting, and regulating, enabling students to identify challenges, adjust strategies, and transform negative socio-emotional experiences into constructive learning signals, while reinforcing positive affect and self-efficacy (Panadero, 2017; Efklides, 2011). Previous studies confirm MASRA’s effectiveness in enhancing cognitive engagement and agency, particularly for low-achieving students in knowledge-building environments (Yang, 2019). However, its role in fostering socio-emotional interactions and the underlying mechanisms remain underexplored. Socio-emotional interactions play a crucial role in collaborative learning. Positive interactions—such as mutual respect, support, and care—are associated with deeper cognitive engagement, successful problem-solving, and higher motivation (Rogat & Linnenbr ink-Garcia, 2011; Näykki et al., 2014; Lajoie et al., 2015; Mullins, Deiglmayr, & Spada, 2013). They also facilitate monitoring information exchange, inclusive planning, and team behavioral engagement (Rogat & Adams-Wiggins, 2014; Kempler & Linnenbrink, 2013). Negative interactions, however, can stem from cognitive, motivational, or interpersonal challenges and may reduce help-giving, impede learning progress, and generate persistent negative emotions (Andriessen et al., 2013; Järvenoja & Järvelä, 2009; 254 Näykki et al., 2014). Recent research has highlighted the importance of multimodal analytics in capturing these complex social-emotional dynamics, offering richer insights than single-source measures (Isohätälä et al., 2020; Huang & Lajoie, 2023). Socio-emotional dynamics are often subtle and difficult for learners to perceive during ongoing inquiry. By integrating multiple data streams, MASRA externalizes these tacit interaction patterns, reducing the cognitive burden of self-diagnosing group climate. Visualizing these dynamics provides concrete evidence that prompts learners to enact repair-oriented transitions, linking metacognitive reflection directly to collaborative regulation. This aligns with recent CSCL insights on how carefully designed analytics-supported dashboards foster reflective assessment (Gutiérrez-Braojos et al., 2025). This approach not only supports individual learning but also cultivates a shared sense of community, collaborative planning, and collective responsibility (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2014). The present study addresses a gap in the literature by exploring how MASRA can support productive socio-emotional interactions in university KB contexts. We designed teaching activities and reflective materials to generate rich multimodal interaction data, which were analyzed using content analysis, lag sequential analysis, and cognitive network visualization. The study investigates (1) the effects of MASRA on socio-emotional interactions among undergraduates, (2) differences in socio-emotional dynamics acr oss students with varying academic performance, and (3) the relationship between socio-emotional interactions and individual learning outcomes in collaborative knowledge construction. By integrating reflective assessment with multimodal analytics, this res earch provides empirical insights into fostering positive social-emotional engagement and enhancing both cognitive and emotional dimensions of learning in higher education. The following research questions were investigated: RQ 1. Do the undergraduates with multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment design perform better in advancing collective knowledge? RQ 2. Do the undergraduates with multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment design engaged more in productive socio-emotion and socio-emotional interaction? RQ 3. How do the undergraduates promote positive socio-emotional interactions through the enactment of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment?

## Research context and participants

The study involved 86 second-year undergraduate students majoring in Educational Technology at a normal university in central China. They participated in a compulsory 18-week course (two 45-minute lessons per week) designed to engage students in expert-like problem discovery and problem-solving within a technology-supported knowledge-building (KB) environment. The course aimed to develop higher-order skills such as collaboration, inquiry, metacognition, and knowledge building, while promoting productive soc io-emotional interactions. A quasi-experimental design was adopted: the experimental class (n = 48) experienced KB augmented by MASRA, whereas the comparison class (n = 38) engaged in portfolio-supported reflective assessment. In the comparison condition, students manually selected representative Knowledge Forum notes, justified their choices, analyzed inquiry strengths, and synthesized themes without data-driven visualizations. This contrast allowed us to isolate the effects of offloading the cognitive demands of reflection onto structured multimodal analytics.  The study received ethical approval from the university's review board prior to data collection. All participants provided informed consent and could withdraw at any time.

KB design augmented by multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment The instructional design (Figure 1) integrated a data-driven three-element reflective assessment model, inspired by van Aalst and Chan’s four-phase KB framework and Yang et al.’s three-phase KB model (van Aalst & Chan, 2011; Yang, Chen, et al., 2020). In Phase 1, students established supportive social relationships and a sense of teamwork and responsibility. Phase 2 (Weeks 3 –8) involved collaborative KB activities focused on creative knowledge through face-to-face and online discussions a nd reflections. In Phase 3 (Weeks 9-18), MASRA provided three specific analytics outputs via dashboards  shown in Figure 3: (1) radar charts of collaborative KB behaviors generated via GCA, (2) KBDeX social network visualizations of idea connections, and (3) sequential transition graphs of socio-emotional patterns. To ensure replicability, the instructor first modeled data interpretation, explicitly framing analytics for regulation rather than evaluation. Groups then followed a fixed sequence—diagnosing KB performance prior to examining socio-emotional conditio ns—guided by structured metacognitive prompt sheets organized as a reflection cycle. 255 Data sources and analysis To examine the effects of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment (MASRA) on undergraduates’ socio-emotional interactions and collective knowledge building (see Figure 2), we collected 2,603 Knowledge Forum notes from the experimental class and 1,431 notes from the comparison class. Notes were preprocessed and analyzed using content analysis, lag sequential analysis (LSA), and epistemic network analysis (ENA). The impacts of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment on undergraduates’ improvement of collective knowledge (RQ1) Group Communication Analysis (GCA) was employed to assess collaborative knowledge building. GCA combines computational linguistic techniques with sequential interaction analysis to capture micro-level patterns of group communication. Six dimensions —participation, internal cohesion, overall responsivity, social impact, newness, and communication density—were used as indicators of collective knowledge advancement. Independent sample t-tests compared the experimental and comparison classes to examine the effect of MASRA on knowledge construction.

Effect of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment on the undergraduates’ productive socio-emotional interactions (RQ2) Students’ notes were first organized into distinct inquiry threads, with each thread containing a cluster of notes addressing the same inquiry problem. Notes were grouped according to their logical sequence and timestamps. In total, 32 inquiry threads (1,431 notes) were identified in the comparison class, whereas 68 threads (2,603 notes) appeared in the experimental class.

A content analysis framework was developed to examine students’ socio-emotional interactions. The framework drew on the work of Kempler et al. (2013) and Rogat and Adams-Wiggins (2014) to identify basic types of socio-emotional interaction. Subdimensions of positive socio-emotional interaction were further refined using the concepts of emotional, interactive, and cohesive social presence proposed by Lajoie et al. (2015).  Each note was coded into a single socio-emotional category, with the dominant type selected when multiple expressions occurred. Two researchers independently coded over 20% of the notes (experimental: n  = 550; comparison: n = 256 340), achieving high interrater reliability (Cohen’s κ  = 0.891 for experimental, 0.853 for comparison). Discrepancies were resolved through discussion, and one researcher coded the remaining notes. To examine changes in socio-emotional interactions and the effects of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment, chi-square tests were conducted to compare interaction patterns between classes and across pre-intervention and mid-intervention phases. Lag sequential analysis (LSA) was employed to investigate transition patterns of socio-emotional interactions. Using GSEQ software, Z-scores were calculated for each transition, with values above 1.96 indicating statistically significant sequences (Ba keman & Quera, 2011). Comparison Between Classes and Over Time LSA compared socio-emotional interaction sequences between the experimental and comparison classes. In addition, notes from the experimental class were divided into two phases —before and during MASRA implementation—to examine temporal changes. This analysis revealed how MASRA influenced the development of productive socio-emotional interactions among undergraduates. Mechanism of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment for promoting undergraduates’ productive socio-emotional interactions (RQ3) ENA was used to investigate co-occurrence patterns between metacognitive utterances and socio-emotional interactions, visualizing and quantifying relationships that reveal MASRA’s mechanism in promoting productive interactions. Epistemic networks were generated using the ENA web tool, and co-occurrence coefficients were calculated in RStudio to examine changes in social network characteristics across phases in the experimental class. This multi-method approach allowed us to capture both the quantitative improvements in collaborative knowledge and the qualitative shifts in socio-emotional interactions, providing insight into how MASRA fosters productive emotional engagement and supports higher-order collaborative processes. The impacts of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment on undergraduates’ improvement of collective knowledge (RQ1) Group Communication Analysis (GCA) framework developed by Dowell et al.  (2019) evaluates learners’ knowledge-building behaviors through semantic analysis of their contributions. It includes six key indicators: Participation, Internal Cohesion, Overall Responsivity, Social Impact, Newness, and Communication Density.These indicators reflect collective knowledge advancement. As shown in Table 1, the experimental class outperformed the comparison class across all six dimensions, with differences reaching high statistical significance (p < 0.01). In particular, Internal Cohesion, Overall Responsivity, and Social Impact showed the largest effects. On several dimensions, the experimental class achieved average scores roughly twice as high as the comparison class. These results indicate that the integration of multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment effectively enhanced students’ collaborative knowledgebuilding behaviors and strengthened the collective advancement of ideas. 260 Impacts on collective knowledge (RQ1) Using Group Communication Analysis (GCA), this study examined how multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment influenced undergraduates’ collaborative knowledge building. GCA evaluates collaboration across six dimensions —Participation, Internal Co hesion, Overall Responsivity, Social Impact, Newness, and Communication Density—capturing key cognitive behaviors. The results showed significant improvements across all dimensions in the experimental class compared wi th the control class, confirming that reflective assessment can enhance participation, collective epistemic agency, and the advancement of shared knowledge (Yang, Yuan, et al., 2022; Yang, Chen, et al., 2024; Yang, Feng, et al., 2024). Impacts on productive socio-emotional interactions (RQ2) Data-driven reflective assessment also promoted positive socio-emotional interactions. Students in the experimental class exhibited higher proportions of supportive and cohesive behaviors and demonstrated more positive-to-positive and negative-to-positive interaction sequences over time. These findings extend previous work showing that reflective assessment enhances cognitive engagement and epistemic emotions (Yang, Yuan, et al., 2024), revealing that  it can also scaffold beneficial socio-emotional dynamics. By integrating visualized dashboards and reflective prompt sheets, students were able to monitor their knowledge and regulate emotions, supporting proactive collaboration and a shared sense of community. Mechanisms of data-driven reflective assessment (RQ3) Epistemic network analysis revealed stronger co-occurrence patterns between metacognitive discourse and positive socio-emotional interactions in the experimental group, particularly during the second phase of the intervention. These results suggest that re flective assessment enables students to actively monitor and reflect on their socio-emotional states, plan strategies to address negative interactions, and maintain productive group dynamics. Consequently, negative interactions can be transformed into positive ones, highlighting the interplay among metacognition, socio-emotional engagement, and collaborative knowledge construction.

## Practical implications

This study offers several implications for designing technology-supported learning environments. First, embedding data-driven reflective assessment within a collaborative culture fosters positive socio-emotional interactions and collective responsibility. Second, reflective prompts and visualized dashboards help students interpret learning analytics, monitor progress, and regulate both cognitive and emotional processes. Third, structured activities can guide students in identifying and addressing negative interactions, supporting constructive socio-emotional dynamics. Overall, positive socio-emotional interactions are crucial not only for learning outcomes but also for sustained engagement and higher-order skill development.

## Limitations and future directions

The study has several limitations. First, the lack of a pre-test limits the precision of measuring knowledge gains. Future studies should include pre-and post-tests to assess learning progress. Second, while multiple data sources were collected, this stud y focused mainly on knowledge forum notes. Future research should leverage other artifacts (e.g., reflective sheets, audio/video recordings) and employ qualitative analyses to explore mechanisms more deeply. Third, manual coding of discourse, while rich in context, is time-consuming and limits scalability. Automated methods for analyzing socio-emotional interactions could enhance efficiency and support real-time feedback.

This study demonstrates that multimodal analytics-supported reflective assessment fosters productive socio-emotional interactions and advances collective knowledge in a knowledge-building environment. By integrating scaffolds and reflective prompts, studen ts monitored their socio-emotional states, engaged in metacognitive regulation, and enhanced collaboration. The findings extend KB theory by showing that reflective assessment influences both cognitive and socio-emotional processes. Practically, this study  offers a replicable three-phase instructional model and evidence-based strategies for using analytics to cultivate socio-emotional skills, metacognitive regulation, and collaborative knowledge creation. The results also clarify the mechanism: data-driven reflection allows students to transform negative interactions into positive ones, revealing the interconnected roles of instructional practices, socio-emotional dynamics, and reflective assessment in supporting productive collaborative learning.
