# Resolving Conceptual Contradictions through Transformative Integration

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper proposes transformative integration as a more desirable approach to resolving contradictions in contemporary educational collaboration. It conceptualizes transformative integration as the generation of thirdness and operationalizes thirdness as a Principle of Transformation (PoT) or as an actionable solution derived from it. In doing so, the paper lays the groundwork for systematically identifying and intentionally cultivating thirdness in practice, thereby supporting creativity-oriented collaboratio n and students’ meaningful participation in democratic, innovation-driven societies.

Students learn collaboratively by working through differences. Deeper learning occurs when they  confront conceptual contradictions expressed as direct opposition or divergent perspectives. In responding to such tensions, argumentative and summative integration are often prioritized over defense due to their collaborative orientation. While argumentative integration resolves disagreement by specifying the contexts in which particular claims hold (Leitão, 2000), summative integration abstracts a higher-level synthesis from overlapping points across conflicts (Scardamalia, 2002).

Yet these approaches seem suboptimal in contemporary educational collaboration. Due to their emphasis on reasoning within given frameworks or generalizing from shared elements, they produce reactive insights tied to immediate or similar contexts (Engeström, 2014). As situation s evolve and unfamiliar  challenges emerge, however, these insights may require continual revision  to stay relevant, which can be come intellectually exhausting in an increasingly diverse and rapidly evolving world, prompting disengagement or even defensiveness rather than openness to novel experiences in practice.

To address this limitation, collaborative learning should move toward transformative integration  that cultivates students’ capacity to anticipate and adapt to change proactively.  This paper introduces thirdness to describe such an approach. Drawing on relevant literature, it conceptualizes  and operationalizes thirdness so that this construct can be more systematically identified and deliberat ely fostered in collaborative practice. In doing so, the paper seeks to promote creativity-oriented collaboration  more intentionally  in education and support students’ more meaningful participation in democratic, innovation-driven societies. Conceptualizing thirdness in education Thirdness is a term introduced by Peirce in phenomenology, where it represents the law or principle that mediates Firstness (i.e., the pure, immediate quality of experience) and Secondness (i.e., reaction to that immediacy) (Sonesson, 2013). In education, the term appears in a non-capitalized form in expansive learning theory, where it denotes a “germ cell” concept in the form of an abstract principle that provides a basic explanatory mechanism for the origin of the contradiction under investigation and can be expanded into multiple concrete manifestations that push the initial problematic situation intonew phases of development (Engeström, 2014). In collaborative learning, thirdness may be explored through the notion of principled practical knowledge (PPK) (Bereiter, 2009, 2014), a type of internally coherent, generalizable, and systematic knowledge produced to solve problems creatively in knowledge-poor situations and capable of deducting novel conclusions  that help a field of practice advance. It  has also been discussed in dialogic education, where it refers to a position  that constitutes a dialogic space in which different ideas converge in some respects while diverging in others, sparking the emergence of new meanings that allow people to view a situation from at least two standpoints at once and to expand the context to look at viewpoints beyond the immediate communit y (Wegerif, 2013, 2018). These discussions indicate an early recognition and conceptualization of thirdness as a powerful means of resolving contradictions  in inclusively integrative, collectively transformative, and expansively informative ways, helping students generate new understandings that enable them to adapt to novel practices proactively. Operationalizing thirdness in education Nevertheless, although earlier discussions provide concrete examples of thirdness, these instances remain disconnected and largely descriptive, lacking identification of the underlying patterns that would allow the 635 construct to be analytically recognized or intentionally cultivated. To become  a more useful construct in collaborative learning practice, thirdness must be operationalized by specifying its structural features. Since thirdness is an abstract principle that both addresses  and transcends existing contradictions, the extended abstract principle described in SOLO (the Structure of the Observed Learning Outcome) taxonomy (Biggs & Collis, 1982) may provide a useful framework. When applied to reconcile conflicting perspectives, the principle situates the ideas within a relativizing, concept-based abstract structure that shapes  and sustains openended coherence within and beyond the given context. It interprets the immediate, divergent cases as contextual variations of this structure and enables further deduction from this structure to generate new contextual variations. This paper refers to this structure as a Principle of Transformation (PoT), which is often expressed in statements such as “system 1 varies with system 2” or “variations in system 1 depend on variations in system 2.” Nevertheless, since a PoT holds conclusions open, it remains limited as a form of theoretical resolution and may not constitute a genuine resolution in contexts where contradictions are settled only through actionable solutions (e.g., in an engineering class). To make thirdness meaningful across learning  situations, this paper extends prior work by operationalizing thirdness also as an actionable solution derived from the PoT. When a collaborative setting does not require a practical outcome, thirdness is achieved through the proposal of a PoT. When a practical outcome is required, a workable solution derived from the PoT completes the creative process.
