# Augmenting LLMs with Knowledge Seeds for Clinical Question Generation

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
High-stakes clinical assessments require complex, context-rich items that test applied reasoning but are time-consuming to create. This study presents a learning-centered LLM pipeline that generates multiple-choice clinical items using knowledge seeds from validated item banks. The LLM-generated Multiple Choice (MC) items demonstrated comparable option-level ecological validity and distractor plausibility to expert-created items, with the pipeline facilitating item development and accelerating the refresh cycle to maintain item bank integrity.

Creating items for high-stakes clinical assessments is both a psychometric and learning design challenge. Beyond testing factual recall, these items must engage higher-order reasoning, pattern recognition, and decision-making under uncertainty (Case & Swan son, 1998). Yet developing such scenarios —complete with authentic cues and plausible distractors—demands significant expert time and iterative validation (Schuwirth & Vander Vleuten, 2004). From a learning sciences perspective, assessments shape what learners attend to and how they structure their understanding (Black & Wiliam, 1998). Resource constraints in item development thus limit opportunities to align assessments with evolving clinical knowledge and practice. With the rapid development of N atural Language Process ing (NLP) techniques in recent years, Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as a promising tool for clinical item generation in various educational contexts, offering potential solutions to this challenge  (Zuckerman et al., 2023; Kıyak et al., 2024). Previous research has shown that LLMs can generate educational content efficiently, yet much of this work focuses on simpler, surface-level knowledge checks (Artsi et al., 2024). The effectiveness of LLMs in creating high-stakes clinical assessment items remains underexplored.

However, high-stakes clinical assessments demand a higher level of cognitive abilities (Epstein, 2007). These assessments require items integrating contextual and sensory information to simulate realistic scenarios, which is not fully supported by current LLMs (Artsi et al., 2024). Another challenge is that there is no tolerance for errors or “hallucinations”, where LLMs may produce incorrect or misleading information when generating content (Perković, 2023).

To bridge these gaps, this study proposes a learning-centered LLM pipeline designed to automatically generate multiple-choice (MC) items for high-stakes clinical assessment. By incorporating four interlinked LLM modules, we aim to develop a more accurate, reliable, and efficient AI system that facilitates the item development process. The study addresses the following research question: RQ: How effectively can LLMs generate high-stakes clinical MC items that meet expert standards of validity and plausibility?

The proposed pipeline (Figure 1) consists of four core modules to generate multiple-choice (MC) items for highstakes clinical assessment s: (1) a Knowledge Seeds Generator that extracts medical entities from existing item banks using a Transformer-based named entity (NER) m odel, (2) an LLMs Researcher that generates comprehensive clinical reports by querying multiple LLMs in parallel, (3) an LLMs Writer that classifies clinical signs from the previous report into acuity levels and formulates answer options, and (4) an Item Assembler that constructs complete items using a rule-based approach to combine the key, distractor, and question stem. The resulting items are compiled into an item bank for further evaluation. To generate real-world scenarios and mitigate  model hallucinations, both the LLMs Researcher and LLMs Writer module adopt a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)  approach, a framework for  optimizing LLM output s by enabling referencing external knowledge sources (Lewis et al., 2020). In addition, different prompt strategies were employed to further refine the quality of the output. These strategies include crafting specific prompt templates that guide the LLMs to  focus on each subtask and avoid inaccuracy. Two content experts, each with advanced degrees and over twenty years of clinical practice and test development, evaluated the quality of the items at the option level, assessing ecological validity and distractor plausibility using a 5-point Likert scale, and answer-key accuracy on a binary scale (correct/incorrect). As shown in Table 1, the LLM-generated items demonstrated comparable quality to expert-created items in terms of ecological validity and distractor plausibility, suggesting that the pipeline effectively captures human-like clinical reasoning. However, three generated items exhibited a double-key issue, suggesting occasional ambiguity in the model’s classification of correct answers. ## Practical implications

The findings demonstrate that the proposed LLM-based pipeline has the potential to facilitate high-quality MC item development for high-stakes clinical assessments, with item options comparable to those created by human experts in terms of ecological validity and distractor plausibility. By automating the early stages of item construction, the pipeline reduces the time and cognitive load required from content experts, enabling them to focus on higher-order review and validation tasks.  Moreover, streamlining item generation facilitates more frequent updates to the item bank, strengthening its integrity and ensuring ongoing alignment with evolving clinical guidelines and standards of care. Its modular design supports integration with existing quality assurance workflows, preserving human oversight while improving scalability. Nonetheless, the occurrence of multiple-key items underscores the importance of continued refinement in answer validation and distractor differentiation.
