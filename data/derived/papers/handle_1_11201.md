# Comparing the Difference of Teaching Behavior Between Urban Teachers and Rural Teachers in Synchronous Classroom: Evidence From a Behavioural Sequence Analysis

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Many regions have adopted the blended synchronous classroom (BSC) to connect urban and rural schools and to bring good teachers from urban schools to rural schools, in order to promote education equity and narrow the gap between urban and rural education. BSC has brought new changes and influences to the interaction between teachers and students in urban and rural areas. However, it lacks studies to evaluate the quality of BSC interactions. To address this gap, we proposed an interaction analysis framework for BSC to explore the characteristics of interactive teaching behaviours. Video analysis and Lag sequential analysis were utilized to assess the quality of BSC interactions. Finally, data analysis found that there exist many differences in the distribution characteristics of behaviors and behavioral sequences between the two groups of teachers. Based on the results, this research proposed pedagogical implications for BSC.

## Introduction

China is one of the most populous country in the wo rld with an uneven distribution of educational reso urces, especially between urban and rural areas. Blended synchronous classroom (BSC) is one of the effective measures to narrow the gap between urban and rural education quality and promote educational equity (Li et al., 2023). This particular type of classroom setup can connect classrooms in different locations so that teachers teaching in local classrooms can teach students sitting in remote classrooms. In BSC, both urban teachers and rural teachers need to adjust their teaching behaviours to adapt to the  new teaching model, which is a huge challenge for teachers. Specifically, whether teachers can give equal atten tion and interaction opportunities to students at both ends will affect the effectiveness of the classroom, as well as students' learning experience and learning outco mes (Shi et al., 2021). However, few research had focused on interactive teaching behaviours in BSC. To address this gap, we used descriptive statistical analysis and learni ng analytics to explore the characteristics of teac hing behaviors in BSC, in order to reveal the logic behind the ins tructional design of BSC and provide data-driven evidence for improving BSC. The study addresses the following three research questions:

• RQ1: What were the differences in behaviours between urban teachers and rural teachers?

• RQ2: What were the sequential characteristics of teaching behaviours of urban and rural teachers?

## Participants

The study took 24 videos of 12 synchronous classroo ms recorded in 10 primary schools in  China as the sample. All these videos focused on teaching new knowledge of the subject.

## Data collection

Flanders Interaction Analysis System (FIAS) is a po pular method used by many researchers around the wo rld. It was mainly applied to teachers' speech analysis. However, FIAS was mainly designed to analyze single teacher’s teaching behaviors in traditional face-to-face or online learning environments  and did not consider situation like synchronous classroom where there are urban and rur al teachers working together in two connected class rooms. Therefore, the study put forward a "Teachers’ Behavior Analysis System for Synchronous Classroom (TBSSC)," adapted from FIAS coding systems (See Table 1). In addition, this system also specifically defines the interaction objects of teachers, including “All students”, “Urban students”, “ Rural students”, “Remote teacher” and so on. ## Data analysis

The study adopted descriptive statistics and Lag Se quential Analysis (L sA) to analyze data. First of all, t wo researchers were invited to independently conduct v ideo coding. To ensure reliability, the study test ed the consistency of the encoding data of two coders and reached an inter-rater reliability of 0.85 in terms of Cohen’s kappa. We adopted GSEQ 5.1 software to analyze the behavioral sequences. Then, Gephi0.9.2 software is used to draw the conversion diagram of behavioral sequences.

## Discussion and suggestions

The study mainly explored the characteristics of ur ban and rural teachers’ teaching behaviors in synch ronous classrooms in China. We found some interesting findings: (1)  There are obvious differences between urban teachers and rural teachers in their teaching behaviors. This might be due to differences in teaching roles of urban and rural teachers. Rural teachers, as classroom collaborators, generally kept silent and did not have many teaching behaviors. (2) Urban teachers interact with urban s tudents more frequently than rural students. This r esult is inconsistent with the conclusion of the study by Yang et al. They found that the interaction between teachers and urban and rural students was equal in the synchrono us classroom (Yang et al., 2019). However, we also found that rural teachers paid more attention to rural students, so to a certain extent, this defect was mad e up by rural teachers. Based on these findings, some suggestions are propo sed: urban teachers and rural teachers should pay attention to the possible learning gap that may exi st between urban and rural students, prepare instru ctional materials, design meaningful learning activities and promote rural students’ participation.

## Conclusions

To better understand urban and rural teachers’ beha viors in synchronous classrooms, "Teachers’ Behavio r Analysis System for Synchronous Classroom" was prop osed based on previous studies. Through video analy sis and LSA analysis, we obtained the characteristics and differences of teaching behavior of urban and rural teachers. Finally, some suggestions were put forward to improve the quality of urban and rural classrooms.
