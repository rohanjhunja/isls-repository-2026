# A Theoretical Proposal for Employing Temporal Hypergraphs to Model and Analyze Many-Body Interactions in Learning Processes

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Learning is a dynamic and social process involving various relationships that unfold over time. Current analytical network models in Learning Sciences, grounded in classical graph theory, are inherently restricted to modeling pairwise relationships in learning. This study proposes the use of temporal hypergraph approach to Learning Sciences, a framework that enables the dynamic analysis of many-body interactions in learning contexts.

## Introduction

Learning processes are complex, nonlinear, and evol ve dynamically over time within social settings. Re cent studies in complex networks have highlighted the si gnificance of many-body interactions (or higher-order interactions), involving three or more entities, in shaping soci al dynamics (Battiston et al., 202 0; Battiston et al., 2021; Bianconi, 2021; Bick et al., 2023; Cencetti et al., 2021). Al though temporal networks have been instrumental in capturing the time-varying nature of learning, the role of many-body (higher-order) interactions within the temporal dimension of learning remains u nder-explored, particularly within the field of Learning Sciences. To overcome these limitations, this study proposes an analytical approach via temporal hypergraph, a framework that enables the dynamic analysis of many-body (higher-order) interactions in learning contexts. Related prior works Temporal Networks-based Learning Sciences has emerged as a promising approach for investigating the temporal aspects and relationships inherent in learning (Saq r & López-Pernas, 2022; Saqr & López-Pernas, 2023; Saqr & Peeters, 2022). Despite these advancements, however, significant challenges remain. Firstly, the approach adopts classical concepts of networks. These classical net work concepts are essentially graphs as defined in the graph theory. A graph is composed of a set of vertices (n odes) and a set of edges (links), where edges are d efined as (ordered or unordered) pair of vertices. This implies that edges can only represent dyadic (pairwise) relationships between two vertices. Within this definitional fram ework, learning relationships can only be captured as one-toone correspondences. Thus, current analyses (includ ing temporal network-based approach) using graphs are limited to capturing pair wise relationships between nodes, making it impossi ble to analyze interactions among multiple nodes (many-body social interactions). Secondly, the convention al analysis method heavily relies on classical network analysis, and the employed concepts such as centrality are based on node degrees. Consequently, it is impossible to capture the temporal evolution of learning time and space as a whole. To analyze l earning as many-body interacting processes, it is necessary to rede fine network measures to accommodat e many-body interactions.

In addition to Temporal Network-based Analysis, Epistemic Network Analysis and Orde red Network Analysis have been actively studied in the field of learning sciences in recent years (Bowman et al., 2021; Shaffer et al., 2016; Shaffer & Ruis, 2017; Tan et al., 2023). Furthermore, Transition Network Analysis has been proposed as an approach to address the shortcomings of these  methods (Saqr et al., 2025).  However, since they are based on graph theory, these approaches are limited to dy adic analysis of relationships between nodes. Consequently, the analysis of many-body (higher-order) interactions is explicitly excluded from the focus of these approaches. Interaction Geography is another novel methodology that incorporates not only the te mporal aspects of learning but also its spatial elements (Shapiro et al., 2020; Shapiro & Garner, 2022). This analytical approach is effective for analyzing interactions within the learning space from both temporal and spatial perspectives. Instead, this method primarily focuses on the visualization of interactions and is not well-suited for mathematically analyzing phenomena considered to be related to many-body (higher-order) interactions, such as burstiness. By adopting this approach, we can more accurately mode l the intricate patterns of learning activity and g ain new insights into Learning Sciences.

Figure 1 Graph, Hypergraph, Simplicial Complex (Left), Time Evolution of a Hypergraph (Center) and Time Evolution of Degree Distributions of the Hypergraph (Right) Beyond graphs: Temporal hypergraphs for deeper insights To the best of our knowledge, there have been no at tempts in Learning Sciences to investigate dynamical and many-body interactions in learning processes via a tempo ral hypergraph approach. There are at least two advantages to applying temporal hypergraph analysis to Learning Sciences. Leveraging these advantages can also provide a direction for future research in Learning Sciences, particularly in understanding the complex dynamics of learning processes.

Firstly, the analysis methods employing temporal hy pergraphs, particularly those utilizing simplicial complexes, are grounded in the language of algebraic topology. Consequently, applying the temporal hypergraph approach to Learning Sciences enables us to directly leverage the rich body of knowledge and tools from topology within this domain. Recent advances in Topological Data Analysis (TDA) have led to novel methods for capturing the evolving topological structure of time-varying hypergraphs using zigzag persistence (Aktas  et al., 2023). Additionally, techniques for effectively classifyin g the temporal information within the topological s tructure of temporal hypergraphs have been introduced (Myers et al., 2023). Moreover, centrality measures and other network characteristics for temporal networks have been redefined and proposed (Serrano & Gómez, 2020). By integrating these insights, we can potentially analyze and clas sify the temporal evolution of effective learning c ommunity activities as a whole, providing new perspectives on the dynamics  of learning processes and interactions. Secondly, building upon the first point, simplicial  complexes provide a foundational framework to decompose and reconstruct network components, enabl ing us to express various many-body interactions in learning contexts in a precise and formal manner. T his implies that the framework of temporal hypernet works offers a unified perspective for comparing and cont rasting diverse theories of learning, such as situa ted learning theory, legitimate peripheral participation, and ac tivity theory. What's more, this approach may pave the way for a more rigorous mathematical treatment of learning phenomena, enabling us to empirically test and refine theories.
