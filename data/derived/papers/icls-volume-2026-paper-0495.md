# Evaluating the Impact of a Virtual Collaborative STEM Project on Adolescents’ Social Awareness and Identity Development

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The study reports initial results from a comparative analysis of students’ pre-and post-assessment in a virtual collaborative STEM-focused learning project. Utilizing epistemic network analysis (ENA), findings included a strong connection between challenges and social awareness and global cultural awareness, indicating that participation in the learnin g community increased adolescents’ willingness to engage in discussions on personal challenges and an ongoing process of identity development.

Adolescent identity reflects how they see themselves as individuals with the capability to interact with members in the community (Albarello et al., 2017, 2020). Adolescence is a critical development trajectory marked by changes and influences from internal expectations, such as intellectual growth and future aspiration, as well as external influences, such as family, social, and cultural norms. Despite the change in physical, emotional, and social dynamics, adolescent learners seek to refine their self-understandings through the engagement of learning activities among peers and groups (Main et al., 2025). Virtual learning opportunities provide an alternative platform for young learners to form individual mindset and social well-being in a collaborative environment (Sheffler & Cheung, 2023). Therefore, this study applies social identity theory to examine adolescent identity development through participation in community learning, in which learners exchange their unique experiences and increasingly build their self-cognition from multiple perspectives (Branje et al., 2021; Stets & Burke, 2000; Tajfel & Turner, 1979).

The Asset-Based Learning Environments (ABLE) project was designed to expand and support students' informal learning through an online, global setting. With over 100 adolescent participants from more than 5 countries, including Brazil, Ghana, Kenya, Mexico, and the USA, this project created a virtual platform for learners to interact and create projects in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) subjects (Espino et al., 2024). Researchers conducted pre-assessment interviews with student participants in September 2024, before the start of the program, and post-assessment interviews in May and June 2025, after the program had completed. This study reports a preliminary analysis of a subset of students from one school site for a focused examination.

Pre-assessment interviews were conducted via an online video platform, with a researcher asking 10 open-ended questions and follow-up questions about  the student's self-identity and learning experience. Post-assessment interviews were conducted via an online platform to capture participants' responses  to the same questions. All responses were transcribed and reviewed by researchers. Qualitative data was  collected from students who completed both pre-and post-interviews. Of the various sites, 10 students from one school site were selected for further analysis, 3 female and 7 male students.

After a comprehensive review of the data, researchers inductively developed a codebook that  consisted of 11 constructs that reflected meaningful and recurring themes within participants’ responses: Emotional Awareness, Social Awareness, Math Orientation, Science Orientation, Global or Cultural Awareness, Collaborative Openness, Aspirational Orientation, Personality Attributes, Perseverance, Self-efficacy, and Challenge or Deficiency Awareness. Each interview was separately coded by two researchers, who then conducted meaningful discussions for differences in the coding, to come to a final agreement on coding in a process of social moderation. Furthermore, this study applied Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA), a primary computational analytical tool in  Quantitative Ethnography, to develop models that present the interconnections and their strengths between the codes (Shaffer, 2017). The visualization of the network of the constructs offers a multidimensional perspective for understanding participants' pathways of learning and communication. 2913 over time. However, the post-assessment revealed several additional connections that emerged during the project. The pre-assessment network model on the left and the post-assessment network model on the right share a highly similar structure, showcasing that students' responses remained mostly stable and consistent before and after their involvement (see Figure 1). The models also highlight the strong connection between students’ math orientation and science orientation, reinforced by initiatives on enhancing students’ knowledge and problem-solving skills in STEM subjects.

For example, a connection between Challenge/Deficiency Awareness and Social Awareness was evident in the post-assessment ENA model. As one of the students reflected, they became aware that working as a group could create challenges: I'm just really nervous. Like I don't want to let them down but I don't want to be like too hard on them. Sometimes I'm just not sure myself like people. It's not really easy, all that pressure. But okay. It's important though because it's how you, for me, it's how you help everyone pull their weight and get like a goal accomplished. Additionally, it was identified that the adolescent learners developed a greater Social Awareness and Global/Cultural Awareness, which plays a critical role in shaping their identity and characteristics  in a diverse learning environment. As one participant noted: Learning more about their culture, religion, everything about them. Where they live, what they do. And learn from that. I think that’s the most important, you know. Differences you learn, some cool things about their life maybe. Amazing things you’ve never heard of. That’s cool. I think that’s a great, important thing when working with someone. Overall, the preliminary study demonstrates that participation in the informal, virtual collaboration STEM learning community had an impact on adolescent learners' identity development, particularly the awareness of challenges that can come with collaborat ion, and as a point to grow from. Building on this foundation, future research will expand the analysis in a cross-cultural perspective and investigate the differences  across multiple international project sites. Furthermore, the next phase of analysis will seek to investigate how gender affects learners’ pre-and post-performance.
