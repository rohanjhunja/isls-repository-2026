# A Multi-Level Framework for Exploring the AI interaction through Joint Reasoning

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Conversational AI, like GPT-4, has grown in sophistication, necessitating an evolved understanding of human-AI interaction. There is a notable lack of comprehensive understanding in the literature regarding the progression of human-AI interactions, particularly in contexts of joint reasoning. Our research extends Graesser's question classification scheme into a multi-level framework that captures the complexity of user inquiries, from simple fact-checking to meta-cognitive exploration, informed by educational, cognitive, and HCI theories.

## Introduction

The proliferation of conversational AI has reshaped  human-computer interaction, highlighting the need to understand long-term user engagement and the evolution of user reas oning in prolonged dialogues (Bickmore & Picard, 2005). Our approach, grounded in Vygotsky's  sociocultural theory (SCT) and the Zone of Proxima l Development (ZPD), explores how AI interaction enha nces and transforms user reasoning, with AI serving  as a more knowledgeable partner scaffolding cognitive growth. Our classification system extends Graesser's taxonomy (Graesser et al., 1992) to examine and foster the d evelopment of users' cognitive processes alongside AI advancements. We introduce additional levels, such as Creative Extension and Meta-Cognitive, supported by research on creative problem-solving (Sawyer, 2012) and metacognitive monitoring (Flavell, 1979) in AI-assisted environments. Although conceptual, this work lays t he foundation for future empirical research on AI-user interactions in education. By providing a theoretic ally grounded framework, we aim to guide the design  and analysis of AI-supported learning environments that foster cognitive growth and metacognitive reflection. The classification scheme for conversational AI interactions Graesser et al.'s (1992) taxonomy for understanding  questions that stimulate cognitive engagement and learning is the foundation for our proposed system. We extend this work by considering longitudinal interaction and joint reasoning with AI, drawing from Vygotsky's SCT and the ZPD. In AI-user interactions, the AI can act as a more knowledgeable partner, scaffolding users' cognitive growth through collaborative problem-solving and knowledge co-construction. Advancements in conversational agents  like GPT-4 n ecessitate an expansion of Graesser's categories to capture the increasing sophistication  of AI-user interactions (Amershi et al., 2019). The proposed classification system is a non-linear, iterative process, with users moving betwee n levels during joint reasoning with AI. Each level represents a distinct form of cognitive engagement, with higher levels building upon lower ones. This aligns with the opportunities Dang et al. ## Conclusion

In advancing Graesser's taxonomy, our proposed clas sification system serves as a conceptual framework for researchers and developers investigating the dynamics of user-AI interactions in educational contexts. By drawing from Vygotsky's sociocultural theory and the concept of the Zone of Proximal Development, we posit that AI can serve as a more knowledgeable partner, scaffolding users' cognitive growth through joint reasoning and collaborative problem-solving. The framework offers a forward-looking perspective that not only tracks but also fosters the maturation of users' inquiries and cogn itive processes in tandem with advancing AI's conve rsational abilities. However, we acknowledge that the current work is conceptual and requires empirical validation. Future research should focus on conducting studies to refi ne and validate the proposed framework in real-world educational settings, analyzing student-AI interactions, assessing learning outcomes, and exploring the impact of AI-assisted learning on students' cognitive development and metacognitive skills. Moreover, the framework could be further expanded to include additional dimension s, such as the role of affect and motivation in AI-user interactions, as well as the potential for AI to adapt to individual learners' needs and preferences.
