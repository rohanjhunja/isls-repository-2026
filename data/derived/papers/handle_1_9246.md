# Classroom Talk for Learning: Support for and From Microblogging

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster presents a study of a teacher professional development initiative to develop teachers’ strategies for promoting students’ exploratory talk when using digital technology. We analysed 57 lessons featuring an explicit focus on talk rules and teachers who used a microblogging tool. Our results reveal how the teachers wove talk rules into the fabric of classroom talk during microblogging activities. These results further an understanding of teachers scaffolding strategies and pedagogical use of digital technology.

## Introduction

We focus on teacher professional development (TPD) of pedagogical strategies to promote students’ exploratory classroom talk through digital technology. Exploratory talk is defined in this study as dialogue in which students share ideas, engage critically and constructively with these ideas, ask questions, and give reas ons for their ideas; it is considered effective for thinking and learning (Mercer et al., 2019). We know that what teachers do and how they do it is vitally important to students ’ development of talk skills (Alexander, 2020). Importantly, findings indicate that student skills for engaging in exploratory talk are rarely developed during ‘everyday’ classroom activities. This insight has informed several TPD initiatives, yielding prom ising results. In one TPD initiative, ‘Thinking Together’, teachers and students collaboratively develop a set of ‘talk rules’, with the intent to engage in forms of talk that enables the sharing of ideas and the co-creation of knowledge and understanding (Mercer et al., 2019). Therefore, talk rules should encourage students and teachers to collabor ate by elaborating upon, reasoning through, sharing and collectively considering ideas and relevant knowledge.  Scholarship that investigates classrooms by explicitly focusing on talk rules remains rare, and one reason is teachers’ unfamiliarity with strategies for introducing, promoting and developing such rules (Hennessy & Davies, 2019). The increased use of digital technology – such as tablets, laptops and mobile devices – in classrooms has led to more focus on how these digital technologies could support and promote student involvement in exploratory talk (Warwick et al., 2020). While digital technology offers new possibilities such as involving more students, it can also pose new challenges to classroom teaching and learning. The field of computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL) reveals the importance of using digital technologies within the context of a focused pedagogical approach; furthermore, within this context, teachers must adapt and develop their  teaching strategies tonew advantages that digital technologies offer. Therefore, we are concerned with the use of technology in the context of dialogic pedagogy, particularly the use of talk rules for promoting explorator y talk for learning. This study aims to broaden scholarly understanding of teachers’ explicit work to combine classroom talk with microblogging. Microblogging is the process of posting short messages in a shared digital environment for all participants to see. Within education, microblogging is a recent tool; some studies on microblogging tools re veal promising results about student engagement and participation (e.g., Warwick et al., 2020). This study describes how pedagogical elements can be combined with microblogging to support teachers at engaging students in exploratory talk. The analyses were undertaken to investi gate: What characterised the teachers’ strategies to make talk rules relev ant during microblogging activities, and how did the microblogging tool support these strategies?

## Research context

The research project described in this paper is Digital Dialogues Across the Curriculum (DiDiAC), a collaboration between the University of Oslo, the University of Cambridge and 20 teachers in lower secondary schools in Norway and England. This project draws on design-based research and teacher-researcher partnerships. Scholars using these methods both design research-informed resources for teaching and learning and study educational phenomena that emerge when the designed resources are used. The teache rs and researchers together explored and discussed central elements of the TPD initiative Thinking Together  (Mercer et al., 2019) and the key functionalities of Talkwall in four workshops. Talkwall is a tool that was developed in collaboration with teachers over several years (Smørdal et al., 2021). Talkwall  is specifically designed to allow teachers and students to be dialogically active through microblogs that are shared with others to create new opportunities in digital and faceto-face interactions. Each of the 20 teachers ’ classrooms were video recorded in 3 lessons of their subject CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 391 specialisms, followed by 4 teacher-researcher meetings after each les son. This resulted in 57 lessons lasting between 45 and 90 minutes. We systematically reviewed the video recordings and tra nscripts of all the lessons and the text in Talkwall. In the analysis of the data set, the authors drew upon reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2019). Through an iterative and inductive process, the authors developed the mes and subthemes by closely examining the data set to capture specific aspects of each th eme. This analytical process was conducted over several iterations and by frequently consulting with the third author. Cases of uncertainty were then discussed among all authors to set rules for analysis and avoid bias.

## Findings

The analyses yielded five main themes characterising the strategies th e teachers used to make talk rules relevant resources for developing students’ exploratory talk for learning: 1) Teacher s displayed visual representations of talk rules in various ways, taking advantage of tools: wall posters permanent ly fixed to the wall, handouts, and PowerPoint presentations. 2) Teachers reminded the students about the join tly established talk rules, either in general or by focusing on a few specific rules suitable for an activity. 3) T eachers made the talk rules relevant through invitations, prompting the students to answer questions and share their ideas in alignment with the class’s talk rules. 4) Teachers encouraged students to talk explicitly about talk rules  and to evaluate their own or their groups’ talk (metatalk) in the microblogging activities. 5) Teachers designed le arning activities and tasks that integrated explicit work with talk rules.

The teachers’ methods and techniques are similar to common pedagogical clas sroom strategies. However, the teachers used Talkwall to support their strategies for making the talk rules relevant in the activities by: 1) raising teachers and student awareness about productive ways of interac ting during Talkwall activities; 2) encouraging the teacher to use students’ group walls and individual Talkwall micr oblogs to promote extended dialogue, which in turn promoted reasoning, elaboration, justification and collaboration; 3)  functioning as a starting point for metatalk about the students’ talk and thoughts about following the  talk rules during Talkwall activities; 49 connecting the group talk with the classroom community, giving the student s access to more ideas for discussion; 5) providing a provisional ‘dialogic space’ for students’ ideas to be considered, discussed, adopted or rejected. This gives students a scaffold in the form of the teache r’s and other students’ vicarious presence though the microblogs.

## Conclusion and implications

Pedagogy is paramount to using digital technology to engage students in exploratory talk for learning (Mercer et al., 2019). Thus, this research project examined Talkwall and talk rules. We s how that the use of microblogging gained support from the teacher s’ focus on talk rules and vice versa. For example, microblogging seemed to stimulate teachers to invite students to build on, elaborate upon, evaluate and explicitly reason, in turn encouraging students to share and justify their ideas. Talkwall encouraged the teacher s to focus explicitly on talk rules, i.e. ways of talking together productively, during microblogging activities. A microblogging tool can thus become a valuable structuring resource, together with the dialogic approach, that supports teachers in re-framing participation structures and design learning activities to support students’ engagement in exploratory talk.
