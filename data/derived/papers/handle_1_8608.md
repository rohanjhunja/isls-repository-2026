# Grounded Creativity

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We used complex conceptual systems theory to analyze conceptualizations of creativity in the context of a course in which students engaged in group projects to develop creativity tests and creativity games. Using semantic network analysis, we were able to identify conceptualizations of creativity and emergent practices. Groups which developed games tended to conceptualize creativity as organic, while groups which created tests conceptualized creativity as an object or state. Different practices were emergent from each conceptualization.

## Background

In constructivist perspectives, learning is a generative and creative process. This paper uses complex conceptual systems theory to argue that practices in creativity emerge from conceptualizations of creativity. The Creativity Landscape conceptual framework (Donaldson, 2016, 2018) describes the rich landscape of creativity research by identifying key tenets of the construct of creativi ty and then categorizing them into four domains: creative environments (generativity, playfulness, reduced regulation, and lowered inhibition); creative mindsets (perseverance, openness to experiences, tolerance o f ambiguity, autonomy, creative self-efficacy, and mindfulness); creative processes (identification of problem, framing, preparation, idea generation, incubation, unconscious cognition, insight, and refining); and creative cognition (divergent or convergent thinking, including metaphor creation, perspective taking, analogical t hinking, frame creation, conceptual combination, ab ductive reasoning, synthetic thinking, pattern recognition, and elaboration).  Each domain is characterized by  practices that through their interplay and dynamic interaction help demonstrate how one’s conceptualization of creativity impacts the subsequent materialization and manifestation of creativity. Optimal facilitation of creativity involves a balance among all four categories, as well as a m ix of practices from within each category. The Crea tivity Landscape framework was applied to analyze a projec t-based learning task that had groups design unique  and original board games or creativity tests.  Complex conceptual systems theory (Donaldson & Allen-Handy, 2020) views conceptualizations of creativity as complex systems, and practices in creativity as emergent from different conceptualizations. We investigated the relationshi p between conceptualizations of creativity and emer gent practices, fully exploring how the operationalization of creativity influenced the approaches and actions that were enacted in the genesis of these artifacts.

## Methods

Third-year university students in a course for the Creative Studies minor were put in 14 small groups to work on semester-long creativity projects. Half of the grou ps chose to develop creativity games, and half deve loped creativity tests. Each group created a document des cribing in detail the game or test they created, in cluding the creativity concepts and literature which informed their designs. We collected these documents and coded them in MAXQDA for creativity practices as organized throug h the Creativity Landscape framework, as well as conceptualizations of creativity using categories n ecessary for complex conceptual systems analysis—na mely, metaphorical and non-metaphorical characterizations of creativity. Coding resulted in 257 codes covering 2,331 coded segments. We then calculated correlations bet ween pairs of codes within each category to determi ne the likelihood that any pair of codes would appear within proximity in the data. We used these correlations to construct symmetrical correlation matrices which we then ente red into the UCINET and NetDraw software where we constructed semantic network maps and used Girvan-N ewman cluster analysis (Girvan & Newman, 2002) to identify clusters. Clusters of metaphors were inter preted as conceptual metaphors, and clusters of non-metaphorical characterizations were interpreted as conceptual stories. The codes belonging to each cluster were then collapsed in MAXQDA, and correlation matrices were constructed to determine the relationships bet ween conceptual metaphors, conceptual stories, and practices emergent from conceptualizations of creativity.

## Implications and Discussion

We identified salient themes and significant findings in several domains across both projects. For the  creativity games, metaphorical perspectives related to the lif e-like and organic nature of creativity significant ly impacted practices related to creative cognition, processes, and environment. This perspective led to the designing of gameplay elements with lower regulation that featured tasks related to generating conceptual combinations along with the eliciting of insight. Analysis revealed differe nt patterns for groups developing creativity tests,  with metaphorical perspectives related to the physical properties and environmental elements of creativity impacting practices related to creative process and cognition.  The test questions that were designed emphasized  framing, refining, and elaboration of responses in a much more structured way.  Perspectives related to the creative mindset were apparent in both projects, with practices rela ted to cultivating creative self-efficacy (confiden ce in one’s competence) inherent in responses processes for both.  A takeaway from this analysis is that the labels “test” vs. “game” significantly influenced the perspectives of the groups as they designed their projects and influenced the practices that they put into place in the attempt to measure or generate creativity amongst their participants.
