# How a Teacher’s Personal Reflections Shape Her Decision Making for Classroom Management

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We investigate how a teacher’s personal reflection shapes her decision-making process in classroom management. Following open coding, we analyze the teachers’ reflections seeking to identify her rationale in the decision-making process during the language arts lessons. Our findings indicated that her thinking was based on the student’s behavior, the student’s personality, the educational context, her own emotional perspective and condition, her pedagogical knowledge, and her reflective experience. We discuss the theoretical and methodological implications.

## Theoretical Framework

Teachers’ personal reflection is a powerful professional development tool, which contributes to the improvement of educational practice, leading to positive educat ional change (e.g., Colwell et al., 2015). It can b e adapted in several educational fields, such as classroom manag ement, which is a process by which teachers establi sh a productive learning environment able to promote cog nitive and socio-emotional development of all stude nts (Evertson & Weinstein, 2006). It is described as a complex process, which requires continuous, immediate, and effective decision-making during educational practi ce (Aho et al., 2010). Adapting reflection in class room management is associated with strengthening teacher s' ability to reflect on their daily teaching work,  making informed decisions for classroom management, addres sing students’ behavior, and it can result in a pos itive educational change for the school unit (Bilac& Milijkovic, 2017). Although reflective practice has been recognized as  an important element of the effective decisionmaking process (e.g., Allan, 2018), there is a lack of evidence about how teachers' personal reflection can guide a successful decision-making process (Lloyd, 2019) in classroom management (Aho et al., 2010). Therefore, there is a need to investigate our understanding of teach ers’ decision-making for classroom management and t he elements that consist the teachers’ rationale during the process of decision-making. Thus, the purpose of this study was to investigate how a teacher’s (the first author’s) personal reflection captures and shapes her decision-making process for classroom management during the language arts lessons during a school year, seeking to describe how her rationale in decision making is been constructed.

## Methodology

This is a self-case study (Yin, 2017) utilizing action research strategies (Somekh, 2006). The participants were 11 first grade students of an urban primary school. We collected data during the Language Arts lessons for 7,5 months during 2019-2020 school year. The main source of data was 66 teacher’s personal reflections before, during, and after teaching, around classroom management issues that occurred during her teachings. The first author identified 112 classroom critical incidents lasting 1-5 minutes during teaching that at the time they seemed interesting and memorable. We broke down the 112 critical incidents into 312 smaller units (sub-incidents) based on a teacher’s response to a student’s action. Then, we analyzed each line based on the Louca et. al (2012) 3-dimensional scheme structure. We coded the data related in respect to the way in which the first author thought, interpre ted, and evaluated each student's action, including data fro m the first author’s personal reflections, multiple  readings of critical incidents, her research notes during the actual event, and memos during data analysis.

## Findings

The analysis of the teacher’s reflections revealed that the rationale of the teacher's decisions for h er students' actions was based on six elements: (1) 18.59% of th e cases were based on the student behavior (accepta ble or non-acceptable); (2) 34.94% of cases were based on the student personal information such as family, la nguage skills, social and cultural background; (3) 6.09% of cases were based on the educational context at the moment of the incident; (4) 6.41% of cases were based on the emotional perspective and the emotional condition o f the teacher at the moment of the incident; (5) 23.4% of the cases were based on the teacher’s pedagogical knowledge;

(6) 10.58% of the cases were based on the teacher’s  reflective experience which was developed through the

reflective process during the data collection period.

In other cases, i t was based on the information available to the tea cher at the time of the incident that was related to the person ality of the students involved in the incident. The  teacher’s response to critical incidents was also based on the educational context at the moment of the incident, and on her emotional perspective directly related to her personal characteristics, which guided her way of thinking. In some instances, the teacher’s rational was based on her pedagogical knowledge, which included the teacher’s knowledge from the relevant literature about classr oom management, her previous experiences in managin g student behavior, her pedagogical knowledge of clas sroom management and teaching methodology, as well as her understanding of her personal and professional limitations. Finally, the teacher’s reflective rationale was based on her reflective experience which was built throug h the systematic reflection of her practice during the data collection period and included her knowledge from p revious reflections on action (e.g., effective stra tegies in similar classroom management incidents), as well as her knowledge acquired from the management of a previous event through reflection in action.

Our analysis also revealed that in 13.74% of the to tal cases, the teacher's reflective rationale about  the critical incidents emerged and resolved through a reflective conflict, in which the teacher reflectively examined her conflicting rationale about the way to respond to the incident. These instances included among others, cases in which the teacher misresponded to student’s action in a previous incident which had a negative impact on the current incident, a student behavior was identified by the teacher as both acceptable and non-acceptable and cases that the teacher was thinking to respond to either non-acceptable behavior or depending on the teacher ’s knowledge of student's personality.

## Discussion

Although our findings have some similarities with A ho et al.’s (2010) work, our research differs in a methodological and theoretical manner. In a methodological level, the action research strategies we used allowed us to describe aspects of the investigated area tha t could have not be visible by using other research  strategies, leading to the development of better understanding about the process of classroom management (Somekh, 2006). Furthermore, the analytic structure that we used fo r the data analysis (Louca et. al, 2012) allowed th e data to receive a second level of reflection and, thus, a d eeper level of analysis, which let more pluralistic  and detailed themes to emerge. In a theoretical level, we aimed to direct the attention to the connection among cla ssroom management, the decision-making process involved, and the reflective practice, which lacks evidence. Hence, we contend that the classroom management through the r eflective practice lens could lead to more informed, conscious, and student-centered decision-making, and better understanding of the theories and tacit kn owledge that guide the teacher’s thinking and actions in managing students’ behavior.
