# Nobody Lives Without Regrets

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract

N obody Lives Without Regrets Nobody Lives Without Regrets is a work of interactive speculative fiction in which you, the reader, interview Carmen Torres, a formerly prominent researcher of AI-aided learning, in the year 2056. Depending on the questions you ask, you can guide Carmen’s story to one of twenty-two unique conclusions, each conveying a different speculative future for the integration of AI, education, and society. Inspired by the framework of speculative education, this work instructs the reader to fix their gaze not on the present moment but on the various potential futures that this moment could produce. Through Carmen’s story and your interaction with it, the piece asserts the centrality of the individual’s decisions in shaping the future of our shared society at large. It says that through difficulty and fear, hope always exists for a better future if we work for it. #SlowLS

This video/performance explores time, movement, observation, and rest through the experience of a scholar disabled at the intersection of ‘academia x chronic illness.’ Multiple videos of the subject in the midst of daily campus foot traffic will playback at different speeds, along with several textual provocations, allowing viewers to consider the rushed pace of ‘two-a-year’ academia. Viewers are prompted to consider how the pace at which we engage with research may change the kinds of work that become possible. #SlowLS asks us to consider our pace through our pipeline: from grant to human relation, analysis, rest, and writing. #SlowLS asks us to consider whether we are holding space for humanizing journeys, and the complicated ways disability might help us push back on the oppressive rush of academia. Making Art with Children for Research + Dissemination This entry to the Arts Gallery & Performance events is a collection of artifacts collaboratively made with young children as part of the Learning, Being, and Doing (LBD) in Families study. This submission features three research artifacts co-created by children, parents, and uni-based researchers. These artifacts include: (A) A construction of cardboard, paper, marker, and tape creation meant to illustrate “perspective” in conversations; (B) A cardboard and paper representation of a family at a campfire with the imagined additions of a uni-based researcher’s two cats; (C) A video co-created by a family and uni-researcher that could be shared in research presentations to introduce our shared work. We will prepare accompanying story cards to elaborate on how each artifact represents a blending of child + parent + uni-based researchers’ repertoires. The co-created artifacts represent innovative co-research methodology for dissemination to audiences that matter to collaborative research teams, especially children. D Teo Keifert, Andria Ellis, Little Poof, Bunny, Carrot, Cheetah, Owl, Unicorn Life is a Pure Flame: Thermochromic Kirigami Lotus In the face of today's overlapping crises — environmental, social, existential — it is easy to become preoccupied with endings. This rising tide of anxiety and death-focus can leave individuals and communities searching for meaning and comfort amidst uncertainty. In response, we seek to revive ancient traditions and reintroduce acts of communal making and letting go. Inspired by Eastern cultural traditions and Western psychological insights, and considering the current age in which origami itself is being reconceptualized as a powerful tool for scientific innovation, we envision a new kind of origami lotus—one that brings together ancient symbolism and modern technologies. By incorporating materials such as thermochromic paint, we can vividly visualize transformation and growth, turning each lotus into a living metaphor for change and renewal. For children, the project offers a cognitively engaging experience; for more mature participants, it serves as a ritual for personal reflection on life’s cycles. Jacob Puthipiroj, Supanat Deawrattanakun
