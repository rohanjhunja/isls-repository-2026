# TeamLearn: A Multimodal, AI-enhanced Dashboard for Reflection During Collaborative Problem-Solving

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This demo presents TeamLearn, a multimodal collaboration analytics dashboard designed to facilitate reflection and provide feedback on developing group artifacts during long-term collaborative problem-solving projects. By integrating meetings, documents, and collaboration experience analytics, the dashboard offers visualisations that help groups monitor participation, task progress, and collaboration dynamics. Developed through a design-based research process, the TeamLearn dashboard provides structured opportunities for reflective dialogue. The demo will showcase the dashboard’s core features and illustrate how it can be integrated into collaborative project activities.

Collaborative problem solving (CPS) has become a central element of university teaching, particularly in programmes where students work on extended, open-ended projects that demand sustained coordination and joint knowledge construction. Despite its pedagogical value, collaboration of this kind is often difficult for students to manage. Prior research shows that groups frequently encounter problems such as uneven contributions, fragmented communication, and uncertainty about how their work aligns with shared goals (Damşa, 2014). These difficulties persist partly because CPS depends on several intertwined processes—understanding and advancing the task, monitoring strategies and progress, and maintaining effective social interaction—which students cannot easily observe or evaluate on their own (Graesser et al., 2020). For teachers, supporting these processes is equally challenging. Much of the interaction that shapes collaboration occurs outside formal teaching events, leaving teachers with limited access to the day-to-day dynamics of group work. As a result, they often lack the information needed to intervene at timely moments, identify emerging issues, or help students adopt productive regulatory strategies (Schneider et al., 2021). In longterm, project-based settings, this lack of visibility means that misunderstandings, disengagement, or coordination breakdowns may only become apparent at the end of the project, when opportunities for formative guidance are largely lost (Muukkonen et al., 2020).

Tools that visualise traces of interaction such as learning analytics dashboards (LADs)—have been explored to support both students and teachers in understanding and reflecting on collaborative work. These tools can help make aspects of group activity more transparent, encouraging reflection on participation, task progress, or communication patterns (Kaliisa et al., 2024). However, existing dashboards typically present only basic or high-level indicators (e.g., the number of logins), limiting their usefulness when learners need deeper support in interpreting what the displayed data mean for their ongoing collaboration. Although advances in multimodal analytics and AI have broadened the types of data that can be analysed—from speech and documents to selfreports and behavioural cues—relatively few systems translate these capabilities into pedagogically grounded support for use in authentic higher education settings (Martinez-Maldonado et al., 2019). Recent reviews highlight that analytics tools often remain technically sophisticated but pedagogically under-integrated, leaving learners without adequate scaffolding to make sense of their collaborative processes (Esterhazy et al., 2025, Kaliisa et al., 2024).

To address these challenges, this demo introduces ‘TeamLearn’, a multimodal collaboration analytics dashboard explicitly designed to support reflection in small-group CPS. TeamLearn was developed through a participatory, design-based process involving teachers and students to create pedagogically meaningful visualisations. The dashboard integrates data from meetings, documents, and collaboration experience surveys to o ffer groups shared, interpretable views of how their work is progressing. In this demo paper, we outline the pedagogical motivations and design principles behind TeamLearn, present its key features and underlying technical components, and discuss how it can be incorporated into instructional designs that embed structured, data-supported reflection throughout collaborative project work. Introducing TeamLearn and its design process TeamLearn is a multimodal collaboration analytics dashboard designed to help students navigate the complexities of CPS in project-based tasks. The system brings together data from group meetings, shared documents, and students’ reported collaboration experiences, offering integrated visualisations that make central aspects of group work easier to interpret. The overarching goal of TeamLearn is to provide groups with a shared frame of reference that supports ongoing reflection, planning, and coordination during extended collaborative activities. The pedagogical foundations of TeamLearn draw on well-established perspectives from the learning sciences. CPS is widely understood as an activity that unfolds through the interplay of epistemic, metacognitive, and social processes, all of which must be monitored and negotiated for groups to be effective (Graesser et al., 2020, Järvelä et al., 2016). These processes, however, are typically implicit and distributed across interactions, tools, and time, making them difficult for students to observe, evaluate and act upon. TeamLearn is designed to make these dimensions more transparent by externalising participation patterns, task-related progress, and relational dynamics. In this way, the dashboard operates as a mediating artefact (Säljö, 2002), supporting groups in developing a more coordinated understanding of how their collaboration is unfolding. Its interface design follows human-centred and participatory principles (Giacomin, 2014), prioritising clarity, low cognitive load, and ease of interpretation.

The development of TeamLearn followed an iterative design-based research (DBR) process conducted over three course iterations. This process combined needs analysis, collaborative design sessions with students and teachers, and successive cycles of prototyping and refinement. Early stages focused on identifying the challenges students and instructors faced in monitoring long-term group work. At the same time, later iterations concentrated on translating those needs into multimodal analytics that were both technically feasible and pedagogically meaningful. Co-design activities played an important role in shaping which data were prioritised, how analytics should be visualised, and how the dashboard could best support reflection at different stages of collaborative work. While this demo paper provides only a high-level overview of the design trajectory, the complete methodological account—including empirical insights from each DBR stage is presented in Damşa et al. (2024) and other work in progress. The outcome of this multi-year process is a dashboard that integrates multimodal data streams and presents them through three interrelated components: meeting analytics, document analytics, and collaboration experience analytics. The following section describes these components in more detail, along with the technical mechanisms that support their generation. The TeamLearn dashboard: Features and technical setup TeamLearn brings together multimodal data from group meetings, shared documents, and students’ self-reported collaboration experiences to create a unified environment for reflecting on collaborative problem solving (CPS) (See Figure 1). The system combines automated data processing pipelines with institutionally hosted AI services, such as transcription services to generate analytics, and large language models (GPT LLMs) to summarise documents and meeting transcripts in a way that is both pedagogically meaningful and aligned with Europeandata-protection requirements (GDPR). The dashboard organises these analytics into three components: 1) Meeting Analytics, 2) Document Analytics, and 3) Collaboration Experience Analytics—each produced through dedicated pipelines that transform raw data into interpretable feedback. Meeting Analytics is based on recordings of group meetings captured in Microsoft Teams, whether these occur entirely online or in hybrid/physical formats. Once a meeting is recorded, the audio is transferred to an automated processing pipeline. Speaker diarisation and extraction of speaking times and turn patterns are performed by combining Teams' transcription service (Azure AI Speech) and autotekst.uio.no (NB-Whisper), both secure, university-provided speech services that ensure accurate segmentation, which are then calculated and visualised using programming code. The content of the meeting is analysed by an internally hosted AI model (GPT LLM) guided by customised system prompts. These prompts specify how the discussion should be summarised, including identifying key topics and extracting up to 10 actionable items. This feature helps students recall the content of group meetings, monitor participation patterns, and track progress across meetings. Document Analytics draws on the documents that groups produce and store in their Microsoft Teams (SharePoint) folders. TeamLearn retrieves these drafts through institutional integrations and applies AI-assisted s ystem prompts that encode the task requirements and expected elements of the group report. The resulting analytics highlight central ideas addressed in the draft and indicate aspects that may require further attention. The intention is to support epistemic monitoring by helping students assess how well their developing work aligns with the project’s academic expectations.

Collaboration Experience Analytics visualises students’ perceptions of their group processes based on short surveys administered at key stages of the project. These surveys are completed through a secure university survey system, and the dashboard automatically retrieves responses. The resulting visualisations show aggregated perspectives on dimensions such as communication, contribution, confidence in progress, and role clarity. By revealing areas of alignment or divergence in group members’ perceptions, this component supports socially shared reflection on the relational and organisational dynamics of collaboration. Instructional use cases TeamLearn is intended to be integrated into courses where students work on extended collaborative projects that require structured opportunities to reflect on their progress and collaboration dynamics. The dashboard can be embedded at predetermined points in the course—such as after major meetings, milestone submissions, or before supervision sessions—to help groups take stock of their participation patterns, task alignment, and shared experiences. Instructors can use the Meeting Analytics to support discussions about coordination and role distribution in supervision meetings. At the same time, Document Analytics can help students evaluate their evolving output against project requirements. The Collaboration Experience Analytics can serve as a prompt for conversations about communication quality, group climate, or emerging relational issues that might otherwise remain unspoken. Because each module offers a different perspective on collaboration, the dashboard can be used flexibly to support different pedagogical goals. For example, it may support reflective debriefs after major group activities, serve as a shared reference point during teacher–student consultations, or be incorporated into structured reflection tasks that encourage students to plan next steps. Although TeamLearn does not replace teacher guidance, its analytics can help students arrive at supervision sessions with a clearer understanding of their current situation, enabling more focused and productive conversations about how to move forward. P lanned demo session The demonstration will be delivered as a presenter-led walkthrough of the TeamLearn dashboard. Because the system currently operates entirely within institutionally managed and protected environments, external users cannot access the live interface. All data including meeting recordings, documents, and survey responses—are stored and processed within secure university services, and analytics are visible only to members of each student group. These data-handling requirements ensure strong privacy and confidentiality, but also mean that the demo will rely on example data displayed by the presenter rather than participant interaction with the tool. During the session, the presenter will guide attendees through the dashboard’s three components: Meeting Analytics, Document Analytics, and Collaboration Experience Analytics, illustrating how each module processes and visualises different forms of collaboration data. The walkthrough will highlight how the tool is used in practice within a course context, how analytics support structured reflection at various stages of group work, and how the system aligns with broader pedagogical aims related to CPS.
