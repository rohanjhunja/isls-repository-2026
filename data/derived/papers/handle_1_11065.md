# Toward Writing Effective Solutions for Computational Problems: An Analysis of LeetCode Solutions

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Solving computational problems plays a significant role in learning computer science. However, the complexity involved often leads to frustration. This study aims to address these challenges by finding out how we can write effective solutions. The author analyzed top-voted community-posted solutions and identified their common characteristics: detailed descriptions, clear code, mention of computer science terms, and efforts to engage readers. The results can inform future developments of guidelines for writing solutions.

## Introduction

Solving data structure and algorithm problems is an essential part of computer science (CS) education. However, the complexity for beginners to develop reasoning f or solving computational problems leads to frustrat ion and a high dropout rate (Silva et al., 2019). Understanding the type of explanations and examples that help learners the most when solving computational problems can provide insight into how people learn CS and assist in developing supports that help alleviate the struggle in learning CS. Prior research has identified the characteristics o f good code examples for general CS Q&A forums (Nasehi et al., 2012). However, there is no similar research on computat ional problem-solving online communities. This study focuses on LeetCode, a popular website for coding competitions and technical interview preparation (LeetCode, n.d.). It offers over two thousand compu tational problems that users can solve. For each problem, users can also post their solutions and explanations to the solutions forum. Each post can be upvoted or downvoted. The user-generated nature of the posts provides diverse approaches to writing solutions, a nd the voting system gives us a metric to evaluate what readers consider helpful. The research question is: What are the common attributes of top-voted LeetCode solutions? Top-voted solutions on LeetCode were analyzed to identify the characteristics of solutio ns readers prefer. The results can guide future research on the social and cognitive f actors that make these solutions effective, which c an improve the delivery of computer science knowledge to learners and lead to the creation of guidelines for writing effective solutions for computational problems.

## Methodology

The analysis was performed by a master’s student at  a university located in the northeastern part of the United States of America. The three  most upvoted solutions of each of the first ten que stions in LeetCode’s “Top-100 Liked Question” list were selected, totaling 30 samples. The next step was open coding, which was used to identify the relevant characteristics of the solutions. This process is similar to open coding in grounded theory (Charmaz, 2006). Four different parts of the solutions  were coded: the writing  style, the code style, the use of computer science terms, and the engagement with readers. After coding each solution, the codes were categorized, and their frequencies in the dataset were recorded.

## Findings

Twenty codes were identified and organized into four categories: writing style, code style, computer sci ence terms, and engagement (see Table 1).

Writing style A detailed writing style is preferred. The most com mon approach is to write a step-by-step description of how their code works. Some authors also include thought processes, intuitions, and dry runs that use an example to go through each line of the code. Many posts utilize headers to guide what each section is about. Common  header names include code, approach, and complexity. Another approach is to include multiple solutions. Frequently, a brute-force solution, which is the more intuitive but less efficient method, will be described first. Code style Most code examples in the solutions don’t use embedded comments. This is particularly prevalent in older posts. Some solutions use comments for structural purposes: to segment their code into sections and provide descriptions. Complexity, which describes the efficiency of algorithms in Big-O notation, is the most common. Other categories of terms include problem-solving strategy (e.g., dynamic programming), data structure (e.g., linked list), and algorithms (e.g., BFS). Writing proofs is also common. Of the eight posts with proofs, six wrote about the comple xity, and four wrote about the correctness. Most pr oofs were informal, with only two posts with mathematical formulas. Engagement 83% of posts include means to engage readers. The m ost common method is to highlight the post’s clarit y, followed by highlighting the efficiency and conciseness of their code. Other ways to engage with readers include asking for upvotes and suggestions. Emojis and memes are also oftentimes incorporated into the solution posts.

## Discussion and future works

There are three main limitations in this research. First, the age of the solution can affect how many upvotes it gets. 40% of the posts were made in 2015. Older posts that have high upvotes tend to have less expl anation and use fewer headers. In our sample, only 33% of all posts use headers, but all five posts in 2023 use headers. One solution is to select only newer posts to reflect the community’s recent opini on. The second is that this study did not analyze posts by problem categories. Each categ ory could have different criteria for effec tive solution posts. Collecting more data can enable category-level analysis. Third, there is only one coder. Therefore, there is  no coding validation from inter-rater reliability.

Possible future research includes studying why user s find these characteristics helpful. This can lead  to a better understanding of the learning process in C S education and, in turn, to the development of gui delines and templates for writing effective solutions for computational problems.
