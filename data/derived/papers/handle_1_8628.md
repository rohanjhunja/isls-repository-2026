# Growing Student-Workers as Informal Learners

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We investigate an urban farm as an informal learning setting and the interactions between experts and novices using situated cognition as our framework. The poster is situated within a large research practitioner partnership (RPP). Initial findings are reported as they relate to learning in a novel environment, and that it relates to the 2022 ICLS theme of “elucidating theories and processes of learning and teaching.”

## Introduction

Consistent with the “elucidating theories and proce sses of learning and teaching” theme for ICLS 2022, researching innovative processes of learning plays an important role to diversify educational experien ces. This research is part of a broader project to understand  how an urban farm can strengthen the connection be tween student learning and engagement in perceived commun ity spaces through a research practitioner partners hip (RPP). In this poster, we present the first phase of our analysis, which: (a) identifies informal learning that occurs at an urban farm embedded on a university campus; and, (b) initiates the discussion for interactions between the expert(s) and novices on the urban farm. The research questions guiding this poster is: What are the characteristics of learning embedded within the urban farm project?

## Theoretical Framework and Methodology

There is a wealth of literature on informal learnin g, interest-based spaces, and expert-novices (Azeve do, 2018; Harris et al., 2016; Hmelo-Silver et al., 2007; Rogoff et al., 2016). However, there is a dearth of research within the learning sciences surrounding agriculture and f ood-based spaces. Additionally, this research is gu ided by literature focusing on the Black lived experiences and how that influences their learning (Vue et al., 2017). This research is grounded using situated cognition as a theoretical framework. This framework states that learning is situated in a particular context, social in nature,  and distributed across the individual, other peopl e, and tools (Brown et al., 1989). The urban farm is situated within an informal learning and interest-based site, the learning involves multiple participants (farm staff, student-workers, and the researchers), and the knowledge is distributed across the individual, other farm workers, and the tools being used at the farm. This participatory research study was conducted at a historically Black work-study college’s urban farm in the Southwest U.S. Participants included two sta ff members of the farm and seven student-workers. T he researchers’ data collection included observations, microethnographic field notes, semi-structured interviews, and artifacts. The first round of data analysis included inductive coding using situated cognition (Brown et al., 1989) and expert-novice frameworks (Hmelo-Silver, 2007). Future iterations of analysis and this research wil l aim to pull from literature of lived experiences of the Black students, and how that influences their learning.

## Initial Findings

Learners – staff and student-workers – fully partic ipate on the farm, from planting crops, tending to the raised beds, harvesting crops, marketing, and delivering the produce to clients. Additionally, the more novice studentworkers collaborate with the more expert farm staff to ask clarifying questions related to different aspects of the farming cycle. Student-workers can apply their exis ting knowledge of gardening/farming, biochemistry, or business with their experiences at the farm. One student-worker worker was able to utilize their experience with growing peppers to experiment with different types of soil and growing methods, to identify what works best for their operation.

In Figure 1, a farm staff member presents the proper techniques to plant collard green seeds. The situated and distributed nature of the learning is presented  by first showing the student the steps and providi ng a verbal explanation of the steps and justification. The student then demonstrates his understanding by verbally restating the steps while practicing the planting.

## Discussion

Learning in a garden-based setting was examined by Dewey (1916), and he argued that in occupational and vocational education, there is a focus more on the trade or skill which, inhibits and perpetuates the existing industrialized complex (Dewey, 1916; Kliebard, 1987). Focusing solely on the trade or skill, limits th e more ICLS2022 Proceedings 1894 © ISLS expansive and holistic type of learning that can occur. For example, during the planting process, there is a missed opportunity for the expert to discuss the importance of the larger science concepts and processes that are and will be at play, such as, biology, chemistry, and physic s. However, during an interview there was an exampl e of vocational education stepping beyond the intended industrialized idea of social control and occupational training. A student was able to use the agricultural science knowledge from the farm and apply her skills obtain ed from her classroom instruction to develop and implement a marketing plan for a community farmers market. Th is parallels the expert-novice literature, such as Hme lo-Silver and colleagues (2007), which asserts and points to more productive learning gains in not only the procedures of farming but also the STEM topics embedded in such activities (soil composition, growing cycle, chemic al components of fertilizers, biomass growth). The situated nature of the learning between the experts and novi ces at the urban farm provide an interesting insigh t to the learning and knowledge building for those involved. To reiterate, this is only at the beginning stages of analysis and will continue to build the situated nature between the experts and novices, as well as using Dewey’s work to move forward and beyond vocational and occupational learning of a skill.
