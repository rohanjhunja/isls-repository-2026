# Developing an Evaluation Method for Inquiry-Based Courses Using ‘Dialogical Reports'

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores dialogical reports as an assessment method for inquiry-based learning in higher education. These Q&A-style reports, structured as interview articles, enable students to articulate and reflect on their learning experiences. An analysis of 144 reports identified two main question types: factual clarification (35.8%) and interpretative reflection (61.8%), the latter linking class content to personal experiences. The findings highlight the potential of dialogical reports to effectively make learning processes visible and deepen student engagement.

## Introduction and research purpose

In this study, Inquiry-Based Learning (IBL) and Project-Based Learning (PBL) are collectively defined as "courses incorporating inquiry activities." A defin ing feature of such courses is that learners formul ate questions to solve problems and engage in self-directed learning.

To address this aspect, Makino et al. (2021) proposed “Dialogical Reports” as a final assignment in PBL. This task requires students to create a Q&A-style interview article, emphasizing the importance of adopting others' perspectives. Through this process, students engage  in comprehensive reflection on their learning expe riences, generate questions, and deepen their understanding (Makino et al., 2021). A fundamental aspect of inquiry-based learning is that learners generate their own questi ons and continue their inquiry process. Therefore, “Dialogical Reports”, which integrate question generation into the writ ing process, are considered highly compatible with inquiry-based learning.

The purpose of this study is to investigate how the  use of “Dialogical Reports ” in higher education courses incorporating inquiry activities enables in dividual learners to reflect on and visualize their  learning processes. Furthermore, this study aims to propose “Dialogical Reports ” as a novel assessment method for evaluating the learning process.

## Method

The participants in this study were undergraduate s tudents enrolled in the course "R," offered in 2022  and 2023 at the Faculty of Q, University P, as part of the l earning sciences curriculum. The course provides students with an understanding of learning mechanisms and support  methods through lectures, practical exercises, and  group discussions, while also engaging them in individual  inquiry projects based on their self-defined research topics. The course was conducted over 15 sessions (90 minutes each) in 2022 and 14 sessions (100 minutes each) in 2023, with adjustments to the schedule based on university policies. “Dialogical Reports” were assigned as a mandatory end-of-semester task. At the beginning of the report, students are required to describe the intended audi ence of the fictional interview article as well as the characters involved in the interview. They are then instructed  to compose a Q&A-style interview article featuring fictional characters. Below is a representative example of an interview a rticle. In this case, the interviewer asks a studen t about their experience in the course.

Interviewer: What was the most memorable aspect of the course for you? Student: I struggled with selecting a theme for the inquiry project. This study analyzed a total of 144 Dialogical Reports, consisting of 93 reports submitted by 111 students in 2022 and 51 reports submitted by 61 students in 2023. To examine what and how students learned from the course and how they reflected on the learning conte nt, this study focused on analyzing the "questions"  in the fictional interview articles. However, rather than analyzing the "questions" in isolation, the study also considered the corresponding "responses" and conducted both quantitative and qualitative analyses.

## Results and discussion

This study focused on the potential of dialogical reports to facilitate holistic reflections on the learning experience. A quantitative and a mixed-methods approach was employed to analyze 144 dialogical reports, yielding a total of 941 questions (Table 1). The questions were categorized into two types: factual-oriented questions (337, or 35.8%) and interpretation-oriented questions (582, or 61.8%). Fact ual-oriented questions aimed to elicit explanations about class design, theories, and concepts covered in the sessions. In contrast, interpretation-oriented questions were classified into three subcategories: (1) refle ctions that comprehensively reviewed the entire cla ss, (2) reflections on experiences related to class exercis es or projects, and (3) reflections that contextual ized past learning in relation to future learning. The predom inance of interpretation-oriented questions, comprising approximately 60% of the total, suggests that dialogical reports have a tendency to encourage inductive reflection on experiences.

Next, the proportion of students who generated each type of question was analyzed (Table 2). In 2022, the most frequently observed category was (3) reflections contextualizing past and future learning, written by 62 out of 93 students (66.7%). Conversely, in 2023, the ma jority of students, 43 out of 51 (84.3%), engaged i n (2) reflections on class exercises or projects. These r esults suggest that dialogical reports are effectiv e tools for evaluating how learners reflect on their growth and  challenges. In 2023, students were encouraged to actively utilize generative AI to refine their self-defined inquiry topics. As a result, the 2023 repor ts included reflections on the novelty of generative AI. However, across bo th years, a common feature of the reports was t heir in-depth reflection on the course experience.

The findings of this study are discussed from three  perspectives. First, the relationship between Q&A-style interviews and the promotion of reflection is considered. Q&A-style interviews encourage learners to reflect on their classes by thinking critically  about “to whom” and “how” questions should be addre ssed. This process allows students to take on the role of a monitor and shift perspectives to those of others, facilitati ng class reflections through perspective-switching (Miyake, 1986). Moreover, dialogical exch anges involving multiple voices integrate diverse perspectives, enhancing students’ reflective expression and deepening their understanding of the activity’s significance (Gergen & Gill, 2023).

Second, dialogical reports enable learners to reflect on inquiry-based activities as processes. Inquiry-based activities often require extended periods to yield tangible results. However, the use of dialogical re ports allows learners to focus on the proces s, reflecting on their activities while assigning m eaning to them. This approach supports learners in conducting self-evaluations with a “growth mindset” (Dweck, 2006), cultivating proactive engagement with future inquiry-based practices.

Third, dialogical reports help learners connect exp eriences beyond the classroom with their in-class learning. By fostering internal dialogue, dialogical reports integrate students’ inquiry experiences in the classroom with their personal, past experiences. This process enables learners to ascribe meaning to their experiences and develop the capacity to generate new questions. Consequently, reflections facilitated by dialogical reports do not merely conclude with the inquiry learning conducted  in a single class. Instead, they nurture shifts in  students’ perspectives on learning through the process of constructing meaning from their experiences.

In conclusion, dialogical reports may be proposed as a novel method of evaluating how learners construct knowledge and skills by focusing on their learning processes.
