# Learning Health Sanitary Practices Through Scaffolded Questioning in Interactive eBooks for Young Children

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Adult assistance during the usage of digital devices may not always be available for children in certain circumstances. Taking this into account, we seek to study how far young children can learn in a scaffolded environment through interactive eBooks without adult intervention. Using the topic of learning sanitary hand practice concepts, the eBook will use scaffolded questioning methods to guide the child in the self-learning process.

## Introduction

Since the pandemic, children's dependency on digita l devices has increased with the number of work-at-home parents and quarantine lifestyles (Wong et al., 202 1). This adds to the issues where parent/teacher di rect supervision in informal learning environments may not always be available, especially for those not as privileged to have that timely and economical benefit. Meanwhile, the significance in health awareness education has also risen, where many digital learning platforms using different instructional methods emerged to help children learn related issues and information during the pandemic (Tiwari, 2020). The usage of interactive eBooks or mobile apps as a medium between an adult and a child for educational purposes have been examined. Some have looked into the strategies that are used to steer the learning process (e.g. Tiwari, 2021), and some have investigated how  scaffolding methods during mobile and science-rela ted learning increased performance and motivation (Call aghan & Reich, 2021; Georgiou & Angeli, 2021). Uscianowski et al. (2018) examined storybook sharin g methods, which was effective in easing the cognit ive understanding process for young children. Saliminen et al. (2021) studied the usefulness of scaffolded dialogues in toddler classrooms which enhanced learning in academic activities. However, there is a need for further research on scaffolding features that contribute to the success of young learners (Shoukry et al., 2015, as cited in Callaghan & Reich, 2021). Therefore, this study seeks to expa nd existing theory by identifying how young childre n can cognitively process and engage in understanding the importance of health practices through eBooks. Our research questions are: 1) What aspects of scaffolded messages through technology are most effective for children during the early learning stages? and 2) How much are they able to foster deeper learning by themselves with the guidance of scaffolded eBooks?

## Theoretical background

Scaffolding uses cognitive support methods which are known to be an effective strategy towards young students who especially need help in learning difficult tasks (Georgiou & Angeli, 2021). Diverse scaffolding methods exist, such as feedback using hints to foster motivation and guidance (Callaghan & Reich, 2021). Salminen et al. (2021) mention the importance of dialogue with early child  learners as it helps them build “deeper thinking a nd communication skills through shared meaning making”  (p.13). Like feedback and dialogue, questioning approaches help children develop reasoning and reflection of their own thoughts (Uscianowski et al., 2018; Wasik & Jacobi-Vessels, 2016). Since the use of praise wi th feedback can stimulate learning and connect real  life situations (Callaghan & Reich, 2021), we will integrate questions in quiz form with their corresponding feedback into the eBook in lieu of adult questioning methods to probe deeper thinking (Wasik & Jacobi-Vessels, 2016). Overall layout of the eBook The design of the eBook will apply a storybook flow  using the topic of teaching sanitary health practi ces in relation to the pandemic era. It aims to use scaffolded questioning and feedback methods in the form of hints and answers to guide preschool and early elementary children towards comprehending new concepts. The story will incorporate structured questions, feedback, and dialogue in the form of hints along the journey for th e child. As an example (see Table 1), the first quiz will be presented after the opening narration and will question the actions needed after an outdoor activity. It will be examined if this will help replace the role of an adult questioning the ICLS2022 Proceedings 2136 © ISLS child about a certain topic before delving deeper into the contents (Wasik & Jacobi-Vessels, 2016). This narration will guide the reader to reflect on everyday activities until they reach the quiz probing self-thinking towards the topic to be learned throughout the book. Praise and hints will be used to provide encouragement and instruction for the children when they answer the quizzes (Call aghan & Reich, 2021). As an additional function, an  audio option will be provided in consideration for childr en with different needs, such as age-based, or thos e with dyslexia and the visually impaired (Tiwari, 2021; Chang et al., 2022). This button feature next to the written text will read aloud the sentences for the children, hence, becoming an interactive feature and acting as a role for the need of adult supervision when understanding the writing. ## Future work and implications

With our prototype, we plan to investigate how interactive eBooks can be used as a scaffolding tool to aid young children in understanding health-related concepts. We plan to observe how they navigate through the eBook and how questioning can scaffold their understanding of sanitary practices during pandemics and flu seasons. While previous research examined teacher/parent and child interaction (Chang et al., 2022; Callaghan & Reich, 2021), this study will probe into how far technology can i tself play the role of a physical adult, and how fa r into the learning process should the scaffolding be preserved (Georgiou & Angeli, 2021). In addition, the study hopes to provide information for creating instructional meth ods directed at young children for diverse needs, i ncluding those who cannot always be supervised by an adult during a digital learning process.
