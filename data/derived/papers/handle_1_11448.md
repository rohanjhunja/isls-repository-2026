# Curatorial Public Pedagogy Across Digital and Natural Spaces: Enhancing Interdisciplinary Learning and Audience Engagement

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines the integration of digitally enhanced indoor spaces and outdoor natural environments in efforts to support transdisciplinary learning through curatorial public pedagogy. By incorporating biodegradable artworks and digital media, student curators engaged in participatory practices aimed at connecting audiences with art across distinct settings. The findings suggest that blending natural and digital spaces may enhance collaborative learning, audience engagement, and ecological awareness. These efforts aim to contribute to ongoing discussions on inclusive and interdisciplinary educational approaches within curatorial practices.

## Introduction

Curatorial public pedagogy, a framework merging cur atorial practices with public engagement, reimagine s exhibitions as dynamic spaces for collaborative lea rning. By positioning curators as learners and audi ences as active contributors, it challenges tradit ional notions of curatorial authority and fosters c o-creative relationships between curators and viewers. O’Neill (2012) emphas ized that this approach shifts the curator’s role f rom an authoritative figure to a reflective facilitator, e ngaging critically with audience perspectives. Simi larly, HooperGreenhill (2007) observed that interactive exhibiti ons democratize learning by enabling audiences to s hape curatorial narratives through their feedback and pa rticipation, thereby transforming exhibitions into dialogical spaces of shared meaning-making.

Recent trends in digital and outdoor exhibitions hi ghlight their educational value, yet integrating th em meaningfully remains a challenge. This study responds by proposing curatorial approaches that unite digital tools and natural settings within a shared pedagogical fr amework. Digital technology amplifies these interac tive possibilities by introducing innovative methods of engagement. Jenkins (2009) claimed that participatory culture within digital environments allows audiences to act ively contribute to in terpretive processes, blurring the lines between creator and observer. In curatorial pedagog y, digital tools such as video projections, interac tive installations, and multimedia displays serve as bridges between traditional and experimental approaches, fostering deeper audience involvement. In addition, these tools also enable collaboration across psychology, environmental studies, and urban planning, expanding curatorial practice to support interdisciplinary narratives. Natural environments offer complementary opportunities for learning and engagement, particularly when integrated with curatorial public pedagogy. Outdoor  exhibitions and installations enable experiential learning, encouraging ecological awareness and sustainability. Louv (2005) argued that immersive natural settings cultivate mindfulness and ecological literacy, fostering a deeper connection to environmental education. When these natural spaces are paired with digital elements, they creat e a distinctive int erplay that balances sensory immersion with analytical engagement, offering a multidimensional educational experience. This paper discusses how these two distinct exhibition spaces, a digitally enhanced indoor gallery and an outdoor garden featuring biodegradable artworks, ca n enhance learning through curatorial public pedago gy. Specifically, the project investigates how c uratorial practices can engage diverse audiences, s upport interdisciplinary collaboration, and promote sustai nability. The findings of the study aim to contribu te to the ongoing discourse on curatorial public pedagogy by examining the integration of natural and digital spaces within contemporary art education.  As educational settings continue to evolve toward hy brid and experiential models, curatorial public pedagogy offers a timely response  to current demands for more inclusive and particip atory learning environments.

## Framework and study design

This study applies the principles of curatorial pub lic pedagogy, integrating curatorial practices with  public engagement to create dynamic learning environments. By positioning curators as learners and audiences as active contributors, this approach facili tates collaborative meaning-making and democratizes the educational process. Grounded in the works of O’Neill (2012) and Hooper-Greenhill (2007), the framework explores how the interplay between curated spaces, audience engagement, and in terdisciplinary co llaboration fosters a reflective, participatory experience. Digital tools and natural  environments are key elements in this model, servi ng as platforms for both sensory and intellectual engagem ent, thus advancing sustainability and transdiscipl inary learning.

Description of the exhibition spaces The study spanned a sixteen-week period and centered on two interconnected exhi bition spaces: Gallery 3 at the School of Art and Design and the Red Oak Rain Garde n in Champaign, Illinois. Gallery 3, a digitally en hanced indoor space, featured multimedia displays such as video projections and audience voice recordings, of fering an immersive perspective on the curatorial process (Fi gure 1). Exhibits included looping video interviews  with student artists, audio collages of audience feedbac k, and motion-triggered projections that reflected evolving curatorial themes. Complementing this, the outdoor Red Oak Rain Garden showcased biodegradable artwork s created by student artists, emphasizing ecological mindfulness and sustainability (Figure 2). Sculptures embedded in the landscape and ephemeral works designed to decompose over time were featured. The two distinctive spaces encouraged transitions between environments and eng agement with themes of connection, collaboration, a nd experiential learning. Audience partici pation occurred in both locations, with gallery vis itors leaving audio reflections and garden visitors interacting through QR-based prompts and informal conversations. ## Methods

This study employed a qualitative research methodology to investigate how curatorial public pedagogy facilitated interdisciplinary learning and audience participati on across digital and natural exhibition spaces. Ni ne student curators maintained curatorial  journals throughout the project, documenting their decisions, challenges, and the impact of audience participation. Merriam and Tisdell (2016) described journals as effective tools for understanding participants’ processes in dynamic and iterative context s (e.g., curation). Audience participation was a key element. During the indoor exhibition, visitors provided voice recordings reflecting on their experiences with the artwork. These recordings were later integ rated into the outdoor exhibition, connecting the two spaces. Informal conversations and written feedback further  enriched the understanding of public involvement, aligning with Falk and Dierking’s (2000) argument that publi c engagement shapes educational practices in exhibi tion settings. Student curators also submitted two struc tured written reflections, analyzing their learning  experiences, curatorial strategies, and interdisciplinary collaboration. Creswell and Poth (2016) emphasized that s tructured reflections provide rich data for exploring participants’ thought processes. The project involved nine student curators from div erse academic disciplines, including psychology, urban planning, engineering, and studio art. Their collaborative efforts were evident in exhibit plann ing: psychology students crafted prompts related to emotional engagement, while urban planning students considered spatial navigation and environmental interaction. E ighteen student artists contributed artworks and in terviews, while audience input shaped curatorial decisions and supported participatory, interdisciplinary learning. Semi-structured interviews with student curators provide d additional perspectives on their experiences. These interviews, lasting 15 to 60 minutes, explore d interdisciplinary collaboration, audience engagem ent, and the integration of digital and natural elements. Ch armaz (2014) noted that semi-structured interviews balance guided inquiry with flexibility to address emergent themes. Observational notes documented behaviors, logistical challenges, and audience interactions during planni ng and exhibitions. Braun and Clarke (2006) argued that observational data enrich understanding by capturing non-verbal and contextual aspects. Thematic coding (Guest et al., 2011) began with open codes and was refined  as patterns emerged. The analysis explored how cur atorial public pedagogy shaped interdisciplinary learning, audience participation, and the connection between digital and natural environments. This approach ensured a compr ehensive understanding of the study’s educational objectives.

## Results

This study explored the dynamics of curatorial publ ic pedagogy within two distinct exhibition spaces: a digitally enhanced indoor gallery and an outdoor garden featu ring biodegradable artworks. The findings, drawn fr om curatorial journals, written reflections, interviews, and observational notes, revealed how these spaces shaped the student curators’ learning experiences and audience  interactions. Key themes that emerged include the interplay between digital and natural settings, audience enga gement’s influ ence on curatorial decision-making, and the impact of transdisciplinary collaboration.

Interplay between digital and natural exhibition spaces The dual exhibition settings offered unique learnin g opportunities by merging digital and natural envi ronments. The indoor gallery, equipped with multimedia tools, served as a controlled space where curators could experiment with technology to present artist narratives and audience reflections. Students frequently mentioned the challenges and rewards of managing interactive digital components, such as configuring audio collages and video projections. One student curator noted, “The technical setup required us to think critically about how digital tools could tell a cohesive story.” In contrast, the outdoor garden re quired adaptability to less predictable conditions,  such as weather and public accessibility. Curators reflecte d on the need to consider sustaina bility, noting the importance of aligning biodegradable artwork with ecological principles. These challenges signified how the settings shaped curatorial practices, encouraging curators to think holistically about the relationship between space, audience, and environmental context.

Audience engagement as a catalyst for curatorial development Audience participation emerged as a central influen ce on curatorial practices. In the indoor space, vi sitor voice recordings provided real-time feedback that informed subsequent curatorial d ecisions. For instance, curators integrated these recordings into the outdoor exhibition to establish a narrative connection between the two spaces. One curator observed, “Hearing visitors’ personal stories about nature made us rethink how to present the outdoor artworks in a way that would resonate with those reflections.” The outdoor exhibition amplified public interaction through activities like scavenger hunts and informa l conversations. Visitors’ spontaneous engagement, particularly among children, prompted curators to a dapt their approaches, emphasizing inclusivity and accessibility. For example, curators implemented QR codes to replace printed tombstones, aligning with feedback about sustainable practices. These interactions underscored the value of audience-centered curation, where public input directly informed exhibition design.

Transdisciplinary collaboration in curatorial practices The integration of diverse academic backgrounds amo ng the student curators enriched the curatorial pro cess, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration. Psycholo gy students designed audience prompts to evoke emot ional connections, while urban planning studen ts addressed spatial accessibility and safety conce rns. Graphic design students crafted promotional materials that effecti vely engaged the audience, and art history students contextualized the artworks within broader cultural  narratives. This collaboration  not only propelled the exhibitions’ depth but also underlined the importance of flexibility in addressing diverse challenges. One curator reflected, “Working with peers from other disciplin es pushed us to think beyond our usual perspectives, which made the exhibition more dynamic and inclusive.” The findings manifest how the integration of digita l and natural spaces, audience engagement, and transdisciplinary collaboration collectively enhanced curatorial public pedagogy. By navigating the complexities of these distinct environments, student curators developed adaptable, audience-centered approaches that redefined their understanding of curatorial practice as an ed ucational and participatory process. This study con tributes to the growing body of research on curatorial pedagogy, providing evidence of its transformative potentia l in fostering interdisciplinary learning and public engagement.

## Discussions

This study examines how curatorial public pedagogy enables interdisciplinary learning and audience participation by connecting digital and natural environments. The  interaction between these settings allowed student  curators to engage with audiences, navigate technical and environmental challenges, and develop collaborative approaches. These findings align with previous studies that emphasize the participatory nature of curatorial practices (O’Neill, 2012; Hooper-Greenhill, 2007).

The digitally enhanced indoor gallery provided an i mmersive space where curators worked with multimedia tools such as video projections and audi o collages. These tools encouraged interactive expe riences, facilitating connections between artists, curators,  and visitors. By integrating visitor voice recordi ngs into the outdoor exhibition, the curators demonstrated how digital elements can create meaningful links across spaces. This approach resonates with Jenkins’ (2009) discus sion of participatory culture in digital environmen ts, where audiences actively co ntribute to curatorial narratives. To this extent, the project resonates with Bishop’s (2023) assertion that participatory practices can reshape exhibitions into collaborative processes, shifting focus from curatorial authority to shared experiences. The out door garden, on the other hand, required curators t o balance artistic vision with environmental awareness. The u se of biodegradable artworks reflected ecological p rinciples while challenging curators to adapt their approache s to the natural setting. Louv’s (2005) concept of experiential learning in nature aligns with the curators’ reflec tions on ecological awareness and audience interact ion. This outdoor environment allowed for spontaneous public engagement, especially among children, reinforcing the importance of accessibility in curatorial practices.

A key takeaway from this study is the collaborative approach enabled by the students’ diverse academic backgrounds. The contributions from fields such as psychology, urban planning, graphic design, and art  history enriched the exhibitions and expanded the curatoria l process. This interdisciplinary approach supporte d the development of critical thinking, adaptability, and  creativity, making it an effective educational mod el for contemporary art practices and beyond. This diversi ty mirrors Charmaz’s (2014) vi ew that collaboration across disciplines generates richer understandings by drawing from multiple knowledge systems. Audience participation was another significant element in shaping the curatorial process. By responding to visitor feedback, curators demonstrated the valu e of flexible, audience-centered approaches in educational practices. These findings suggest that participator y curation helps build connections between art, aud iences, and broader social and ecological themes, enhancing the  relevance and inclusivity of exhibitions. While pr omising, the findings are limited by the university context and participant group, which may affect transferabi lity to other curatorial settings. Future research c ould explore how this approach functions in communi ty-based or museum settings beyond higher education. Expanding partici pant diversity may also reveal new ways audiences e ngage with curatorial learning across cultural and social contexts.

## Conclusion

This study examines how curatorial public pedagogy connects digitally enhanced indoor spaces and outdo or natural environments to support interdisciplinary l earning and audience participation. The findings su ggest that combining digital tools and biodegrada ble artworks can create dynamic, interactive educat ional experiences emphasizing sustainability, inclusivity, and collaboration. By working in these distinct environments,  student curators explored new approaches to curation as an educational and participatory practice. The implications extend beyond art education, encouraging interdisciplinary  collaborations that engage diverse fields such as environmental studies, psychology, and digital medi a. These efforts may inspire further research and p ractices aimed at creating inclusive learning environments that integrate technology, ecology, and participatory ar t. This project shows how curatorial pedagogy connects peop le, disciplines, and environments through education al exhibitions.
