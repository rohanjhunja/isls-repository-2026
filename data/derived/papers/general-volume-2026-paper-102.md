# What’s In A Game? Framing Undergraduate Improvisers Contributions as Legitimate Participation in Improv’s Oral History

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract

W hat’s In A Game? Framing Undergraduate Improvisers Contributions as Legitimate Participation in Improv’s Oral History This work is a collection of improv forms (also known as games) developed by students in my undergraduate game-studies course, "Improv for Games." Students first learned games during class and by watching performances. Then, they modified the rules of those games. Finally, they all created games that they performed for each other. My students, few of whom considered themselves improvisers before taking my course, created brilliant new forms that demonstrate a deep understanding, and they deserve to have that recognized. I have tried to motivate my pedagogical approach with the idea of meaningful making. I want my students to feel that they have contributed something towards furthering an art form and that they have become better designers in the process. These improv games (demonstrated by members of Spicy Clamato, the University of Illinois' longest running improv troupe) are "good enough" to be performed, enjoyed, and modified for years to come. Tong Li “We Know Something You Don’t Know”: A Live Actual Play Performance This presentation will be an improvisational “actual play” performance of the table-top roleplaying game “We Know Something You Don’t Know.” This game, designed by the authors to support educator learning, guides players in creating characters based on commonly marginalized troublemaker archetypes in classrooms. We invite listeners to consider ways the creation of “complex yet capable” troublemakers in co-constructed schools opens space for educators to challenge power hierarchies in figured worlds of schooling and cultivate critical civic empathy.
