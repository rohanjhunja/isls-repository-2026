# Designing Scientific Practices Through Exploration, Collaboration, Creation, and Reflection: A Multi-Layered Model

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examined a knowledge-building environment mediated by Knowledge Forum®, enhanced by a multi-layered scientific practices model designed to foster collective knowledge advancement. Forty-eight Grade 5 students participated in scientific practices centered on Lighting a Small Bulb, engaging in the model comprising four interconnected layers: exploration, collaboration, creation, and reflection. Quantitative analyses revealed significant improvements in students’ contributions to collective knowledge advancement across phases, with notable variations among high-, medium-, and low-contribution groups. Qualitative findings illustrated how students engaged in increasingly sophisticated scientific practices, transitioning from group exploration to scientific communication, extending scientific field knowledge, and incorporating epistemic reflection. This study highlights the role of the multi-layered model in scaffolding young learners to participate in authentic scientific practices, promoting collective epistemic agency and sustained knowledge advancement.

## Introduction

Science education has long emphasizes engaging students in authentic practices that mirror the  dynamic, collaborative, creative, and iterative nature of real-world science. Yet, classroom implementations often fail to capture this complexity, as students are frequently positioned as passive task f ollowers, constrained by pre-designed activities  (Krajcik & Merritt, 2012)  that limit opportunities for dynamic exploration, s uch as posing questions, constructing explanations, and  gathering evidence  (Osborne, 2014). This passivity often results in scientific knowledge being presented as static and finalized, neglecting its iterative and progressive  nature and preventing students from engaging in cycles of reflection and knowledge advancement (Chinn & Sandoval, 2018; Greene et al., 2016; Kuhn, 2012). Furthermore, the inherently social and collaborat ive dimensions of inquiry are underdeveloped, leaving students disconnected from the collective processes that drive scientific prog ress (Bereiter, 2002; Miller et al., 2018; Stroupe et al., 2018; Zivic et al., 2018). Opportunities for creativity and innovation—the hallmarks of real-world science—are rarely emphasized, limiting students’ ability t o build on extend prior work and develop further investigations. These limitations necessitate a shift toward frameworks that emphasize the collaborative, iterative, and socially constructed processes underlying real-world science.

To address these challenges, the knowledge-building approach (Bereiter & Scardamalia, 1993) provides a transformative framework for integrating authenti c scientific practices into classrooms. This approa ch introduces knowledge-building communities, where students collectively advance knowledge thr ough sustained idea improvement (Zhang et al., 2007; Muukkonen et al., 2004). Grounded in the core idea of collective epistemic agency, these communities treat knowledge as dynami c and improvable rather than static  (Bielaczyc & Collins, 1999; Khishfe & Abd‐El‐Khalick, 2002). The o nline platform Knowledge Forum enable s students to post questions, propose explanations, and refine ideas o ver time, fostering collective inquiry. Science creates new knowledge, and knowledge building is based on the premise of scientific communities, where collective efforts and sustained inquiry drive progress (Bereiter et al., 1997; Chuy et al., 2010). Within knowledge-building research, several promising models have em erged to support authentic scientific practices, emphasizing the collaborative  and iterative nature of inquiry that mirrors real-world science. Tao and Zhang (2018)  demonstrated how students could codesign collective  research cycles by engaging in activities such as asking questions, generating theories, and revising ideas, highlighting the importance of enabling students to actively explore phenomena and engage i n i nquiry-driven processes. Zhang et al. (2009)  further underscored the importance of dynamic social configurations, such as opportunistic groups, where students form and disband groups based on emergent goals, facilitating flexible and adaptive collaboration. Beyond these practices, epistemic reflection has been shown to d eepen students’ understanding of the nature of scie nce by encouraging them to critically examine how knowledg e is p roduced (Sandoval & Morrison, 2003). Taken together, these studies underscore the potential of integrating inquiry-driven exploration, flexible collaboration, iterative creation, and epistemic reflection into classroom practices. Building on this foundation, this study situates scientific practices within the framework of a knowledgebuilding community, where students actively constru ct, critique, and advance ideas together. Drawing on knowledge-building theory, which emphasizes collective episte mic agency and progressive knowledge improvement (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2014), this study introduces a multi-layered model that organizes scientific practices into four interconnected layers: exploration, collaboration, creation, and reflection. Each layer represents a critical dimension of scientific practice, collectively supporting the development of bot h individual and collective knowledge advancement.  As shown in Figure 1, t he proposed model begins with exploration, engaging in dynamic inquiry within small groups to pose questions, gather evidence, and draw conclusions. This progresses to collaboration, where students participate in community-wide synthesis of findings and collective reflection. In the creation layer, students extend prior work through iterative studie s. Reflection, embedded throughout all layers, ensures continuous refinement of understanding and practices. By integrating these layers, this model aims to reframe classrooms into spaces of authentic scientific practices, fostering collective knowledge advancement.  The research questions are: (1) What were the effects of the designed multi-layered scientific practices on student contribution to collective knowledge advancement, and how did student groups differ in their contribution s to collective  knowledge advancement? (2) What were the trajectories of students’ collective knowledge adva ncement for the different contributing groups? ## Participants

The participants were 48 fifth-grade students from a primary school in Shenzhen, mainland China. They were divided into eight groups of six students each. The  teacher and researchers collaborated as co-investigators throughout the study.

Design of multi-layered scientific practices in a knowledge-building community Working on Knowledge Forum over five ses sions (Figure 2), the eight student groups explored Lighting a Small Bulb across three phases, engaging in multi-layered model of scientific practices that mirrored the research process of scientists.  Through Knowledge Forum activities, students enrich ed their inquiry by posting questions, generating theories, co-constructing explanations, revising ideas, and collaborating with other groups. Phase 1: Scientific group exploration Focused on the topic of Lighting a Small Bulb, students utilized scaffolds on the Knowledge Foru m (e.g., I need to understand, my theory, and my evidence) to pose research questions and construct explanat ions. Working collaboratively within their groups, they designed and conducted experiments to test and refine their ideas, adhering closely to the scientific inquiry process:  posing questions → constructing explanations → gathering evidence → drawing conclusions.

Phase 2: Scientific communication for collaboration Building on the first phase, students worked with t he teacher to categorize research questions within the field of Lighting a Small Bulb. Through the Knowledge Forum view Scientific Communication: Summary and Reflection, they utilized scaffolds (e.g., improving existing ideas and putting our ideas together) to organize and refine their findings. This process progressed from summarizing others’ research findings to reflecting on our own research process, fostering the integration of diverse ideas, critical evaluation of methods, and collaborative refinement of collective knowledge.

Phase 3: Creating new knowledge in scientific field Students engaged with the Knowledge Forum view New Questions  New Research, using scaffolds such as generating new questions based on existing ideas to identify promising research directions. They the n revisited the scientific group exploration process, undertaking a new cycle of posing questions → constructing explanations → gathering evidence → drawing conclusions (Phase 1). This iterative process enabled them to b uild on prior research, conduct more advanced studies, and make progress in the scientific field of Lighting a Small Bulb. Notably, students reflected on their learning proce ss through the Knowledge Forum view How do we conduct scientific research?  They utilized Knowledge Forum scaffolds such as Scientific Group-Gathering Evidence, Scientific Communication-Reflecting on the Research Process, and Scientific Field-Promising New Research. These reflections were seamlessly embedded within  the three phases of the multi-layered scientific practices, as illustrated in Figure 2.

## Conclusion

This study contributes to the knowledge-building literature by demonstrating how a designed multi-layered model of scientific practice supports young children in w orking like scientists within a collaborative commu nity. The findings highlight the positive impact of the desig ned environment in fostering collective knowledge advancement. Analyses of different contributing groups, using pivotal notes, offer novel approaches to examining collective progress and reveal diverse trajectories  in how students engage with problems to advance knowledge. Additionally, the study sheds light on the dynamics, processes, and pathways through which young child ren deepen their inquiry and collaboratively build knowledge within the multi-layered model.
