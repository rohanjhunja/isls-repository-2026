# Coding Identity: Exploring Creative Self-Expression in 5th-Grade Computer Science Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores how 5th-grade learners express their identities through creative computing projects using, a platform for coding music with Python. Through case studies, the research highlights the integration of personal interests, societal themes, and advocacy in learners’ coding work. The case studies demonstrate how humanizing the learning environment by centering creative self-expression fosters personally meaningful learner engagement, amplifies learner voice, and supports equity in CS education.

## Introduction

Computer Science (CS) education in the U.S. has experienced rapid growth, supported by substantial funding and legislative efforts. However, the dominant justifications for this expansion often rest on ideologies that emphasize workforce readiness and national security (e.g., NSF, 2022) over broader educational goals such as critical thinking, creativity, and social responsibility. In contrast, critical approaches to CS education emphasize social justice, learner empowerment, and the integration of personal and sociopolitical contexts (e.g. Morales-Navarro & Kafai, 2022; Philip & Sengupta, 2021; Vakil, 2014; Vakil, 2018). An under-explored aspect of critical approaches is the welcoming of student identity through the amplification of learner voice and creative selfexpression. Our study was conducted in 5th-grade classrooms across a diverse school district in the Midwestern United States, using qualitative methods to analyze learner semi-structured interviews and Python coding artifacts. All 5th-grade students in the district received our learning unit for 40-minutes per week across 11 weeks in 2024. Cases presented here are from interviews and artifact analysis of student work.

## Case study 1: Soccer

This case study explores how a young learner’s identity emerges through their creative self-expression project: a soccer game soundscape. Their choices in composition, narrative design, and technical implementation reveal layers of identity, including personal passions, emotional values, and reflective thought processes. The learner’s focus on soccer reflects a personal hobby and facilitation. Explaining their theme choice, they state: “I mean, I always watch soccer [and] I play soccer also, so it’s a fun topic.” This strong connection to soccer ties their identity to experiences as both a participant and observer. Their familiarity with the sport informs an authentic soundscape, featuring applause and referee whistles to evoke a live match atmosphere: “I just wanted the applause to happen so it’s like there’s a lot of crowd, and then the whistle, like in the game, the referee blows the whistle.” Their creative choices show an alignment between their coding efforts and an important aspect of their identity.

The learner introduces a surprising narrative twist: a player injury requiring an ambulance. This adds drama to the soundscape: “I just wanted to make a twist, because in a soccer game, always something happens that makes the audience feel a surprise feeling.” The twist reflects mixed feelings about soccer, particularly its physical risks: “I like soccer and all, but I don’t like when there’s injuries. […] I just wanted [my listeners] to feel like […] if you’re playing a game, don’t get that much [into it] that you got to injure a player.” The learner employs their empathy to critique the aggression and violence that is sometimes brought to the game and advocates for a friendlier version. The injury sequence combines personal values with creative storytelling, communicating their views on the emotional stakes of problematic aggression in competition.

## Case study 2: Taylor Swift

This case focuses on how a learner’s identity is expressed in a project centered on Taylor Swift’s music and life journey. The project uses recreated snippets of Swift’s songs, as well as sound effects and narration, to express the learner’s own reflective thought process and personal interests. The learner’s decision to center their project on Taylor Swift stems from a personal connection to and admiration for her music, which they describe as empowering and meaningful. They explain: “Her songs are empowering and they’re cool […] Some of them are more about her life [and some are] about world problems.” The learner’s enthusiasm for the personal and social issues Swift takes on in her music reveals how their identity as a fan and as a critical thinker shapes the topic and direction of their project. They choose to use their project, like Swift’s songs, as a vehicle for exploring broader social themes. They take inspiration from Swift’s personal and musical evolution, with which the learner is deeply engaged, when deciding the structure of their project. “She’s had eras […] her first three albums were country, and then it got more into pop and rock […] then soul and folk.” This approach demonstrates their reflective ability to connect Swift’s artistic journey with the larger narrative of personal growth, reinforcing their identity as a thoughtful and detail-oriented creator.

The learner incorporates a reflective tone in their project, aiming to challenge stereotypes and misconceptions about Taylor Swift. They share: “A lot of people think that Taylor Swift’s like, overrated and plastic. So […] getting through the message of her life story […] would make people have a different thought about her.” This desire to reshape perceptions highlights the learner’s empathetic nature and their commitment to addressing bias through creative expression. By humanizing Swift’s story, the learner extends their project’s focus beyond fandom, showcasing an awareness of societal attitudes and an aspiration to provoke thought in their audience.

## Case study 3: Deforestation

This case study examines how a young learner’s identity emerges through a TunePad project on deforestation, using personal values and emotional narratives as a platform for advocacy and self-expression. The learner’s choice of deforestation reflects their identity as an environmentalist:“I’m an environmentalist. I care a lot about the environment. […] The more we cut down trees […] we won’t have any air to breathe.” This declaration establishes their identity and underscores their sense of responsibility toward the planet. The learner emphasizes deforestation’s far-reaching impacts, from oxygen loss to habitat destruction, signaling an awareness of interconnected ecosystems.

Their inspiration also stems from admiration for figures like Greta Thunberg: “I also learned about Greta Thunberg […] I like her a lot, and she really seems powerful, and I want to be that powerful and I want to help people and the environment.” This statement reveals a desire to emulate role models who align with their values, suggesting an aspirational aspect of their identity tied to environmental advocacy. The learner uses their project to tell a story about deforestation’s progression and effects, using music to convey emotional impact: “What’s happening in my project right now is basically how people are living when there’s lots of trees around them. […] Farther down [in the project], the more we cut down trees, the slower the music goes because we're having a harder time breathing.” This narrative structure reflects their ability to use music as a medium for both storytelling and emotional impact. The transition from vibrant, energetic sounds to slower, somber tones symbolizes the degradation of the environment, mirroring their concerns about the longterm effects of deforestation. Planned elements like buzzsaws, falling trees, and labored breathing deepen the immersive experience and amplify their message: “I think I’m going to add people talking […], a buzzsaw and the sound of a tree falling, and […] people having a hard time breathing.” This creative approach highlights their ability to combine technical and artistic elements to amplify their message. The learner’s project reflects not only their environmental values but also their emotional connection to the issue. Their description of the project as a means to spread awareness demonstrates a deep sense of purpose: “It spreads more awareness of how deforestation affects us, and not just animals. […] It’s bad for the entire Earth.” This focus on raising awareness reveals their identity as an advocate who seeks to educate others and inspire change. They express an understanding of the cascading effects of deforestation, framing the issue as a global concern that demands collective action.

Their reflections as an environmentalist and coder highlight an aspirational aspect of their identity. They envision their project raising awareness while expressing interest in activism: “I would like to go to protests too, and maybe post stuff on social media to spread more awareness.” This blend of coding and direct action underscores their belief in multiple modalities of environmental advocacy.

## Discussion

Across the case studies, learner identity emerged in diverse and interconnected ways through creative, selfexpressive coding projects. These projects provided a platform for learners to explore their interests, express values, and engage with personal and societal themes. Key themes included personal passions, emotional expressions, advocacy, cultural connections, and aspirations. This study seeks to demonstrate how a critical, constructionist, and creative self-expressive approach in CS can create inclusive, equitable learning environments. By emphasizing learner voice, personal expression, and the social relevance of computing, CS education can move beyond a narrow focus on workforce preparation to foster a holistic, humanizing learning experience. Findings will contribute to understanding how such an approach can make CS education more inclusive and empowering for all learners.
