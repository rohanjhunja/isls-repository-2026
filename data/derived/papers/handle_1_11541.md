# A study Design to Identify Group-Level Regulation of Learning and its Role in Collaborative Construction of TPACK

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study design explores the role of group-level regulation of learning in pre-service teachers’ co-construction of Technological, Pedagogical, and Content Knowledge (TPACK). Teacher education students participate in a university course on technology-enhanced learning and working. Self-report data, learning products and video recorded data are collected to reveal how TPACK develops through social interaction and highlight the importance of regulation in the process. Practical implications for teacher education that support effective training will be discussed.

## Background

Research on developing teachers’ pedagogical digita l competence (PDC) has been a growing area of focus, especially in the light of rapidly growing technological presence in education. PDC can be conceptualized as a combination of teachers’  individual cognitive and affective dispositions and traits (e.g., regulation skills, motivation and self-efficacy beliefs), technological, pedagogical, and content knowledg e (TPACK), and their practical skills all of which are adjusted to and shaped by a specif ic situational context (Blömeke et a., 2015; Skantz- Åberg et al., 2022). Regulation skills, including self-regulated learning (SRL) on the individual level as well as co-and socially shared regulation (CoRL and SSRL) in g roup contexts, lie at the core of competence development since they enable an individual to identify what knowledge and skills, they or their group members might lack and apply various strategies to develop in those directions (Järvenoja et al., 2015). In group contexts, CoRL and SSRL  serve as the underlying mechanism for successful collaborati ve interactions, guiding group members towards managing joint cognitive processes such as planning and task execution, as well as sustaining a positive socioemotional and motivational atmosphe re (Järvenoja et al., 2015). Prior research highlights the importance of regulation of learning  for the other elements of PDC such as TPACK, as it enab les teachers to monitor and evaluate their TPACK development (Huang et al., 2020). TPACK has been mostly regarded as highly individualized knowl edge that individual teachers possess (Di Blas et al., 2014). Therefore, most research investigating the role of SRL for teachers’ TPACK development has been focusing on individual level (e.g., Poitras et al., 2018). However, recent studies started acknowledging its social nature and viewing it as jointly co-constructed by teachers in professional communities (Yeh et al., 2021; Carpenter et al., 2020). With the rise of collaborative learning (CL) as a pedagogical approach, group work in preservice teacher education has been found especially  useful to facilitate deeper knowledge acquisition among learners and to promote adoption of educational innovation and implementation of digital technologies in teaching practices (Bell et al., 2013; Nipyrakis et al., 2023). Nevertheless, there is limited research on TPACK development in CL contexts in teacher education that would also consider the importance of group-level regulation (CoRL and SSRL) in it by utilizing multimodal process data. Thus, our study design addresses this gap by aiming to understand how TPACK develops through social interaction and how group-level regulation of learning supports it in teacher education students in the university course on technology-enhanced learning and working containing collaborative and situated learning activities.

## Methods

Course design The participants of the study are teacher education students (total n=113; 82% female, 16% male, 2% non-binary; mean age=22) enrolled in a 4ECTS university course “Technolo gy-enhanced learning and working”. The aim of the course is to prepare teacher education students  to work in digitalized environments, understand th e basic concepts and phenomena related to digital competence, media literacy and programming competence, and to equip them with knowledge and skills to notice, interpret  and make informed decisions related to technology implementation in classroom.  The training methods include knowledge instruction provided in 10 hours of lectures, 20 hours of hands-on seminars, and 70 hours of independent individual and collaborative group work on course tasks. Students are required to keep a digit al portfolio where they are asked to reflect on the ir knowledge and skills development in the course and create in small groups (4-5 students) a lesson plan for a digital workshop they have to implement at the end of the course in a primary school.

## Data collection and analysis

The data analysis was based on the complementarity mixed-method approach in this study, meaning that quantitative and qualitative and observational methods were integrated to uncover different facets of the learning processes to ensure a reliable result and an elaborated, multidimensional understanding of the phenomena under investigation (Teddlie & Tashakkori, 2009; Tondeur et al., 2019). The data collection include d several different measures and data channels targeting different components of PDC (see Figure 1). Self-report data was collected before and after the course. The pre-tests collected information about students’  background, self-efficacy for digital teaching (Van Acker et al., 2013) and for S RL promotion in classroom (De Smul et al., 2018), o wn SRL skills (Karlen et al., 2023), motivational beliefs (Viljaranta & Tuominen, 2018), digital competence (Lucas et al.,

2019) and situational TPK test (Lachner et al., 2019). The post-tests, in addition to self-efficacy, digital

competence and TPACK, focus on training satisfactio n (Ritzmann et al., 2014) and quality  (Richter & Richter,

2024) as well as  intended adoption of technology-enhanced methods (Ley et al., 2022). Statistical analyses

methods were applied to understand the effect of th e course on students’ dispositions, knowledge, and attitudes and the moderation effect of one’s own SRL skills and motivational beliefs on these PDC components. Additionally, observational and qualitative data including video and audio recordings of s tudents’ collaboration on the lesson plan  as well as lesson plans and digital portfolios  were collected to trace how their knowledge and skills develop through social interac tion, identify the critical moments when group-level regulation of learning was activated, and investigate how the quality of these processes mani fested in the final learning outcomes. These data sources allowed us to identify meaningful moments of learning through qualitative video coding, speech recognition methods used for speech analysis, and content analysis of the learning products. ## Expected results and implications

The results of the study will reveal how teacher ed ucation students develop their TPACK and practical teaching skills through social interaction during collaborative group work and the mediating role of group-level regulation of learning in it. The findings will provide practical implications for the improve ment of teacher training programs. Moreover, it will lead to a better understanding of the effectiveness of different training approaches for different elements of PDC  and contribute to policy development, answering a r ecent call from the European Commission (2022) for more evidence-based research of effectiveness and efficiency or teacher training programs targeting PDC.
