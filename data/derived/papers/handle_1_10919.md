# Fostering Lesson Plan Design Competence via Knowledge Building in Teacher Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study compares eight SDGs lesson plans using the framework of the 12 principles of knowledge building, aiming to assess the quality of lesson plandesign competence among teacher trainees. The findings show that cultivation through the concept of knowledge building assists teacher trainees in enhancing their lesson plandesign competence. However, some differences remain between the instructional approaches of teacher trainees and those of award-winning teachers regarding ideas, agents, and community.

## Introduction

In 2015, the United Nations introduced 17 Sustainab le Development Goals (SDGs) (United Nations [UN], n.d.). These goals guide many governments in policy formul ation and play a crucial role in school education. In response, integrating SDG indicators into the curriculum has emerged as a growing trend in education, presenting teachers with the challenge of crafting lesson plan s rooted in the SDGs. The 108 Curriculum, launched by the Taiwan Ministry of Education in 2019, aims to culti vate students' three core competencies: autonomy, communication, and social participation (Ministry of Education [MOE], 2023). These competencies align closely with the 12 principles of knowledge building (KB) o utlined by Scardamalia (2002), which can also be classified into three categories: agent (emphasizing autonomous learning), idea (highlighting communication and interaction among different individuals), and community (emphas izing the importance of a collective vision). These  three competencies hold considerable significance in the current educational context in Taiwan. However, onl y some cases still exist where lesson plans are designed primarily around knowledge building. Therefore, this study tried to sample a few lesson plans with SDGs as the theme for comparison, aiming to scrutinize the disparities in lesson design between award-winning (non-KB) teachers and KB teacher trainees. The objective  was to ascertain whether designing through KB guidance could effectively augment teacher trainees’ design proficiencies. Hence, the research questions are: (1) What is the quality of lesson plans designed by teacher trainees after they grasp the principles of knowledge building? (2) What are the differences between lesson designs of award-winning teachers and teacher trainees?

## Context and method

This study examines eight lesson plans: four from t he “SDGs X Sustainable Living for All” website in 2 022, a renowned platform for SDGs lesson plans competition in Taiwan, and the remaining four were created by teacher trainees who participated in the “Instructional Med ia and Application” course, where the teacher intro duces the concept of knowledge building (KB), ensuring that students candesign lesson plans based on these KB principles. After scoring all eight lesson plans based on Scard amalia's (2002) 12 principles of knowledge building, a T-test was conducted using the 21st edition of SPSS to examine differences between the two types of lesson plans across three perspectives (i.e., the three core competenci es mentioned above). Content analysis was then employed to identify specific performance differences between t he two types of lesson plans designed. ## Result

The statistical findings indicate no significant di fferences in the two types of lesson plans across t he three perspectives (see Table 1). Both lesson plans have the highest scores in the "community" perspective, which indicates that cultivating the abilit y of shared vision among students is highly valued.  However, according to content analysis, differences  between the two type s of lesson plans can be observed. Taking the award-winning lesson plan with the code AW01 as an example, it co vers two indicators, SDGs 10 and 12, with environmentally friendly food delivery method as the main theme. In  this lesson plan, students are divided into differ ent heterogeneous groups, and specific seating arrangem ents for each group are clearly arranged. Teachers require students to engage in discussions using the ORID meth od after watching videos or images to enhance stude nt interaction. Additionally, teachers conduct teacher or peer assessments to evaluate students' performance. Finally, students are responsible for distributing survey questionnaires and engaging in discussions on sustainable delivery methods. In contrast, lesson plan KB03 focuses sole ly on SDG 12, with the issue of turtles with straws  stuck in their noses as the core concept. Students are cast as time-traveling explorers and asked to determine how to t ake environmental actions based on different time-space scenarios to prevent turtles from being harmed again. In this lesson plan, students utilize Facebook groups for discussions, inviting them to respond to and brainstorm on topics of interest, fostering opportunistic  group discussions. For the assessment, they are ask ed to conduct self-assessments using online questionnaires, which allo ws them to reflect on their progress immediately (s ee Figure 2).

## Conclusion and discussion

Lesson planning is an essential skill for teachers.  By understanding the theory of knowledge building,  teacher trainees can cultivate their teaching knowledge and  improve the quality of instruction (Chang, 2011). This study emphasizes the significance of knowledge building i n shaping the lesson planning abilities of teacher trainees. Statistical results indicate no significant differe nces in design competence between novice and  award-winning teachers. The result  suggests that, with a deeper grasp of the principles of knowledge building, teac her trainees could enhance the quality of their lesson plans, even without extensive real-world teaching experience compared to award-winning teachers. The content comparison reveals that aw ard-winning teachers tend to use fixed-group discussion methods and dominate most assessments. I n contrast, teacher trainees encourage student inte ractions by employing opportunistic groupings and self-assessment. Furthermore, influenced by the class environment and the time pressure of delivering instruction, the design of both types of lesson plans still primarily revolves around school or home settings, with fewer opportunities for students to  indeed apply their knowledge to the real-world context. Therefore, investigating how to design a l esson plan that encourages students to apply their knowledge to the world surrounding them is worth exploring in the future.
