# Leveraging Online Videos for Elementary Science (LOVE Science): An AI Tool for Identifying Developmentally Appropriate, NGSS-Aligned Science Videos

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Multimodal presentation of science content can support science learning; however, elementary teachers lack the time and support to find age-appropriate, freely accessible, and Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS)-aligned science videos. This interactive demo will showcase our Artificial intelligence (AI)-powered Video Analyzer, a feature of the Leveraging Online Videos for Elementary Science (LOVE Science) platform, which analyzes YouTube videos to determine alignment to NGSS performance expectations, conceptual complexity, vocabulary level, and relevance across NGSS domains. The LOVE Science Video Analyzer helps teachers make more effective use of the open educational resources already available to them—particularly on YouTube—by providing an AI-powered plug-in that streamlines the process of finding, reviewing, and selecting high-quality science videos to enhance instruction.

Scores from the most recent National Assessment of Educational Progress indicate that less than 30% of U.S. 8th graders are proficient in science and that students show post-pandemic losses in science achievement and interest (Jacobson, 2025). Supporting learners through science instruction in the early elementary grades can set them up for later success in science (Curran & Kitchin, 2019; Kaderavek et al., 2020; Morgan et al., 2016). However, at this grade level, teachers generally spend less time teaching science compared to other subjects (Bassok et al.,

2016) and often lack access to high-quality instructional materials for teaching science, relying instead on fee-based websites or creating lessons themselves (Plumley, 2019). Elementary teachers need support finding engaging instructional resources suitable for young learners who are still developing language and literacy skills (Kaderavek et al., 2020).

The multimodal nature of video—combining images, narration, and sound effects—supports understanding of science concepts by presenting information through multiple channels, consistent with Mayer’s

(2009) Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning. Young learners can engage in scientific meaning-making when

using multimodal sources (Tang et al., 2014), especially when their reading ability is limited. Videos allow children to observe phenomena directly—such as the movement of the sun, the survival features of animals, and cause-and-effect processes—without needing advanced reading skills to access the content. Videos can be used in science instruction to introduce new concepts in engaging, accessible ways for young learners, or as part of teachers’ formative assessment practices. Video contents can be aligned with NGSS performance expectations (e.g., modeling how plant and animal parts solve human problems) and scaffolded with teacher prompts or formative assessment questions to help teachers surface student thinking and misconceptions while keeping assessments accessible to linguistically and developmentally diverse learners. Text-heavy assessment formats can inadvertently mask students’ conceptual understanding, leading teachers to underestimate their actual grasp of scientific concepts (Billman et al., 2021). Using videos as part of elementary science assessments can be especially helpful for students who are still developing language and literacy, such as English language learners, because the visual and auditory supports reduce barriers that text-heavy assessments often create (Schnotz & Lowe, 2003; Zimmerle et al., 2024). When assessments reveal gaps in student understanding, videos can also be used to reinforce learning and clarify conceptual challenges. Integrating high-quality science videos into assessment and instruction has the potential to enhance student learning, increase engagement, and spark early interest in STEM. LOVE Science: Using AI to help elementary teachers find appropriate, highquality science videos. To harness the benefits of integrating videos into science instruction, teachers need access to high-quality video content. Elementary teachers face a persistent challenge: finding and vetting developmentally appropriate, freely a ccessible, and NGSS-aligned science videos can be daunting and time-intensive. We have developed a teacherfacing Artificial Intelligence (AI)-powered YouTube Video Curator and Video Analyzer called Leveraging Online Videos for Elementary Science (LOVE Science) to support elementary teachers in finding multimedia content aligned to science standards. LOVE Science helps teachers find high-quality, NGSS-aligned science videos for instruction and formative assessment. Teachers can enter search terms into the Video Curator to find online science videos. The URLs from the Video Curator can then be entered into the Video Analyzer, which provides a detailed summary of the video that teachers can use to determine the appropriateness of specific video content, evaluations of conceptual complexity, vocabulary level, and a list of prerequisite content (see Figure 1). LOVE Science enables teachers to search for “states of matter, grade 2” or “animal adaptations” and instantly receive vetted, instruction-ready video options, so they do not have to spend valuable preparation time watching several videos to decide what to show students.

In LOVE Science, APIs serve as the connective tissue that brings together Gemini’s large language models (LLMs), YouTube’s vast educational video library, and an intuitive user interface built on Gradio (Figure 1). By integrating these existing technologies through thoughtfully designed API calls, the system can automatically search, filter, and summarize videos using Gemini’s LLMs to analyze video transcripts, visuals, and descriptions to determine alignment with NGSS standards, while YouTube’s API provides access to millions of open educational videos (see Figure 2). Because the LOVE Science platform relies on multiple API connections between Gemini’s LLMs, YouTube, and Gradio, strict and careful prompt engineering is essential to ensure that the system extracts accurate and relevant information for teachers and students. Each API call must be precisely structured to retrieve science-based, age-appropriate, and NGSS-aligned content while filtering out unrelated or low-quality videos. To maintain this level of precision and reliability, we implement verification agents at key points in the data flow; serving as quality checkpoints that review, validate, and refine the AI’s outputs before presenting them to users (Figure 2). The Gradio interface consolidates the process into one simple, web-based environment. Teachers can input a few keywords and receive standards-aligned results without needing technical expertise in AI or programming. This layered design minimizes errors, strengthens trustworthiness, and ensures that every recommended video meets high educational and technical standards. The model achieved a 92.7% match with an expert annotator for a set of 247 videos across the three NGSS content domains (Earth and Space Science, Life Science, Physical Science). Grade-level misclassifications accounted for most of the disagreements, and there were very few content relevance errors. Using APIs to connect existing resources, allows LOVE Science to innovate without reinventing the wheel. LOVE Science can support teachers without requiring them to learn multiple platforms or tools, allowing them to focus their energy where it matters most—helping students engage deeply with science. We are currently working on improving the technical feasibility of LOVE Science and will be piloting with teachers in authentic settings soon.

Session description: Activities and timing We plan to start the session with a brief presentation and demonstration of the LOVE Science Video Analyzer in the first 10 minutes (in which we will require access to a projector). The presentation will introduce the context for the technology and demonstrate how it can be used to support teachers in vetting YouTube videos. Then we will allow 10-20 minutes for session attendees to interact with the engineering design interface and respond to a few questions raised during our initial presentation. Attendees can interact with the LOVE Science Video Analyzer using their own laptops (either in person or virtually) but will need internet access. We will provide two additional laptops that attendees can use. Attendees will be given example assessment tasks and scenarios that elementary teachers use and experience when integrating videos into instruction or assessment, which will guide the exploration of the Video Analyzer. The goal of this demo is to showcase the LOVE Science Video Analyzer and explore how the tool supports efficient lesson planning, fosters student engagement, and opens opportunities for research on AI-mediated video learning in early science education. The session will invite feedback on how AI-driven tools like this can enhance teacher efficiency, increase student engagement with science concepts, and open new possibilities for adaptive video-based learning environments. The discussion will also explore collaborative opportunities to extend the system’s capabilities – for example, linking it to formative assessment platforms, studying its effects on teacher decision-making, or adapting it for other grade bands and interdisciplinary contexts.

C ontributions of this technology LOVE Science fills a critical need in supporting early science instruction. It can provide teachers with access to vetted resources which can support integration of video content into instruction to support early science learning. The LOVE Science Video Analyzer can analyze video URLs from YouTube, a resource that is commonly used by teachers, which reduces the need for teachers to learn about another new technology. Future iterations of LOVE Science will function as a plug-in for already existing educational platforms and resources. For students, LOVE Science offers engaging, developmentally appropriate science content that reinforces understanding and builds early enthusiasm for science. This work also has the potential to contribute to research on the utility and effectiveness of video-based instruction in science learning. Future work would need to examine how LOVE Science-mediated video selection shapes classroom enactment, student engagement, and science learning outcomes. Participants in this session will have the opportunity to experience how AI can be leveraged to support elementary science teachers in providing accessible, engaging science instruction and assessment aligned to content standards.
