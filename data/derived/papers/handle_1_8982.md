# Designing to Enhance Academic Buoyancy: A Snapshot of First Steps in a Second Level School Setting

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Academic buoyancy generally refers to students' ability to respond to everyday challenges in academic settings. This paper presents insights in to a design-based research pilot intervention that is informed by a multi-ontological framework that included conceptual pillars of: engagement, collaboration and reflection. Preliminary findings are presented, the emergence of co-creation as a core element of the success of the pilot is explored, and next steps discussed.

## Introduction and Conceptual Background

Clearly identified by UNESCO as Sustainable Develop ment Goal 4 (UNESCO, 2021) the provision of quality education opportunities is critical to the developm ent of our global citizenship. However, in order to  support a sustained engagement with education, student adaptability, particularly in the form of academic buoyancy, has a significant role in how individuals respond to the challenges they will experience (Martin & Marsh, 2009) both within and outside formal educational settings. Con sequently, there is a clearly established need to d evelop innovative pedagogically informed initiatives, programmes and interventions that aim to enhance the ac ademic buoyancy of second level students (Edwards et. al, 2007) as they move ever closer to post compulsory education and the world of work where the link between achievement and buoyancy (Martin et al. 2016) is even closer. The conceptual framework that underpins this work has three pillars. Firstly, collaborative activity is seen to provide students with opportunities to chal lenge themselves and to develop the ability to be d iscerning about ideas and content (Hase, 2015). Second, through such collaborative exchanges students are more likely to demonstrate enhanced engagement with both content and social interaction with their peers (Shneidermann, 1998) creating opportunities for positive learning outcom es (Trowler and Trowler, 2010). The third pillar, reflection, seeks to foster student’ capabilities for critical thinking and self-evaluation resulting in a more buoyant learner.

When students actively participate in directing th eir own learning, agentic engagement can occur (Lawson and Lawson, 2013). As an empowering process  this can can have a positive impact on participant engagement with a pedagogical intervention. A core aim of the intervention in this study is to produce  lifelong learners capable of working collaboratively, assess ing their own learning and monitoring their perform ance. In conjunction with collaboration, reflective practice provides students with opportunities to develop the capability for critical thinking and ongoing self-evaluation t hus improving the likelihood of enhancing their aca demic buoyancy (Martin, 2013). In order to promote awareness among the students, reflection emanated as a keystone of the intervention. Reflection seeks to foster stu dents’ capabilities for critical thinking and self-evaluation resulting in a buoyant learner.

This paper reports on the outcome of a Design-Base d Research (DBR) (DBRC, 2002; Barab & Squire

2004) pilot project and an emergent need to develop a pedagogical framework to support a curricular co-creation

process particular to the development of academic buoyancy with second level students.

## Methods

Employing a DBR methodology, this multi-cycle study  seeks to iteratively design and refine an innovati ve pedagogy that supports the enhancement of second le vel students’ academic buoyancy set in the real-wor k messiness of a second level school setting (Barab and Squire 2004). Such pedagogy will be a guide rather than a proscriptive learning process. Additionally, it will promote higher order thinking skills and the deve lopment of self-regulated learners (Shvarts and Bakker 2019). Given that this was a pilot study and the lead researcher is an in-service teacher, convenience sampling was employed for the recruitment of participants into this study. A data collection plan was designed that included the administration of a pre-and post-questionnaire, students' artefacts, practitioner observations and student reflective journals. A process of data triangulation informed and contributed to a thematic analysis.

The lead researcher implemented the pilot interven tion with a cohort of second level female students (N=51) with an average age of 15.5 years enrolled in yearlong an elective programme in an Irish secondary school called the Transition Year Programme. The module was delivered through six, single weekly classes of 40 minutes duration and included an introductory session invol ving co-creation of the main areas of concern for t he group ICLS2022 Proceedings 1848 © ISLS and in doing so, establishing learning outcomes for  the module that related to key characters of acade mic buoyancy: metacognition, reflection, goal setting and decision making (Martin & Marsh, 2009).

## Preliminary Findings

The conceptual pillars of engagement, collaboration and reflection are used here to frame the results of this pilot intervention. Firstly, we found, particularly through our thematic analysis of the student reflections, that students’ perceived usefulness of the module and individual c lasses influenced their level of engagement. It was  evident that this was linked to the role that students play ed in the co-creation of the module which ensured t hat the outcomes were reflective of the needs of the students. This was corroborated through the observations of the lead researcher who is an experienced teacher and noted a positive change in the classroom atmosphere, teacher-student relationship and inter-student relationships.

Secondly, throughout the six-week pilot interventi on students worked in groups of 3-5, depending on the physical layout and the number of students in the class group. Participating students developed a range of social soft-skills as an outcome of their engagement with collaborative aspects of the module despite social distancing restrictions imposed as a consequence of COVID-19.

Thirdly, triangulated data highlighted a relations hip between student agency in the co-creation of th e module, student engagement and collaboration facilitating a mechanism for students to meaningfully address and design for their own needs as part of an ongoing process of selective practice.

## Discussion

The preliminary findings of this pilot study indica te that the approach taken has the potential to enh ance the academic buoyancy of second level students. The initial conceptual framework afforded an opportunity to develop an informed, real-world, experiment and at the same time an opportunity to determine the proximal workings of the design. It has become clear to the research tea m that students played a pivotal role in the design  and development process of this module through their pa rticipation in a consultative, student-centred lear ning experience. This process of co-creation, initially not considered a component of the conceptual framework must notw be considered an essential pillar of its initial s uccess. Therefore, next steps include an explicit enabling of the collaborative social learning environment afforded by the co-creative approach embodied in this pilot study.
