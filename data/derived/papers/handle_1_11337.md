# Less Speed, More Thought: Message Pacing in Student-AI Interactions

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigates the impact of interaction pacing on student engagement and problem-solving when using an AI chatbot embedded in a programming code editor (Integrated Development Environment, IDE). By comparing two groups—one with unrestricted messaging and one limited to sending a message every 60 seconds—we observe that slowing down AI interactions leads to more thoughtful communication, deeper cognitive processing, and improved problem-solving skills. The goal is to determine whether deliberate pacing of student–AI interactions can enhance learning quality and foster deeper engagement with programming concepts. Findings support slow computing principles, suggesting that deliberate pacing in AI-assisted learning environments enhances educational interactions.

## Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools have significantly transformed educational practices, particularly in programming education where AI-powered chatbots offer real-time support and personalized feedback (Luxton-Reilly et al., 2018). These tools enhance learning by providing im mediate assistance, allowing students to resolve is sues quickly and progress in their understanding. Howeve r, this immediacy may also lead to cognitive overlo ad and superficial engagement with the material (Følstad & Brandtzæg, 2017). The convenience of instant AI interactions can encourage students to seek quick fixes rather t han engaging deeply with underlying concepts, poten tially hindering the development of essential problem-solving skills (Sharples et al., 2019; Selwyn, 2017). Specifically, there is limited empirical evidence on how the temp oral design of these interactions influences cognit ive engagement within programming contexts.

To address these concerns, the concept of slow computing has emerged, advocating for a more mindful and deliberate use of technology that emphasizes quality over speed (Lindley, 2015). Slow computing aligns with theories of self-regulated learning, which encourage learners to reflect on their understanding and take control of their learning processes (Zimmerman, 2002). By inte ntionally slowing down technology-mediated interactions, students may have more time to process information thoroughly, engage in metacognitive activities, and develop deeper comprehension of the material (Sweller, 1988). Prior research suggests imposing constraints on technological interactions can enhance cognitive en gagement and improve learning outcomes (Gourlay et al., 2015; Selwyn, 2017).

This study investigates how pacing AI chatbot inter actions within a programming integrated development environment (IDE) affects student engagement and the problem-solving process. We experimented with two groups of students: one allowed unlimited messaging with the chatbot, and another restricted to sending a message every 60 seconds. By analyzing their comm unication patterns and problem-solving approaches, we aim to understand the impact of deliberate pacing o n students' programming task engagement and so lution development processes. We hypothesize that slowing AI interactions encourages students to engage more thoughtfully with programming tasks, leading to more detailed queries and improved problem-solving skills. This research contributes to the discourse on slow compu ting in education and provides guidance for designi ng AI tools that strategically use pacing to promote deeper engagement in learning environments.

## Theoretical background

Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies have incr easingly supported learning across various domains,  notably in programming education where AI-powered tools such as intelligent tutoring systems and chatbots provide personalized feedback and guidance (Kumar et al., 2 023; Luxton-Reilly et al., 2018). These tools adapt to individual learner needs, offering scaffolding that enhances understanding and skill acquisition (VanLehn, 2011). However, the effectiveness of AI in education depen ds significantly on how students interact with thes e technologies and the pedagogical designs underlying them (Luckin et al., 2016). While immediate feedback from AI tools can be advan tageous, it may lead to over-reliance and superficial learning (Roll & Wylie, 2016). Students might depend on instant solutions without engaging in deeper cognitive processes necessary for problem-solving and knowledge construction, impeding the developmen t of critical thinking skills (Bae & Bozkurt, 2024; Baar s et al., 2017). Rapid interactions with AI tools c an inadvertently increase extraneous cognitive load by overwhelming students with immediate feedback, potentially decreasing comprehension (Sweller, 1988;  Paas et al., 2003). Managing the pace of educationa l interactions is thus crucial to prevent cognitive overload and faci litate meaningful learning experiences (Chandler & Sweller, 1991).

The concept of slow computing advocates for mindful  and reflective engagement with technology, emphasizing quality over speed in interactions (Lin dley, 2015; Hallnäs & Redström, 2001). In education al settings, deliberately slowing down technology-mediated interactions can encourage deeper cognitive engagement and reflection, allowing learners to mak e sense of information thoroughly and develop a str onger understanding of the material (Wear et al., 201 5; Sørensen & Pica, 2005). This approach aligns with  seve ral established learning science principles: the concept of desirable difficulties, where introducing challenges during learning can enhance understanding (Bjork & Bjork, 2011), and productive failure, where allowing stude nts to initially struggle with problems before receiving support leads to deeper conceptual understanding and improved problem-solving skills (Kapur, 2008). Thoughtful pacing cre ates opportunities for students to develop critical thinking abilities through exploration and independent problem-solving attempts rather than relying on immediate solutions.

Studies have shown that imposing time constraints o r delays in assistance can encourage learners to attempt problem-solving independently before seeking help, enhancing critical thinking and self-efficacy (Gweon et al., 2016; Aleven & Koedinger, 2000). Despite th e recognition of the potential drawbacks of immedia te AI assistance, limited research has explored how the deliberate pacing of AI interactions affects student engagement and learning quality in programming education. Prev ious studies have primarily  focused on the benefits of AI tools without adequately considering the impact of interaction speed on cognitive processes and outcomes (Kumar et al., 2023). This study addresses this gap by exa mining how restricting the frequency of messages to  an AI chatbot influences students' communication styles, prob lem-solving approaches, and overall engagement with programming tasks.

## Methodology

Twenty students from an introductory computer scien ce course at a Hispanic-serving community college in the northeastern United States participated in this stu dy. All were freshmen majoring in computer science or related fields, having completed at least one prior programming course. Participants were randomly assigned to either the unrestricted messaging group (n = 10) or the restri cted messaging group (n = 10), ensuring equal chanc es of assignment and controlling for potential confounding variables such as prior programming experience.

The study employed a between-subjects experimental design to investigate the imp act of interaction pacing with an AI chatbot on student engagement and  problem-solving skills (Gravetter & Forzano, 2018). The experimental platform was a custom-built Python Integrated Development Environment (ID E) designed specifically for this research. The IDE featured a code editor with syntax highlighting, real-time error detection, and a console for code execution. Integrated within  the IDE was an AI chatbot interface powered by the  Claude3.5-Haiku language model developed by Anthropic. The chatbot allowed students to request assistance via typed messages but did not have direct access to the stud ents' code unless they chose to share snippets by c opying and pasting into the chat. Messages were limited to 160 characters to encourage concise queries. Participants were tasked with completing programmin g exercises focused on constructing if-else statements in Python and applying conditional logic to solve problems related to library book borrowing policies. Before beginning the tasks, all participants attend ed a briefing session explaining the study's purpos e and procedures. They received instructions on using the  custom IDE and AI chatbot interface. Each particip ant was informed only about their own messaging condition w ithout knowledge of the other grou p's existence or constraints. Those in the unrestricted group were t old they could send messages to the chatbot without  delay, while those in the restricted group were informed t hey would need to wait 60 seconds between messages,  with a countdown timer displayed after each message. Participants were advised to solve the problems independently as much as possible, using the chatbot as a supplement ary resource. Each group was given 20 minutes to co mplete the activity.

All interactions with the AI chatbot were automatically logged, capturing message content, timestamps, and anonymized participant IDs to ensure privacy and confidentiality in accordance with Institutional Review Board (IRB) guidelines. A content analysis was cond ucted on the messages to understand communication s tyles and problem-solving strategies (Merriam & Tisdell, 2015). A coding scheme, adapted from prior research (Sun et al., 2021), categorized messages into five categori es: Syntax Inquiries (e.g., "h ow to write if condition?"), Error Troubleshooting (e.g., "getting NameError for varia ble x"), Conceptual Questions (e.g., "explain diffe rence between == and ="), Debugging Strategies (e.g., "tr ied printing variable, still not working"), and Ref lection and Planning (e.g., "ok I think I need to check the typ e first"). Following educational data mining and le arning analytics approaches, the categorization was automa ted using a Python script that analyzed the presenc e of specific word sequences and phrases asso ciated with each category (Kovanović et al., 2015). The research team collaboratively developed and refined these categor y markers through multiple iterations, reviewing me ssage samples to ensure accurate classification. Statistical analyses examined the groups' differences in metrics such as message length (average number of words per message) and message frequency (total messages sent per participant). Independent samples t-tests compared group means on these metrics.

## Key takeaways and limitations

This study revealed that imposing a 60-second delay between AI chatbot messages significan tly influenced how students engaged with programming tasks. The restri cted messaging group demonstrated more thoughtful engagement, composing longer, detailed messages ind icative of independent problem-solving and conceptual understanding. Their communications showed a balanc ed distribution across message types, including tho se requiring deeper cognitive processing like conceptu al questions (26.4%), debugging strategies (15.1%),  and reflection/planning (9.4%)—categories absent in the unrestricted group, which focused more on syntax (15.4%) and error troubleshooting (22.5%).

These findings suggest deliberate pacing can enhanc e student engagement quality and promote self-regulated learning. However, limitations must be co nsidered: the small sample size (n=20) from one ins titution limits generalizability (though appropriate for an exploratory study); individual differences (e.g., typing speed, AI familiarity) were not controlled; and the study focused on specific Python concepts. Crucially, the  fixed 20minute task duration imposed an absolute ceiling of  20 possible messages for the restricted group, mak ing it difficult to definitively disentangle whether the lower message frequency stems solely from the enforced delay or is partially an artifact of this time limitation. F urthermore, this study measured interaction pattern s, not d irect learning outcomes or student perceptions of the AI's helpfulness, highlighting areas for future investigation. Future research questions and goals This study's findings suggest slow computing princi ples could enhance technology-mediated learning. Future research should explore how deliberate pacing inter sects with learning science frameworks, particularl y how enforced reflection supports metacognitio n and self-regulation. Investigating whether benefits extend b eyond programming to other domains where rapid feedback might similarly shortcut cognitive effort is crucial. Immediate directions include studies with larger, diverse populations to examine varied pacing strategies (fixed vs. adaptive intervals) across different exp erience levels and task complexities. Adaptive syst ems could tailor delays based on real-time stude nt struggle or progress indicators, offering a pers onalized approach to fostering reflection. Applying pacing in other domains (e.g., math, science) and educational levels (e.g., K-12) is needed. Incorporating qualitative methods, like interviews or surveys gauging student perceptions and confidence in AI, could offer deeper insights into their experiences and reasoning, addressing why engagement patterns differ. Another key design question is exploring who contro ls pacing (learner, AI, or instructor) and t he resulting implications for learner agency versus pedagogical scaffolding. Longitudinal studies assessing long-term effects on direct learning outcomes (e.g., conceptual understanding, problem-solving success) and transfer to independent problem-solving are vital. Understanding these dynamics is critical for educators and designers aiming to harness AI effectively. This research line informs the design of educational technologies, guiding the integration of pacing to balance immediacy with reflection, potentially mitigating cognitive overload while maximizing learning depth, and ultimately promoting deeper understanding over quick fixes.
