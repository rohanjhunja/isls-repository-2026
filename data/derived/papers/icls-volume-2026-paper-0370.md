# The Impact of Discourse in Knowledge Building Teacher Communities on Professional Agency in Interdisciplinary Instructional Design

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This stu dy examines how discourse within a Knowledge-Building (KB) teacher community shapes professional agency in interdisciplinary instructional design. Sixty-nine teachers participated in collaborative design tasks, and their discourse was categorized i nto sharing-oriented and knowledge-building-oriented groups. Mixed-method analyses revealed a shift from exploratory sharing to knowledge-building discourse, characterized by negotiation and i ntegration of teaching strategies. Teachers’ professional agency im proved across all dimensions, particularly in positive interdependence and collective efficacy. The findings underscore the critical role of knowledge-building discourse in fostering teacher agency and supporting interdisciplinary instructional innovation.

Interdisciplinary instructional design requires teachers to engage in collaborative discourse that integrates diverse perspectives and supports innovative teaching. In this context, teacher professional agency, conceptualized as a dynamic and contextually situated capacity (Soini et al., 2015; Tao & Gao, 2017), has become increasingly important. Although Professional Learning Communities (PLCs) are widely regarded as supportive environments for developing professional agency, research indicates that collaborative structures alone do not guarantee its effective enactment (Kauppinen et al., 2020; Mohammad Nezhad & Stolz, 2024). Moreover, empirical studies on teacher professional agency, particularly within PLC contexts, remain limited (Pyhältö et al., 2015; Yl i-Pietilä et al., 2024). Discourse, especially knowledge-building discourse, plays a crucial role in fostering reflective interaction and collaborative knowledge construction (Brugha & Ahmed, 2025). Grounded in Knowledge Building (KB) theory (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 1994), this study examines discourse processes and the development of teacher professional agency within KB teacher communities. The research questions are as follows:

1. How does discourse within teacher communities progress in KB environments?

2. How does professional agency within teacher communities develop in K B environments?

3. What types of discourse contribute to the development of teacher professional agency?

## Method

This study involved 69 in-service teachers enrolled in a Master’s program in Curriculum and Instruction in Taiwan.

Using a mixed-methods design, it examined how teachers’ discourse orientations influence professional agency within Knowledge Building (KB) t eacher communities. Over 18 weeks, participants engaged in a structured interdisciplinary instructional design process, including experience sharing, theoretical inquiry, collaborative design, and reflective reconstruction, supported by the Knowledge Forum  (KF) platform and guided by KB principles. Teachers’ online discourse was collected and analyzed. Discourse was categorized into sharing discourse, involving the expression of perspectives, questions, and clarifications, and knowledge-building discourse, focusing on identifying differences, using evidence, and collaboratively refining ideas. Each post and response were treated as a discourse unit (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.82), and participants were classified as knowledgebuilding–oriented or sharing-oriented using a 60% threshold. Teacher professional agency was measured using a seven-dimension, seven-point Likert scale (Pyhältö  et al., 2015) administered before and after the semester. Based on discourse orientation, participants were categorized into sharing and knowledge-building groups. Descriptive statistics, paired-sample t-tests, and one-way ANOVA were conducted to examine changes and group differences in professional agency, with effect sizes reported following Cohen (1988).
