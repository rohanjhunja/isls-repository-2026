# When One Tutor Handles Students from Different Educational Levels: Their Dialogic Interactions in Online One-on-one Tutoring

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
To investigate how a single tutor interacts with students from different educational levels in online one-on-one tutoring, this study analyzed 300 dialogues from these tutors and students using epistemic network analysis. Our findings reveal that when tutoring higher-grade-level students, tutors employed a higher frequency of constructive questions. Tutors’ knowledge-sharing and instruction strategies were related to advanced constructive expressions among students at higher educational levels, while primary school students primarily demonstrated simple acknowledgement without constructive engagement.

## Introduction

Online one-on-one tutoring has emerged as a supplementary approach to  traditional classroom teaching, transcending spatial and temporal boundaries  and enabling students to be matched with tutors tailore d to their specific needs. Given the scarcity of tutors,  a single tutor may be responsible for addressing qu estions from students across primary, middle, and high school le vels, requiring them to adopt diverse and adaptive tutoring strategies. To further improve tutoring effectiveness, this study compares whether tutors can effectively employ different dialogic strategies to instruct students from various educational levels in online one-on-one tutoring.

## Method

We collected data from an online one-on-one tutoring platform  and focused on mathematics tutors who simultaneously addressed queries from primary, midd le, and high school levels. We selected 100 tutoring dialogues from each educational level, resulting in  a total of 300 dialogues. Next, we annotated the messages within the tutoring dialogues using a coding scheme  specifically designed for online one-on-one tutoring (see Table 1) and an artificial intelligence (AI) model developed by Shan et al. (2023). Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA; Shaffer, 2017) was employed to examine the di alogic interactions between tutors and students acr oss different educational levels, unpacking the co-occurrence patterns of these dialogic strategies. Figure 1 The Overall Networks of Dialogic Interactions between Tutors and Students at the Primary (red, left), Middle (blue, middle), and High (purple, right) School Levels Figure 2 The Subtracted Networks Showing Differences in the Patterns of Dialogic Strategies between Ttutorship for Different Levels (left: Middle – Primary; right: Middle-High) In Figure 1, the node sizes of T.OT (off-topic), T.KS (knowledge sharing and instruction), and T.RFQ (reflection inducing question), the largest nodes, indicate that tutors frequently engaged in off-task dialogues, which constituted the majority of their interaction s. Additionally, tutors primarily employed knowledge sharing and instruction strategies and prompted student reflection. In Figure 2, the correlations between tutors’ knowledge sharing and instruction (T.KS) and students’ constructive expression (S.C) in tutorship for high school students were stronger compared to those in tutorship for middle school students. Furthermore, the correlations in tutorship for middle school students were stronger than those  for primary school students. Conversely, the corre lations between tutors’ knowledge sharing and instruction (T.KS) and students’ acknowledgement (S.AP) in tutorship for primary school students were stronger than those in tutorship for middle school students.
