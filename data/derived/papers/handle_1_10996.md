# A Quasi-Experimental Study of the Introduction of Machine Learning Concepts to a High School Age Audience as Part of a Mentored Scientific Research Program

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
As Machine Learning (ML), a branch of Artificial Intelligence (AI), proliferates across society, youth need to learn basic fundamentals of this new technology. Furthermore, the field of AI reflects the persistent lack of diversity seen in broader STEM fields, which may impact historically underrepresented groups pursuing these fields. In response, we created an ML-embedded, out-of-school-time program for high school age students focused on scientific inquiry, constructed through a justice-lens. This paper reports findings from a repeated measures study of 35 youth (82% from underrepresented groups in STEM) introduced to ML concepts through a 4-week Institute followed by an academic-year-long research internship with a mentor-scientist. Student learning outcomes were measured using a new ML concept inventory. Findings suggest ML knowledge increased after the Institute and persisted 9 months afterwards for those participating in mentored research.

This paper addresses the need to teach ML to high school age youth in an equitable way by sharing (a) the development of a new ML concept inventory for measuring youths’ learning on ML general concepts, as well as

(b) findings from a study of an introductory ML education program for high school age youth, called the Science

and Research Mentorship Program (SRMP), which is composed of two components. The Summer Institute in Machine Learning (hereafter, called the Institute) introduced student participants to  ML knowledge and skills using a newly designed curricular intervention (100 hours). Immediately after, all participants began an academic year of mentored scientific research, called the Research Year, during which they took part in one of 21 research projects (130 hours). Participants, in project teams of 3, were mentored by a scientist as they worked together in a laboratory on an authentic research project in the natural sciences. A subset of 4 projects specifically required participants to use ML in th eir research. This study compares participating student (n=35) ML-related cognitive outcomes using a repeated measures survey, which was administered at 3 time-points during the program: pre-Institute, post-Institute, and post Research Year. Analysis was conducted to answer the research question: To what degree do high school students develop machine learning (ML) knowledge and skills through the curricular as compared to the mentored research interventions?

The science research mentorship program (SRMP) The program was conducted at the American Museum of Natural History in New York City. The Museum has a large research scientist community, some of whom use ML methods to conduct their research. Participating youth (referred to here as scholars) begin the S RMP program in early August of each year to participate in a 4-week (100 hours) training program called the Summer Institute. The Summer Institute was designed using a justice-informed lens to introduce ML concepts and draw connections between ML, Science, and society while preparing scholars for research experiences more broadly. Weeks 1 and 2 introduced scholars to general concepts in ML and ML methods, i.e., linear regression, decision trees, k-means clustering. Week 3 consisted of a field trip to a nature reserve. Week 4 introduced additional ML methods, i.e., neural networks, and provided opportunities for scholars to discuss bias in ML and societal implications of ML in science. The mentorship intervention (130 hours) occurred during the Research Year.  Nine scholars were given their 1st or 2nd choice mentorship that required the application of ML methods in their research. Scholars who did not rank the ML mentorships as their 1st or 2nd choice were assigned to mentorships that did not use ML methods. 2321 Participants. The majority of the total 35 consenting participants identified as female (63%, n=22) and in the 10th grade (71%, n=25). Nine study participants (26% of the total) applied ML methods during the Research Year; the remaining 26 participants (74%) joined a mentorship that did not use ML during the Research Year. Research Design & Data Collection.  This quasi-experimental study used a pretest-posttest design with repeated measures across 3 time points during the intervention: 1) before Summer Institute, 2) at the end of the Institute and 3) after participants completed their mentored Research Year. Participant data was grouped by whether they participated in a mentored research project that used or did not use ML. Instruments. To measure the impact of the Institute’s ML curricular activities on student learning outcomes, we used the ML Concept Inventory (ML-CI), which is based on a previously validated instrument composed of 15 items adapted from the Artificial Intelligence Concept Inventory (AICI; Zhang et al., 2023).
