# Examining Community Development in an Online, Global, Collaborative, Learning Environment

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper examines how community develops among adolescent students in an online, collaborative, learning environment. Transcripts of discourse from four video conference calls (meetups) were examined using Epistemic Network Analysis to identify key patterns of community development amongst the students. Results show changes in the patterns of discourse corresponding to the development of community over two time periods in 2021.

## Introduction

The landscape of online education has been transformed by the evolution of new technol ogy and the Covid-19 pandemic. The education community must consider the impacts of a shift to online le arning, including the potential effect on community development. With that, Brown (2001) offers a unique and a pplicable framework on the community development process in online learning communities. Originally stu died in an asynchronous environment, Brown (2001) determined that the process of community development occurs in three distinct levels, further outlined in Table 1.

Table 1 Levels of Community Development Level Description

1. Acquaintances Learners make online acquaintances by finding individuals with similarities.

2. Contribution Learners contributing to the community through discussion and discourse.

3. Camaraderie Long-term or repeated association with others via personal communication.

This paper seeks to examine how community develops among adolescent students in an online, informal learning environment referred to as IC4 (International Community for Collabor ative Content Creation). The present study expands on previous community development research by Espino et al. (2019) by analyzing the same adolescent learning environment across a new post-pandemic time layered against Brown’s (2001) three-level framework. In IC4, student participants from over four continents partic ipate in collaborative multimedia projects on self-selected STEM-focused topics, meeting each week via video conference to share their work.

## Methods

The data analyzed in this study consists of four meetups. Each meetup was tra nscribed and reviewed separately by two researchers and then coded independently by two raters who followed a proc ess of social moderation to reach final consensus on the coded data (Herrenkohl & Cornelius, 2013). The data was uploaded to the Epistemic Network Analysis webtool for analysis and visualization. Each utterance, sepa rated by turn of talk, represented the unit of analysis, and the meetups were defined as the conversations in which connections were limited. ## Discussion

The initial examination of the four global meetups gives insight into how community develops in the IC4 environment. In the February 2021 meetup, the discourse was strongest surrounding Inclu sive Disposition, Info Sharing and Social Disposition. The March 2021 meetup carried similar patterns but  demonstrated more vibrant connections. The shift towards greater richness of discourse may be explained by two students who presented on similar topics, which elicited an engaged discussion during the second meetup. T his pattern exemplifies the first two levels of Brown’s (2001) research findings of shared interest and contribution. Looking next to the Fall meetups, the October 2021 meetup reflects discourse that was strongest between Inclusive Disposition and Social Disposition, while the November 2021 meetup show a sig nificant shift to the right towards Info Sharing, Content Focus, and Curiosity. This shift may be expl ained by the presence of two student learners from Brazil in both meetups and their shared culture all owing for increased engagement in the second meetup.
