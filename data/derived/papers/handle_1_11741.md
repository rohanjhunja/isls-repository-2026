# What Tasks Do Learners Delegate to ChatGPT? Analysis of Student Queries

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigated how students use ChatGPT during their exam preparation period. We explored 3,047 messages from students to ChatGPT to assess what kinds of questions and tasks students are keen to delegate to AI. Using the OpenAI API, we annotated these messages, categorizing question types and mapping them to Bloom's taxonomy levels. The results show that students are using ChatGPT not only to recall information but to deepen their understanding and analytical skills.

## Introduction

Students are using Generative AI assistants, like ChatGPT, to address learning tasks, and that process seems to be irreversible. While students show increasing trust in the quality of AI output (Dakakni & Safa, 2023),  teachers and learning sciences professionals are alarmed at the consequences of such insufficiently controlled use. These warnings relate to an increase in plagiarism and, i n the longer term, a decrease in the level of criti cal thinking among students (Grassini, 2023). On the other hand,  integrating the use of conversational agents, such  as ChatGPT, into the curriculum could help students autonomously break down difficult concepts or train for specific assignments. This assumption is supported in the paper by Diwanji et al., who suggests that incorporating AI into flipped classrooms can not only increase student pr eparation rates but also foster a more engaging and  effective learning environment.

While numerous research studies have been conducted  to understand students' attitudes toward Generative AI (Chan & Hu, 2023), there has been rel atively little research based on actual data of int eractions. One of notable examples is research conducted by Park et al., 2018, which demonstrated that when communicating with chatbots, students tend to use less complex sentence structures and have a more limited vocabulary. However, there remains a significant gap in the literature regarding the specific content of these conversations and the types of tasks students are willing to tackle using tools such as ChatGPT. This research seeks to address this gap in knowledg e. As a consequence, our research question is: How do students interact with a generative AI: what kin ds of tasks students are ready to delegate to AI, w hat types of questions do students ask?

## Procedure

The study involved a sample of 46 undergraduate stu dents (32 female, 14 male). The academic programs o f recruited students varied from technical and quantitative disciplines, such as Data Science and Business Analytics, to social sciences and humanities, i ncluding Economics and Politics, Politics and Inter national Relations, and Psychology. Participants were recruited through the  procedure of convenience sampling via email invita tions, their participation was voluntary. Informed consent was obtained from all participants. To organize data collection we provided students with 12 ChatGPT 4 accounts, each used by a group of 4 students. This grouping was dictated by cost efficiency, and no group work was involved.

Participants interacted with ChatGPT 4 for a period of 6 weeks. They were encouraged to use the assistant for their exam preparations, and were not restricted by any limits or specific goals. After 6 weeks, all conversations were extracted into an anonymised data set for further analysis.

## Data analysis

To conduct the analysis we started with labeling th e dataset of 3047 messages with the help of ChatGPT 4o-mini API. A Python script was developed to interact with  ChatGPT 4o-mini via the OpenAI API. The script included a detailed prompt, in which the principles of effec tive prompting were respected (Lin, 2024). Two inde pendent prompts were designed: one for categorizing questio ns by content, the other for categorizing questions  by cognitive complexity. The question categories (Tabl e 1) were developed based o n prior literature (Graesser & Person, 1994) and expanded inductively by the resea rchers during preliminary data exploration. Bloom's taxonomy revised by Krathwohl (2002) was chosen for  this analysis due to its extensive use in educatio nal research to categorize cognitive complexity, making  it suitable for understanding the depth of student s’ queries and tasks delegated to AI. The prompt required the model to provide reasoning before outputting an answer. This reasoning was then recorded in a separate column in  the output file, making it accessible for a human reviewer. All output was verified by two human experts, resolving discrepancies through consensus discussions.

## Results and discussion

Overall, 408 conversations were collected containing 3047 student messages. The findings of the research are presented in Table s 1 and 2. The most common type of question asked by students to ChatGPT was Procedural (699 messages, 22.94% of the total). That may suggest that stude nts frequently requested steps or instructions to accom plish specific tasks or understand processes. Defin ition questions were the second most frequent, with 441 m essages (14.47%), which may indicate that students use ChatGPT as a source of information.
