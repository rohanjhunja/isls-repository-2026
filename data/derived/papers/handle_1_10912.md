# Trauma-Informed Tertiary Systems: A Quantitative Pilot Study

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
While higher educational instructors have been leading the charge to implement trauma-informed practices, less is known about how university systems address trauma. This pilot study enlisted college instructors and administrators (N=44) to complete a 51-question survey to determine their knowledge and use of trauma-informed practices within their institutions. Descriptive statistics were used to characterize the level of understanding of trauma-informed practices in Institutes of Higher Education (IHE). Results indicate trauma aware, sensitive, responsive, and informed practices in higher education were generally low.

## Introduction

Reduction of h arm and attempts to create safety are the impetus for building trauma-informed systems. In an educational environment, harm can interfere with a student’s ability to learn and safety facilitates t hat learning. Simply put, trauma-informed practices facilitate and encourage safe learning environments. In the midst of the COVID-19 pandemic there was an explosion of research on t rauma-informed practices and trauma affected student learning in IHE. The result is that we know vastly more than we di d previously about how to support students who have experienced trauma or preventing additional trauma in the first place. The bulk of p andemic initiated work was examined through an instructor’s perspective, or with the pu rpose of supporting instructional pedagogy (Carello & Thompson, 2022; Harrison et al., 2020). We know less about how educational systems understand and implement trauma-informed practices.

The extent of what is known about trauma-informed practices at an institutional level is so essential to the success and well-being of students, that there have been numerous calls to rectify this negligence (Concepcion & Nadolski, 2021; Henshaw, 2022). There have prev iously been gray papers and peer-reviewed articles that provide a framework for trauma-informed practices that support students in their tertiary education (Carello & Butler, 2015; Davidson, 2017). However, the majority of this work is only applicable to the classroom. This study intends to lay the groundwork for a deeper understanding of trauma-informed practices within IHE.

## Method

The present study is part of a broader research end eavor focusing on faculty and administrators’ persp ectives of trauma-informed practices in IHE. Data for this study were gathered through a survey administered to faculty and academic leaders within IHE. T he purpose of the survey was to understand the exte nt to which institutions implement trauma aware, sensitive, responsive, and informed practices. Our work was based on, and the survey was constructed, from a commonly used framework titled the IHE Framework for Trauma Informed Development (Berke, DaCunha, Killian & Wilson, 2022). The surve y consisted of two major components: the first enta iled collecting participants' demographic information, w hile the second consisted of 51 Likert-scale items that were intended to measure the prevalence of trauma-informed practices within the higher educational context. Analysis is based on categories described in the framework a s stages (aware, sensitive, responsive, and informe d) for becoming a trauma-informed IHE. Result s were limited by the small number of respondents t hat had thus far participated.

Participants for this pilot study were recruited th rough social media platforms, leveraging the reach and accessibility they provide. The survey is open to participants from all countries; however, most of the respondents, at this early stage, are predominantly from North America. In total, 44 participants responded to our call, thereby constituting our participant sample. Data analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics version 25. To ensure the internal consistency of the dimensions of trauma-informed practices, we conducted a Cronbach's alpha test of reliability. The results indicated a high level of internal consistency, with Cronbach's alpha coeffic ients ranging from 0.72 to 0.95. We conducted quantitative analys is, using descriptive statistics, i.e., mean and standard deviation to describe the level of trauma-informed practices.

## Results

The results  (See Table 1) indicate that trauma aware, sensitive, responsive, and informed practices in higher education were generally low. More specifically, faculty and administrators reported lower levels of trauma aware knowledge and practices (M= 2.40), applications (M=2.03) and attitudes (M=2.94) in their institutions. Similarly, faculty and administrators’ responses indicate that  the implementation of trauma sensitive knowledge (M=2.50), applications (M=2.70), and attitudes (2.71) was less than average. ## Discussion

The intention of this exploratory research is to ex amine and test the IHE framework for Trauma-Informed Development (Berke et al., 2022). With this framework in place, the authors wished to develop a fuller picture of the effectiveness of the framework by teasing out different levels of an organization’s implementation of traumainformed practices. Findings indicate that ther e are tremendous opportunities for growth as trauma-informed practices and knowledge were generally low as reported by respondents. Because of the low response rate for this pilot study, further research would require greater numbers of participants and participants from countries around the globe to gain a comprehensive understanding and  more accurate appraisal of trauma-informed practices. Ultimately, the framework by Berke et al. (2022) looks deeper into creating a guide for IHE to explore and support trauma-informed practices system-wide.
