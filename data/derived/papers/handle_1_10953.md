# Cocreative Llearning is More Effective than Direct Instruction Argument on Basis of Hanna Arendt’s Vita Activa

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
When learning must contribute to resilience, and community cocreative learning is more effective thandirect instruction. This position paper draws on Hannah Arendt’s Vita Activa: Labor, Work, and Speech and Action. Labor is about meeting basic needs and creating a ‘home’ where life is good. Work concerns creating languages and culture and therefore an artificial world. Speech and Action involve organizing society and engaging in politics.Linking learning and education to the Vita Activa, an educational picture emerges that mainly focuses on Labor and Work, with a strong emphasis on direct instruction and the acquisition of skills and knowledge. Speech and Action, e.g., the development of ideas and working together for a better world, remain underdeveloped in education. Direct instruction may be efficient, but it is not effective in view of our overall development as human beings. Space is needed for idea development, co-creation, knowledge building, and dialogical learning.

Vita activa There is an enormously  impact of (direct) instruction on education, but do es it contribute to  ‘learning as a cornerstone of healing, resilience, and community’?  To answer this questions an interesting starting point is the analysis of the human condition of philosopher Hannah Arendt (Arendt, 2023). Arendt analyses and describes the Vita Activa through the centuries. She does so base d on the idea that we are part of a world that  we share with others. (Dasein, in-the-world-being-with-others) She wonders what we actually do in the world as human beings? Activities that are specific to humans and therefore  characteristic of humanity. She boils these down to three activities: Labor, Work, and Speech and Action.

Labor Arendt refers to the activities we engage in to sta y alive and continue to exist as a species as Labor. As animal laborans, laboring animals, we are bound hand and foot to the eternal cycle of life. This refers simply to production and consumption, to working on the land, in the fac tory, in a store, or in an office. Labor produces c onsumables. The ideal of Labor is abundance: enough food, enough homes, and ideally a second home and a car. There is essentially not a lot of challenge or creativity in Labor, in the sense of coming up with new ideas, this is labor that simply needs to be done, like cooking every day and washing your dishes. Work As human beings, our expert creativity lies mainly in the creation of languages, this includes mathematics, chemistry, music, geometry, programming, aesthetics, architecture, didactics, etc. These ‘languages’ s hape our world and society, essentially creating an artificial ‘world of our own’. A world that is less consumption-oriented, less finite than our own lives, more sustainable, mo re durable, but still transient. Arendt calls these activities Work. The activity of homo faber, human being the maker, is not tied to the life course of our existence. Languages shape our culture.

Speech and action The third and most human activity mentioned by Aren dt is Speech and Action. Speech and Action are acti vities through which people work together to organize soci ety, their company, their household, and in a broad er sense: politics. The basis of this organizing or politics is our ‘plurality’, or multiple perspectives in educational terms. This plurality is the existential uniqueness of every human being, of every person alive, and it means that we each have our own perspective on the world, which is dif ferent from that of any other person. Every person is uniq ue in this respect. This plurality, the multiple persp ective, is anchored in our natality. Every newborn human being is a new beginning, with the potential to see how w e shape our society, our work together, and how thi ngs could be done differently, in short, the potential to do ‘the improbable’. The hope for the future. The link with learning We think that, in education, Labor and Work, in the form of learning the ‘languages’, is seen as the ba sis. The question is: how do these activities translate into learning and didactics in current education? And how is education organized, didactically, for Speech and action? Efficient versus effective education Direct instruction and its associated learning are efficient because of the ongoing industrialization of education, in which economic thinking predominates, but this does not make direct instruction and education effective if you want to include the development and richness of being human e.g. including uniqueness of Speech and Action.

## Conclusion: More space for big ideas and co-creation didactics

The activities of Labor and Work are approached too strongly from a learnin g and knowledge information perspective (rational mind). The reasoning, the wonder, the contemplation of ‘big ideas’ in this knowledge is left out however ideas are everywhere: in Labor, in Work, and in Spe ech and Action. Which means that we can also use knowledge building and co-creation as didactics in teaching and learning in L abor and Work and be more effective than with direct instruction alone. We plea for a better balance between these didactics in practice.
