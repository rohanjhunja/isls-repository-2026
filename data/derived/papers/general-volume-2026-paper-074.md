# Developing Systems Thinking Related to Sustainability Among Urban Indian Middle School Students Using Cognitive Apprenticeship

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The present study focuses on developing systems-thinking among middle school students (11-14 years) through a cognitive apprenticeship-based learning approach. The study aims to develop interventions to foster students' systems thinking, as an important competence for solving sustainability problems. The study will first examine experts’ understanding of systems and processes as they solve system-related problems. Subsequently, the insights from it will be integrated with the cognitive apprenticeship learning approach to develop interventions for middle school students. Research goals This study aims to design an effective learning environment to develop systems-thinking skills among urban Indian middle school students, focusing on sustainability concepts and related problems. Specifically, my research focuses on understanding how systems thinking related to water sustainability can be developed among urban Indian middle school students, and my research questions are: How do middle school students think about water as a system from a sustainability perspective? How do experts think about systems from a sustainability perspective? How does a cognitive apprenticeship-based learning environment facilitate changes in systems thinking about water sustainability among middle school students? What is the effect of cognitive apprenticeship-based intervention on students’ systems thinking about water sustainability?

Sustainability problems are complex because they involve associations with different stakeholders, multiple perspectives, the ambiguous nature of the concepts surrounding them, and competing values (Morris & Martin, 2009). Given the complexity of such problems, systems thinking is an important skill for solving them (Tomkinson, 2009). Systems thinking is a higher-order thinking skill that focuses on analyzing a complex topic to identify its parts and put them together to gain a holistic view of the system (Kali et al., 2003). It helps to view the “Sustainable world” as a system and to analyze its essential components and their interconnections (Strachan, 2009). It also helps us understand the complex nature of the interconnected world and apply our diverse learning experiences (Strachan, 2009).

Research suggests that a practice-based environmental education intervention, embedded in a context that promotes community participation, helps develop people’s systems-thinking (Dutta & Chandrasekharan, 2018). However, how systems-thinking evolves among learners has not been captured in this research. Also, the research does not map the specific features of the intervention with different kinds of changes in systems thinking. Past research also suggests that experience-based learning does not always help improve systems thinking skills (Sadira, 2021), as quoted by Ekselsa et al. (2023). Explicit emphasis must be placed on the interconnections between structures operating at different organizational levels (Raved & Yarden, 2014). Together, this literature suggests the need to explore other learning approaches to develop systems-thinking among middle school students.

Cognitive apprenticeship is a learning approach in which concepts and facts are situated in the context of their use (Collins et al., 1987). It is based on the principles of learning theories such as situated learning, communities of practice, communities of learners, scaffolding, articulation, and reflection (Collins & Kapur, 2014). This study aims to use the cognitive apprenticeship model to combine explicit scaffolding and practicebased interventions to develop key components of systems-thinking among middle school students. The aim is for students to develop these skills to address the sustainability problems they encounter. One such problem that we have chosen for our research is water sustainability, as water is a natural resource and an intrinsic part of the environment. Students would be able to connect with sustainability issues related to water. T he research design for this research will be based on educational design research (EDR), a type of research design that aims to solve an educational problem through an iterative process and generate new knowledge through scientific inquiry (McKenney & Reeves, 2013). The present work will follow the EDR model, using qualitative, quantitative, and mixed-methods approaches to address the research questions. An expert study in the initial stage of the research will provide insights into how experts solve sustainability problems using a systemsthinking framework. These findings will be combined with the features of cognitive apprenticeship to design a learning environment for students to develop their systems-thinking. In the later stage, the study to understand the effectiveness of the designed learning environment will be conducted with the Indian middle school students (ages 11 to 14 years).

## Data gathered and expected findings

To synthesize experts' understanding of systems and the processes and skills they use to solve sustainability problems, I have interviewed five experts. The experts identified for the study were academicians and practitioners working in the fields of hydrology, climate change, carbon sequestration, ecology, and environmental science. They were identified using purposive sampling. I used a think-aloud protocol, in which experts were given a primary problem related to a sustainability issue to solve, followed by a semi-structured interview to understand their analysis of systems and reasoning, either in support of their proposed solution for the system or in opposition to alternative solutions. I am in the process of analysing the data using thematic analysis, with a systems-thinking framework. I expect to gain deeper insights into how experts use systems thinking to solve sustainability problems. These findings, i.e., the systems thinking approaches used by experts to solve sustainability problems, will be externalized for students during the modeling phase of the cognitive apprenticeship-based learning environment. This will help students build their conceptual models of the processes used in systems thinking. Expected contributions This research aims to contribute to a cognitive apprenticeship-based learning environment to address the problem of water sustainability among urban Indian middle school students. It is conjectured that the learning environment will have features such as real-world problems, collaboration and dialogue among students, problematizing and structuring scaffolds, and reasoning. The expert study in this research will contribute to understanding the systems-thinking model used by experts to solve sustainability problems.
