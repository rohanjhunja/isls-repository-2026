# Do Current Visions of Engineering Literacy Capture Family Engineering Practices? A Microgenetic Analysis of Engineering Learning in a Museum Makerspace

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The recent Engineering Learning framework (ASEE, 2020) calls for research on how students can be supported to reach full engineering literacy in PreK-12 education. The current study analyzes a microgenetic engineering moment with a child, parent, and staff member in a museum makerspace. We found evidence of the Engineering Learning framework, but instances where the framework did not fully represent some nuances during making.

Although many states have adopted the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS Lead States, 2013), which integrate engineering and science practices, engineering education has overwhelming adopted a single vision of engineering, the Engineering Design Process (Carr, Bennett, & Strobel, 2012; Arik & Topçu, 2020). Recently, a new three-dimension framework, called the Engineering Learning framework, has been proposed for formal PreK-12 curriculum that includes Engineering Habits of Mind (EHM), Engineering Practices (EP) including the Engineering Design Process, and Engineering Knowledge (EK). The ASEE (American Society for Engineering Education, 2020) report calls for research to apply the Engineering Learning framework in order to understand how engineering learning is progressing to reach the “end goal” of full engineering literacy for all students. However, the current framework is unclear on how the vision of full engineering literacy is achieved or even visible when still in development. We take up the call to use the Engineering Literacy framework with a preliminary exploration focused on applying the framework to the documentation of microgenetic engineering learning.

Data and Analysis As a preliminary exploration on the potential of Engineering Learning (ASEE, 2020) as an analytical framework, we utilized data collected as part of a larger proj ect developing a cardboard-focused makerspace at a large Midwestern science museum. Data was collected from May-June 2021 during museum-hosted private events with community partner groups who invited families that are historically and currently underserved by museu ms to experience and critique the makerspace. Based on a data review with engineering practitioner partners, we selected a single video clip that was consistently identifie d as an example of engineering. The Engineering Lea rning framework (ASEE, 2020, p. 25-26) proposes three primary components to engineering education that should be woven throughout curriculum; for the purposes of this analysis, we used the definitions of these components as supplied in the ASEE report as our coding framework. The authors thematically coded (Braun & Clarke, 2 006) the transcript based on each of these three components for each major sub-clip of the video clip.
