# Tangible Mathematical Tinkering Toolkits as a Platform for Understanding Drivers and Trajectories of Curiosity based Exploration

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster examines how a paper-based tangible tool, the Zine Machine, facilitates curiosity-driven explorations in math and engineering design. By enabling learners to create mechanical linkages, the Zine Machine promotes open-ended self-expression and fosters deeper conceptual and disciplinary learning. Through observations, we identify the critical roles of manipulatory, conceptual, and goal-based curiosity in driving mathematical and engineering-related engagement. These findings provide initial insights into analyzing learners and designing environments conducive to creative, multidisciplinary learning.

## Introduction and background

Making and tinkering activities often involve impli cit mathematical reasoning. This study seeks to lev erage this for developing mathematical curiosity in informal s paces (Pattison et al., 2017) through tangible enga gements. Informal learning environments offer opportunities for rich mathematical thinking and learning, yet there remains limited research on facilitating mathematical learning through open-ended tinkering activities (Cooper, 2011) Recognizing tinkering as a key practice in STEAM and making-based learning environments (Resnick & Rosenbaum, 2013), often inspired by Papert's constructionism (Papert, 1980), recent constructionist approaches tend to emphasize complex computing technologi es (Weiner et al., 2018), potentially limiting acce ssibility and material engagement. Traditional physical math tools (like abacuses and Cuisenaire rods) are less interactive and tend to be regarded as “fun math” and not “real mat h” (Moyer, 2001). To addre ss this, there's a rise in low-tech, unplugged activities (Lee, 2014; Thompson, 2023). W e extend this inquiry, exploring how designed tools  foster mathematical exploration through a curiosity lens (Gruber et al., 2019). Curiosity, conceptualized in various forms (To et al., 2016), offers insights into learner engagement, design, and facilitation (Gillings Rodney, 2021), guiding our analysis of learners' interactions with tangible tools. Design In this poster, we present Zine Machine, a paper-based math tinkering kit. Zine Machine integrates t wodimensional mechanical movements within a zine form at, merging math and storytelling (see Figure 1). U sers manipulate static and dynamic pieces to explor e mathematical functions, fostering creative engage ment and learning. These pieces include a circle, three squa res, a rigid slot link, four linkages of different lengths and a linkage with a central slot.

Through analysis of user interactions, we aim to un derstand how embodied curiosity contributes to mathematical meaning construction. The design emphasizes accessibility and playful exploration, utilizing paper and simple mechanical elements (brass fasteners, circles, squares, links) to facilitate multidisciplinary engagement (Gillings Rodney, 2021). This design contributes to ongoing efforts to integrate mathematics into playful learning experiences.

## Methodology

Participants, aged 6 to 27, engaged in testing sess ions via video conferencing, interacting with the Z ine Machine tinkering kit and sharing their experiences. Data, transcribed and filtered iteratively, yielded four vignettes showcasing various curiosities  driving mathematical and engineering exploration. T he discussion synthesizes findings, emphasizing goal choice, material manipulation, and conceptual connections as key drivers of curiosity for future analysis and design refinement.

## Findings

We analyzed four participant vignettes for our preliminary study: Ben, aged seven, constructed a drawing machine resembling a robotic arm using linkages and colored brads, emphasizing color-coding for functionality and smooth movement. Cathy iterated linkages to understand rig idity and flexibility, then traced ranges of motion  for her character Greg, connecting material exploration to mathematical concepts. Project 3 showcases Viola's shift from mathematical functions to sculpt ure concepts, creating a quick return mechanism rep resented by a fish. Lastly, Emanuel, a math PhD student, mapped locus points of  output linkages to explore mathematical concepts systematically.

## Discussion and conclusion

In this poster, we explore how curiosity drives eng agement with creative constructionist toolkits like  Zine Machine, identifying three key drivers: goal choice, material exploration, and conceptual investigatio n. Makers' goals, ranging from playful self-expression to ambitious projects, set unique exploration paths. Concepts often emerge tangentially to goals, fostering deeper unde rstanding through applied contexts. Material explor ation encompasses diverse ways of using materials, foster ing micro and macro pursuits. Zine Machine aims to democratize math engagement, fostering self-efficacy and curiosity. Future work includes expanding user testing and integrating paper circuits for multisensory exp eriences, contributing to ongoing efforts in mathem atics education research on open-ended tinkering and curiosity-driven learning.
