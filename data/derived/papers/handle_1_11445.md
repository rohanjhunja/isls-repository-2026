# Effect of AI-Powered Virtual Reality Simulation on Pre-Service Teachers’ Empathy towards Multicultural Students

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Although the number of multicultural students is increasing in South Korea, many pre-service teachers are undertrained when it comes to understanding the challenges these students face in the classroom. This study developed a virtual reality (VR) training simulation aimed at fostering empathy among pre-service teachers toward multicultural students. The simulation helps pre-service teachers experience linguistic challenges from multicultural students' perspectives, enhancing empathy through perspective-taking. Twenty-four pre-service teachers participated in this study which included pre-and post-tests, pre-and post-activity, VR simulation, and interviews. This study found a significant enhancement in pre-service teachers' empathy and multicultural attitudes after experiencing the VR simulation. The VR simulation enabled pre-service teachers to establish meaningful connections with multicultural students, enhancing their cultural sensitivity and commitment to inclusive teaching strategies. These findings highlight the substantial benefits of VR in fostering empathy through perspective-taking in pre-service teacher education.

## Introduction

South Korea has become a multiethnic and multicultural society, with the foreign population exceeding 5% (Kim, 2024). Due to language and cultural differences, multicultural students frequently struggle to adapt to school life. Teachers’ competence, especially empathy, is crucia l for helping multicultural students integrate into  the classroom. Empathy enables teachers to build positive relation ships and create supportive educational environments that enhance both student success and teacher well-being (Wink et al., 2021). Conversely, difficulties in expressing empathy may lead to empathy-related burnout in teachers, negatively impacting students and teaching effectiveness  (Wink et al., 2021).  This highlights the need for empathy training.  Despite its importance, many pre-service teachers have limited opportunities to enga ge meaningfully with multicultural perspectives during their practical training  (Gay, 2010). Gaps between theoretical knowledge  and real-world application present challenges for pre-service teachers in translating their learning into  inclusive classroom practices. This may result in rigid, top-down strategies that intensify rather than solve cu rrent issues (McGarr, 2021). Moreover, pre-service teachers often find it challenging to empat hize with students from diverse cultural, and social backgrounds, emphasizing the need for empathy training programs (Gay, 2010). Virtual Reality (VR) offers a novel solution as "th e ultimate empathy machine," enabling users to experience others' perspectives (Hassan, 2020). For teachers, VR allows them to assume multiple roles, experience diverse perspectives through virtual body transformation, and tailor res ponses to students' needs (Stavroulia & Lanitis, 2023; Cho et al., 2015). This innovative a pproach shows promise in enhancing empathy and educational effectiveness (Hassan, 2020). However, VR-based learning remains in its early stages, particularly in developing and utilizing VR simulation for teacher education (Stavroulia & Lanitis, 2023). Poorly designed VR environments may lead to cognitive overload or disorientation, limiting their educational effect (Lee et al., 2020). Effective VR learning requires systematic design based on a clea r understanding of VR affordances  (Dalgarno & Lee, 2010). Therefore, this study aims to develop and validate AI-powered VR simulation to enhance pre-service teachers’ empathy toward multicultural students. It seeks to answer the following research questions: (1) To wha t extent did the VR simulation affect pre-service teachers' empathy and multicultural attitud es? (2) How did pre-service teachers perceive the VR simulation with regard to its effects on their empathy and multicultural attitudes?

## Literature review

The teaching profession has become increasingly com plex due to globalization and socio-cultural changes. Beyond delivering knowledge, teachers must possess social-emotional skills, particularly empathy, to create positive classroom environments and address students' diverse needs. According to Meyers et al. (2019), teacher empathy is defined as the extent to which instructo rs work to deeply understand students’ individual a nd social situations—often described as perspective-taking (cognitive empathy), feel concern and care in response to students’ emotions and experiences (emotional empat hy), and communicate this understanding and care th rough their actions in the classroom (behavioral empathy). Teachers with high empathy exhibit more positive at titudes toward student, are better equipped to handle problematic behaviors, and frequently use effective problem-solving strategies (Wink et al., 2021). They build close re lationships with students, effe ctively address their educational needs, and create supportive learning environments, while experiencing lower levels of job burnout (Wink et al., 2021). Conversely, teachers  experiencing empathy-related burnout often overlook students' perspectives, make unilateral decisions, and adopt detached, authorita tive attitudes, which can lead to frustration, burn out, and reduced teaching effectiveness (Cooper, 2004; Gay, 2000). These behaviors negatively affect students, resu lting in lower academic performance, increased feelings of isolation, and a greater likelihood of disruptive behavior. VR technological advancements offer new possibiliti es for perspective-taking interventions in a virtual setting that could be used to improve teacher empat hy. For instance, Stavroulia and Lanitis (2023) pro vided teachers with VR environments that allowed t hem to take on the perspectives of both students and teachers, enabling them to experience the challenges faced by students in real-life classrooms. Similarly, Cho et al. (2015) provided an opportunity for pre-service teachers to collaboratively solve school bullying problems, conduct roleplays (school bullies, victims, instructor) in a vi rtual world, and reflect on their activities. The  findings indicate that the VR system demonstrated significant potenti al as a valuable tool for integration into teacher training. Despite the growing interest in VR, most current applications i n teacher training are limited in scope. Many do not use immersive equipment like Head-Mounted Displays (HMDs), which are crucial for crea ting realistic and impactful training experiences (Stavroulia & Lanitis, 2023). Furthermore, with the advancement of AI technology, integrating AI into VR learning environments can offer more interactive and dynamic experiences; however, such attempts remain relatively scarce. Therefore, this study aimed to develop an AI-powered VR simulation and deliver it through HMDs to effectively enhance pre-service teachers' empathy by enabling them to exper ience challenges from the perspectives of multicultural students through perspective-taking.

## Participants

A total of 24 pre-service teachers (male=9, female=15, age M=22.6) from diverse subject majors at the universities in South Korea participated in this study. Among them,  nine pre-service teachers had experience guiding multicultural students during their teacher trainin g, and four had received formal training in multicu ltural education. Additionally, eighteen participants  reported ha ving prior experience with immersive VR. Before commencing the usability evaluation, it was confirm ed that all participants we re capable of completing the VR experience without significant discomfort while usi ng the immersive VR HMDs. The research was approved  by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Seoul National University in South Korea and conducted accordingly. Scenario and VR simulation The VR simulation was developed using the Unity© game engine and desi gned through an iterative cycle based on the rapid prototyping instructional systems desi gn model (Lim et al., 2020). The simulation puts pa rticipants in the shoes of a multicultural student  (see Figure 1), providing them with the opportunity to interact w ith peers and teachers in a virtual classroom and experience difficulties with speaking, listening, reading, and writing.  To help Korean participants understand the language challenges faced by multicultural students, various types of simulated barriers were implemented. These included modifying the text to simulate reading difficulties, warping the voices of teachers and peers to represent listening challenges, and generating s loppy handwriting to simulate writing difficulties. While rule-based simulations provided predefined scenarios and limited interaction, we aimed to further enhance the reality and interactivity of the VR experience by integrating generative AI  (Gen AI) and large language models such as GPT  to simulate speaking challenges. The technology, which was developed by combining OpenAI's automatic speech recognition sys tem, Whisper and GPT-4 to simulate speaking barriers, transcribes participants' fluent Korean speech, transforms it into text that reflects the pronunciation characteristics of multicultural students, and then re-synthesizes it into audio with disfluent, accented pronunciation. ## Data collection

We collected quantitative data to measure participants’ empathy (including cognitive and emotional empathy) and multicultural attitudes, along with qualitative data from semi-structured interviews to understand the mechanisms behind changes in empathy and multicultural attitudes. Figure 2 shows the experimental procedure of this study, which spanned 150 min. Participants began with an i ntroduction to the study objectives, procedures, and VR usage, followed by pre-tests to measure empathy and multicultural attitude s. They then engaged in pr e-activities predicting multicultural students' thoughts and act ions before experiencing a VR simulation using a VR  HMD. After the simulation, participants reflected on the ir experience in post-activities, completed post-tests to reassess their empathy and multicultural attitudes, and conc luded with individual semi-structured interviews to provide insights on their VR experience.

## Data analysis

Paired-samples t-tests were conducted to examine changes in pre-service teachers’ empathy and multicultural attitudes before and after the VR intervention. In addition, Pearson correlation analysis was performed to explore the relationships between empathy and multicultural  attitudes. To investigate the mechanisms behind changes in pre-service teachers’ empathy and multicultural attitud es, interviews were conducted on their experiences using the VR simulation to understand multicultural stude nts' language difficulties. The interview data w as analyzed using the thematic analysis method (Braun & Clarke, 2006). Two researchers inde pendently and iteratively reviewed the transcriptions, exchanging preliminary interpretations aligned with the research question, which led to the generation of initial codes. Through collaborative comparison and refinement, we then synthesized similar or conceptually related codes, identifying 4 themes and 16 sub-themes.

## Results

Effect of VR simulation on pre-service teachers’ empathy This study found that the VR simulation effectively improves pre-service teachers' empath y. Results from the Paired-samples t-tests showed a significant increase in cognitive empathy (perspective-taking items) scores from the pre-test (M=3.76, SD=0.51) to the post-test (M=4.06, SD=0.55, t=4.11, p=0.001). Similarly, emotional empathy (empathic concern items) scores significantly increased from the pre-test (M=3.90, SD=0.43) to the posttest (M=4.04, SD=0.52, t=2.07, p<0.05). Overall, empathy scores rose from the pre-test (M=3.83, SD=0.34) to the post-test (M=4.05, SD=0.38, t=3.94, p=0.001). This indicates that the VR simula tion improves pre-service teachers' empathy. The mechanisms by which empathy was perceived to be  improved were further investigated through interviews. By engaging in body-swapping and perspective-taking, participants were able to construct their awareness of multicultural issues and more concretely from a perspective of multicultural students.  This included recognizing challenges like language barri ers and limited participation in classroom activities. This sensory stimulation immersed users in the emotional atmosphere of the virtual environment, triggering emotional empathy—for instance, feelings of discrimination, pain, or sadness. For instance, one participant noted. “When I learned about multicultural education from b ooks, it felt abstract—diversity was just described as ‘diverse.’ But in VR, when my speech w as altered to sound like a multicultural student’s, I truly felt how language barriers creat e gaps between inten tion and expression, which made me feel unconsciously frustrated and uneasy” (S3). Not only to feel the pain stemming from the limitat ions experienced by multicultural students themselv es, the immersive VR environment also allowed participants to experience the emotional impact of exclusion by teachers and peers, such as diminished self-esteem and increased sensitivity to social feedback. For example, S7 said "I was extremely angry with the teacher's attitude from that perspective. I couldn't understand how the teacher could humiliate a student like that." Effect of VR intervention on pre-service teachers’ multicultural attitudes Results from the Paired-samples t-tests showed a significant increase in multicultural atti tudes scores from the pre-test (M=4.05, SD=0.49) to the post-test (M=4.41, SD=0.30, t=4.26, p<0.01). This indicates that the VR simulation contributes to improving pre-service teachers' multicultural attitudes.  Results from the Pearson correlation analysis revealed a significant positiv e relationship between empathy —particularly cognitive empathy—and multicultural attitudes at post-test (r=0.62, p=0.001). According to the post-interview, perspectivetaking experience enabled participants to empathize with the negative emotions that multicultural students go through, which led them to reflect on their own language and behavior as teachers. They also considered their role in the classroom and realized that they needed to t ransform from being passive responders to understanding and proactive. As participant S12 noted.

“Previously, I approached multicultural education in  a somewhat superficial and formal way. But now, I realize that it is essential for teacher s to first try to truly understand and grasp the student as an individual.” In addition, the experience prompted participants t o reconsider previous misunderstandings and reflect  on redesigning instructional strategies to better support multicultural students. For example, S10 noted. “Initially, I thought multicultural students would e xperience significant stress from being labeled as ‘multicultural’, so I believed teachers should be very careful. However, after this experience, I realized that rather than equal treatment, what these students truly need is timely and appropriate support.” For participants who had yet to give much thought to multicultural education, this experience served as a turning point, shifting their attitudes from indifference t o understanding and challenging existing stereotype s about multicultural students. For instance, S1 reflected.

“I found myself thinking—why hadn’t I thought about this before? I’ve definitely seen students like this at school, but I assumed they would naturally adapt. Now I feel that, as a future teacher, I should be more attentive and caring.”

## Discussion and conclusion

The most significant finding of this study is that the VR simulation elicited significant improvements  in both empathy and multicultural attitudes. Fostering cogn itive empathy through immersive experiences appears  to be an effective way to cultivate inclus ive attitudes among future educators, as it enhance d participants’ ability to understand the perspectives of multicultural studen ts through virtual body transformation and contribu ted to positive shifts in their attitudes toward diversity. These results, consistent with Stavroulia and Lanitis (2023) and Cho et al. (2015), highlight the potential of VR as  a powerful empathetic tool, offering  teachers a unique, perspective-taking experiential training. Extending prior research, this study integrated Gen AI technology with HMDs to provide pre-service teachers with a more interactive and immers ive learning environment, which is expected to lead to better learning outcomes in VR-based teacher training. Additionally, this study systematically analyzed through  interviews the mechanisms underlying pre-service teachers' changes in empathy and multicultural attitudes before and after the VR int ervention, offering valuable insights for designing  future VRbased teacher training programs. Our research findings can provide several education al implications. First, VR technology has demonstrated strong potential  in enhancing educators' perspective-taking and empathy skills. Through "multicultural student identity transformation ", it enables teachers to gain deeper insights into and support for multicultural and diverse student groups, making it a valuable tool for addressing real-world classroom challenges. Second, AI-powered VR environments can provide more realistic and context-specific interactive experiences, effectively boosting learner engagement and foster more authentic empathetic experiences. Third, High-quality HMD-based VR offers an immersive experience that effect ively enables first-person perspective-taking. This unique feature not only provides insights that trad itional training approaches cannot achieve but also  fosters empathy and enhances professional competence, enabling teachers to navigate diverse educational environments better. Finally, although th e finding suggests that VR has potential for teacher education, the results sh ould be viewed cautiously.  The small number of participants, short duration of the intervention, and relatively limite d impact suggest that further research is needed to c onfirm these results in more diverse and extended s ettings. Future studies could examine long-term effects, compare VR-based empathy training with other approaches, and assess the impact of design elements such as AI integration on learning outcomes.
