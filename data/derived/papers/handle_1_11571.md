# Exploring Teaching Methods in Education for Sustainable Development: Finnish Pre-service Teachers’ Perspectives on Sustainable Development and the Green Transition

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores pre-service teachers’ perceptions of teaching methods for sustainable development. Using focus group interviews, the research investigates holistic, pluralistic, and action-oriented approaches to Education for Sustainable Development (ESD). Thematic content analysis was used to analyze the data. Challenges include aligning sustainability topics with the curriculum, student fatigue, and generating relevant examples. Findings highlight critical thinking, hope, partnership with external partners, and integrating sustainable practices into everyday life.

Theoretical and methodological approach This study examines how pre-service teachers perceive Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) and its teaching methods, as well as how school-workplace partnerships in upper secondary education can be enhanced. The research explores how these partnerships are en visioned, what opportunities exist, and participant s’ familiarity with different collaboration possibilities. ESD teaching methods include pluralistic, holistic, and action-oriented approaches (Sinakou et al., 2019). Pluralistic pedagogy promotes critical thinking by exposing students to diverse perspectives on sustai nability, encouraging them to reassess their prior knowledge (Harness & Drossman, 2013; Rudsberg & Öhman, 2018). Holistic pedagogy addresses ecological, economic, a nd social sustainability, highlighting interconnect ions and conflicts while addressing the space and time horizons  (Berglund & Gericke, 201 6; Summers & Childs, 2007; Öhman, 2008). Action-oriented pedagogy focuses on practical engagement, such as recycling and community involvement, to raise awareness and address sustainability challenges (Sass et al., 2020; Sinakou et al., 2019). ESD goals, as outlined by the UN, UNESCO, WHO, and the European Union, focus on sustainability content, teaching methods, and values (Sinakou et a l., 2019; UNESCO, 2013). The Finnish National Curriculum emphasizes global citizenship, equality, and respec t for human rights (Opetushallitus, 2019). Recent r esearch shows that both in-service and pre-service teachers often focus on ecological sustainability while neglecting social aspects (Birdsall, 2014; Kilinc & Aydin, 2013). Furthermore, teachers show l imited interest in action-oriented ESD methods due to conflicts with traditional teach ing practices that prioritize individual performanc e over collective sustainability goals (Sinakou et al., 20 22). Research by Sinakou et al. (2023) identified two te acher profiles in Flemish schools: one group supports ESD  principles but does not implement them, while anot her actively uses ESD methods but is less enthusiastic about them. The research questions addressed in this study are: How do pre-service subject teachers describe teaching methods and challenges related to sustainability ed ucation and the green transition? What challenges e xist in implementing school-workplace partnerships in sustainability education?

## Methods

Data collection involved focus group interviews, allowing teachers to share and reflect on their ESD experiences (Barbour, 2007). The study included 74 pre-service subject teachers from the University of Oulu, Finland. A total of 18 recorded and pseudonymized focus  group interviews were conducted, each lasting about  10 minutes with 3-5 participants per group. Participants had diverse majors and minors. Most participants were women (75.7%) and in their fourth year of study (78%).  Focus group interviews provided valuable insights, as pre vious studies have mainly used surveys (Birdsall, 2014; Summers & Childs, 2007). Thematic content analysis (Braun & Clarke,

2006) was conducted using Nvivo software (QSR Internation al, 2023), identifying key themes related to ESD

(holistic, pluralistic, and action-oriented) and associated challenges.

## Findings

Pre-service teachers struggled to identify themes related to the green transition or sustainable development within their subjects. This challenge was particularly evi dent among students specializing in mathematics, vi sual arts, and music, who felt that sustainability themes didn’t naturally fit their disciplines. Pre-service subject teachers t ended to focus on ecological sustainability, which made it difficult to incorporate social and economic aspects into their teaching (Birdsall, 2014). Additionally, they expressed concerns about sustainability fatigue  among the students, as the themes were often repeated across differen t subjects.

Suggestions included diversifying the teaching of s ustainability topics to increase student motivation (Mulder et al., 2015).  Participants also noted that the green transition was not emphasized in teacher training, and the role of educators in promoting sustainability and linking school-workplace partnerships to sustainability topics posed a challenge due to a lack of practical examples and resources. In the holistic approach, the prominent theme among pre-service teachers was ecological sustainability, particularly sustainable consumption. Topics like s econd-hand shopping and resource use in clothing producti on were discussed. Social sustainability was also addr essed, with themes s uch as gender equality, community engagement, and anti-bullying initiatives. However, economic sustainability was not directly addressed, although certain elements were included in the discussions (Borg et al., 2014). In the pluralistic approach, critical thinking was emphasized in nine groups while critical literacy was highlighted in four.  Critical thinking was seen as essential for develop ing personal opinions on sustainability issues, helping students recognize the significance of sustainable living. The following quote is an example from Focus group R: I think it would be important to somehow give young  people the  opportunity to reflect and to reflect on things together with them (…). Supportin g them in their own reflection is, in my opinion, also important, so that we don’t just rush  through everything quickly but instead provide space for personal reflection and deeper consideration. In the action-oriented approach, pre-service teachers recommended school-workplace partnerships, suggesting partnerships with companies, universitie s, and museums  (see Figure 1). Practical examples were discussed as ways to integrate sustainability into daily life, such as promoting eco-friendly transportation, recycling, and reducing food waste. ## Discussion and conclusions

Pre-service teachers struggled to integrate sustainable development into subjects like art, music, and mathematics, with a focus mainly on ecological sustainability. M otivational issues and fatigue also emerged. Althou gh actionoriented practices like recy cling and collaborations with organizations were co mmon, school-workplace partnerships were underutilized due to  lack of  resources. The research calls for refining teacher education, promoting interdisciplinary collaboration, and crea ting practical models for facilitating real-world sustainability (Dhungana et al., 2021; Rauch et al., 2022).
