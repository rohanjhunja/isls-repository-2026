# Designing for Radical Imagination

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This dissertation examines how learning environments for K–12 teachers can be designed to foster radical imagination through speculative activities. Data were collected across four mathematics teacher education courses at a large U.S. university, and a core group of participants joined a collective inquiry group to explore how to create elements of their imagined futures in their present classrooms. Anticipated findings include design principles that promote radical imagination among teachers, the forms of collectivity that emerged within the inquiry group, and the focal points of teachers’ radical reimaginings of mathematics teaching and schooling. Goals and background The aim of the present study is to design and analyze various learning environments where K-12 mathematics teachers engage with radical imagination and collective futuring. Radical imagination is the act of crafting new possibilities for the future that are not constrained by systemic structures or the status-quo of the “possible.” Neoliberal logics typically locate the source and the solution of (educational) problems in the individual, and thus leave unexamined the structures and collective action needed to challenge systemic forces (Barker & Scheele, 2016). Radical imagination by collectives of teachers, and fostering that imagination through teacher education, intentionally endeavors to rewrite the dominant power structures that maintain the status quo of capitalist, white supremacist, and settler-colonial systems in education. This is of particular importance in mathematics teacher education, as the discipline of mathematics is viewed as politically neutral (Harouni, 2015), which ultimately serves to maintain hierarchies of power and underscores patriarchal ways of knowing (Hesse-Biber et al., 2004). Specifically, this research explores: (1) How can learning environments in mathematics education be designed to contribute to the development of teacher radical imagination, and (2) How can collectives of teachers act on their imagined future in the present? ## Study design and methods

This qualitative dissertation comprises of three studies with data collected in the mathematics teacher education department of a large U.S. university. Across the studies, 40 participants consented to include their data for writing prompts into four education courses to explore the content of teachers’ imagined futures. The participants’ written responses to the speculative prompts will be analyzed through thematic narrative analysis following the Study 2 is a design-based research study (DBR; The Design-Based Research Collective, 2003) surrounding the co-design of an elementary math methods course to foster radical imagination. Consistent with recent critical approaches in DBR (e.g. Bang & Vossoughi, 2016), I apply this theoretical and analytic approach to analyze how the learning environment enabled the expression of radical imagination by the prospective elementary teachers. I will analyze their speculative writing, course artifacts, classroom recordings, individual interviews, and co-design meetings with my faculty co-instructor attending to how their expressions enable or constrain the transformation of educational institutions. For Study 3, I facilitated collective inquiry groups for participants from the courses to collectively act on their imagined futures. Aligned with participatory methods (Bang & Vossoughi, 2016), the teachers were codesigners and researchers, shaping the aims of each session and leading their own speculative experiments to take up their futures in their classrooms and communities. The analysis of the video recording and artifact data from the sessions is one of the foci I will address during the consortium.

## Preliminary findings

Ongoing analysis of the speculative writing across the courses shows that teachers engaged directly with key issues they encounter in their daily work and imagined alternatives to constraining schooling practices. The teachers’ writing revised and expanded the meaning of school mathematics and challenged longstanding norms such as ability grouping, tracking, mathematics as apolitical, and the boundaries between school mathematics and youths’ lives and communities. In the design of the elementary math methods course, my co-designer and I have g rappled with design considerations together, memoing about the tensions the teachers face between imagining new practices and supporting youth within the current U.S. system. In the speculative workshops, the teachers have identified various areas of their daily practice in schools to implement their imagined futures, and the crossgrade, cross-subject collectivity emerging among them offers rich material for analysis and discussion. Expected contributions This dissertation will offer theoretical and practical contributions. Through the analysis of multiple learning environments where teachers engage in speculative writing, I will posit empirically-informed design principles to promote radical imagination among teachers. The aim is to inform practice in teacher education and contribute to the growing field of speculative pedagogies for and with teachers. Further, through the collective inquiry groups, my aim is to share considerations for participatory futuring by teachers towards enacting justice-driven practices in the present (within and/or beyond schools), and to highlight the collective nature of these futuring practices.
