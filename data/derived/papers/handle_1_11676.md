# Establishing Radical Spaces for Dismantling Scientific Objectivity in Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
It is necessary to engage in dialogue that deconstructs the normative structures and perspectives of Western Modern Science (WMS) and include the voices of non-white racialized groups and non-cis male genders. Design innovations should engage in speculative and justices-based methodologies to help learners radically reimagine STEMM. This paper proposes an emerging research design innovation that engages learners in reimagining the dominant perspective of WMS using syllabus co-constructions and action research cycles.

## Introduction

Diversity is necessary for scientific, creativity, discovery, and innovation, however Black, Indigenou s, and other non-white racialized groups and non-cis male genders remain underrepresented in STEMM (science, technology, engineering, mathematics and medicine/health scienc es). Intersectional racial and gender disparities are evident not only in the persistent underrepresentation of B lack, Indigenous, Latinx and female/non-binary students, but also in the underlying paradigms that structure research opportunities and approaches. Transdisciplinary scholarship has been advocated as  a route to more expansive research areas that can address the challenges of our times  (Takeuchi et al., 2020). While the act of breaking down barriers between disciplines may create an “open concept” Ivory Tower where social scientists collaborate with medical and natural scientists, the tower walls themselves still stand.  These tower walls, the very foundations of Western Modern Science (WMS), are built upon coloniality, antiblackness, white supremacy and capitalism, all of which have been especially detrimental to Black communities and Bla ck people (i.e., Ideland, 2018) as well as intersectional racialized and gendered communities. To create expa nsive and inclusive spaces for research it  is necessary that we engage with critical questions that consider: (a)What is the hegemonic history of science and how has it been structured around racial ideologies (b)Whose presence and perspectives have historically and currently dominate science, and how does this influence scientific res earch, communication, and engagement? (c) Whose presence and perspectives are made absent, erased, and/or appropriated by WMS and how? and (d) What are the speculative possibilities of radically reimagining STEMM?

Dismantling existing structures and advancing pluri versal possibilities (Leit ão, 2022) requires identifying and challenging oppressive structures and replacing them with ways of being and knowing in STEMM that exist but have not been attended to in the aca demy; ones that are more inclusive of diverse bodies, minds, (Schalk, 2018) and sociocultural paradigms. This would allow for a more expansive STEMM that co nsiders various viewpoints at all levels of research. Think ing with STEMM alongside critical, radical social s ciences theories creates a promising space for STEMM learne rs to develop a critical transdisciplinary lens (Strong et al.,

2016) that aims to shift the status quo of bodies and per spectives rarified in STEMM. As such learners would  be

positioned to address societal issues by challengin g normative systems, working across disciplines, va luing diverse perspectives, and posing new questions and projects towards inclusive and equitable futures. This paper presents an emerging approach that uses speculative and justice-based design in STEMM learning.  This approach “allows individuals and groups to imagine future configurations of [STEMM] and society within the world by bridging radical creativity and critical theory” (Bray & Harrington, 2021). With a pluriversal notion of transdisciplinarity to “create a world where ma ny worlds fit,” (Escobar, 2018, p. 16) these integrated methodologies would allow participants to recognize  oppressive systems while collectively imagining new liberatory systems.

## Research design

This research uses a justice-based design research (Costanza-Chock, 2018) and speculative approach and will be an iterative process centered around design ing a syllabus that reim agines the domain cultural norms in WMS. Speculative methodologies allow authors and readers to subvert historical realities and create new ones that allow readers to reconfigure and reimagine normative structures (Arada et al., 2023; Nxumalo & Ross, 2019; Toliver, 2022; Schalk, 2018). Using speculative fiction as a metho dological tool in STEMM can help learners negotiate the hegemonic histories of WMS and participate in a ctivism toward imagining alternative structures in STEMM that acknowledge the perspective of historically un represented groups. In order to engage learner s in deconstructing the dominant narratives of STEMM, we propose a co-constructive design innovation that engages students in syllabus co-construction in actions research cycles. The emerging methodological design model can be seen  in Figure 1. By engaging in sy llabus co-creation, that leverages the social-justice oriented syllabus movement, learners will be able to engage in interactive ac tivities that engage them in reimagine the speculat ive and transdisciplinarity possibilities of STEMM while building community. To  create our syllabus students and facilitators will participate action research cycles with iterative reading, dialogue, and reimaging activities in a salon-type setting. These cycles consist of four phases that will  emphasize non-linearity and reiterating with intention by attuning to learners’ curiosities and priorities: (1) Describing the problem of modern STEMM: Students read literature that addresses the colonia lity of STEMM and critical theory and discuss their  own experiences as well as that which they observe, (2) Reimagining STEMM: Students will engage with materials that consider transdisciplinarity, pluriversality, alongside speculative, creative and imaginative res ources and engage in a series of design exercises that allow t hem to re-envision STEMM, (3) Sharing the vision: Students will create a plan to enact their speculative vision for STEMM and share their vision and receive feedback from their engagements, and (4) Reflecting on vision and sharing and next steps: Students reflect on their engagements and together describe their visions of transdiscipl inarity, and return to and reconsider the syllabus and discuss revisions. By engaging in these actions research cycles, learners will be able to critically reflect on the normative practices of STEMM and co-create a syllabus that reflects their speculative i magination towards the creations of practices that acknowledges underrepresented communities. ## Conclusion

Having the space to radically rethink science allows learners to envisi on new solutions to existing societal challenges that range from their day-to-day experiences with race and STEM and more global challenges that affect the most marginalized humans and more-than-human beings. Furthermore, it positions learners as agents of transformation along with the tools and lenses t hat they will acquire during the speculative activi sm process. Learners will be attuned to thinking about and actin g upon normative systems and approaches that contribute  to inequity in STEMM and society more broadly as point s to dismantle and rebuild.   They will be able to use speculative/justice design-based thought and approaches to think beyond what is given in socioscientific problem solving as they progress through their undergraduate studies and careers.
