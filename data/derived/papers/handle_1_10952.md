## Introduction

Digital technologies permeate social, economic, and cultural relations, etc. In contemporary times and in schools, they can also be used in the administrative sector to teach practices in the classroom. The possibilit ies for pedagogical use have been explored and reflected on over the last few years (Raabe et al., 2020). However, remote teaching experience during the COVID-19 pandemic has shown the gulf between the various regions of the country and the possibilities for configuration based on experience (Gatti, 2020). Regarding learning assessment, reframing the evalua tion processes to suit the time and tools available during the pandemic was necessary. However, conside ring the traditional evaluation model through written tests and quizzes magnified the existing difficulties.

In this sense, this article presents the results of  the first two cycles of a research project that ai ms to investigate the elements that should be considered in the development of an assessment proposal for learning from a formative perspective in hybrid spaces based on the experiences lived in the contex t of and the post-pandemic associated with Covid-19, enabling the construction of an assessment fram ework for personalized learning – APRENDA.

Using a qualitative approach developed from Design-Based Research (DBR), it was possible to design and experiment with assessment paths with teachers from the state public school system, identifying th e limits and possibilities of using digital resources.

## Methodology

The study was developed from a qualitative perspective, as it "focuses on understanding phenomena by exploring them from the perspective of participants in a natu ral setting and relation to their context" (Sampier i, 2014, p. 358). The design used the Design Based Research-DBR (Collins et al., 2004). Given the specificities of DBR research, we have ta ken Scott, Wenderoth, and Doherty's (2020) guidelines for structuring the stages of research d evelopment as follows: a) Design-the phase of developing instructional tools based on theories of gain to solve a learning problem; b) Test-implementing the instructional tools in the classroom, continually revising as necessary. We, therefore, initially divided our research into three cycles. Given the ongoing research, this text presents the first two iterations. The third cycle, evaluation/reflection, will be carried out later. Four teachers from two high schools in the state of Pernambuco collaborated: two from Geography, one from Mathematics, and one from Portuguese Language. The choice of components from different areas was aimed at gaining an understanding of the digital resources available and the difficulties and possibilities in the different areas of the curriculum.

The cycles took place between May and July 2023 and were structured around face-to-face meetings and messaging apps. The activities were applied in five first-year high school classes. Data sources Design and test – May – June 2023 The teacher's selection includes those who proposed  assessment activities in their planning, given tha t the unit had started in mid-April 2023. In the proposal's construction and implementation: Portuguese Recife 1, Geography Recife 1, Geography Olinda, and Mathematics Olinda. They were chosen because they could In this iteration stage, we conducted the interviews, listened to the teachers, and conducted the training work based on technological, content, and pedagogical knowledg e-TPACK (Mishra  & Koehler (2006) added to the experimental knowledge, considering what was experi enced during the pandemic, building a proposal call ed CTPEC (De Araújo Santos &  Giraffa, 2023). We then compiled a pilot proposal for assess ment activities using Google Sites and Google Forms, which we presented remotely to the teachers. After defining the collaborators, identifying their perceptions and experiences in the field of assessment and technology, as well as drawing up the initial p roposal for pilot activities, we began the explorat ory phase for the subjects of Portuguese Language and Geography at the Recife School and Geography and Mathematics at the Olinda School during the second unit of the 2023 school year, in May and June. The activities were made available to students from  the first years A, B, C, and D of the Recife Schoo l and first B of the Olinda School.

The development of the activities considered the pr inciples of DBR in outlining theoretically oriented, interventionist, collaborative, responsive, and interactionist research with the possibility of generalization (Matta et al., 2015) with a view to similar contexts.

## Conclusion

In the context of contemporaneity, digital resource s and their potential for educational processes can not be ignored, especially after the remote teaching resul ting from the COVID-19 pandemic, which isolated people and imposed new forms of social organization, highlighting and aggravating social inequalities. In this study, we started from the experiences of teachers in the Pernambuco state school system to build a framework that would allow teachers to assess for learning, considering the resources and digital interfaces that are free and accessible to public school teachers. Our main objective, which is to in vestigate the elements that should be taken into account when developing a proposal for assessment for learning from a formative perspective in hybrid spaces, is being achieved when we jointly select, experiment with, and evaluate formative and evaluative paths that demonstrate how the digital can contribute significantly to carrying out assessment. It was possible to identify the difficulties in imp lementing a digital culture in-state public schools, but also the many possibilities and the protagonism of teachers and students in reframing their experience s and building ways to enhance learning with the support of digital resources, which only make sense with the effective action of the subjects involved.

Continuing the research will provide more substantial data on how to carry out assessments using digital interfaces and improve the planning of continuing education courses, considering the reality of the state system and serving as a basis for other schools that can identify elem ents that can be used and re-signified. The idea is that the resources will be available for free access, allowi ng teachers and schools to study the theoretical fr amework and adapt and remix the digital tools to their teaching and assessment processes.
