# Multimodal Analysis of Mathematical Statistics Learning in a CSCL Environment

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study, a multimodal analysis of learning data collected using a video recorder, high-resolution microphone, and dynamic geometry system, focused on pairs of university students engaged in a mathematical statistics task while collaboratively using HTML-based dynamic content. The qualitative analysis of transcribed text data f rom participants’ discourse was based on Sfard’s commognitive perspective. The results were then compared to the coded data of their behavior, the log data of their manipulating the dynamic content, and the audio data of their conversations. Our findings indicate that there is a relation ship between the transition of learners’ behavioral patterns and the emergence of refined discursive routines.

While mathematical thinking is grounded in the interplay between cerebral networks for language processing and those for visuospatial processing (Dehaene et al., 1999), it  emerges also from the mental simulation of dynamic imagery, which is supported by the interlocking network of the visual system and the motor system (Lakoff & Nunez, 2001). Therefore,  Multimodal Learning Analytics (MMLA) should be an indispensable approach to investigate the CSCL process in math. As indicated in a previous study (Schwarz et al., 2018), there might exist some “critical moments ” which are identified through the occurrence of certain types of verbal behaviors. Accordingly, this study investigates the verbal and nonverbal behaviors of several pairs of university students as they were engaged in a statistical task to uncover how the qualitative change in participants’ discourse is projected onto the temporal transition of their observed behavior.  To elucidat e the progress of learners’ thinking, we employed commognitive perspective (Sfard, 2008). During the first two intervals, participants mainly compared  the symbolic expression and dynamic content with utterances for target setting. In contrast, in the third interval, participants’ behavior moved between the manipulation of dynamic content and pointing to symbols with a new discursive routine involving symbolic expressions and the word “ mean value”. As seen in lower right, the quantity of both participants ’ utterances remarkably increased along with this change. Contrarily, in the former half of the fourth interval, a new discursive routine using the word “the ratio of the distance” related to the geometric configuration emerg ed while manipulation by participant A and writing by participant B continued. Then, in the latter half, this routine led to more refined one for certifying the effect of manipulating data points, such as “ The distance is maintained since the average moves in the same amount ” with the increase of utterance from A. A small amount of behavior and utterance indicates that the thinking remained in a germinal stage. In fact, in the last interval, a conclusive routine including “standard deviation in the denominator”, “covariance in the numerator”, and “reduction of fraction with the same factors”, together with pointing to the symbolic representations, appeared while the dominance of utterance largely shifted from A to B. Comparison with the data from other groups shows that  the emergence of reified discursive routines refrained participants from manipulating data points at an earlier stage as seen in lower left. Thus, the emergence of refined discursive routines is seen to be associated with a large temporal change in the quantity of utterances or the power balance and behavioral pattern. Although this observation gives insights into the specification of critical moments, the observed result is inconclusive because of small number of samples.
