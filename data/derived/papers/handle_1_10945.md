# Exploring the Effect of Gestures in the Learning of Spatial Abilities Based on Process Data in Educational Games

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
A quasi-experimental study was conducted to investigate the effect of gesture on students' spatial learning. 132 students were randomly assigned to the gesture condition and the non-gesture condition to learn cube folding and unfolding. Based on students’ behavioral data, we found that students in the non-gesture condition would check the tips at the beginning of game, whereas students in the gesture condition tended to try to solve the problem first before checking the tips.

## Introduction

Embodied cognitive theory considers that learning o ccurs when body movement, physical interaction and sensorimotor skills are associated with learning content (Anderson, 2003). Gesture scaffolding is one of the most commonly used embodied-based interventions. Several studies have demonstra ted that the use of gestures in the learning process can enhance students' memory, acad emic performance and knowledge application(Yohannan et al., 2022). However, existing research has mostly fo cused on the effects of using gestures on learning outcomes rather than on the learning process.  Log data in educational games record a large number  of details of students' learning process and can provide rich evidence for students' problem-solving process (Rowe et al., 2021). As a result, researchers have attempted to track the learning process recorded in log data to investigate learner behavior (Liu et al., 2023). Therefore, based on the learner behavior data from an educational game, Cube Elimination, this study attempts to explore whether there are differe nces in the behavior patterns of students who use g esture and those who do not use gesture by applying process mining method.

## Participants and materials

This study developed a digital educational game cal led Cube Elimination for mathematical geometry. The goal of the game is to help students construct spatial c oncepts, learn the process of cube folding and unfo lding, so as to understand the relationship between plane and 3D graphics and improve their mental folding ability. The participants consisted of 132 fourth-grade students in two classes at a primary school in China. The two classes were randomly assigned to two condition s: non-gesture condition and gesture condition. In the nongesture condition, students were able to rotate the 3D cube and watch a  dynamic animation of the transition between the 3D cube and the planar unfolding in Cube Elimination  (see Figure 1a). In the gesture condition, students were not only able to use Cube Elimination, but were also guided to use gestures to represent the transition process (see Figure 1b).  These gestures were closely related to the process of folding and unfolding the cubes. The teacher ensured that student's learning process included the participation of gestures. ## Data collection and analysis

Cube Elimination logged each action of each student as they interacted with the game environment. Each item in the game log data includes the student's ID, a time stamp, the type of action with descriptive characte ristics (e.g., start or select), and other specific information. The filtered actions are shown in Table 1 below. We used the Fuzzy Miner process model to analyze the learner behavior data recorded in the game. ## Results

Based on the behavioral data from the game, we plotted the process mining for two groups, as shown in Figure 2. In non-gesture condition, we can infer that learners always start a game level first and then check the introduction or tips. After viewing the tips, there is a certain probability that the learner will RESTART the level. After viewing introduction, the learner will start trying to solve the level's problem, accompanied by PAUSE and  CONTINUE behaviors. In gesture condition, we can infer that students read the INTRODUCTION aft er starting a game and continue to try to solve the problem. But unlike the non-gesture group, students don't seem to check the TIP at the beginning of the game. Instead, they seem to try to solve the problem first and then check out the answer if there are still difficulties.

## Discussion and conclusion

To explore the role of gesture in the learning of spat ial ability, we developed an educational game Cube Elimination, and logged the data of students' behaviors in the  game environment. The results of process mining showed that there were significant differences in t he behavioral patterns of students in two condition s. Students in the non-gesture group would check the tips for correct answ ers immediately after starting the game, while students in the gesture group tended to try to solv e the problem first. We suspect that the pause before checking the TIP may be the student using gestures to simulate the process of the cube folding and unfolding. The results shed light on the impact of gestures on the learning of spatial abilities. Folding and unfolding cubes is challenging to learn, and without proper scaffolding support, students are likely to give up the opportunity for learning in favor of checking the prompts directly. However, gestures can help students offload cognition onto their bodies, reinforcing the mental representation of 3D objects and thus supporting their learning process.
