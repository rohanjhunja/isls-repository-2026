# Understanding Students’ Motivation for Face-to-Face and Online Learning: A Self-Determination Theory-Based Comparison

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examined higher education students’ attitudes toward F2F learning vs. online learning using a Self-Determination Theory (SDT) framework. Overall, 113 undergraduates completed a 12-item SDT-based questionnaire twice, once for each learning format. Exploratory factor analysis supported the SDT framework. While students’ sense of competence and relatedness were higher for F2F learning, their sense of autonomy was higher for online learning. Specifically, the ability to focus was a barrier to benefit from learning online.

## Introduction

Although higher education has been using online lea rning for decades, only during the COVID-19 pandemic did almost all students worldwide experience it, raising the question about higher education learning format delivery, Face-to-Face (F2F) vs. online. Students’ learning format preferences may be shaped by the conditions for their motivation to learn  (Chiu, 2021). Self-Determination Theory (SDT) concerns the psychologic al conditions that promote motivation and well-being. SDT points to three basic needs, which are n ot dependent on culture: autonomy, competence, and relatedness (Ryan & Deci, 2019). In a previous study, we found that students’ learning format preferences directly relate to the three SDT needs (Shlomo & Rosenberg-Kima, 2024). Thus, this study aimed to quantitatively examine students’ attitudes to F2F learning vs. online learning using an SDT framework. Specifically, we were interested in quantitatively measuring the themes that emerged in our previous qualitative study (Shlomo & Rosenberg-Kima, 2024). Accordingly, the research question is: What are students’ attitudes to F2F vs. online learning from the lens of SDT?

## Participants

Overall, 113 (77 males, age mean = 18.4, SD=0.56) students volunteered to answer a questionnaire. The students were freshmen undergraduate students from Guangdong Technion, Isr ael Institute of Technology (GTIIT). The university ethics committee approved the research, and the students signed a consent form. Research tools To measure students’ attitudes toward a learning format, we developed the SDT-based learning format attitudes questionnaire, which is a 12 Likert-type items questionnaire that included themes related to the three basic needs presented in SDT: competence (5 items), autonomy (4 items), and relatedness (3 items). The students completed this questionnaire twice, once with respect to F2F and once with respect to online learning (see Table 1).

## Procedure

During the 2023 fall semester, students learned a higher education mechanics course. The course was taught F2F with the addition of asynchronous online recordings for the students to choose whether to watch them in addition to the F2F lessons. At the end of the semester, students learning with the first author completed the questionnaire.

## Results

Exploratory factor analysis of the SDT-based learning format attitudes questionnaire for online learning resulted in three components: competence (𝛼 = 0.852), autonomy (𝛼 = 0.843), and relatedness (𝛼 = 0.843). The item “Online learning format is good for me because it helps to understand” was found to be  associated not only to competence, but also to autonomy. The mean values, from high to low, of students’ sense of autonomy, relatedness, and competence in online learning were 4.09, 3.26, and 2.88, respectively (see Table 1 and Figure 1). E xploratory factor analysis  of the SDT-based learning format attitudes questionnaire for F2F learning resulted in two components, where competence and relatedness were categorized as one component. Nevertheless, a request for three components resu lted in three factors: competence (𝛼 = 0.889), autonomy (𝛼 = 0.896), and relatedness (𝛼 = 0.814). The item “F2F learning format is good for me because it helps to understand” was associated with autonomy and competence. The mean values, from high to low, of students’ sense of competence, relatedness, and autonomy in F2F learning were 4.09, 3.86, and 2.95, respectively (see Table 1 and Figure 1). A paired sampled T-test was conducted between the SDT factors in relation t o online and F2F learning. Students rated their sense of competence to learn F2F higher than online t(112)=12.260, and relatedness in learning F2F higher than in learning online, p<.001,  t(112)=4.996, p<.001. On the contrary, students rated their sense of autonomy in F2F learning lower than in online learning, t(112)=-8.256,p<.001 (see Figure 1). ## Discussion

The exploratory factor analysis of the SDT-Based Learning Format Attitudes Questionnaire for F2F learning revealed students’ sense of competence and their se nse of relatedness as one component. Research shows that people who pay attention to something while another indivi dual also watches it synchronously dedicate higher cognitive resources to the material  (Shteynberg & Apfelbaum, 2013). By requiring the factor analysis to reveal three components, the items in each factor were aligned with competence, relatedness, and autonomy. F2F learning was strongly associated with higher le vels of competence and relatedness, while online learning was linked to greater autonomy. Notably, t he results indicate that students often lack  the competence to focus, highlighting focus as a crucial skill for ef fective online learning. Developing and implementing strategies to improve students’ focus, such as self-regulation training and environmental adjustments, could significantly enhance their ability to benefit from all learning formats.
