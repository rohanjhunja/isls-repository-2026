# Examining the Development of Perspective-Taking Through Social Annotations in Collaborative Text Study

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Reading literature has been found to be positively related to perspective-taking. We studied perspective-taking through the use of social annotations of texts and asked how perspective-taking develops during collaborative text study, and which elements of social annotation support perspective-taking development? Specifically, we studied an undergraduate education philosophy course in order to gain insights about perspective-taking in online spaces, using a social annotation tool.

Literacy and perspective-taking Studies have shown that reading literature is positively related to perspective-taking (PT) (Ivey & Johnston, 2013) and that reading literary fiction, more specifically, led to higher levels of perspective-taking as opposed to other types of genres (Kidd & Castano, 2013). A literary text also provides resources for reading a text thr ough new voices. By doing so, the person who reads engages i n perspective-taking as she reads and uses their so cial imagination of the other as part of a conversation with one’s “self” (Lysaker et al., 2011). Thus, reading literature leads to increased perspective-taking among students who constructed meaning in their lives (Ivey & Jo hnston, 2013). A strong engagement with the text can be viewed as going beyond just “putting yourself in another person’s shoes” but rather “be” that other person.

One of the ways to teach students how to read through the perspective of the character is through dialogic pedagogy. In such settings, the teacher demonstrates authentic responses to text such as empathetic response and perspective-taking. This is then an invitation for the students to make similar connections in their reading process. Through this kind of modeling, the teacher is activ ely presenting his relationship to the text as seen  in facial expression or intonation and thus demonstrating how  one reads. This process includes creating a connec tion between the text and personal life experiences, exp ressing different feelings toward the characters, a nd taking their perspectives while reading (Lysaker et al., 2011). The benefits of reading may extend to other abilities such as understanding and sympathizing with others’ emotions or motivations. Kozak & Recchia (2019) refer to this set of abilities as social understanding and propose different classroom activities to promote them. For example, asking students to retell the story, or parts of a story, from the perspective of a different character, writing a letter to one of the characters, writing reading responses, or turning a novel into a play requiring the students to interpret the perspectives of the characters in this process. In this study, we chose to use social annotations as a prompt to do close-reading of the text as stude nts ask questions, make comments, or create a connection between the text and additional sources. Our goal was to use social digital annotation to facilitate interaction with the text both individually and collaboratively. Social digital annotations Annotations are usually informal and unstructured comments written in the margins of a text as one reads the text. They may include a question regarding the text, a c orrection, explanation, interpretation, and more (Johnsrud et al., 2017; Nielsen, 1984). A comment is one kind of text that is reactive, short, and asynchronous (Kalir & Garcia, 2021). Annotations can also be used to present an a greement or disagreement with the author, mark impo rtant information, or answer a question (Marshall, 1998).  In addition, annotations can include drawings or l inks to additional texts (Marshall, 1997). In this regard, annotations can be viewed as the reader's way of ta lking to or with the text. A text according to Kalir & Garcia (2021), has an author and is defined by its content as it communicates a message through different modes or styles. Kalir & Garcia (2021) demonstrate how previous interdisciplinary research has shown that annotation, whether written by hand or in a digital format, ass ists learners in thinking, reading, and writing, and can also motivate their learning, both in the K-12 and highe r education classrooms. Therefore, such online anno tation platforms increase student learning. However, annotation can help the learning process when it is supp orted by other activities. Today, annotations can be digital as part of online annotation systems which also enable users to share their annotations, making this process collaborative. By working collaboratively in online networked envi ronments, students are required to generate and share their own understandings. Such participation creates a dynamic knowledge-building process through social interaction, an important characteristic of computer-supported collaborative learning (Järvelä et al., 2004). Textual ICLS2022 Proceedings 1970 © ISLS computer-mediated communication can also establish productive argumentation as learners reread and revise their comments, both before and after they post them, all owing them to reflect on their work (Asterhan & Sch warz, 2016). Such reflectiveness can also promote critical thinking and perspective-taking (Tay et al., 2018). As a computer-mediated communication tool, the option of social annotations allows learners to collectively read and annotate different material online. Social digital annotations in educational assignments have been referred to by scholars as a type of learning arrangement called anchored discussion. This occurs when students “talk” with one another about a shared text through digital annotation. Other studies have shown that annotations enable a rich conversation requiring students to actively read the text and use the social annotations to elaborate their thoughts as well as learn from other perspectives through additional interpretations of the text (Kalir & Garcia, 2021). Higher-level discussions are characterized by mutual negotiations including theory-based postings and postings of new points or questions. Therefore, as students engage in high-level discussions, they are likely to show high levels of PT and this study aims to provide an online space that supports such learning outcomes (Järvelä & Häkkinen, 2002).

## Methods

We used Perusall, a platform designed to allow students to share an notations and sometimes respond to each other's comments while creating shared understanding. This social annotation tool allowed students to reflect upon their own reading process as well as their peers', making this a collaborative knowledge-creation experience. By analyzing changes in PT levels that occurred as a r esult of collaborative discussions in the form of s ocial annotations, we used qualitative methods to study PT on an individual and collective level. Although this study is still underway, it contributes to the literature on PT in humanities classes and teaches us how social annotations can be used to foster PT.
