# An Evidence-centered and Process-oriented Assessment of Computational Thinking for Learners with Autism

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Currently, there is a lack of established computational thinking (CT) assessment that is sensitive to the unique needs of learners with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). This paper proposed an evidence-centered, process-oriented CT competency model consisting of three constructs: computational analysis, computational practices, and dispositions. The model was pilot-tested with 61 data points collected from two adolescents with autism for up to 15 months. Principal component analysis was adopted to assess the structural validity of the proposed model. We then used correlation analysis to verify the convergent and discriminant validity. Preliminary results indicated that the empirical data generally supports the outlined structure of this theory-driven model and the orthogonality of the constructs.

## Introduction

Core characteristics  of (Autism Spectrum Disorder) ASD include persistent impairments in social interaction, communication, and executive function, and a pattern of repetitive behaviors. Those with ASD exhibit difficulties in reciprocal social interaction and problem-solving, which contribute to unemployment and a diminished quality of life (Van Heijst et al., 20 15). As such, it is essential to craft educational initiatives th at are sensitive to the unique needs of individuals with ASD, cultivating skills essential for autonomy and fuller participation in society. Recent research has proposed involving individuals with ASD in learning computational thin king (CT) (Barrett et al., 2022). CT, defined as an attitude and skill set involving analytical and algorithmic problem-solving inspired by  computer science  concepts (ISTE & CSTA, 2011; Selby & Woollard, 2013), is increasingly recognized as a critical 21st-century skill. Previous studies have shown that learners with ASD are able to learn CT skills with appropriate support (Barrett et al., 2022). CT activities, primarily project-based and design-based learning, include individuals with ASD in socially constructe d and negotiated environments, with rich opportunities for communication during problem-solving processes. While CT learning brings fruitful outcomes for individuals with autism, one major challenge is that there is a lack of established assessments for learners with special needs, who might have learned CT skills but struggle to manifest their thinking in a standardized way. Moreover, the essence of CT education lies in the active construction of knowledge through experiences, engaging learners in creative problem-solving and contextualized reasoning through iterative, hands-on projects (Papert, 1980). However, the conventional reliance on summative assessment fails to capture the dynamic and contextual nature of CT learning.

Recognizing these challenges, adopting an Evidence-Centered Design (ECD) approach in assessment development emerges as a viable solution, emphasizi ng understanding the evidence needed to support claims about learners' capabilities (Mislevy et al., 2003). This study aims to develop and pilot an in-situ, process-oriented assessment of CT learning that considers the specia l needs of learners with autism and capitalizes on the ECD approach to map out the progression of CT learning in contexts. Pilot study on the CT assessment model The CT model consists of  three constructs: computational analysis, computati onal practices, and dispositions (ISTE & CSTA, 2011; Selby & Woollard, 2013). Each construct comprises two to three competencies, evidenced by two to three observable behaviors (Table 1). Teachers could observe learners’ learning process es and encode the presence of task-related CT behaviors using a structured set of CT i ndicators. W e use the modifiers “independent” and “assisted” to signify whether cap tured CT behaviors or skills are initiated autonomo usly or with assistance for computational analysis and computational practice. Considering CT learning tasks might pose challenges to the attention, persistence, and communication of learners with ASD, we adopted three modifiers for the indicators of the disposition construct: strong, weak, and undesirable. This pilot study engaged 2 adolescents (19 and 16 y ears) with ASD in CT modules in the virtual world for 10 or 15 months, where they designed a human-like non-player character that can act as a dialogic agent i n a simulated scenario. The module features several design and block programming tasks. A total of 61-hour sessions were screen and audio recorded and coded using the competency model. Inter-rater reliability was.86.

## Results and Discussion

Descriptive statistics of all indicators across ses sions (n  = 61) showed that participants were observed to demonstrate algorithmic thinking most frequently as  they devised solutions to problems, whereas generalization was the least evidenced or observed one in the current data set. Principal component analysis (PCA) with varimax rotation revealed that KMO of sampling adequacy was.58. Barlett’s test of sphericity was significant (= 258.58, df = 105. p <. 001), suggesting the assumptions of PCA were largely satisfied. Correlations among the three main constructs were consistently low, indicating the de sired orthogonality of the constructs.  Correlations between constructs and sub-constructs were mostly high.

This study provided preliminary validation results, including convergence and discriminant validity. The result of PCA partially supports the proposed compe tency model, as it is generally aligned with the outlined structure. The correlation analysis largely verified the desir ed orthogonality of the sub-constructs, with three constructs significantly correlated with their sub-constructs but not with each other, and sub-constructs from different constructs being orthogonal to each other. The findings are consistent with previous researc h on CT models (ISTE & CSTA, 2011; Selby & Woollard, 2013). The main contribution of this study is  the evidencecentered, process-oriented assessment model that iteratively assesses CT for learners with ASD. The validation results suggest that the proposed evidence-centered competency model is a promising tool for a ssessing CT competencies. Identifying sub-constructs and task-level indicators can help teachers better understand what CT skills should be learned and assessed for learners with ASD, and guide the competency-oriented CT task design.
