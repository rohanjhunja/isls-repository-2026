# From Youth Participatory Art to Vision and Action: A Framework for Game-based Research-Creation

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This research explores the integration of Youth Participatory Action Research (YPAR) with game-based learning (Garcia,2020; Aleman,2023; Zhou et al., 2023) to enhance student engagement and collaboration in addressing polarised societal attention. Using a co-design methodology, students transition from passive subjects to active researchers, creating and interacting in a game-based learning environment that reflects contemporary issues such as structural oppression, privacy infringements, and environmental unsustainability. In the "Fall of Artica: A Way Back Home" (FoA) game, in the University of Toronto Schools’ Grade 11 visual art and Grade 7 English classes, students work in groups to tackle wicked problems in a novel dystopian narrative. Through collective inquiry in diverse social contexts, students develop new narratives and visual assets, foster collective inquiry and critical reflections in visual and literary art creations, and form a culture of community and practice. Through several rounds of enhancement of this game-based curriculum, we have articulated a YPAR framework that could potentially advance FoA and other serious game-based research regarding student engagement beyond participation in gameplay, co-design, co-contribution, and content creation.

Rationale Empowering Youth through Game-based Participatory Action Research offers a novel approach to engaging students actively in co-creating knowledge. This research explores the impa ct of game-based learning environments on critical engagement in collaboratio n that challenges their ability to confront polarizin g views and beliefs. The current cultural production landscape, marked by increased digital activism and the necessity for critical literacy (Amgott, 2018), sets the stage fo r this investigation. We also address the ethical dilemma of data ownership and its benefits in youth research (Brown  & Campione, 1996), exploring how games can facilit ate changes in power structures and foster collaborativ e, active participation in communicative action (Habermas,1990).

Theoretical perspective Youth Participatory Action Research (YPAR) embodies a transformative and collaborative approach where youth actively address complex societal issues, aligning closely with critical literacy and digital activism  (Amgott, 2018). With some roots in the broader methodology o f design-based research Brown (1992), YPAR fosters dynamic and participatory learning environments, interweaving the principles of critical pedagogy (Freire, 2020) to help prepare students for the challenges of the 21st century. It incorporates Gee’s (2005) concept of semiotic social spaces, adding depth to contemporary social-cultural contexts and power dynamics. We also seek to establish and scaffold a knowledge community (Raman. et al.,2021; Peters & Slotta, 2010), emphasizing collaborative, asynchronous learning through a dystopian game, Fall of Artica (Zhou et al., 2023).  Fall of Artica (FoA) values diversity in cultural backgrounds (Qui ntero, 2009; Sahni, 2001), advocating for a collect ive and participatory learning approach. Integrating insigh ts on cross-context curriculum design (Stille & Prasad, 2015; Souto-Manning, 2008) and drawing on Squire’s (2006) persp ective on game-based learning spaces, YPAR promotes active, critical, and creative engagement with pressing issues in their commu nities, equipping youth with resilience and the skills needed to navigate, respond to and even thrive in this complex, unsettl ing, and imperfect contemporary world.

## Research questions

1) How does a game-based youth participatory action research framework influence student engagement, critical

thinking, and collaboration in responding to system ic issues?  2) How does a game-based learning environment with a dystopian theme affect students’ awareness and inquiry of systemic problems in the real world? 3) How do a game-based narrative and interactions, such as collaborative storytelling and multimodal expression, contribute to students’ critical inquiry and resolution on social conflict and community dissensus?

## Methodology

The experiment connected Grade 7 (English) and Grade 11 (visual art)classes within a secondary school.  By codesigning with the teachers across the curriculum, youth contributed to a shared narrative content and game assets pool available to both teachers. Through co-design iterations that included gameplay sessions in both classes, we conducted online discussions, co-editings, in-person co-design meetings, and test plays among teachers, researchers, game designers, technologists, and med ia experts. At thi s point, students were not involved with formulating research questions, designing the exper iments, collecting and analyzing data, writing the research paper, and presenting the findings. However, they a re engaged as co-designers who help test-play the gam e, develop visual and text assets of the game, give cr itical reflection on the gameplay experiences and d ystopian contexts, and draw parallels to real-life issues. Data collection is centered around stu dent interactions within the FoA game environment. When submitting this paper, students discuss data ownership, copyrights of their creative products, remixes, and open-source culture from invited expert guest speakers. The focus is on their decision-making processes, collaborative efforts, and how th ey engage with the game narrative and mechanics. Methods include observations, interviews, and audio and vid eo recordings of focus group discussions to capture  students' nuanced experiences as they navigate the social challenges and dystopian themes situated in the game. Consistent with Sahni’s (2001) approach to literacy and learni ng, the students' unique perspectives are revealed while FoA stories unfold. A critical aspect of this methodolo gy is the students' creative input. Each group deve lops stories and game assets, such as resource cards, story cards, and environmental drawings, illustrating the social problems within their assigned game settings. These creative  outputs serve as discussion themes and become lear ning materials for their peers and future partici pants in the FoA game. This practice opens up new o pportunities for students to participate in co-designing the game and becoming researchers who are  empowered to shape community practices. Echoing Stille and Prasad’s (2 015) emphasis on creative, multimodal works in educ ation, This study explores how a dystopian game can engage  youth in learning about critical literacy and responding to pressing issues of their time through processes of storytelling and critical making. This transformati ve approach aims to explore the effectiveness of a game-based learning community in fostering critical thinking, collaboration, and meaning-making through addressing social and environmental issues.

## Findings and discussion

This educational project significantly shifts from traditional approaches to a more empowering and engaging form of storytelling and problem creation.  Placing stud ents at the center of a narrative where they become  heroes revolutionizes their educational experience and influences their perspective on life. Utilizing a game-based environment to foster critical thinking, democratic  engagement, and social responsibility prepares stu dents to confront and actively shape the evolving world. This initiative is a dynamic, ongoing effort, with plans to further develop a mixed-reality tabletop game prototype in 2024 in collaboration with the visual art teacher and teachers from other disciplines within the same school (e.g., English, social studies). In the game-based component, students engaged in collaboration and a deeper understanding of real-life systemic problems embedded into the game settings. This was particularly evident in their conversation with AIpowered NPCs and their strategic navigation of the game mechanics. Through the art-based component, students developed critical literacies using creative storyt elling and multimodal expression, reflecting on and  articulating responses to social injustices and power imbalances. The effectiveness of  game-based learning in fostering a participatory and collaborative environment support s the hypothesis that such methods candisrupt trad itional classroom power dynamics, aligning with the ideas of Squire (2006). Figure 2 A Framework for Game-based Youth Participatory Action Research The framework (see Figure 2.) includes eight elements: ● Participation: Engage in gameplay that centers on y outh-led critical action. This approach encourages a blend of fun and creativity in tackling community needs. ● Partnership: Create a diverse team of players, each bringing different backgrounds and skills. ● Inquiry: Seek resources and information, contributing to the community knowledge base. ● Problematization: Identify and discuss issues withi n community practices. ● Co-design: Work alongside youth to formulate research proposals, treating them as equal contributors. Ensure that the research topics and methodologies are relevant to and centered on the youth. ● Documentation: Chronicle the research journey and gather data. Keep a systematic record of the research activities, including data collection methods that mirror community practices. ● Analysis: Make sense of the gathered data. Analyzing it in a way that aligns with Design-Based Research (DBR) and Youth Participatory Action Research (YPAR) standards. ● Reflection: Disseminate findings and influence comm unity practice. Publish results embody the research's cooperative and participatory essence, e mphasizing continuous learning and refining evidence-based practices.

Research suggests that a game-based and youth participatory action research frame work can be a substantial guideline for our next stage of game development and enactment of YPAR with students at the University  of Toronto School, offering hands-on research experiences and empowering students to take ownersh ip and governance of their own data. These methodologies promote digital citizenship and new media literacies that help students build competencies, partnerships, and reso urces in responding to contemporar y challenges and an uncertain future. The study provides a foundation f or future research in this area, particularly in ex amining the long-term effects of such educational interventions on sustaining student learning and engagement.
