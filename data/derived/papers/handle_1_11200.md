# Constructing an Action Model for Educational Systems in Response to Pandemics: A Textual Grounded Analysis

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The outbreak of Covid-19 has presented unprecedented challenges to educational systems, forcing educational institutions to adopt innovative approaches to maintain the continuity of student education. While many educational authorities worldwide have published survey reports and policy guidelines, offering practical guidelines and recommendations for pandemic response, educational systems have not established a reliable action model in practice. To bridge this gap, this study employed grounded theory to analyses 12 policy documents from UNESCO official website, aiming to extract core measures for educational systems in response to pandemics and then construct a Three-factor Action Model. The action model revealed four core measures, including promoting the use of information technology, optimizing service delivery, improving school response mechanisms, and establishing collaborative communities. This action model not only has practical guidance implications but also provides a solid theoretical foundation for the resilience and sustainable development of educational systems.

## Introduction

Covid-19's outbreak has severely impacted global society and education, triggering e conomic instability, social unrest, and significant educational disruptions. No tably, it forced the closure of schools in 194 coun tries, impacting nearly 1.6 billion students or 91.3% of t he global student population (Mo et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023; UNESCO, 2022). Despite these challenges, the necessity for continued learning persisted, with many institutions lacking effective response models. With the pandemi c waning and educational systems resuming norm alcy, lessons from the past three years are crucial for future crises (Wang et al., 2024). Scholarly efforts have aimed to guide educational s trategies, including governmental frameworks for pandemic responses (Gouëdard & Viennet, 2020) and a daptations in medical education (Newman & Lattouf, 2020). However, the application of UNESCO's guideli nes in educational systems remains underexplored. Our study employs Grounded Theory to assess UNESCO's po licies, aiming to understand and address the needs of education stakeholders in pandemic contexts. This r esearch seeks to establish a comprehensive action  model for educational systems worldwide to navigate future in fectious disease crises effectively, ensuring educa tional continuity and responsibility.

## Research method

Grounded Theory is a qualitative research method in troduced by Glaser and Strauss, which emphasizes th e construction of theory based on empirical materials  (Khan, 20 14). Researchers, after systematically collecting data, aim to identify concept categories that reflect the essence of the subject's interpretations of their speech and behavior. These concept categories are then used to  construct relevant theories by examining the conne ctions between them (Charmaz, 2006). In this process, it i s crucial to refine core concepts, and researchers must be theory-sensitive, paying attention to emerging data, extracting conceptual categories and theories hidde n within the rich data. This approach advocates not preconce iving a position but rather entering the research f ield with research questions, delving deeply into the subject  area, and systematically descri bing and reflecting on the research phenomena, extracting theories that can be used to address the dual challenges. Constructing an action model for educational system s in response to pandemics requires referencing a substantial amount of empirical material to help re searchers identify the critical steps and measures taken by educational systems when dealing with pandemic outb reaks, such as risk communication, social distancin g policies, and distance education. Additionally, in the face of a pandemic, the response of educational  systems involves multiple variables and stakeholders, including students, teachers, parents, and government agencies. This diversity and complexity are challenging to compreh ensively capture with quantitative data because the y often involve unstructured factors and interpersonal rela tionships. Therefore, in this study, we employ this  research method and utilize Nvivo 12 qualitative analysis software to systematically code and analyze the relevant policy texts from UNESCO.

Data sources Using policy texts as a point of entry for studying issues in public policy and public management is both logically sound and operationally feasible, as it can provide rich and reliable empirical data for policy process analysis. The policy text data used in this study is sourced from  the official website of UNESCO. We conducted an in itial selection of policy texts and their interpretive te xts from UNESCO's "UNESCO's education response to C ovid19" column, resulting in a total of 12 policy artic les. Of these, two are region-specific, and the remaining ten are global in scope. Since this study focuses on the me asures taken by various countries' educational syst ems in response to pandemics, policy documents unrelated t o this topic were excluded to ensure the t hematic relevance of the policy texts.

The coding process This study follows the relevant processes of Ground ed Theory, analyzing policy text data through open coding, core coding, and selective coding, extracting and s ummarizing the core measures taken by education sys tems in response to the pandemic, and subsequently abstracting to generate a systematic action model. Open coding is the first phase. During open coding,  researchers maintain an open attitude, examining, breaking down, and coding policy text word by word.  We strive to use the policy's original wording for con cept naming and then compare and merge duplicate or homo geneous concepts, achieving the conceptualization a nd categorization of the raw data. In this study, text  material that aligns with the research top ic and expresses actionable measures in the policy text is considered as valid original statements. After removing irrelevant content related to the research topic, the conceptual content of the original statements with high relevance to the research content is further identified, resulting in the extraction of 52 initial concepts and 14 initial categories, representing the overall measures taken by the education system in response to the pandemic. Core Coding is the second stage of the Grounded Theory method, which involves deducing higher-level core categories based on the logical and classifica tory relationships among the initial categories. In  this study, considering the broader context of C ovid-19 and the collaborative relationships among variou s stakeholders in the education system, the 14 initial categories obt ained from open coding were thoroughly compared and  deeply analyzed. This process resulted in the further refinement of their main content into four core categories. Figure 1 Action Model with Three Factors Selective coding is the third phase of the Grounded Theory method, which involves a further refinement and synthesis of the logical relationships between core categories. It aims to describe the framework architecture of these categories in a "storyline" that reflects the overall context. Through a meticulous analysis of open coding and axial coding, and a comparative examination of conceptual categories and their relationships, this  study established "The Action Model of Educational System s in Response to Pandemics" as the core category. Under the coordinated interaction of four factors: Promot ing information technology deployment, Optimizing s ervice delivery, Improving school response mechanisms, and  Establishing a collaborative community, it constru cts the action model for educational systems in response to pandemics (Fig. 1). Explanation of the action model for the education system The three-tier coding analysis results based on grounded theo ry suggest that "Promote information technology deployment," "Optimize service delivery," "Improve school response mechanisms," and "Establish a collaborative community" are the core measures for  the education system's response to pandemics. Build ing upon this, this study establishes the "Technological Empowerment-Environmental Support-Organizational Construction " Three-factor Action Model for the education system's response to pandemics. Technical empowerment: Deployment of educational infrastructure and literacy cultivation The outbreak of Covid-19 has severely impacted the normal operation of sc hools and classroom teaching worldwide. With widespread school closures, student s face the risk of learning disruptions (Rundle et al., 2020). To ensure students' continued learning during the p andemic, education systems worldwide have strongly promoted online education. Online education is a ne w educational approach that utilizes modern informa tion technologies such as the internet and artificial intelligence to facilitate teaching. It has innovated the organization, teaching methods, and service models of education, establishing a new educational ecosystem in the dig ital age. The implementation of online education requires two factors: technical support and the enhancement of technical literacy.

The enhancement of technical literacy in online education involves training both educators and students in information literacy. Firstly, educators need to possess a high level of information literacy to effectively conduct online teaching. Online educators must search for and evaluate online educational resources to ensure the accuracy and quality of content (Green, 2020). Secondly, students' information literacy is crucial for their success in online learning. High-level information literacy enables students to actively participate in online classes, ask questions, share insights, and interact with classmates, thereby promoting academic interactions and knowledge sharing. Environmental support: Health service provision and financial assurance The outbreak of the C ovid-19 pandemic brought about significant changes in th e educational environment, compelling the education system to optimize its service delivery. Faced with a major public health crisis, schools need to implement corresponding hygiene measures to  ensure the physical health of both teachers and students. They must also provide psychological counseling to help alleviate the stress and pressure on students and staff and offer necessary economic assistance to regions,  schools, teachers, and families facing financial difficulties due to the pandemic. Additionally, during this unique period, there has been an exacerbation of resource allocation disparities. Therefore, the education system must act as a bridge to bridge these gaps to the greatest extent possible. During a pandemic, the education system needs to pr ovide a range of health services and psychological support to address the impact of crisis situations (Kang, 2021). Firstly, the provision of health services is crucial. This includes ensuring the cleanliness and hygiene of school facilities, providing handwashing facilit ies, disinfectants, and protective equipment. In addition to health services, psychological support is equally important. The pandemic has had a negative impact on the mental health of both teachers and students, leading to issues such as anxiety, loneliness, and stress. Therefore, providing mental health services and counseling support, along with necessary moral education, is essential (Chen et al., 2023). Organizational construction: School response mechanisms and educational collaborative communities During the pandemic, the fluctuating nature of the situation has increased the complexity of resuming school activities. Therefore, the education system needs to make adjustments to the internal organizational management of schools to address the challenging circumstances. This means that schools need to enhance their res ponse mechanisms, including adjusting school development policies and providing professional training for te achers. Additionally, individual capabilities are limited, so in order to navigat e this special period more effectively, the education system needs to establish collaborative c ommunities. This includes collaboration between hom e and school, community collaboration in nurturing students, as well as international educational alliances. During significant public health crises, the education system must recognize the limitations of individual efforts and urgently establish collaborative commun ities to address the constantly changing educationa l needs. This initiative includes promoting collaboration between home and school, community involvement in nurturing students, and the establishment of international ed ucational alliances. First and foremost, the collaboration between home and school becomes particularly crucial. Families need to actively engage in their students' learning process, providing support and supervision. Additionally, community involvement can offer additional resources and support, such as mental health services, learni ng resources, and encouragement for students to par ticipate in social activities. Secondly, international educatio nal alliances can provide schools with broader resources and experiences. In the context of globalization, the e ducation system can share best practices and educat ional resources by partnering with international counterparts.

## Conclusion and implications

This study utilized policy texts from UNESCO as the  primary data source and employed a qualitative res earch approach based on Grounded Theory. It constructed a n action model for the education system in response  to pandemics and conducted an in-depth analysis and interpretation of this model. Af ter completing open coding, core coding, and selective coding, the study extrac ted the core measures that education systems should  adopt in response to pandemics. These measures include Promot ing information technology deployment, Optimizing service delivery, Improv ing school response mechanisms, Establish a collaborati ve community. Through the coordinated interaction of these four core factors,  an action model for the education system in respon se to pandemics was developed. Additionally, this paper c ontinued to clarify the internal relationships among the four factors and provided a specific description of this  action model in terms of enabling technology, envi ronmental support, and organizational construction, elucidating the profound content of each core mea sure. Most importantly, this research provides a valuable theo retical foundation for the resilience and sustainab le development of the education system, contributing t o the establishment of a more robust education syst em to address various challenges in the future and safeguard the overall well-being of students.
