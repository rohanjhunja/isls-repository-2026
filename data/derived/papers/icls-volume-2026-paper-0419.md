# Emergent Structures of Thematic Change Across Decades of Learning Sciences Research 1996 -2025

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This proof-of-concept study applied complex systems framing and network analysis to trace the thematic evolution of the learning sciences across three decades of International categories. Network maps reveal the field's trajectory to an increasingly integrated intellectual ecosystem. Equity considerations may have shifted from peripheral to central, while collaborative structures may have become formally theorized.

Complex systems theory offers a paradigm (Kaplan & Garner, 2020) for understanding how scholarly fields evolve. Academic communities function as complex adaptive systems where self-organization, emergence, and phase transitions (Jacobson, 2020) drive devel opment. Individual ideas, analytical approaches, and researchers act as agents whose localized investigations collectively generate emergent thematic structures at the field level, shifting over time from fragmented to integrated configurations as communities develop shared frameworks (Wenger et al., 2002; Wenger-Trayner & Wenger-Trayner, 2015). This perspective is fitting for studying the learning sciences, a field that has embraced complexity as a theoretical commitment (Jacobson & Wilensky, 2022). Network analysis provides a methodological bridge between individual research outputs and field-level patterns (Froehlich et al., 2020), revealing how isolated topics cluster into coherent communities, how peripheral concerns migrate to the center, and how parad igm shifts materialize as structural reorganizations (Fortunato, 2010). This pilot study applies these methods to trace the learning sciences’ thematic evolution across three diversity, innovation, pedagogies, and policy.

177 papers was analyzed using a hybrid human –AI coding approach. Each paper was processed by a large language model guided by a custom-developed a priori codebook. The five target categories were selected to capture dimensions consistently emphasized across multiple editions of the Cambridge Handbook of the Learning Sciences (Sawyer, 2006, 2014, 2022) and the International Handbook of the Learning Sciences  (Fischer et al., 2018). This AI-assisted process served as an analytic scaffold for the first author, who then conducted manual coding in MAXQDA with human final determinations for all decisions. Both authors met regularly to discuss coding issues, and all final decisions were grounded in consensus. In total, 3,537 coded segments were identified, and the absence of a theme in a paper was explicitly coded as not found. The dataset was aggregated into three periods, with the Early period (1996–2008) containing 54 documents, the Middle period (2010–2018) containing 53 documents, and the Recent period (2020– 2025) containing 70 documents. 2008) captures a field establishing its intellectual foundations through distinct lines of inquiry. The green squares

cluster centers on powerful learning approaches such as game-based learning, role playing, collaborative learning, and making, designing, creating. These pedagogies connect with early social-context codes like socioeconomic status, special education needs, and underrepresented minorities, with learner agency acting as a structural hub linking experimentation in pedagogy with early attention to empowerment. Equity appears here not as a standalone topic but as a subtle thread within broader pedagogical innovation, revealing the field’s initial awareness of learning’s social dimensions. The purple triangles cluster focuses on observable instructional routines (quizzes, tests, short lectures, discussion, reflection, and peer learning) linked to emerging technologies, 2686 reflecting an early phase where digital tools were used to refine rather than redefine instruction. Its narrow scope but strong internal coherence reflects how technology was initially viewed as an enhancement to existing practices. The blue circles cluster includes project-based learning and inquiry learning alongside several “null” nodes (no policy mentioned, no collaboration mentioned, and no DEI mentioned). While pedagogies like projectbased learning and inquiry learning often involve collaboration in practice, the co-occurrence of these “null” codes suggests that explicit theorizing of collaboration as a construct and the situating of pedagogical work within systemic or policy contexts had not yet become common in the field’s published discourse. The middle period (2010– 2018) shows growing intellectual cohesion. The yellow diamonds cluster unites design thinking, project-based learning, making and creating, co-design, and community of learners, often intersecting with emerging technologies. This pa ttern marks a conceptual shift where learners are framed as designers and co-creators of knowledge, establishing constructionism and design-based research as core methodological identities of the learning sciences. The pink squares cluster represents the m ost significant structural transition of this period. Eighteen interlinked nodes connect play, inquiry learning, learning by doing, and scaffolding with injustice and inequity, underrepresented minorities, socioeconomic status, and educational policy analysis. The tight correlation between pedagogical and equity nodes demonstrates that equity moved from emergence of collaborative infrastructures linking classroom learning to institutional reform. The light blue circles cluster shows former “null” codes now coexisting with professional learning communities, community partnerships, research-practice partnerships, and critical pedagogy. This configur ation suggests a self-aware turn where researchers began placing collaboration and critical awareness as integral components of learning, with learner agency shifting from individual attribute to collectively mediated process. By 2020– 2025, the network achieves high density and complexity, reflecting both maturity and specialization. The dark green circles cluster becomes the structural core, with twenty-three interconnected nodes unifying collaborative learning, co-design, design thinking, and game-based learning with equity dimensions (cultural diversity, underrepresented minorities, special education needs, socioeconomic status) and community structures (community partnerships, parental involvement, professional learning communities). The inclusion of systemic change initiatives and critical pedagogy underscores a broadened understanding of learning as a distributed process embedded in social and institutional systems. The lavender diamond cluster interweaves emerging technologies and disruptive innovations with creative pedagogies like tinkering, remix, play, and interest-based learning, while inclusion, accessibility, and reflection appear as structural anchors, suggesting a paradigm where innovation is defined by participation and justice. The maroon squares cluster groups short lecture, drill and practice, modeling, and curriculum development with “null” categories, reflecting a “big tent” tradition that welcomes conventional research lines even as the field’s core evolves to ward situated, equity-oriented inquiry.

This proof-of-concept pilot study offers a preliminary view of the learning sciences’ developmental trajectory as alongside frames from multiple editions of handbooks in the learning sciences (e.g., Fischer et al., 2018; Sawyer, 2006, 2014, 2022). Based on analysis limited to five thematic categories, our network maps suggest a potential pathway from fragmented pedagogical explorations toward a more integrated field, where equity and community considerations become more centrally interwoven with pedagogical and innovative themes over time. The early years suggest that inventive pedagogies developed in relative isolation. The middle period appears to have integrated design, equity, and systems thinking, consistent with works such as Esmonde and Booker’s (2017) Power and Privilege in the Learning Sciences. The most recent network represents a tentatively interwoven ecology of pedagogical, cultural, and systemic dimensions. The apparent disappearance of “null” clusters in the later periods tentatively suggests growing self-reflexivity, but this trend requires validation against the full dataset. The primary implication of this work is methodological. While hybrid human-AI coding approaches are becoming more common across educational research, this study extends that growing body of work by applying such methods specifically to map the structural evolution of a field’s intellectual trajectory through network A fuller analysis of over 5,300 ICLS papers will require significant reliance on AI-driven methods, and a broader analytical frame grounded in complex systems theory, drawing on concepts such as phase transitions and adaptive dynamics, is needed for more confident claims. The next phase will apply this coding methodology to the enti re rather than through lexical-based approaches, to yield more robust findings about the evolution of the learning sciences.
