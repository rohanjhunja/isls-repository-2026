# Effects and Mechanisms of Learning Analytics-Supported Reflective Assessment in Promoting KB Competences and Data Science Competences among Preservice Teachers

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Developing data-literate citizens who can effectively use data is a critical educational goal. Knowledge Building (KB) offers a promising approach, as it engages PSTs in reflective, inquiry-driven learning supported by data. Building on prior research, this study examined the effects and mechanisms of learning analytics-supported reflective assessment (LAsRA) in enhancing preservice teachers’ (PSTs) KB and data science competences. Using a quasi-experimental design, the experimental class received LAsRA within a KB environment supported by KBDeX. Multi-methodological analyses —including content analysis, lag sequential analysis, and epistemic network analysis —showed that LAsRA significantly improved PSTs’ higher-order conceptual development, epistemic engagement, and data science skills. Mechanism analysis revealed that LAsRA fosters deeper KB and data-informed decision-making by strengthening links between metacognition and advanced discourse behaviors. These findings provide both theoretical and practical guidance for integrating learning analytics and reflective assessment in teacher education.

In today’s increasingly digital world, data science competences are essential for everyone, including preservice teachers (PSTs), who will shape the next generation of learners (Bergstrom & West, 2017; Grawe, 2011). PSTs’ ability to collect, analyze, and use data collaboratively underpins their professional development and equips them to integrate data-driven approaches into future classrooms (Hardy et al., 2020; Rubin, 2020; Wise, 2019; Yang et al., 2023). With data science competence, PSTs can leverage learning analytics, educational data mining and other approaches to transform multi-source data, such as classroom behaviors and academic assessments into evidence for instructional decision-making, thereby achieving the transformation from experience-based teaching to datadriven precision teaching and evidence-based education. Therefore, developing data science competences among PSTs is not only a technical requirement but also a pedagogical necessity that supports effective teaching practice. Data science competences encompass skills in data collection, management, analysis, visualization, and evidencebased decision-making, as well as awareness of data ethics.. Despite their importance, these skills are often underdeveloped among PSTs due to limited exposure to data use during preservice training and the complexity of teaching computational skills to diverse learners (Merk et al., 2020; Schwab-McCoy et al., 2021). A promising strategy for addressing these challenges is engaging PSTs in knowledge building (KB), a collaborative inquiry approach emphasizing collective responsibility for real-world problem solving and continuous improvement of community knowledge through data-supported assessment (Scardamalia, 2002; Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2014; Yang et al., 2023). Effective KB requires PSTs to collect data, engage in dialogue, reflect on evidence to identify knowledge gaps, and make data-driven decisions to guide furthe r inquiry. Consequently, scaffolding strategies and supportive tools are needed to help PSTs develop both KB competences and data science skills. Learning analytics-supported reflective assessment (LAsRA) has emerged as a potential solution, as it can foster inquiry engagement, support iterative knowledge construction, and enable data-informed reflection (Yang, van Aalst et al., 2020; Yang, Chan et al., 2024). Data science education, a growing interdisciplinary field combining statistics, computer science, and communication (Satyanarayana, 2013; Cleveland, 2014), aims to equip learners with the skills to reason with data, including computational techniques, basi c statistics, data visualization, and communication of results (Baumer, 2015; Hardin et al., 2015; Broatch et al., 2019; Lazar & Franklin, 2015). Data science competences in this study refer to learners ’ abilities to interpret, analyze, and use data to sup port reasoning and decision-making within inquiry processes, which are essential for supporting teaching practices such as student assessment and instructional adaptation. Integrating data science into teacher education is challenging. Data science is not widely recognized as an independent educational field, and teacher preparation programs often provide limited training 1018 in data usage (Finzer, 2013; Hazzan & Mike, 2023; Merk et al., 2020). PSTs face particular difficulties, including heterogeneous prior knowledge and the need to tailor instruction to diverse learners (Heinemann et al., 2018; Schwab-McCoy et al., 2021). Whi le some initiatives have successfully integrated data science into K-12 and higher education contexts (Rosenthal & Chung, 2020; Heinemann et al., 2018), little research has focused on PSTs, leaving gaps in how to teach, assess, and develop data science competences in this population. KB emphasizes not only individual knowledge acquisition but also collective knowledge refinement, innovation, and advancement (Bereiter, 2002; Bereiter et al., 1997). Core KB principles —epistemic agency, reflective assessment, collective responsibility for  idea improvement, and rise-above thinking—support the development of both KB and data science competences in PSTs (Scardamalia, 2002; Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2003). Knowledge Forum®, a computer-supported collaborative-learning environment, operationalizes  these principles by providing shared spaces for PSTs to co-construct multimedia notes, contribute questions and theories, and build on each other’s thinking (Chen, 2017; Oshima et al., 2012). Analytical tools, including KBDeX, enable visualization of discourse and support reflective assessment, helping learners identify gaps, evaluate progress, and iteratively improve knowledge.  Fostering PSTs’ collaborative inquiry in the KF environment candevelop their KB competences, particularly conceptual development and epistemic engagement. Learning analytics-supported reflective assessment (LAsRA) refers to an instructional approach that integrates learning analytics tools with structured reflective activities to support learners in monitoring, evaluating, and improving their inquiry processes. It allows learners to monitor and regulate their learning based on evidence and outcomes, fostering higher-order competences such as inquiry, knowledge creation, and autonomy (Lei & Chan, 2018; Yang, Chan, Zhu et al., 2024; Yang, Feng et al., 2024). Learning analytics involves measuring, collecting, analyzing, and reporting learner-related data to improve learning environments (Siemens, 2013). In KB contexts, analytics-supported reflective assessment has been shown to support productive knowledge construction and development of data science competences across educational levels (Yang et al., 2023; Yang, van Aalst et al., 2020).

Although LAsRA shows promise for enhancing KB competences and data science skills, few studies have examined its effects and underlying mechanisms among PST s. This study addresses this gap by implementing a KB environment enhanced with LAsRA to explore how such scaffolding affects PSTs’ data science competences and KB competences. Using KBDeX as a visual and analytical tool, the study investigates how reflective assessment supported by learning analytics fosters collective knowledge creation, evidence-based decision-making, and iterative improvement, ultimately informing both teacher education and the integration of data-driven approaches into classrooms. This study addressed three research questions: RQ1: Do undergraduates in the KB design augmented by LAsRA improve more in collective knowledge building than preservice teachers in a traditional KB environment? RQ2: To what extent has LAsRA improved the KB competences and data science competences of PSTs? RQ3: How can LAsRA help PSTs improve their KB competences and data science competences?

## Research context and participants

This study was conducted at a teacher education university in central China using a quasi-experimental design. Two undergraduate classes of teacher training majors participated, totaling 100 PSTs. The experimental class (N = 53) engaged in a KB environment augmented with learning analytics-supported reflective assessment (LAsRA) using KBDeX, while the comparison class (N = 47) studied in a regular KB environment without LAsRA. Both classes followed the same 4-month general education core course on scientific inquiry and knowledge creation, meeting twice weekly for 1.5 hours. The course aimed to foster PSTs’ KB competences and data science skills. Participation was voluntary, with informed consent obtained, and PSTs could withdraw at any time. The instructor held a Ph.D. in Learning Sciences and had four years of teaching experience. Design of the KB environment augmented by LAsRA (intervention) This study designed a three-part Knowledge Building (KB) environment, enhanced by LAsRA, to facilitate student development of data science competences. Knowledge Building principles include: improvable ideas, rise-above, epistemic agency, knowledge building discourse, and reflective and transformative assessment. These principles, particularly rise-above, epistemic agency, and transformative assessment, guided the design of the environment. During the course, PSTs worked with inquiry-based discussion data generated within Knowledge Forum, including their own discourse contributions and group interaction patterns. These data served as the primary resource for reflection and analysis, enabling PSTs to examine their collaborative inquiry processes and 1019 make data-informed decisions. We  designed a three-part, principle-based set of activities. Figure 1 presents the three-component design with principle-based activities. Part 1 (Week 1–6): PSTs engaged in collaborative inquiry and data science practice, selecting questions to discuss in Knowledge Forum within small groups (6– 7 PSTs) and across groups. Activities included whole-class and small-group discussions and individual reflections. Part 2 (Week 7–10): PSTs conducted problem-centered inquiry and iterative idea improvement in Knowledge Forum, supported by frequent collective reflection. Groups analyzed their knowledge building processes, identified issues, and developed action plans for further inquiry. Part 3 (Week 11–16): PSTs continued collaborative inquiry while using KBDeX to visualize group discourse and conduct analytics-supported reflective assessment. They extended and refined group ideas, wrote reflective notes, shared learning outcomes, and created concept maps to summarize knowledge gains.  This design integrated learning analytics with reflective assessment to scaffold PSTs’ engagement in iterative knowledge construction and data-driven decision-making, supporting the development of both KB competences and data science skills. The primary data source was Knowledge Forum notes from 100 PSTs, with 2,871 notes from the  experimental class (n = 53) and 1,490 notes from the comparison class (n = 47). These notes reflected PSTs’ data science skills within the collaborative KB environment. Given the collective nature of KB, analysis focused on group-level competence, emphasizing how PSTs jointly advanced knowledge. First, inquiry thread analysis was conducted to organize KF discourse into coherent threads. An inquiry thread reconstructs original discussion sequences to capture holistic problem-focused dialogue (Zhang et al., 2007). Notes addressing the same question were clustered into threads, resulting in 73 threads for the experimental class and 32 threads for the comparison class.  The experimental class produced a larger number of notes and inquiry threads than the comparison class. This difference attributed to the integration of LAsRA, which encouraged more frequent reflection, interaction, and iterative idea improvement. Changes in collective knowledge building (RQ1) To examine RQ1—changes in collective knowledge building—we applied Group Communication Analysis (GCA) (Dowell et al., 2019; Dowell & Poquet, 2021). GCA uses computational semantic analysis to quantify 1020 group discourse across six indices: participation, internal cohesion, overall responsivity, social impact, newness, and communication density. It captures temporal alignment, sequential ordering, and coordination in meaning, enabling comparison of collective knowledge advancement between experimental and comparison classes (Dowell et al., 2019; Dowell & Poquet, 2021).

Mechanisms of LAsRA on PSTs' KB competences and data science competences (RQ3) For RQ3 —mechanisms by which LAsRA supported competence development —we conducted Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) (Shaffer, 2017). ENA constructs networks where nodes represent coded discourse elements, and edges reflect co-occurrences, capturing how metaco gnitive processes (monitoring, reflection, regulation) support higher-level KB and data science practices. Networks were normalized and reduced using singular value decomposition (SVD) to visualize structural differences between classes. ENA allowed examination of stronger connections among metacognitive, conceptual, epistemic, and data science discourse moves, illustrating how LAsRA facilitated coordinated knowledge construction and skill development (Hod et al., 2020; Swiecki et al., 2020).

## Summary of results

The results show that LAsRA significantly improved collective knowledge advancement. The experimental class outperformed the comparison class in participation, internal cohesion, overall responsivity, and social impact, indicating that PSTs contributed more actively and formed dense r connections between their ideas, peers’ contributions, and prior knowledge. In contrast, Newness and Communication Density did not differ, suggesting that LAsRA enhanced the relational and interactive aspects of collaboration without reducing novelty or interaction volume. These findings align with prior studies highlighting the positive effects of analytics-supported reflective assessment on collaborative knowledge building (Yang et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2022). Analyses of online discourse revealed that experimental PSTs  engaged more frequently in high-level conceptual development, epistemic engagement, and data science practices than their peers in the comparison class, with clear progressive improvements. Notably, they engaged in analyzing, interpreting, and transforming data, and making data-informed decisions, indicating productive engagement in data science practices. These science competences, extending prior research by explicitly linking reflective assessment to data science practices (Yang, Chen, et al., 2020; Yang, van Aalst, et al., 2020). Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) revealed stronger connections in the experimental class between higher-level metacognitive moves and higher-level conceptual, epistemic, and data science actions. LAsRA prompted PSTs to synthesize ideas, construct deeper explanations, identify gaps, and engage in problem-centered inquiry. Reflective assessment also scaffolded core data science practices, such as data analysis and data-informed decision-making. Across stages, PSTs  formed increasingly complex connections betwe en metacognitive, conceptual, epistemic, and data science moves, suggesting that LAsRA fostered iterative reflection, collaborative synthesis, and integrative knowledge advancement.

## Implications for educational practice

The study underscores the importance of designing LAsRA to cultivate a collaborative, reflective, and data-driven culture. This approach helps preservice teachers (PSTs) engage in Knowledge Building (KB) inquiry and data science practices, supporting metacognitive monitoring, reflection, and regulation.  These processes are closely aligned with teaching practices, as they mirror how teachers use classroom data to monitor student learning, reflect on instructional effectiveness, and adapt their teaching strat egies. Educators can leverage learning analytics to structure meaningful collaborative activities, promote data-informed decisions, and enhance both KB competences and data science competences.

## Limitations and future research

This study focused only on online discourse, excluding other data sources such as face-to-face interactions or classroom artifacts. Additionally, it lacked pre-and post-tests and relied on manual coding, which may limit generalizability. Future research should integrate multiple data sources, automated coding, and domain knowledge assessments to better understand the transferability and broader impact of LAsRA on PSTs’ knowledge-building and data science competences.
