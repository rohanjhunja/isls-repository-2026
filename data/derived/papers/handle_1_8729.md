# Othering: Overcoming a Sociocultural Obstacle to English Proficiency Among ESL Learners in Malaysia

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study aimed to see how othering affects Malaysian students' English language learning. This article presents insights from data acquired during an interview and examined thematically. It explores three themes that emerged from the interview with Malaysian ESL learners regarding their experiences with othering and how personal growth has helped them overcome the effects of othering.

## Introduction

English, being a global language, is a necessary sk ill for integrating into the real world. Accordingl y, English plays a significant role in the Malaysian education al system, where it is viewed as a second language and a language of knowledge that may aid Malaysians in obtaining better work (Kasuma et al., 2021) and hence a higher social position (Zulkefly & Razali, 2019). Learners are encouraged to practice the languages outside o f class to improve their skills. However, speaking English outside the classroom is a greater challenge for its users. When learners interact in English, they claim to feel 'o thered' by their community members. Othering happen s when English language learners are purposely segregated from their ethnic group due to a biased mindset (Le e et al., 2010). This research investigated the impact of othering on Malaysian learners' English language acquisition. The study's findings can provide English learners with an understanding of how to recover from the effects of othering.

## Methods

An interview protocol was used to acquire a thoroug h overview of the participants' experiences with ot hering when attempting to communicate in English at school or in public areas. The questions were designed to extract information regarding the participants' personal experiences as ESL learners, especially on the problem of othering faced by learners in Malaysia. The interview questi ons centered on identity, marginalization, motivati on, and barriers to learning English. Thematic analysis was conducted by a six-step approach (Braun & Clarke, n.d.; Braun & Clarke, 2006): familiarization, coding, theme gen eration, theme review, theme definition and naming,  and writing up.

## Findings

Data from the interview produced three themes: othe ring belittles one’s linguistics ability and cultur al identity, being othered compels learners to adjust their viewpoint, and learners experienced personal growth. Theme 1: Othering belittles one’s linguistics ability and cultural identity Mocking is one of the negative behaviors associated with othering. Many of the incidents include ridiculing the learners' willingness to communicate in English in the presence of non-English users. The mockery made  the learners feel embarrassed and upset. In addition, t hey were mocked for trying to communicate in a fore ign language and accused of abandoning their cultural identity. Selalunya saya senyap.” (“Why do you want to speak in English when your language is Iban?” It was sad because English is universal. Everyone can speak English. Usually I just stay silent.) Student 4:  “I remember the first time my family went to the Iban church. I remember everyone was like, "They talk English. Orang putih." Then they went to ask us, “Orang putih ka?” Then I said, “No, no. I'm Iban.” (I remember the first time my family went to the Iban church. I remember everyone was like, "They talk English. White people." Then they came to ask us, “White person?” Then I said, “No, no. I'm Iban.”) Theme 2: Being ‘othered’ compels learners to adjust their viewpoint ICLS2022 Proceedings 2090 © ISLS Following a firsthand encounter with othering, the participants reflected on the experience. As a result, they came to understand the point of view of people who mocked them. This thoughtfulness is a critical adjustment-seeing through the mockers' eyes may assist learners in mo ving beyond their pain and continue learning and us ing English.

Student 7:  “Mereka rasa jijik; mcm pelik. Orang ini dia Iban atau Melayu. Lepas tu dia cakap BI macam dia cakap BM tu. Pelik lah. Lepas tu mereka rasa terancam sebab mereka tak boleh bercakap BI. Jadi mereka rasa pelik atau cemburu, jadi mereka mula ejek.” (They felt disgusted; it was weird. This person is Iban or Malay. Then he speaks English the way he speaks Malay. Odd. Then they feel threatened because they can't speak English. They feel strange or jealous, so they start mocking.) Theme 3: Learners experienced personal growth Surprisingly, personal growth develops as a result of the encounter. Despite the unpleasant experience, the learners chose to rise above it by refusing to let incidences of othering impede their language acquisition. Student 1:  “Kena jadikan itu sebagai galakan untuk cakap BI, lepas tu jangan fikir apa yang orang lain fikir. Just do whatever you want.” (You have to use that as an encouragement to talk in English, and then don't think about what other people think. Just do whatever you want.) Student 4: “Beri saya semangat untuk lebih belajar BI ni supaya dapat buktikan bahawa saya ni boleh, dan mereka akan hormat.” (Encourages me to learn more English so that I can prove that I can, and they will respect me.)

## Discussion and Conclusion

Attacks on English learners' cultural identities show that they have lost their cultural identity and become outsiders in their ethnic community when they speak English. This is an example of sophisticated othering, as defined by Brons (2015), in which the person committing otheri ng expects the othered to communicate in their nati ve language. This odd method of preventing people from  speaking English may be related to the mockers' la ck of linguistic abilities and feelings of insignificance when they encounter someone who speaks or attempts to speak English well (Ibrahim et al., 2018). However, langu age acquisition is crucial to a learner's personal growth (Schmidt, 2014); therefore, the participants chose to improve their English skills instead. Furthermore, they did not appear resentful or conflicted, as they knew that not everyone would support them. Instead, they believed that learning English would benefit them in educational, social, and professional aspects.
