# Integrating the Interdisciplinary Research Process (IRP) into the undergraduate course Theory and Application of Motor Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper presents the design, implementation, and evaluation of a semester-long motor learning course embedding the Interdisciplinary Research Process (IRP) in undergraduate PE students’ education. Employing a design-based research approach, it examines how structured interdisciplinary collaboration fosters autonomy, critical thinking, and competence. Findings suggest that integrating IRP improves engagement and interdisciplinary understanding, though challenges remain in coordination and task clarity. Students developed deeper insights into selected curriculum areas. Implications for future iterations are discussed.

## Introduction

Motor learning is a complex and multifaceted phenomenon. It can be viewed from the perspective of neuroscience, psychological, pedagogical and didactic aspects, ph ysiology and biomechanics, social sciences, enginee ring and technology, and last but not leas t, application in clinical and practical contexts. An interdisciplinary approach is required for developing a deeper understanding of this complexity. The presented study reports on an innovated semester course for PE teacher students. The newly integrated Interdisciplinary Research Process (IRP)  is a guiding framework that will allow students a more c omprehensive understanding of motor skills learning  in different contexts. T hrough a creative and integrative process, it will  increase their level of knowledge in accordance with Bloom's taxonomy of intellectual behavioral levels (Anderson et al, 2000). Students are introduced to interdisciplinary research in a pract ical way through collaborative learning. Interdisci plinary cooperation works on the principle that each partner is an expert in its own domain and a newcomer to the domain of the partners (Hermann et al., 2001). This approa ch promotes students' autonomy and responsibility b ut also teaches them to communicate and collaborate effecti vely. The challenge for the teacher is to specify a nd coordinate the objectives of the activities, the distribution of tasks among participants, the management of activity dependencies and their time synchronization. Other challenges associated with interdisciplinary collaborative learning include communication barriers between tea m memb ers, knowledge integration issues, and unequal contribution as a common collaborative learning pro blem (Repko, 2008). Design-based research can be used to work with complex systems of interactions between e ducators, students, and components of the environm ent (Collins et al., 2004). It is usually used to solve  authentic problems in real-world contexts and is thus a suitable methodology to obtain an answer to a research quest ion: Will students acquire interdisciplinary competencie s through an innovated course while maintaining the level of knowledge required by the curriculum?

## Methodology

The study is the first iteration of a design-based research study. The main objective of this study is to address the pedagogical challenges associated with the integration of interdisciplinary collabora tive learning into the compulsory curriculum. Two  sub-objectives are focused on the student:  to get practically acquainted with interdisciplinary research, and to gain a deeper insight into motor learning th rough collaborative learning. Participants were 85 master's students enrolled in t he compulsory course "Theory and Application of Motor Learning" at Charles University. The age of the par ticipating students ranged between 21 and 25 years.  Students were divided into groups of 6-8, each of which chose a topic related to the acquisition of motor skills. By the 10step integrative interdisciplinary IRP methodology (Repko & Szostak, 2021), students defined the probl em, identified relevant disciplines, conducted literatu re searches, and synthesized the findings into a co llaborative research output. Evaluation criteria and data collection tools are shown in Table 1. The students were meeting in person and collaborating for 12 weeks. All the while, they had access to university reso urces for researching information and an interactive course in Moodle  (Rice, 2007) that covered the compulsory curriculum. Throughout the process, the teacher provided support to groups and individuals, especially in achieving the above course objectives. Emphasis was placed on formative assessment. ## Findings

The amount of data collected provided a rich, multi dimensional and multi-layered picture of the implementation of the proposed semester course. In the monitored c riteria, students highly rated Autonomy (95 T-scores), associated Responsibility (81 T-scores) and Collaboration (77 T-scores) at the end of the course. The biggest problem was Time synchronization (51 T-scores) both for individual students, intra-group and from the teacher's point of view inter-group. Reflection from the teacher's point of view emphasized the need for clearer articulation of goals and better alignments of group timelines. To assess interdisciplinary competence, participants completed a questionnaire at the end of the course (Lattuca e t al., 2013) in which they reported their perceived interdisciplinary skills. They showed high average scores in the areas of Appreciation and Application (M = 4.04), Reflection (M = 3.97) and  Recognizing Interdisciplinary (M = 3.69). To assess  the mastery of the compulsory curriculum of the course (criterion of knowledge and application), the study participants were given a knowledge test that measures learning outcomes in the field o f motor learning. Students were evaluated with a grade of 1-4. A comparison of the knowledge tests showed slightly  lower performance than peers from previous cohorts (N = 263). The Mann-Whitney U test showed at a level of 0.05 (U-statistic: 20.0; p-value: 0.0151) that the tested difference was statistically significant to the det riment of the study participants, probably due  to the selective focus on deeper conceptual problems within the chosen topics. Students demonstrated creativity in their research questions e.g. "How to identify motor learning?"; " How are motor skills related to the environment?"; "Anticipation in Sports Games-An Interdisciplinary Analysis". Students approached  the solution from the perspective of many disciplines, theories and metho ds. The final stages of the IRP (steps 7 –10) were cognitively most demanding and included the integration of theories and the defense of synthesized interdisciplinary work.

## Conclusion

The inclusion of IRPs in the curriculum fostered me aningful interdisciplinary collaboration. Students developed metacognitive awareness, collaborative skills, and the ability to apply disciplinary insights to complex problems. In response to the research question, it can be sta ted that the students perceived their interdiscipli nary competencies acquired through the innovated course as high. The level of knowledge required by the mot or learning curriculum was slightly lower among  study participants than peers from previous cohorts. It can be concluded that the study participants paid more att ention to interdisciplinary overlaps and gained a d eeper understanding of selected areas, than to cover the entire field in terms of knowledg e. While the course fostered students’ autonomy, cooperation, motivation, and cr itical thinking, it also revealed challenges relate d to the communication of objectives, task coordination, and —most notably —time management, which in some cases hindered overall group performance. To address these challenges in future iterations, t he proposal will include time slots for each step of the IRP, improvements to study support in Moodle, and the use of the Boix Mansilla et al. (2009)  rubric, which has more universal application and allows students to be evaluated fro m a faculty perspective. In addition, it is planned to publish student projects in the course environment in Moodle to increase visibility and peer-to-peer learning. Overall, the study supports the valu e o f interdisciplinary project-based learning in teacher education, highlighting both its transformative potential and practical limitations.
