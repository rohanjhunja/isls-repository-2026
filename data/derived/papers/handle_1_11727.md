# Interaction Patterns in Community College Students’ Use of ChatGPT for Computational Problem-Solving Tasks

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores how community college students interact with ChatGPT during computational problem-solving tasks in Excel. A mixed-method approach was employed through thematic coding and Epistemic Network Analysis. Results highlight that instructor guidance enhanced perceived task motivation and task value, fostering the use of ChatGPT as a collaborative partner. Findings underscore the importance of instructor guidance and designing instructional environments that promote the ethical and effective use of generative AI for supporting student agency in computational problem-solving tasks.

## Introduction

AI-based tools enhance productivity by augmenting spec ific capabilities, such as problem-solving and creative thinking, and serving as collaborative partners acr oss various fields (Hamdan et al., 2023). This rece nt trend highlights the need to develop AI competencies for professional tasks while exploring effective ways to integrate Generative AI(GAI) into skill-based education systems like community colleges to enhance students’ practical abilities (Park & Woo, 2022).

Previous research indicates that ChatGPT supports t asks like idea generation, coding, and document drafting (Cooper, 2023; Liao et al., 2024). However, concerns about students' over-reliance on GAI, such as directly copying ChatGPT-generated responses, highlight the need to cultivat e GAI skills that focus on student agency and critical thinking skills (Chan & Hu, 2023). Furthermore, existing studies on GAI have predominantly focused on using ChatGPT for academic writing tasks, reflecting its strength in lan guage-based applications. While some studies have examined ChatGPT’s applications in programming and coding education, little research has explored its potential to facilitate computatio nal, procedural, and analytical thinking, especiall y in tasks involving data organization and analysis. Computational pro blem-solving skills are critical to understanding complex systems and automating processes. In this s tudy, Excel-based tasks are examined as a representative example of computational activities, given that  Excel is widely used for managing large datasets, a utomating calculations, and generating structured outputs. Ef fective computational problem-solving requires both technical proficiency and motivation to engage deeply with the task. According to the Expectancy-Value Theory (Eccles & Wigfield, 2000), students’ perception of task value  plays a key role in shaping motivation. However, l imited research exists on how GAI tools like ChatGPT influence these motivational factors. To address these challenges, strategies such as collaborative instructional design, i ncluding instructor guidance, are necessary to sustain longterm, self-directed learning motivation and ensure the ethical use of ChatGPT (Alhajri et al., 2023). Against this backdrop, this study aims to investigate community college students’ behavior patterns when utilizing ChatGPT for computational task completion  and examines how their perceptions, task motivatio n, and task value evolve under the condition before and af ter instructor guidance is provided. This study add resses the following research questions: (1) How do community college students engage in computational problem-solving tasks using ChatGPT in unguided and guided sessions ? (2) How do community col lege students interact with ChatGPT during computational problem-solving tasks in unguided and guided sessions? The findings aim to guide the development of instructional strategies that pr omote the meaningful use of GAI and support student  agency in skill-based education.

## Method

The participants of this study were eight students majoring in Big Data Management enrolled in a compu ter utilization course at a community college in South Korea. These students had prior knowledge about GAI from a course conducted in the previous semester, which co vered fundamental concepts and prompt engineering techniques for using ChatGPT effectively. The study was conducted over two sessions. The use of ChatGPT was entirely voluntary, allowing students to decide how and when to incorporate ChatGPT into their task completion process. A mixed method was employed to explore com munity college students’ perceptions and usage of ChatGPT during the computational task completion. D ata collected included video recordings of each stu dent’s live screen during the task completion, weekly refl ection memos, and reflective reports documenting students’ perceptions of ChatGPT. Quantitative data were anal yzed using descriptive statistics with Jamovi 2.6.2. Qualitative data were analyzed using a tri angulation approach proposed by Denzin et al. (2023) and coding techniques recommended by Corbin and Strauss (1990). An epistemic network analysis (ENA) was also conducted using the ENA Web Tool to examine students' interactions.

## Findings

In this study, students exhibited distinct ChatGPT usage patterns before and after receiving instructo r guidance. First, during the unguided session, students demonstrated minimal use of ChatGPT. In contrast, during the guided session, students not only ac tively utilized ChatGPT to complete tasks, which co ntributed to improved learning outcomes, but also experienced a shift in their perception of ChatGPT. Initially, in the unguided session, students viewed ChatGPT as merely a tool within a hierarchical relationship. However, with instructor guidance, students began to perceive ChatGPT as a collaborative learni ng partner. Thematic analysis of students’ reflecti ve memos revealed a perceptual shift from ChatGPT as an “easy explainer like a teacher” to a “trusted problem-solver like a close friend”. The results highlight the critical  role of instructor guidance in facilitating the pr oductive integration of GAI tools into learning activities, demonstrating that ongoing support can transform students’ perceptions of GAI from perceiving GAI merely as a tool or dependency to recognizing GAI as a collabor ative and reciprocal partner.

Second, the ENA explored how students’ interaction patterns with ChatGPT varied during computational problem-solving tasks depending on whether they received gu idance. During the unguided session, students initially demonstrated low task motivation and perc eived low task value, relying minimally on ChatGPT for understanding. The results revealed a statistically  significant difference along the X-axis, where the unguided session (M = -0.33, SD = 0.42) and the guided session (M = 0.33, SD = 0.28) differed significantly, t(12.08) = - 3.66, p <.01, with a large effect size (d = 1.83). In contrast, no significant difference was  observed along the Yaxis. Both sessions yielded identical means (M = 0) with different standard deviations (SD = 0.45 and 0.26), and the t-test result was non-significant, t(11.13) = 0.00, p = 1.00, d = 0.00. In the unguided session, students primarily formed connections around nodes representing low ta sk value and low motivation, indicating minimal engagement and limited perceived utility. In contra st, the guided session exhibited stronger connectio ns among high task value and motivation nodes. This shift may be attributed to students’ growing awareness of ChatGPT’s usefulness in supporting computational problem-solving, mainly when guidance was provided. Further more, while the unguided session demonstrated some connections to nodes such as ChatGPT Usage and Understanding the Task, these were less prominent than the guided session. The guided session revealed a noticeable connection expansion to these areas, implying that structured guidan ce enhanced students’ cognitive engagement and promoted a more productive use of the AI tool. The ENA results indicate that after receiving guidance, students’ interactions shifted significantly toward hig h task motivation and task value, recognizing ChatG PT’s usefulness in problem-solving. The results suggest that instructor guidan ce was crucial in enhancing students’ motivation and engagement and effectively utilizing ChatGPT.

## Discussion and implications

This study explored the use of ChatGPT, focusing on  community college students’ engagement with computational problem-solving tasks. First, the finding suggests that ins tructors should design relevant instructional strategies that align ChatGPT's capabilities with students' skill levels and task requirements. Second, students’ successful experiences increased their pe rceived task value and motivation while completing the tasks, which aligns with the Expectancy-Value Theory (Azevedo et al., 2004). Lastly, as  students gained confidence in using ChatGPT, their perceptions shifted from seeing ChatGPT as a hierar chical tool to a collaborative learning partner, emphasizing the value of ongoing engagemen t with GAI tools to deepen understanding of their capabilities and limitations.  Overall, this study demonstrates that the guided use of ChatGPT in computational problem-solving tasks enhances students’ task value and mot ivation and fosters a sense of agency in ChatGPT's application. These findings also imply the potentia l of ChatGPT in promoting purposeful engagement and reinforcing its relevance in skill-based learning contexts for community college students. Despite the small sample size and the focus on Excel-based tasks, the findings demonstrate ChatGPT’s pot ential to foster motivation, enhance task value, and promote learner agency with in a computational problem-solving context. Future studies should explore ChatGPT’s application across diverse tasks and learning contexts.
