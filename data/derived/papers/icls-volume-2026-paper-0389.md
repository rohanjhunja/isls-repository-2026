# Immersive Storytelling and Embodied Experience in Informal Learning Contexts

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigates how immersive technologies and narrative strategy enhance embodied learning and presence in informal education. Grounded in embodied cognition, it analyzes three student-designed AR, VR, and smart glasses experiences at a Western Canadian science center. Using a qualitative case study, findings show that bodily interaction, sensory engagement, and storytelling deepen users’ sense of presence. Integrating narrative with physical interaction transforms passive observation into active pa rticipation, reimagining informal learning as engaging, multisensory, and impactful.

Informal learning environments such as museums and science centers provide opportunities for learning through physical, cognitive, and social engagement (King & Dillon, 2012). Embodied cognition highlights how these experiences are shaped by the body’s interaction with the environment, emphasizing the importance of physical and sensory involvement in learning (Wilson, 2002; Macrine & Fugate, 2020). Despite this, limited research has examined how embodiment supports immersive learning—particularly the role o f the body in fostering presence within informal education contexts (Shaby & Vedder-Weiss, 2021; Moon et al., 2025). Immersion, a core dimension of immersive learning, refers to system features that generate vivid, sensory-rich experiences while reducing awareness of the external world (Stracke et al., 2025). Immersive technologies can support flow (deep task engagement)  and presence (the feeling of being situated within a mediated environment), both central to effective immersive learning (Baradaran Rahimi et al., 2018, 2022; Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; Steuer, 1992; Whyte, 2002). Within this framework, embodiment involves bodily movement, sensory input, and spatial interaction—often mediated through VR or AR —to deepen engagement and situate learning (Wilson & Foglia, 2011; Lehrman, 2025). Prior work shows that room-scale VR can strengthen presence through active physical movement (Moon et al., 2025), yet the specific ways embodiment shapes presence in immersive learning experiences (ILX) remain underexplored (Xu et al., 2021).  This study examines how undergraduate-designed ILX in a science center conceptualized and represented the body. By analyzing three projects, we investigate how embodied engagement contributes to presence and supports deeper, situated learning within informal immersive environments. This study draws on a qualitative case study of undergraduate immersive learning projects developed within an interaction design course in collaboration with a science center in Western Canada. Students followed an iterative design process —including contextual research, conceptualizing, designing, prototyping, and user testing—to create immersive learning experiences (ILX) aimed at supporting visitor navigation and engagement. Across ten final projects, students produced a range of prototypes using tools such as Figma, Adobe Aero, and Blender, supported by field visits, consultations with curators, and iterative feedback from instructors and peers. To examine how embodiment was conceptualized and represented in these ILX, we selected three projects that most strongly foregrounded bodily engagement. Data sources included (1) project artefacts (e.g., demo videos, prototypes, design documentation), (2) process materials capturing students’ design trajectories, and

(3) semi-structured group interviews. Artefact analysis and narrative analysis (Collier-Reed et al., 2009) enabled

us to explore how design decisions, story structures, and interaction mechanisms supported embodiment, immersion, and presence (Pearson et al., 2015). The three focal cases were: Trail, an AR mobile experience using narrative guidance to support exhibit navigation; Body Tour, a VR storytelling journey through human body systems; and Astro Experience, a smart-glasses-based AR character interaction designed to enhance sensory and bodily engagement. These cases provided rich examples of how student designers integrated embodied interaction within ILX for informal science learning.

Physical presence and embodiment through narratives supported by immersive technologies Across all three projects, narrative-based ILX using AR, VR, and smart-glasses technologies prompted users to physically and sensorially interact with exhibits —jumping, reaching, moving, looking, and listening. These 2593 embodied actions played a central role in creating presence and supporting meaning-making in immersive learning. The following examples illustrate how each project combined technology and narrative to foster embodied engagement.

AR narrative: Trail The Trail project used AR characters and tactile prompts to guide users through culturally rooted stories embedded in the exhibit. In the Health Zone, an AR grandson invited users to jump and reach toward a height marker as part of the narrative, prompting them to mirror the character’s actions. By blending storytelling with physical tasks such as jumping and reaching, the experience positioned users as active participants rather than observers. This alignment of narrative and bodily interaction fostered a strong se nse of physical presence, supporting findings that movement-based interaction enhances immersion and strengthens learner– content connection (Chen & Lai, 2025). Trail demonstrates how AR storytelling can use embodiment to deepen engagement and situate users within the narrative experience.

VR narrative: Body tour The Body Tour project used VR to immerse users inside human body systems through a guided narrative. As users followed the storyline, they traced the pathways of veins and arteries with their own movements, creating a strong sense of physical presence. This aligns with Mejía-Puig and Chandrasekera’s (2022) argument that virtual bodies can enhance presence and deepen cognitive engagement by linking bodily movement to conceptual learning. Body Tour shows how narrative-based VR can support embodiment and transform abstract biological processes into lived, experiential understanding—offering an alternative to traditional text-or lecture-based approaches. Smart-Glasses narrative: Astro experience Astro Experience explored how Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses can support embodied and sensory engagement through a narrative-based AR interaction. Users engaged with Astro, an AI-driven AR character that responded to voice commands, gaze direction, head orientation, and spatial positioning. As users navigated the space gallery, adaptive audio and visual overlays aligned with their movements —for example, when users turned toward a planet, Astro appeared beside it and provided information. These sensory-responsive features created dynamic feedback between user and environment, strengthening presence and embodied engagement. The project demonstrates how integrating narrative with AI/AR responsiveness candeepen immersion, aligning with research on multisensory, movement-based learning in AR environments (Baradaran Rahimi et al., 2018; Mansour et al., 2024; Stalheim & Somby, 2024).

## Scholarly significance

This study highlights the potential of narrative and immersive technologies to enhance embodiment and physical presence in immersive learning environments. Through artefact analysis of student projects, we found that the integration of storytelling and embodied interaction not only deepened immersion but also strengthened the learners’ sense of presence. In this context, the body served as a medium for immersion, allowing users to experience the exhibit content through embodied involvement. These findings underscore the value of combining narrative structures with immersive media to create more engaging and meaningful educational experiences. Immersive technologies such as AR, VR, and smart glasses along with narratives in the ILX can enhances sense of presence and support embodiment.

We try to reimagine the future of ILX by exploring how narrative-based and embodiment can transform educational engagement. Despite its promise, designing for immersive embodiment presents challenges, including accessibility, technological limitations, and  diverse user needs. The long-term impact on learning outcomes remains underexplored, highlighting the need for further research into how narrative forms, interaction styles, and spatial design influence presence and learning. Through this research we invite audiences at ISLS 2026 conference to initiate dialogue around the pedagogical implications of designing for presence, not just as a technological affordance, but as a deeply human experience rooted in storytelling, embodiment, and spatial awareness.
