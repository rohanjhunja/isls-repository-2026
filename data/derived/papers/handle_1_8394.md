# Personalized Instruction to Teach Secondary Students to Deeply Comprehend and Build Knowledge From Science Texts

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
eBRAVO is a unique, theory driven, web-based teaching tool that simultaneously provides advanced reading comprehension assessment and skill development, knowledge building skills, combined with a standards-based science curriculum in ecology. The instruction is individually paced, has whole classroom activities to aid classroom pacing, teacher and student dashboards demonstrating progress, and is open source. We describe the approach to theory-driven development, co-design and evaluation in the project and the implications for supporting reading comprehension in STEM.

## Introduction

In order to be successful in school, students need to be able to read content-area texts well enough t o learn and remember new content, and they must be able to apply what they know in future reading and learning situations, as well as apply the knowledge in novel contexts. Comprehension instruction is intended to give students specific tools that expert readers use, to be applied in sit uations where comprehension breaks down (e.g., Bian carosa & Snow, 2006; Goldman, 2012; Kim, Vaughn, Wanzek, & Wei, 2004; Pilonieta, 2010; Pressley, 2000). However, in the middle school years this instruction typical ly consists of isolated strategy or skill developme nt taught in remedial reading and English/language arts classes and often ignores the goal of cumulatively building knowledge from texts (e.g., Duke, Pearson, Strachan, & Billman, 2011). The eBRAVO project targeted this well-documented weakness in reading comprehension (particularly for expository texts) that typically persist and si gnificantly impact students’ further academic progr ess (e.g., Anonymous, 2017; Chen & Ho, 2012; Norris and Phillips, 2003).  It addresses the problem with current practices in comprehension instruction by embedding explicit comprehension instruction in a content-area context  that improves reading comprehension skills in middle school readers. The approach is designed to provide scaffolded support to help students understand difficult conte nt-rich, informational text, teaching them how to o vercome misconceptions, understand the text meaning, and build knowledge. Developing eBRAVO For the project, researchers developed and pilot tested the eBRAVO intervention aimed at helping middle school students develop deep comprehension skills necessar y to read, comprehend, and build knowledge from informational texts. This project used a novel appr oach to translating theory to practice by simultane ously addressing parallel learning processes in actual le arning settings: skillfully reading challenging texts to achieve deep understanding while also building knowledge about a complex content area from the content in the reading. At the same time, this project worked iteratively with stakeholders—teachers and students—to design a tool that works practically in both classroom and individual learning settings (e.g., Penuel, Fishma, Cheng & Sabelli, 2011). The eBRAVO development used a theory-driven adaptation and enhancement of a previously-developed paper-based intervention (BRAVO; Fostering Comprehension and Knowledge-Buildi ng in Middle-School Struggling Readers), shown in prior work to have promise for improvin g students' reading comprehension outcomes (Foltz, Littrell-Baez, & Kintsch, E, 2018).  eBRAVO, like BRAVO, is aimed at improving the reading comprehension skills of middle school students who have adequate reading fluency but still have trouble learning from informational text as they read. The intervention, embedded in a STEM curriculum, teaches students how to implement reading comprehension strategies and how to actively build knowledge. Moving from a paper-based to an online curriculum a fforded multiple changes in design to permit individualized comprehension strategy instruction within a curriculum with contextualized texts at different levels of reading difficulty. eBRAVO was developed as a we b-based reading application that contained a comple te ecology curriculum. A design-based implementation research (DBIR) approach was used to carry out the project (Penuel, Fishman, Cheng, & Sabelli, 2011). This app roach consisted of co-design meetings which allowed researchers and instructors to interact as full partners in developing and evaluating materials and instruction. ICLS2022 Proceedings 1009 © ISLS The eBRAVO curriculum is an adaptive reading experi ence with embedded formative assessments on web-enabled devices in which students work through a twelve chapter ecology curriculum. It is designed to allow students to proceed through the lessons at a self-p aced rate, rather than as a class. To accommodate t his personalized learning experience in an intelligent tutoring approach, we adapted the five class-level lessons from BRAVO into videos and a practice problem personalized for each chapter, creating 119 lessons. As students work within eBRAVO, they first read a short chapter of 3  to 11 paragraphs of ecology reading content connec ted to what they have read so far in the curriculum. These  readings incorporate in-text highlighting and clic kable definitions for key vocabulary, along with embedded  figures including images of important components o r diagrams of certain systems. Once students have com pleted their readings, they proceed directly to ope n-ended writing asking them to record the big ideas found i n the text. eBRAVO then probes their deep comprehen sion with multiple choice questions that target both iss ues brought up directly in the text as well as infe rences and connections to greater ideas from previous texts. The open-ended writing is assessed using NLP-based approaches to measure content and inferences that were learned  (Foltz et.al.2020; Foltz, 2017). The system assess multiple literacy skills and if the system determines a stud ent should see subsequent reading lessons based on their comprehension scores, it then gauges which type of reading support (linking ideas, gist, summary, grap hic organizer, or inference) with which the student needs help and sends them to the appropriate lesson for that chapter and content, The  lessons  further incorporate two to five minute videos that outline the key comprehe nsion strategy that is targeted and demonstrate how to apply this comprehension strategy to parts of the chapter that the student has just completed.

Taken together these features provide students with  just-in-time instruction on needed skills to bette r understand and learn from the text as they are reading it  (Foltz et.al. 2020; Foltz, 2017).  The process of development of eBRAVO for one STEM domain further r esulted in a more general curriculum framework that may be applied tonew content areas and that will allow more individualized scaffolding to better meet the needs of a variety of classrooms, students, and instructors in both Language Arts and content area classrooms. Evaluation The first two years of this project involved iterative development of the eBRAVO interactive, web-based tool, in collaboration with teachers and students. Researche rs began by identifying and adapting previously dev eloped and studied BRAVO curriculum components into a form at that could be delivered online through eBRAVO. Participants in this study included approximately 2 88 students in 6th through 9th grades in suburban s chool districts in the Denver, Colorado area.. Data were collected from teachers and students via surveys, i nterviews, and focus groups to examine usability and feasibility as well as data from embedded formative assessments. The eBRAVO system also collected user data (e.g. click streams), and the researchers collected student performance data pre-and post-intervention. During this project, revisions were made to eBRAVO from iterative design with teachers and students as collaborators, followed by  field testing of the upgraded system based on user feedback, use and performance. We implemented additional impo rtant features including an online concept map acti vity used to consolidate knowledge, and to more deeply u nderstand the “Big Ideas.  Additionally, scenarios were developed to bring students together in teacher-led classroom activities and to “catch up” students who engaged individually at a slower pace. Our small classroom efficacy study was impacted, however, by the pandem ic, so due to school shutdowns this team pivoted and made design changes to make the intervention available nationwide over the internet, while preserving IRB protocols.

Proximal outcomes of the eBRAVO intervention included assessments embedded within the eBRAVO system, such as quick quizzes and writing prompts t argeting deep comprehension of the ecology curricul um (Behrens, DiCerbo & Foltz, 2019). Researchers asses sed student education outcomes a pre/post text reca ll task designed to assess their ability to extract/learn i nformation from any novel informational text on a s ubject that was unfamiliar. In addition, click streams and othe r student behaviors using the eBRAVO intervention w ere collected to aid in usage and usability analyses. (e.g., Quigley, Caccamise, Weatherley, & Foltz, 2020).

This project resulted in eBRAVO v1, a fully functional, interactive intervention that is open source and ready for use by educators, parents, and researcher s. Key features of the intervention include the fol lowing: eBRAVO is an online reader that jointly provides standards-based middle school Ecology content AND a reading comprehension assessment, tutor, and knowledge-building assistant on a personalized instructional basis.  Internal skilled reading components addresses local cohesion, global cohesion, inference, and knowledge integration. Each just-in-time lesson includes brief videos with comprehension and metacognitive strategies modeled for students. Content chapters combine to build a body of knowledge about ecology similar to any science text, but in eBRAVO there are activities for students to practice comprehension strategies on an individualized, as-needed basis, using this adaptive technology.

Outcomes and lessons learned The main features of the intervention and findings of the project’s pilot study are as follows: ¥ We developed a unique approach to comprehension ins truction with an open access online instruction aid assisting students while they build knowledge from text using a personalized, interactive approach. ¥ We discovered and addressed a need to incorporate periodic whole-classroom activities to allow teachers to more fully assess what knowledge building had occurred and to adjust the curriculum to catch up the slower learners.

¥ We created an online concept mapping activity designed to help students process the information in the eBRAVO texts to promote deeper learning and knowledge building. ¥ We developed and evaluated NLP and Machine Learning based methods that allowed automatic scoring of the concept maps to measure the student knowledge representations and methods for assessing student summaries to measure the amount of information gained from the reading activities. ¥ We developed and evaluated machine learning algorithms to assess individual comprehension skills on the fly as students needed to use them to build knowledge. ## Conclusion

The research team adapted eBRAVO from the existing paper-and-pencil Boulder ReAding inter VentiOn (BRAVO). By adapting BRAVO for electronic delivery,  the researchers worked to make the curriculum framework and content more flexible while making delivery more accessible and workable for classroom teachers and their students. eBRAVO offers an automated, onl ine curriculum that includes baseline assessments o f students' comprehension skills, as well as formativ e and summative assessments throughout the curricul um. eBRAVO simultaneously combines comprehension instruction with content instruction in an as-needed basis to enhance learning and knowledge building as a student reads a challenging text for that purpose.
