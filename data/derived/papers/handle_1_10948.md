## Introduction and problem space

In recent years, teacher educators have been called  upon to weave content about computing and digital literacies (CDLs) more coherently across education coursework (Warr et al., 2023), and in ways that emphasize equitable teaching and learning. Professional learning (PL) i s needed to ensure those working on curriculum refo rm at teacher preparation institutions build expertise in  CDLs and the capacity to “surface, discuss, and de bate what equity means to them” (Penuel et al., n.d.). While there are studies that analyze teacher education curriculum and implementations at this intersection, we know less about how education faculty build capacity in the a rea. This poster begins to answer the question: How might we support faculty to learn about and app ly equitable pedagogical approaches for integrating CDLs into teacher education?

## Context, purpose and methods

Authors represent an initiative that supports facul ty at a large, diverse, urban university system in NYC to equitably, meaningfully, and coherently integrate CDLs into required teacher education courses. Our in stitutions certify approximately 6000 teachers annually and approximately 30% of our city's publ ic schools’ new hires are graduates. Programs are expected to prepare teacher  candidates to practice equitable pedagogies in cla ssrooms (e.g. culturally sustaining education, translanguag ing pedagogy, universal design for learning) and fa culty are expected to practice these approaches with their ow n diverse learners. For two summers, we engaged fac ulty in over 100 hours of professional learning (PL) includ ing self-paced and guided workshops culminating in t heir creation of artifacts – lessons, assignments, syllabi revisions – integrating CDLs in teacher ed. Between Years 1 and 2, we reviewed the artifacts and reflections of  39 Year 1 faculty members to understand the equity-related lenses and rationale they referenced (if any), and if/how faculty indexed tho se lenses/rationale in descriptions of planned activities. We employed open and thematic coding techniques that leveraged categories synthesized from literature about equity in STEM and computing (see findings below). While we did not explicitly run a formal design based research (DBR) study, we used findings  to iterate our PL designs. Retrospectively, we emp loyed conjecture mapping (Sandoval, 2014) to help us formalize the conjectures, embodiments, processes, and outcomes of the two iterations. We hope to get feedback from ICLS attendees regarding how we might analyze Year 2 data systematically to prepare for more formal DBR.

## Findings

Figure 1 illustrates our conjecture maps. The black  unbolded text represents Year 1 conjectures; the b olded text with asterisks highlights revisions made for Year 2. In both years 1 and 2, we expected faculty to bui ld capacity for equitable CDLs integratio n. We hoped their reflections and artifacts would e vidence consideration of their learners’ strengths and experiences, and demonstrat e engagement with the equity-as-access and transformation discourses in STEM ed (Philip & Azevedo, 2017) from  their disciplines and/or from equitable computing ed frameworks we shared. Reviewing faculty’s reflections on how they attempted to promote equity in their designs after year 1, we found: (1) Faculty’s reflections d id not consistently link considerations about learn ers’ varied identities and experiences to content, purpose, and  moves specific to CDLs (Santo et. al., 2019). This  may have been because our PL team inconsistently asked parti cipants for this type of rationale; (2) Reflections  indexed a variety of discourses about the purposes of equitab le teaching moves along the STEM access and transformation continuum, though most comments indexed one side or the other.

To address these issues, we revised the high level conjecture, presuming it was not enough to support faculty to apply general equity frameworks: they mi ght make stronger theory-practice connections if we encouraged them to apply conceptions of equity spec ifically situated in the context of CDLs-integrated teacher education with their specific students. To embody t his new conjecture, a working group engaged in a mu ltifaceted, polysemic process to create a synthesized framework including goals, approaches, design princ iples specific to equitable praxis in CDL-integrated teacher education (including both access and transformation logics). We asked faculty to identify elements of our framew ork in case studies of Year 1 artifacts that made t ight connections between rationale and design. We designed activities to scaffold design, including ones they could remix for their own contexts. Finally, we strengthe ned opportunities for faculty to take up learner pe rspectives, through a moderated student panel, a “zine” featuring student perspectives, and role-play. ## Discussion and implications

Our findings and revisions highlight the need to not just ask teacher educators to apply general ideas about equity in education or CSed, but to situate these ideas in CDL-integrated teacher education with their students.
