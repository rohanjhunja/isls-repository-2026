# The Use of Open-Ended Questions in Collaborative Learning Contexts: A Case study of Art Appreciation Class in an Elementary School

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines the implications of the question "What do you see that makes you say that?” compared with “Why do you think so?” in facilitating art appreciation classes in an elementary school. An analysis of teacher-child interactions across four classes revealed that children who were asked “What?” questions by teacher were better able to identify visual clues within the artwork’s content compared to those who were asked “Why” questions. These findings suggest that employing “What?” questions may foster more in-depth discussion among children about artworks.

## Background

Questioning plays a crucial role in collaborative learning. Facilitators commonly em ploy open-ended questions to enhance students' understanding (Hmelo-Silver & Barrows, 2006; Zhang et al., 2011). Art Appreciation through Discussion (AAD) is a collaborative learning approach designed to enhance children’s visual literacy and critical thinking skills  through engagement with  visual arts. In AAD classes, teachers facilitate evidence-based discussions by posing open-ended questions such as, "What do you see that make s you say that?" (Yenawine, 2013). However, the effectiveness of these specific questions has yet to be empirically verified.

## Method

We conducted four AAD classes with fourth-grade students (two groups, 32 students each) at the Elementary School affiliated with Ehime University. Each 45-minute class featured a different artwork, projected as slides, with the teacher alternating between the questions “What do you see that makes you say that?” and “Why do you think so?” to facilitate children’s discussion (Table 1). ## Results and discussions

Analysis of children's responses indicated that the “What?” question led more visually grounded statements (facts), whereas the “Why?” question prompted more subjective interpretations based on personal experience s (Figure 1).

The following examples are drawn from discussions on Kano Eitoku «Chinese Lion». These examples illustrate how the “What?” question guided students' attention  back to the artwork itself, whereas  the “Why?” question tended to evoke subjective, experience-based responses that were less directly connected to visual evidence.

(…) Teacher:  Is a Thunder Beast something with mysterious powers? Why do you think so? Child:  I saw a picture like this, and it was the exact same picture as this... [Interpretation] Teacher facilitation was analyzed based on three key behaviors: questioning, connecting, and summarizing (see Table 2). The r esults indicated that the teacher asked fewer questions under the "W hat?" condition but engaged in connecting and summarizing more frequently compared to the "Why?" condition (Figure 2). ## Conclusions

The findings suggest that “What do you see that makes you say that?” fosters evidence-based discussions anchored in visual content, facilitating effective collabora tive learning in AAD. In contrast, “Why do you think so?” often evokes subjective responses, which may limit  deeper collective engagement. Future research shoul d further investigate these questioning strategies across diverse contexts.
