# Natural Language and Computational Thinking: A Tale of Symbiosis

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study addresses Brazilian students' deficiencies in language skills and computational thinking. Pre-service teachers underwent active methodologies, emphasizing formative assessments. Positive outcomes include improved communication, cultural awareness, and computational thinking skills. Challenges in global variables and structured writing persist, emphasizing the need for targeted support. The integrated approach shows promise, presenting opportunities for further exploration in creative expression and computational thinking at the intersection of education.

## Introduction

This study addresses the concern raised by educator s in Brazilian high schools and higher education in stitutions about students' struggles with adhering to standard  norms in written natural language and formulating solutions related to computational thinking (CT) strategies. Brito and Madeira (2015) unders core the significant challenge faced by a considerable portion of high school stud ents in formalizing problems into computational lan guage, attributed to deficiencies in fundamental mathemati cal knowledge and a limited capacity for abstractio n. Similarly, Almeida and Melo (2020) highlight disconcerting results from official exams  like the National High School Exam (ENEM), confirming suboptimal adherence to standard norms in students' writing. Recognizing potential extensions of students' diffi culties to educators, this study aims to address bo th students' inadequacies and potential challenges faced by teachers. By exploring the intricacies of written language and computational thinking, this research contributes insights relevant to both students and future educators. Theoretical foundations The professional development course for pre-service teachers followed active methodologies based on principles outlined by Moran (2018), emphasizing deep learning  through frequent practice, multisensory stimulatio n, and leveraging prior knowledge. For participants lackin g specific training in creative writing and computa tional thinking, the course introduced foundational concepts in formative assessments. Formative and summative assessments, aligned with a ctive methodologies, drive student growth, and facilitate educators' reflective teaching practices. Drawing upon the research of Koch (2020), Wood (2 017), Cristóvão and Stutz (2011), and Beaugrande et al. (1981), a range of textual production strategies were discerned with the aim of augmenting effective communication.

In the process of transforming participants' author ed fables in natural language, crafted to enhance language skills, into a digital narrative using com putational thinking strategies within platforms lik e Scratch, it's crucial to recognize that computer co mmunication operates differently than human interac tion. Unlike human communication, computer communication follows stric t protocols. This entails connecting machine langua ge production to programming languages, where explicit  instructions are employed to encode well-organized steps into algorithms.

In our remediation process, computational thinking plays a pivotal role, involving structured sequence s for problem-solving, encompassing elements like abstraction, pa ttern recognition, and decomposition. Success requires disciplined organization of proble m-solving, emphasizing the central role of algorithms. In this transformation from natural language fables to digital narratives on platforms like Scratch, computational thinking becomes integral to the systematic execution of solutions. In the context of this research, which translated natural language into an interactive digital narrative and integrated written language with multimedia element s in a programming-based authoring environment, participants need to abstractly operate, decompo se the whole, recognize patterns, and structure dat a to build an efficient algorithm using computational thinking strategies.

## Methodology

In the second semester of 2022, a 40-hour professional development course was offered to  twelve pre-service teachers. This strategic selection aligns with the objective of equipping future educa tors with the necessary knowledge and skills to effectively impart learning to subsequent cohorts. Activities: Formative assessment process:

• Creative writing modules.

• Thematic element selection via De Mari & Oliveira's kit.

• Collaborative fable authorship in groups of up to four.

• Peer feedback, text enhancement, and revision.

• Familiarization with Scratch through remediating a chosen fable.

• Collaborative remediation of a participant-chosen fable into an interactive narrative using Scratch.

Summative assessment process:

• Utilization of Bardin's content analysis method.

• Pre-analysis: Establishing objectives, questions, and study population.

• Material exploration: Reading and encoding to identify concepts and categories.

• Results processing: Interpreting data, drawing conclusions, and formulating recommendations.

## Findings and conclusions

Below, we provide a breakdown of findings, highlighting the triumphs and hurdles faced by Brazilian students:

1.  Challenges in Standard Language Fable: In the fable composed in standard language, challen ges

emerged, complicating participants' creative effort s. Ambiguous anaphoric references and significant distances between references and terms created hurd les in text comprehension. Parallelism, aiming f or syntactic balance, posed challenges, requiring a delicate balance of skill and sensitivity. Selecting words for similar meanings introduced multifaceted challenges in creative expression.

2.  Situationality Challenges and Narrative Simplification: The initial fable version posed difficulties related

to situationality due to its use of complex languag e. Subsequent revisions aimed at simplifying the narrative, conveying the essence of the complication through concise dialogues.

3.  Interactive Narrative Development in Scratch: Participants faced challenges in clone creation and coding

errors during interactive narrative development in Scratch, promptly resolved. Persistent struggles emerged with global variables, reflecting their abstract nature in computational concepts.

4.  Summative Assessment and Successes: Summative assessment showcased creative fables, emp hasizing

communication strategies and computational thinking  growth. Well-executed narrative strategies contributed to effectiveness, demonstrating achievements in structured writing.

5.  Persistent Challenges and Conceptual Complexity: Despite successes, challenges persisted, emphasizing

the need for systematic approach to writing. Intera ctive narrative development exhibited successes and struggles, notably with the conceptual complexity o f global variables, suggesting areas for addition al support.

In summary, this study sheds light on the dual challenges faced by Brazilian students in written language and computational thinking. Positive outcomes under score advancements in communication and computation al skills, while persistent struggles emphasize the ne ed for targeted support, particularly in understand ing global variables and structured writing. The integrated ap proach offers  a promising avenue for bridging these gaps. Further research should explore adaptive pedagogica l strategies to enhance students' creative expressi on and computational literacy.
