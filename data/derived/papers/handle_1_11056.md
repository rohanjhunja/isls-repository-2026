# Learning With and About Technology in Rural Western India

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
A program addressing a global challenge in computer and ICT education for youth, was developed in rural Western India. Based on Culturally Relevant Computing (CRC) theories, the program emphasizes cultural relevance and tackles issues faced by rural students, such as limited internet access. Using the framework of a research-practice partnership, 20, 8th and 9th-grade students were recruited for the program. The program design objectives focused on critical thinking, collaboration, and technological understanding. Students selected topics, conducted research, interviewed community members, and authored articles that were published online. Through critical thinking, collaboration, and awareness of social justice in technology, the program aims to empower rural communities by supporting students to explore technologies for enhanced everyday living.

## Introduction

Global educators face the challenge of incorporatin g personal computers and internet technologies into  K-12 education. A primary concern for computer and ICT education researchers is adapting teaching methods to diverse cultures worldwide. Although culturally responsive computing (CRC) strategies have gained traction in US K-12 classrooms, their implementation lags in underserve d communities in South Asia. This underscores a key  gap in teaching computer and ICT skills to youth across di fferent cultures. CRC strategies allow youth from ethnically and racially diverse communities to engage with tec hnology in a meaningful way, where youth can ground  their thinking about computers in their socio-political, cultural, and economic conditions (Lachn ey et al 2021). This project uses CRC as a foundational approach to desi gning a computer and ICT education curriculum for y outh who are from marginalized communities in South Asia. The project aims to understand how computing and ICTbased education can be approached in communities ac ross the world that face different socio-political, cultural, and economic realities (Morales-Chicas et al, 2019). In this paper, we describe the  design and beginnings of a long-term design project that is being implemented in a high school in rural western India. The design of t his program has taken into consideration the multiple c hallenges faced by the communities in rural India i n terms of internet access, affordability of computing tools, and lack of content in the local language on the internet. Toyama

(2015) describes the challenge of computing in India as fo llows  “Computers in Bangalore (technology hub in

India) are locked away in dusty cabinets because te achers don’t know what to do with them.” Technology innovators ignore such issues related to social justice, equit y, and inclusiveness not just in India but  globally. Rural students in India encounter challenges access ing new technologies due to economic constraints and inadequate infrastructure, including limited high-speed internet and unreliable electricity. Moreover, they face linguistic barriers as non-English speakers, with a need for improved educational content in their language. Thus the approach described in this paper involves initi ating a journalism program in a rural Indian school. This program emphasizes local language reporting, enabling students to critically analyze and collaboratively explore how rural communities perceive and utilize technology.

## The context and theoretical background

We based the program's design on theories that are aligned with CRC. CRC connects computer and technology education to culturally relevant issues important t o students’ everyday lives (Lachney et al 2021). In addition to computer and technology-related concerns, the journalism program enables us  to address a variety of social and cultural issues affecting rural youth in Western India. In this region, schools introduce youth to outside knowledge that diverges from local culture and tradition (Jackson, 2003).  It's challenging to support rural youth in finding economic opportunities in cities, so it's urgent to  help them explore ways to sustain themselves econo mically within their communities (Kumar & Sati, 2023). What are alternative economic activities rural yout h can pursue to prevent migration to cities, apart from agricult ure? We think teaching students journalism offers them a n opportunity to understand it as a viable economic p ursuit and broadens their creativity as they cover technology innovations in rural India. Through journalism, students develop critical thinking by assessing technology use and innovation. Furthermore, it fosters collaboration n ot only among students but also with the wider comm unity contributing to the news magazine's content. The pr ogram is based in a rural school in western India, situated approximately 100 km from a tier-two urban city. Serving predominantly rural agricul tural communities, the school teaches its curriculum in the local language, Marathi. However, the student body is representative of the multilingual community that makes up most of this region. Limite d exposure to English adds another layer of challenge to computer and technology education, as much of the content and techniques available online  are in English. While there are limited computing resources available in Marathi these resources are not enough to make students proficient in computing skills that can be applicable universally. The students have access to a computer lab and tablets, however high-speed internet and electricity are unreliable. While personal computers in the form of desktops and laptops have found their way into m iddle-class homes across urban India, these tools are not  a common fixture in homes across rural India.

The program design and implementation The program was developed through a partnership between learning scientists and school administrators from the US and India and is grounded in the research practice partnership framework. During the 2023-24 academic year students worked in groups of 3-4 and published two issues in an online news magazine f ormat. The journalistic articles that the students produced focused on tech nology, culture, and rural communities. This paper presents initial findings following the publication of the first issue of the online news magazine. Twenty students from 8th and 9th grade, 6 girls and 14 boys, took part in the program. After discussing the importance of collaboration with them, they formed groups to explore topics for thei r news magazines, choosing to write, create videos,  or make podcasts. Facilitators stressed the importance of criti cal thinking and digital literacy, guiding students  to check information sources and distinguish between opinion s and facts. They read and reflected on examples of  wellresearched articles and were encoura ged to examine the information they found. Students  researched topics on the internet and learned about broader aspects of t echnology. Students also interviewed community memb ers to understand their views on specific technologies. Fi nally, the students wro te their articles after synthesizing and analyzing the data they had gathered from their internet research and the community members’ interviews.

## Methodology

We interviewed students to assess their critical thinking, collaboration, and technology understanding throughout the program. We conducted initial interviews before  and after the publication of the first issue and w ill continue this process as students engage with the year-long program. Additionally, we conducted participant observations and gathered student artifacts, which will continue  for the next two issues. Our focus is on understanding how students think critically, collaborate, and reflect on technology-related issues in rural India.

## Initial findings

While long-term data collection on students' critical thinking, collaboration skills, and technology understanding is ongoing, we've noted progress through initial interviews, observing participants, and their investigation process. Students exhibited critical thinking skills and und erstanding of technology through the topics they ch ose and the analysis they presented in their final products. Fo r example, students who were writing about plastics  were able to understand the nature of the technology and thro ugh critical thinking skills asked several complex questions. Through scaffolding provided by the facilitators, the students asked ‘If plastic is banned what happens to the millions of people who are employed in the plastic industry?’. In another example, students discussing car technologies thought and wrote ‘the overall annual cost of owning a car needs to add in the cost of maintenance into the price of the car to buy it’. These conversations happened as a part of the program as we created space for students to think and learn about issues related to computing and ICTs.

## Conclusion and implications

Our initial observations suggest that the students are slowly evolving to understand the different aspects of technology design and use. Students are also lea rning about how rural communities use technologies and the social justice aspects associated with technology d esign and use. As we  move forward with the program we are hoping that the students start to think about some of the solutions that need to be in place to make s ure rural communities can use technologies that improve their everyday lives.
