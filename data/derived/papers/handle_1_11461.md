# Designing Project-Based Learning Professional Development: Learning PBL Through a PBL Approach

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines an approach to teacher professional development (PD) that supports the mastery of project-based learning (PBL) in K-12 education. The "Learn PBL through a PBL Approach" PD program, integrated with a custom-designed "PBL Action Kit", facilitated 127 teachers' deep engagement with the essential PBL design elements. Analysis of 18 PBL units developed by the teachers revealed strong incorporation of the core elements, except for the "Reflection" element. The findings underscore the importance of aligning teacher PD with instructional methods and using educative curriculum materials to enhance teachers' pedagogical design capacity, thereby promoting the effective implementation of PBL.

## Introduction

Project-based Learning (PBL) is a teaching method  aimed at increasing student engagement and deepenin g their knowledge across subjects, supported by cognitive s cience theories and empirical evidence (Krajcik & S hin, 2022). This method is gaining recognition as an inn ovative teaching approach. In 2022, Ch ina's Ministry of Education included PBL as a key component in the "C ompulsory Education Curriculum Program and Standard s (2022 Edition)" to boost core academic competencies  (Liu, 2022), leading to supportive regional polici es for its adoption. Teachers ad opting PBL need diverse skills in identifying core concepts, restructuring materials, designing PBL units and managing decentralized classrooms, which requires specific professional development (PD) (Zhao, 2024). PD's ineffectiveness is often due to its theoretical nature and passive delivery (Loyalka, et al., 2019). Recent PD efforts include interactive discus sions, workshops and collaborative learning, yet th e effectiveness of these methods for PBL remains unde r review. This study aims to find an effective PD a pproach to help teachers excel in PBL, offering practical insights for K-12 implementation. Design In July 2023, teachers from 25 schools in Zhejiang Province would engage in a Project-based Learning (PBL) professional development (PD) program to learn and subsequently implement this teaching method in thei r schools. The PBL teacher PD design process will be detailed in terms of content, format and educative curriculum materials.

Content of teacher professional development In June 2023, a field study was conducted at four schools actively engaged in this PD project. Through interviews with educators and students, insights into the desi gn and practical application of the PBL methodology  were gathered. This empirical research, supplemented by literature reviews (Zhao, 2024; Bo, 2024), revealed  two primary categories of challenges that teachers encounter when learning and implementing PBL. The first category, knowledge-related challenges, includes understanding the fundamental elements of a PBL method and identifying its specific manifestations within the classroom setting. The second category, methodological challenges, involves difficulties in initiating projects, formulating effective driving questions, setting clear educational objectives and assessment criteria, supporting students in conducting t horough investigations and reflections, and cultiva ting essential collaborative skills among learners.

To enhance the effectiveness of PBL implementation, the Buck Institute for Education has formulated a comprehensive model grounded in scholarly research.  This model incorporates seven essential project de sign elements: Challenging Problem or Question, Sustaine d Inquiry, Authenticity, Student  Voice & Choice, Reflection, Critique & Revision and Public Product,  all oriented around specific learning objectives (Larmer & Mergendoller, 2015). This model is designed to addr ess the challenges teachers face in implementing PB L in a thorough and systematic way, making it an appropriate choice for PD co ntent. Moreover, the research team has elaborated on each element of the model in Table 1,  utilizing their extensive experience in PBL design  and implementation to provide concise explanations. The learning content of PD focuses on the seven essential project design elements centered on learning goals as well as the r elevant descriptive details. The aim is to develop teachers' skills in fully utilizing these design elements. Format of teacher professional development Desimone's (2009) research identifies five core fea tures (content focus, active learning, coherence, d uration and collective participation) that are essential for ef fective professional development. These features al ign closely with the theoretical foundations of PBL, which are rooted in four major constructs from the learning sciences: 1) situated learning, 2) active construction, 3) social interactions, and 4) cognitive tools (Krajcik & Shin, 2022). This alignment makes the "Learn PBL through a PBL Approa ch" an effective model for teacher PD. Teachers wil l engage in a full PBL experience, experiencing all design elements firsthand and learning how to design PBL. For instance：experts and school administrators are invited to the final presentation to provide professional feedback on each PBL design, which is the application of Authenticity ；middle school students participate in providing feedback on project design ，which is the application of Critique & Revision. Additionally, involving multiple teachers from each school supports the creation of a collaborative learning environment. The PD will be conducted over three days during a summer institute, followed  by ongoing guidance to ensure teachers receive con tinuous support.

Educative curriculum materials Educative curriculum materials support both teacher  and student learning, helping teachers expand thei r knowledge, visualize pedagogical decisions, and better utilize personal and embedded resources (Davis & Krajcik, 2005). Integrating these materials with professional devel opment activities enhances their impact on teaching practices (Pringle, Mesa, & Hayes, 2017). In response, the research team developed the "PBL Action Kit", which includes a product manual, design map, five types o f cards (Learning Goal cards, 57 Learning Activitie s, 63 Toolkits, 25 Teamwork Tips and Flexible Cards), and  templates for both single-discipline and interdisciplinary project designs (here is a design sample at https://s2.yungu.org/kit/example.pdf). The PBL Action Kit supports teacher development by offering design examples that foster creativity and expand knowledge through Learning Activities, Toolk its and Teamwork Tips. The Design Map and cards hel p visualize essential elements, improving PBL design skills. Learning Goa l cards accommodate various themes, academic backgrounds, and student needs, while Flex ible Cards enable personalized content, ensuring adaptability in diverse teaching contexts.

Implementation Teacher PD was delivered through an intensive summer institute, with ongoing support during the semester. In July 2023, 127 educators from 25 schools in Zhejian g Province participated in a three-day institute. Teachers, organized into groups of four from the same school,  engaged as learners in PBL sessions, participating  in interactive and inquiry-based activities such as case studies and group discussions to enhance their understanding of key project design elements. Simultaneously, the y took on the role of course designers, collaborati vely developing project plans, which were subsequently refined through feedback from peers and experts. ## Results

At the end of the summer institute, 77 educators ev aluated the professional development (PD) session u sing reflection forms. 80% of the teachers (n=62) confirmed that the PD met their professional and career needs, while 19% (n=15) reported it generally met their needs. N otably, 98% (n=76) found the PBL Action Kit crucial  for designing project-based learning units. One teacher highlighted that this tool enables  PBL integration with local cultural characteristics, providing clear learning scaffolds for students. A principal from a less dev eloped rural area noted that it promotes innovative thinking and supports teachers transitioning to student-led learning models. Within a month of the institute, 18 schools submitt ed 18 PBL units, analyzed to assess adherence to th e essential project design elements from the PD (see Table 1). The analysis showed 44% of the schools (n=8) fully met the learning objectives and integrated all essential PBL elements, another 44% (n=8) achieved the objectives and incorporated six elements, and 11% (n=2) includ ed five elements. "Reflec tion" was the least implemented element, included by only 55% of the schools (n=10) in their PBL designs.

## Discussion and relevance

The strong endorsement of the "Learn PBL through a PBL Approach" in PD underscores the efficacy of PD programs that blend theory with practical applicati on in fulfilling the professional growth needs of t eachers. Engaging in an actual PBL project allows teachers t o experience firsthand the construction of a studen t-centered learning environment and to directly comprehend the  design logic and key components of PBL units.  This deep engagement prompts teachers to reflect on their own learning processes and instructional practices, thus enhancing the scientific rigor and suitability of their cours e designs. This study offers a crucial insight into  teacher professional development, highlighting the need to ensure PD aligns with classroom practices and both local and national educational standards (Borko, Jacobs, & Koellner, 2010). Additionally, when implementing PD focused on instructional methods, it is vital to maintain consistency between the learning content provided to teachers and the pedagogical approaches being taught. Designing teacher training that closely adheres to the fundam ental elements of the instructional method in question ca n substantially aid teachers in effectively masteri ng this approach.

Most schools incorporated at least six essential de sign elements into their PBL units, demonstrating t he effectiveness of aligning professional development with PBL’s essential elements to enhance teachers' curriculum design skills. However, "Reflection" was the least integrated element, with only 55% of schools (n=10) including it in their PBL units.  Reflection plays a pivotal role in PBL by deepening  student inquiry and fostering self-regulation and metacognitive skills. Its limited pr esence may indicate a gap in teachers’ understandin g of its importance or a lack of practical strategies for its implementation. To address this, future training should focus on the design and facilitation of reflective activities, incorporating concrete examples and adaptable tools to support teachers in incorporating this critical element into their instructional plans. The PBL Action Kit effectively addresses the balance between providing sufficient support and fostering autonomy in using educative curriculum materials, as highlighted by Davis and Krajcik (2005). By merging theory with practical application, the kit equi ps teachers with structured resources that alleviat e cognitive load, boost confidence, and facilitate skill development, while  also promoting their autonomy in creating learning experiences. For example, during the Summer Institu te, teacher groups with the same learning goals and student profiles utilized different activities and tools from the kit to meet these goals effectively. The kit’s Flexible Cards promote innovation, allowing teachers to modify existing materials to craft unique PBL units, suitable for varying class sizes, learning objectives, or student needs. This modularity, akin to constructing with LEGO blocks, enables adherence to predefined templates or the creation of entirely new educational designs, enhancing teachers' ability to make pedagogical decisions and innovate, in line  with Davis and Krajcik’s (2005) vision of improvin g pedagogical design capacity.
