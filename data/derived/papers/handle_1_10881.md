# Research-Practice Partnership as a Context for Teachers’ Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examined how participation in a Research-practice partnership (RPP) initiative contributes to teachers’ professional learning and growth. Twenty teachers and ten school principals were interviewed, focusing on their professional experiences within the RPP. Qualitative data analysis yielded three categories: Development of knowledge and skills; personal, professional growth; and RPP as a framework for professional learning. The findings underscore the significant role of the RPP as a context for teachers' professional learning.

## Introduction and theoretical background

Issues related to teachers learning, and identification of characteristics of effective professional learning have been a longstanding focus of educational research (e.g.,Darling-Hammond et al., 2017). Effective professional learning is associated with active teacher involvement, respecting and building upon teachers’ knowledge and expertise, and engaging them as collaborators in their own learning. Researchers in the learning sciences introduced the participatory view of teacher learning, where researchers and teachers collaborate to advance educational practice. These models are known as research-practice partnerships (RPPs). RPPs are long-term collaborations between practitioners and researchers aimed at investigating problems of practice for schools’ improvement (Coburn et al., 2013). RPPs bring together partners from across the educational field, including researchers, policy makers, and practit ioners, who collectively learn and work to improve educational practices. RPPs are adaptable to changing contexts, serving as a promising approach to integrating research into educational practice (Coburn & Penuel, 2016). RPPs create fertile ground for knowledge exchange, allowing researchers and teachers to collaboratively construct new knowledge and resources. RPPs can inform decision making, address problems of practice, and improve outcomes. Research on RPPs focuses on outcomes and on the effects of interventions developed within RPPs contexts (Coburn & Penuel, 2016). Many studies focus on student learning, support mechanisms for teachers, and curriculum materials design. Some explore changes in teachers' practices, or the development of teachers' skills (e.g., Gu & Xu, 2022). Collectively, this research underscores the significant potential of RPPs to facilitate transformative learning, particularly for teacher s. While existing research has assess ed changes in teachers' practices and knowledge integration, there remains limited understanding of the nature of learning wit hin RPPs (Benichou et al., 2022). The current study addresses this aspect.

## The study’s context: The RPP initiative

The study took place in the context of an RPP aimed at educational improvement through engagement with schoolbased research. Two higher education institutions in Israel partnere d with 21 schools. Each school established a research lab where teachers examined aspects of the  school's practices. The number of participating te achers in each school varied. Each school was assigned one  faculty member from a higher education institution who collaborated with teachers in various aspects of the school’s research project. The partnership was not bound to a specific research methodology or topic but was buil t through adaptation to the schools and their needs. Research was generated by teachers and supported by faculty in a mutual process that placed practice and field-based knowledge at the center. As part of the partnership, school principals, participating teachers from all schools and faculty from the higher education institutions met as a collaborative once a month to discuss issues r elated to the research. The study reported herein is part of a research program, developed at the outset of the RPP.

## Methods

Using a case-study of one RPP, this study explored how participation in an RPP initiative has contribu ted to the professional learning and growth of the teachers involved.  Twenty teachers and 10 school principals from 14 schools participated in the research. The institutional IRB approved all research procedures. Data collection used semi-structured interviews. Participants were asked about their expectations of the partnership, their involvement in school-based research, the alignment of their partnership participation with their initial expectations, and  the personal and professional benefits they derived fro m their involvement. The school principals were asked about the professional learning of the teachers. The interviews were recorded and transcribed for analysis. Data analysis employed a qualitative thematic inductive methodology, following Braun & Clarke (2006): (1) familiarization with the data, (2) generation of initial codes, (3) identification of themes, (4) reviewing of themes, (5) defining the themes, and (6) reaching the final categorization theme. To enhance the research rigor, external checking was conducted, facilitated through peer critique.

## Results

Data analysis yielded three general categories: (1) Knowledge and skill development, including: enhanci ng the ability to establish pedagogical objectives with pr ecision; refin ing pedagogic practice; fostering reflective practice; deepening expertise in the research domai n; and enhancing proficiency in research methods and data analysis. (2) Personal professional growth, including: cultivating a sense of purpose and satisfaction; developing a sense of empowerment and increased abilities; development of confidence; and nurturing curiosity and interest.

(3) RPP as a framework for teachers' professional learning, including: relevance of the school-research process to

educational practice; establishment of teachers’ professional learning community; engagement of infl uential change agents in the school; and cultivation of research-oriented perspective, positioning schools as learning and research-focused organizations. ## Conclusions

These findings point to RPP as a significant context for teachers' professional learning. RPP facilitates meaningful experiences, the acquisition of knowledge and skill s, and the practical application of these skills in pedagogical contexts. RPP affords teachers with opportunities to engage in inquiry, and reflection concerning significant issues in their teaching context. This framework nurtures and reinforces teachers' professional self-concept. For teachers, participation in the RPP represents a unique opport unity for the development of fresh and innovative conceptualizations of their own work as inherently meaningful. These characteristics have been identified as pivotal in effective professional learning programs (Darling-Hammond et al., 2017).
