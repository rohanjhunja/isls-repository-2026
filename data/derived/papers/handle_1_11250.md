# Online Learning Strategy Transition in Blended Courses: Learning Analytics Based on Two Cases in Higher Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Effective online learning strategies consistently correlate with improved learning performance across learning science and learning analytics. However, students' learning strategies are not static, they evolve through a dynamic process of development and refinement. Despite this established knowledge, research examining the process of strategic shifts and their impact on learning outcomes remains limited. To address this gap, this study analyzed 298,773 behavior log entries and final grade data from 346 learners across two identically designed blended courses. Using K-Means clustering, we identified six distinct learning strategies and tracked their patterns over time. Results revealed that video interaction, comment engagement, progress management, and reply behaviors were the dominant strategies. Notably, transition from a cramming to a deep learning strategy trajectory correlated with significant improvements in final grades.

## Introduction

Blended learning has become increasingly prevalent in higher education, integrating traditional face-to-face instruction with online learning. In these hybrid e nvironments, learners' capacity to effectively self-regulate through appropriate strategies is especially critic al during online self-directed components, substantially influencing academic success (Saqr, Matcha, et al., 2023). Recent research has examined various online learning strategies, including metacognitive approaches such  as planning, monitoring, and reflection; social co gnitive strategies like forum participation; and resource i nteraction strategies involving video en gagement (Fan et al., 2023). While these investigations have yielded valuable in sights, emerging evidence suggests that students' strategic approaches are not static but rather evol ve dynamically over time (Saqr, López-Pernas, et al., 2023). Students need to adaptively employ different strate gies as they progress through their coursework and encounter new learning contexts within blended environments. Although adaptive learning strategy use is crucial,  there is limited research on how strate gy use develops, especially within the online components of blended learning environments in higher education (Jovanović et al., 2023). There is a need for more longitudinal studies that track strategy development and transfer across multiple c ourses for the same group of learners. Learning ana lytics approaches applied to digital trace data can reveal  these strategy development processes  and evaluate how they relate to academic performance (Matcha et al., 2022).

To address this gap, this research aims to investig ate how learning strategies transfer over time for the same group of learners during online learning of bl ended courses in higher education. We examine the l earning strategy trajectories of 346 students across two consecutive blended courses with similar instructional designs and content, focusing on their online learning behavior s. Using 298,773 behavioral log during online learn ing and final grade data, K-Means clustering and sequence analysis via TraMine R are applied to identify and analyze students' strategy transition patterns.

## Results

After applying K-Means clustering to 5,036 data points across 13 beh avioral events, we identified six distinct strategy clusters with clear differentiation and mi nimal overlap (Figure 5). Table 3 presents these six typical interaction patterns and their specific indicators,  reflecting diverse student engagement modes and le arning preferences.

Figure 6 illustrates the distinct strategy trajecto ries across both courses, with C1 featuring Compliance (N=236, consistent interaction decreasing over time), Social (N=85, intensive commenting throughout), and Cramming (N=25, minimal early engagement with exam-week spikes) patterns. C2 exhibits Diminishing Engagement (N=72, strong initial interaction that weakens), Process Interaction (N=173, consistent engagement with content and comments), and Deep Learning (N=101, intensive balanced interaction throughout w ith strong end-of-course activity) trajectories. Variance analysis of trajectory transitions revealed no significant relationship with individual course scores in C1 or C2 (p>0.05), but showed significant correlation with score changes between the two courses (p<0.05). The Cramming—Deep Learning transition produced the greatest grade improvement, suggesting learners effectively applied previous sp rint experience toward better planning strategies. Conversely, the Cramming—Process Interaction  transition correlated with grade declines, likely d ue to insufficient video interaction and comment engagement before final exams compared to the more effective Deep Learning approach. ## Conclusion

Research results show that learners transitioning f rom a Cramming Trajectory to a Deep Learning Trajectory achieved the most significant academic improvement,  while those shifting to a Process Interaction Trajectory experienced declining grades. This likely reflects self-regulation as learners recognize the limitations of cramming and attempt different approaches. The successful tr ansition maintained active interaction throughout t he course with increased content rev iew before finals, while the unsuccessful approach emphasized process but neglected pre-exam review. These findings suggest special attention should be given to guiding Cramming learners toward balanced approaches that combine process and outcome orientation. Research findings align with previous studies on self-regulated learning in online environments, showing that time management and metacognitive strategies positively impact academic achievement (Broadbent & Poon,

2015) and that students adopting deep learning approaches  tend to achieve higher grades (Saqr, Matcha, et al.,

2023). The observed strategy transitions from Cramming Trajectory to Deep Learning Trajectory  demonstrate learners' adaptation to online environments and development of metacognitive abilities, which can be interpreted as part of the identity transformation process desc ribed in cognitive apprenticeship theory (Collins et al., 1991), involving the internalization of knowledge, skills,  and online learning cultural practices. While not all strategy transitions improve performance  (Azevedo et al., 2013), these findings highlight the importance of suppor ting students in developing effective learning strategie s and suggest opportunities for creating adaptive l earning environments with personalized guidance leveraging advances in learning analytics.
