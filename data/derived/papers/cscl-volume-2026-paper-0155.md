# Introducing IDEAL: An Intelligent Design Environment for Active Learning

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Text-based curriculum tools inadequately represent spatial, temporal, and interactive dimensions of active learning design in active learning classrooms. Although generative AI offers accessible visualization options, existing approaches prioritize aesthetic plausibility over spatial correspondence with real classrooms. This poster presents the AI-assisted storyboarding component of IDEAL (Intelligent Design Environment for Active Learning), developed over three years of iterative design from 2022 to 2025. Our photobook-based approach constrains AI generation using systematic documentation of a specific classroom, including furniture inventory, technology layout, and spatial configurations, producing planning artifacts that help teachers judge the spatial pl ausibility of instructional scenarios before implementation. Exploratory validation with one experienced instructor suggests proof-of-concept feasibility, while also surfacing design tensions around fixation and cognitive load.

Equipped with flexible furniture and digital technologies, active learning classrooms (ALCs) support innovative pedagogical practices. Educators seek to design more interactive and contextualized learning experiences in these environments.  Learning design research positions teachers as designers (Wasson & Kirschner, 2020); however, teachers’ spatial competency remains a constraining factor in designing curriculum that can effectively leverage learning environments (Byers, 202 1). Diagrammatic tools (e.g., L DVS, LAMS, Learning Designer) support alignment and collaboration (Agostinho, 2011; Conole & Wills, 2013; Dalziel et al., 2016), and storyboarding adds audience-facing narratives (Jacobs & Zmuda, 2023). But existing tools fail  to capture spatial orchestration, for example how activities unfold across room configurations, technology placements, and student movement. Although generative AI opens new visualization possibilities, current applications prioritize inspiration over spatial reasoning.

We introduced Intelligent Design Environment for Active Learning (IDEAL), an emerging design support system that integrates spatial, technological, and pedagogical considerations for active learning curriculum design. This poster focuses on its AI-assisted storyboarding component, which addresses a crucial challenge: teachers designing for active learning need to anticipate whether a specific activity is feasible in a particular classroom, accounting for spatial capacity and constraints, circulation paths  and furniture orientation. Generic text-to-image AI prompts produce aesthetically plausible but spatially unanchored outputs that vary arbitrarily across panels. The component therefore grounds AI generation in authentic classroom documentation, producing storyboards that support pedagogy-space fit reasoning. This poster explores two questions: (1) How can AI support the creation of pictorial storyboards to represent spatial-temporal dimensions of active learning in support of teachers’ design process? (2) How can such AI-generated storyboards support teachers in curriculum design?

Iterative development: from 3D modeling to AI-assisted storyboarding The IDEAL storyboarding component evolved through four phases from 2022, shifting from managing technical complexity toward integrating AI with architectural spatial documentation. Initial 3D modeling with Rhino (Figure 1) demonstrated the value of spatial-temporal visualization but required prohibitive specialized expertise. Generic AI generators (DALL-E 2, Midjourney) lacked spatial authenticity, producing spaces disconnected from real classrooms (Figure 2a). Photoshop AI improved background consistency using real classroom photo base, yet furniture misalignment persisted (Figure 2b). Combining Unreal Engine with DALL-E further improved spatial consistency but introduced inconsistent scale across panels (Figure 2c). Across all phases, the core problem was not tool selection but the absence of systematic spatial documentation. Phase 3 therefore introduced the Classroom Reference Library: seven photographs systematically documenting one active learning classroom, capturing all architectural features and const raints, furniture inventory, and technology infrastructure (Table 1). A five-stage workflow bridges teacher intentions and spatially grounded generation: capturing design intentions, identifying spatial decisions, constructing narrative sequences, matching  panels to reference library images, and generating images via Gemini 2.5 Flash (Figure 3).

## Exploratory validation and conclusion

To assess feasibility, we generated storyboards based on an experienced instructor's graduate course design. The instructor noted that the storyboard "made the flow between multi-screen displays and discussion zones concrete" and helped visualize "who does what when with which technology." While this single exploratory case cannot be generalized, it provides proof-of-concept that photobook-based contextualization addresses authentic design challenges in active learning environments. Design tensions remain: highly realistic outputs may encourage fixation over iterative exploration, and multi-stage workflows may increase cognitive load for novice designers. IDEAL is still in progress, validated in only one classroom with one instructor; transferability, long-term use, and scalability remain untested.
