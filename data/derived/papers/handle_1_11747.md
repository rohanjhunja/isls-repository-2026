# The Relationship between Learning Sentiments and Cognitive Engagement in MOOC Discussion Forums

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Learning sentiments and cognitive engagement are crucial indicators of learning performance in MOOC discussion forums. While their relationship has not been thoroughly investigated in MOOC contexts, this study examined the impact of learning sentiments on cognitive engagement. Data from 220 participants (college students and K-12 teachers) generated 4,341 discussion posts across three forum types (analysis, reflection and discussion, and creation) based on Bloom’s Taxonomy. Using the Interactive-Constructive-Active-Passive (ICAP) framework, findings revealed that creation forums fostered diverse sentiment patterns and higher constructive engagement. While active engagement dominated across forums, positive and negative sentiments correlated with active engagement, insightful sentiments with constructive engagement, and confused sentiments with interactive engagement. These findings inform MOOC discussion design to enhance meaningful learning engagement.

## Introduction and theoretical foundations

Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) have transformed higher education but often lead to reduced interaction and minimal cognitive engagement (Liu et al., 2022). Online discussion forums address these challenges  by fostering meaningful interactions (Crossley et al., 2017; Cheung et al., 2008). While researchers have studied cognitive engagement  in online discussions and emotions ’ impact on learning achievement, existing studies have primari ly focused on basic emotional states (positive, neg ative) and their general learning outcomes. The complex interp lay between diverse emotional expressions and cogni tive engagement behaviors remains thoroughly unexplored (Liu et al., 2022). Learning sentiments-reflecting attitudes toward learning topics during interactions-offer a framework for understanding emotional expressions in learning contexts (Ortigosa et al., 2014). This study adopts Zheng and Huang’s (2016) six-dimensional sentiment model (positive, negative, ne utral, insightful, confused, and joking), which captures nuanced learning-specific sentiments beyond basic affective states. To analyze cognitive engagement, this study  employed the Interactive-Constructive-Active-Passive (ICAP) framework (Chi & Wylie, 2014), which defines four modes of engagement: passive (receiving information without overt actions), active (visible actions lik e note-taking), constructive (producing outputs beyond giv en material), and interactive (meaningful peer dialogu e leading to knowledge co-construction). While this study primarily employs the ICAP framework, it acknowledg es that sociocultural theories of learning and distributed cognition could offer complementary perspectives on  how emotional expressions influence collaborative knowledge construction in online environments.

This study addresses the following research questio ns: (1) What are the patterns of students ’ cognitive engagement across different types of MOOC discussio n forums?  (2) How are learning sentiments distributed across different types of MOOC discussion forums? (3) To what extent do learning sentiments correlate with and predict cognitive engagement patterns in online discussions?

## Methodology

This study examined “The Application of Mind Maps in Teaching, ” a MOOC with 220 participants generating 4,341 discussion posts. Using Bloom ’s Taxonomy (Anderson et al., 2001), forums were categorized into three types: creation (new information construction), analysis (critical assessment), and reflection (experience sharing). The researcher  developed two coding schemes: (1) a six-dimensional learning sentiments framework (positive, negative, neutral, insightful, confused,  joking) and (2) a cognitive engagement scheme base d on the ICAP framework (active: paraphrasing, simple questi ons; constructive: elaborating, knowledge integrati on; interactive: argumentation, meaningful discussion).  Coders identified sentiment categories through con textual cues: insightful posts showed reflective connection s (“I realize how to apply this in my  class”), while confused posts expressed uncertainty (“I'm unsure how this differs from traditional mappin g”). Two researchers independently coded posts, achieving high inter-rater reliability (κ = 0.87). Data analysis employed descriptive statistics and one-way ANOVA tests to examine engagement patterns and sentiment distributions across forum types (RQ1, RQ2). For RQ3, Chi-square tests and multinomial logistic regression analyzed how learning sentiments correlated with and predicted cognitive engagement patterns.

## Discussion and conclusion

This study revealed key relationships between learn ing sentiments and cognitive engagement in MOOC for ums: positive sentiments correlated with active engagement, insightful with constructive, and confused with interactive engagement.

The study uncovered distinctive relationships betwe en learning sentiments and cognitive engagement: positive sentiments correlated with active engageme nt, insightful sentiments with constructive engagem ent, and confused sentiments with interactive engagement. Th is challenges traditional views of confusion, align ing with research showing cognitive disequilibrium can catalyze meaningful interactions (Bosch & D’Mello, 2014). When learners express confusion, they invite collaborati ve problem-solving, creating o pportunities for collective meaning-making.

These findings have implications for addressing educational equity challenges. By recognizing diverse emotional responses-including confusion-as valuable for engagement, MOOC designers can crea te more inclusive learning environments that validate diffe rent ways of engaging with content, potentially red ucing achievement gaps in digital learning contexts.
