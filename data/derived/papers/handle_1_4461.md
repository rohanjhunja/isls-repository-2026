# Exploring Teaching and Course Assistants’ Interventions with Groups During Collaborative Problem-Solving

**Conference:** CSCL 2019

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores the task-related interventions of a teaching assistant and three course assistants with six small groups during collaborative problem-solving activities in undergraduate engineering classrooms. Results indicated that the majority of the interventions were not preceded by monitoring the group activity and were initiated by asking the groups a general question about their activity or progress on the task. Our analysis in the CSCL environment revealed that unprompted, proactive contributions are crucial for effective collaborative problem solving. By examining student behavior in the HoloOrbits simulation, we developed a model of collaborative initiative that distinguishes between task-related and dialogue-based contributions. While existing frameworks, such as OECD’s PISA (2017), emphasize establishing and maintaining shared understanding—aligning with aspects of our model, such as seeking help or inviting contributions—they do not explicitly account for the spontaneity of unprompted actions. Hesse et al. (2015) address social skills and “responsibility initiative” in the context of maintaining task progress, yet our model emphasizes novel, self-initiated actions that emerge without external prompting. Similarly, Sun et al. (2020) highlight shared knowledge construction and negotiation/coordination; nonetheless, their work primarily focuses on response-driven dialogue. In contrast, our approach identifies unprompted contributions, such as sharing divergent ideas and exploring the problem space, as distinct markers of effective collaboration. This nuanced categorization distinguishes between responsive behaviors and truly spontaneous initiatives, ultimately deepening our understanding of how students actively shape both task performance and collaborative discourse in CSCL environments.

This study contributes to the field of collaborative learning by highlighting the critical role of unprompted, proactive contributions in CSCL environments. By distinguishing between task-related and dialogue-based facets of collaborative initiative, our model offers a refined lens for analyzing student interactions and provides actionable insights for designing more responsive learning environments. These findings suggest that encouraging spontaneous, unprompted initiative can support more dynamic, participatory forms of collaboration. Future research should focus on operationalizing these behaviors and exploring how they can be fostered through instructional design. Ultimately, this framework supports the development of educational strategies that not only facilitate but actively promote dynamic, participatory learning and cultivate student agency in CPS.
