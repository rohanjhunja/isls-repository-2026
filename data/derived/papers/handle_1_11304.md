## Introduction

Over two decades ago, Lave and Wenger introduced an image of learning that remains popular to this day (Lave & Wenger, 1991; Wenger, 1998). In that image, learners are viewed as participating in a community of practice, and learning is seen as a movement from the periphe ry of that community toward the center, where they become full participants. That image is a very open one; it leaves room for these trajectories of participation to vary greatly across communities of practice. It is possible, for example, that in some communiti es of practice, learners will immediately begin to move toward the center quickly. Alternatively, it is possible that  learners would need to participate for a time, remaining at the periphery, before they begin to move toward full participation. These two possibilities are represented by the two plots shown in Figure 1. There are, of course, many other possibilities. Figure 1 Hypothetical Participation Trajectories Researchers have begun to produce analyses of just this sort through the study of online communities (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil, West, Jurafsky, Leskovec, & Potts, 2013; Ngu yen & Rosé, 2011; Rowe, 2013)  The metrics employed are typically measures of language usage, and they capture how the language of users comes to more closely align with language of the larger community. In this work, we draw on a corpus of data from the online forums on Reddit, which includes text from roughly the top 20,000 sub-communities, spanning June 2005 to December 2022. Reddit hosts t ens of thousands of sub-communities known as subreddits, with foci that range from physics to pictures of ca ts. The broad aim of our larger project is to develop  techniques for capturing learning  trajectories and to apply them to these varied communities. Ultimately, we seek to map the kinds of different trajectories that exist and to trace them to features of the subreddits, such as the topic of focus, and the makeup of the community. Our aims for this short paper are more modest. To date we have completed an analysis of 14 subreddits. We will explain the methods that we employ to capture trajectories, and we will show that the methods can indeed capture qualitative differences in trajectories acr oss subreddit communities. As we explain below, our methods build explicitly on prior work that also sought to capture learning trajectories in online communities. The work in this paper is novel, we believe, in that it seeks t o identify qualitative differences in learning trajectories across diverse communities.

## Background

Researchers have studied language change in online communities since the earliest days of the internet and online platforms (Bruckman, 2006; Cassell & Tversky, 2005; Lam, 2008). These early studies looked at relatively small communities and typically employed manual analysis techniques. More recently, researchers have begun to use computational techniques, thus facilitating the study of larger communities. As computational analysis of online communities has spread, R eddit and its subcommunities have been the subject of a wide range of research (Medvedev, Lambiotte, & Delvenne, 2019; Proferes, J ones, Gilbert, Fiesler, & Zimmer, 2021)  This is, in part, because there is a large and publicly available dat aset available in multiple forms (Labov & William, 2020). But it is also because the breadth of topics and communities facilitates a wide range of types of investigations. Some research, for example, has sought to measure characteristics of subreddits, such as specificity and volatility (Zhang, Hamilton, Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil, Jurafsky, & Leskovec, 2017). There has also  been research that has looked, in various ways, at how t he language of users changes as they participate in  an online community, both on Reddit and other similar forums. Much of this research has explicitly adopted, as we  do, a community of practice view. For example, Nguyen & Rosé  (2011) examined the trajectories of learning in an online breast cancer support community.  They showed that, over time, the language of users becomes more like the language of more experienced participants. Other studies sought to look close ly at the shape of learning trajectories (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., 2013; Rowe, 2013)  For example, Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil studied two online communities focused on the discussion of beer. Using a range of complementary analyses, they argued that learners in these communities follow a u-shaped trajectory, where they first move toward, th en away from, the prevailing language of the community.

## Methods

Our methods expand on these earlier studies; we produce a variety of plots, each providing a differ ent window into learning trajectories, and we apply them to subreddits of diverse types. All our analyses follow the same general scheme. First, we pool posts based on when they are made in the life of the user. Second, we measure some language property of these pools of posts. A plot of the property versus the pool number gives us a learning trajectory. However, multiple choices are possible for each of the axes in our diagram. The x-axis What choices are possible for the x-axis? One obvious possibility is time; for each post we could look at the date it was posted and  record the time between this post and the user’s fi rst post in the subreddit. However, this approach might give a user too much credit for exposure to the subreddit if th ey have not been visiting the subreddit during the time between posts. An alternative is to use the number of posts made by a use r, up to the time of the current post,  as an index of their exposure (Gangemi, Leonardi, Panconesi, Tan, & Lee, 2015). This approach has the opposite problem; it gives users no credit for time simply looking at a subreddit. A third approach in the literature has been to empl oy a lifecycle approach (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., 2013; Gangemi et al., 2015; Rowe, 2013). The idea  is to take all a user’s posts and to divide them into a number (usually twenty) of equally sized bins. The intuition here is that every user, regardless of how long the y stay in the subreddit, and how many times they post, follows a similar lifecycle. Finally, we introduce our own novel measure for the x-axis, which seeks to combine some elements of time and number of posts. The y-axis For the y-axis, some  researchers have employed  measures of relatively simple statistical properties of user language. In most cases, these measures boil down to looking at whether users at a certain developmental age use words with frequencies that align with some reference norm (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil, Gamon, & Dumais, 2011; Huffaker, Jorgensen, Iacobelli, Tepper, & Cassell, 2006; Lucy & Bamman, 2021; Nguyen & Rosé, 2011; Sharma & Choudhury, 2018). We follow a group of r esearchers who create snapshot language models  for each month that a subreddit is in existence (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2017). In order to deal with bigrams that do not appear in the training data, we employ a Katz-Backoff model with Good-Turing smoothing (August, Card, Hsieh, Smith, & Reinecke, 2020; Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2017). We compute two snapshot language models for each month. The first mod el uses posts sampled from the full set of posts for the month. We will call this model the community model. The second model samples posts only from those by a user who had experience above a certain threshold at the time the post was made. We will refer to this as the pro model.

## The methods, step-by-step

Pragmatically our method proceed s as follows for each subreddit. We begin by cleaning the data, removing an y URLs from posts, and tokenizing the text of each po st. Next, we gather data on all unique users in the subreddit and identify users who made more than 50 total posts. We then label all posts according to where they are in the life of the user. Here, and in what follows, a post can be a submission to a subreddit that initiates discussion  of a topic, or any top-level comments on that initial submission (Lucy & Bamman, 2021; Zhang et al., 2017). Next, we create the snapshot models. For each month of the subreddit, we select 500 random users who posted during that month, and then randomly choose two posts by each of these users. For the community model, these posts are drawn from all posts. For the pro model, we draw only from pos ts labeled with an experience above a threshold value, here taken to be 26 weeks. The posts used in the construction of the models are not used in later steps of the analysis.

Following that, we compute the cross entropy of each post using the relevant snapshot model. Then, for each x-axis measure, we combine the posts into bins, get the average score for each bin, and plot these scores. For the lifecycle method, we exclude from our trajectory analysis any user who posted during the last year of data for the subreddit, on the theory that their lifecycle might not be complete (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., 2013). Since the creation of the snapshot models involves the random selection of posts, we repeat this process multiple times and average the results. For each su breddit, we replicate d the process 16 times each for the community and pro models. The variability across th ese runs is captured by the error bars that appear on plots later in this paper.

## Results

We begin by looking at learning trajectories for the subreddit magicTCG, where users discuss the game “Magic: The Gathering” (MTG). MTG is, at its simplest,  a card game, but the cards are collectible, with new sets being released regularly. This has led to sustained interest over decades. Figure 2 shows the life stage and post number plots for magicTCG, both using the pro language models (as opposed to the community models). The life stage plot shows a steady  decrease in cross entropy –a steady movement toward the language norm. In contrast, the posts plot goes down sharply until about post 100, where it levels off. The difference between these plots is not too difficult to reconcile. Among the users who posted more than 50 times  in this subreddit, the mean number of posts is 118.  This means that, for many users, the full life cycle resides in the first 100 posts, where we see the downward trajectory in the posts plot, Figure 2 Life Stage and Post Number Plots (pro) for MagicTCG.

The lefthand side of Figure 3 shows the experience plot for magicTCG. Here again we are seeing a steady decrease in cross entropy. This raises the question of whether all subreddits will show the same patte rn of steady decrease. To see that this is not the case, we look  at the Fitness subreddit, which describes itself a s “a place for the pursuit of physical fitness goals.” The life cy cle plots, which we will not reproduce here, are la rgely flat. However, movement is visible in the experience plot, which is shown on the righthand side of Figure 3. Here we see a shape something like the second plot in our introduction; there does not seem to be much change during the first 20 weeks of experience. But after that time, there is a decrease. Intuitively, we believed that some subreddits should show flat trajectories. Specifically, we thought that subreddits with very broad subject matter, and a po tentially very wide audience, were unlikely to show  much movement. One such subreddit was explainlikeimfive.  This subreddit is a place where people post questi ons looking for “layperson friendly explanations.” So, the possible subject matter here is very broad, as is the potential audience. The lefthand plot in Figure 4 shows the experience plot for this subreddit. Not o nly is there a clear downward trend, but the magnitude of this change is quite large, in comparison to the plots we have seen thus far. So, although the subject matter is broad, there mus t be clear, somewhat unique, norms for discourse. H owever, there were other subreddits that produced consisten tly flat plots. For example, the plots for the Cric ket subreddit were relatively flat, across the board.

## Discussion

We began this paper with the intuition that movement from the periphery of a community of practice, to the center, must vary across communities. This intuition is pro bably obvious; if there are trajectories that are characteristic of communities, it seems likely that these can be vastly different in communities of different sorts. However, it is less clear whether it is possible to capture this movement, in any meaningful and useful way, with two-dimensional plots, an x-axis measuring exposure to the commun ity, and a y-axis capturing some metric of closeness to the community center.  Building on the work of prior authors, we presented our first attempts to create such plots for some online communities. We saw that the plots do seem to show movement with exposure to a community, and there were qualitative differences in the plots for different subreddits. Ultimately, we would like to be able to say why the plots for subreddits are different. Our intuition was that the subject matter focus of the subreddit shou ld matter, in some manner. However, we are not yet able to explain the differences across any subreddits in this way. In fact, our best example a t this point is probably a negative example: our intuition predicted that the plot for the expla inlikeimfive subreddit sho uld be flat, but it instead showed dramatic movement. It seems there is  a lot of room for users to learn norms that are sp ecific to communities, even if the subject matter might seem wide and non-specialized. In the future, we do want to understand how subject matter and oth er community properties  relate to observed trajectories. There are tools in the liter ature that can help. For example, as we mentioned earlier, researchers have developed methods for capturing th e specificity and volatility of subreddit discourse  (Zhang et al., 2017) Intuitively, specificity–which measures the specificity of a subreddits language in comparison to others– seems likely to be useful in distinguishing subredd its. But it is far from certain  this will be useful in explaining differences in trajectories across subreddits.
