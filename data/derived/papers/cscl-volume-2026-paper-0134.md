# Adaptive Support for Collaborative Learning: Effects of Design Features Across Task Types

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This meta-analysis examines how adaptive instructional support facilitates collaborative learning across task types and design features, based on 80 studies. Cognitive-model approaches were most effective for factual knowledge learning, whereas data science approaches showed the strongest effects in problem-solving. Human-involved adaptation yielded smaller effects. These findings suggest adaptive instructional support is most effective when its design features align with task demand s, with data science approaches and system control being particularly effective for problem-solving tasks.

Collaborative learning  (CL) supports diverse activities ranging from  acquiring factual knowledge to jointly solving complex problems  (Baker & Reimann, 2024). Adaptive instructional support aims to tailor learning environments to learners’ needs but may not work equally across different  task types. Prior research has  often focused on a single task type  or type of instructional support, offering limited insight into which types of instructional support are effective for which tasks in CL. This meta-analysis therefore aims to investigate how key design features address specific demands of different task types in CL. Task types in CL can be distinguished into factual knowledge learning, focused on understanding and organizing domain knowledge, and problem-solving tasks  involving the application of  knowledge to address concrete problems (Lou et al., 2001; Pfister & Oehl, 2009). Because these tasks differ in complexity, regulation demands, and cognitive load, they might require different approaches to support. One key design feature is the adaptative approach that indicates how support is adjusted to learners’ needs (Plass & Pawar, 2020). Cognitive model  approaches compare learner and expert models to identify knowledge gaps and improvement paths, making them effective for knowledge co-construction (Haq et al., 2021). Data science approaches such as learning analytics detect learners’ needs from various learning data and adapt support (Plass & Pawar, 2020). Human-led adaptation  provided by teachers or tutors  benefits from their experience but may become demanding when multiple groups require timely support, especially in complex problem-solving tasks (Lund, 2004). A second design feature is decision authority, referring to whether learners, instructors, or systems regulate the amount, type, or timing of support (Kollar et al., 2018; Plass & Pawar, 2020). Learner control may enhance motivation by  providing autonomy (e.g., Radkowitsch et al., 2021), but it requires learners to strategically apply meta-cognitive skills (Vandewaetere et al., 2011). Recent evidence suggests that system-controlled adaptation often yields stronger effects (Chernikova et al., 2025), especially in problem-solving tasks, where cognitive demands may limit learners’ capacity to request support appropriately (Karich et al., 2014). To examine how  task type, adaptive approach, and decision authority  interact in shaping effectiveness, the following meta-analysis was conducted.

## Research questions

This study aims to address the following research questions: (1)  To what extent do task types m oderate the effectiveness of adaptive instructional support in CL ? (2) To what extent do approaches  to adaptation moderate the effectiveness of instructional support across varied task types in CL ? (3) To what extent does  the decision authority in adaptation influence the effectiveness of instructional support across varied task types in CL? Primary studies were retrieved from EBSCO, Web of Science, and Scopus  using the search terms (adapt* OR computer* OR cognitive tutor OR intelligent tutoring system OR personalized) AND (scaffold* OR instruct*) AND (skill OR competenc* OR performance OR knowledge OR group learning) AND (CSCL OR computer supported collaborative learning OR collaborative learning OR cooperative learning) across all search fields. All records indexed before June 8, 2023 were considered, yielding 7,907 articles after duplicate removal.  Following abstract and full-text screening, 80 studies met the inclusion criteria  (adaptive intervention, CL context, and sufficient statistical data). This meta-analysis confirms that adaptive instructional support facilitates CL in both knowledge co-construction tasks and problem-solving tasks, while its effectiveness varies depending on the interaction between task type and design features. Data science approaches showed particular potential for problem-solving tasks, consistent with previous findings (Tan et al., 2022). Cognitive model approaches were especially effective for factual knowledge tasks (Magnisalis et al., 2011). In contrast, human-led adaptation appeared  less effective in problem-solving contexts, possibly because the complexity of group interaction places high demands on facilitators, making real-time diagnosis and regulation challenging (van Leeuwen & Janssen, 2019). Although human support may have strong potential to respond to interpersonal dynamics, its effectiveness appears limited in problem-solving tasks that require  support for complex coordination and monitor ing. Learner-controlled adaptation show ed smaller effects, likely due to high cognitive load in problem-solving tasks, which leaves learners with fewer resources to adjust support (Vogel et al., 2022), despite its potential motivational benefits. Overall, adaptive support appears most effective when its design features align with task demands. These control may be particularly effective for  problem-solving tasks. A limitation of this meta-analysis is that combinations of design features were not examined due to the limited number of studies.  Future research should examine how specific configurations of adaptive approaches and decision authority differentially support factual knowledge and problem-solving tasks.
