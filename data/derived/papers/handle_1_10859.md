# Citation Analysis for “Student Engagement”

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
For 40 years scholars have published systematic reviews and entire handbooks on student engagement, though to date there has been no citation analysis of student engagement across all academic disciplines. This configurative synthesis review separates “non-English language” from “all-language” publications, and it presents finer-grained analyses of four key Categories. Visualizations of citation “bursts” and calculations of “betweenness centrality” confirm some within-and between-Category relationships previously posited, and they also reveal as-yet-undocumented relationships for further exploration.

## Introduction: Motivation and research questions

Learner engagement has interested educators since a ntiquity, with student engagement emerging as a variable of interest through school-dropout studies in the 1980’s (Reschly & Christenson, 2022). Over the past four decades, student engagement has progressed from a variable t o a construct, and – at least in grades 13-20 education – sometimes a theory, being of interest to practition ers, researchers, and policymakers as an outcome, m ediator, moderator, and predictor (Reschly & Christenson, 2022). The present study tak es a deliberately broad approach to include scholarshi p on student engagement across (sub)disciplines, languages and cultures, and academic/career stages (Galindo et al., 2022; Jackson et al., 2024). This systematic review through citation analysis wa s based on research questions recommended by Chen (2022, p. 6): (1) What are the “major areas of research” on stu dent engagement?; (2) How are those areas connected?; and (3) What, if any, are the “critical transitions in the…development of the field”?

## Methods

To better understand the literature on student enga gement, I conducted a systematic  configurative synthesis review, embracing a “purposive variety in the ident ification and selection of studies” (Newman & Gough, 2020, p. 5) that enabled exploration across traditionally siloed fields. I looked for ci tation patterns within and between disciplines, with the potential of finding connections that might be hidden in smaller-scale studies (Chen, 2022). Disciplines were operationalized as Categories from the Web of Science catalog, a resource that harmonizes breadth and accuracy (Chen, 2022). My initial search for “student engage ment” returned 13,262 records. I deliberately included all languages of publication, seeking to promote cultural and linguistic equity. As a proxy for quality, I included documents marked as  “Article”, “Book”, “Book Chapter”, or “Review Arti cle” (excluding formats such as “Editorial Material” and “Proceeding Paper”). The final sample included 9825 records in what I call the “all-language analysis”. I also performed a “non-English analysis” for the 198 studies written in journals of across 18 languages: Spanish (86), Portuguese (40), Russia n (21), French (14), German (8), Turkish (7), Chinese (5), Croatian (3), Korean (3), Iceland ic (2), Malay (2), and one each for Afrikaans, Dutc h, Italian, Japanese, Persian, Slovenian, and “Unspecified”. Finally, for a more student-centered and finer-grained treatment of connections and transitions, I ran within-Category analyses for Education & Educational Research  and the three Categories most connected to it.

I used the default settings in CiteSpace version 6.1.R3, appropriate for the exploratory nature of my study (Chen, 2022; Newman & Gough, 2020). CiteSpace takes “bibliographic records…to visualize  the underlying networks” at levels such as author, record(/citatio n), or (sub -)discipline (Chen, 2022, p. 1). Numerical o utputs include the quantity of records within a given Category (the “Count”) as well as a measure normalized between 0 and 1 to indicate the betweenness centrality for nodes of citations (the “Centrality”; Chen, 2022, p. 44). Centrality “measures the extent to which the node is part of p aths that connect an arbitrary pair of codes in the  network” (Chen, 2022, p. 44). To represent the relative impact of individual Cate gories, I used spreadsheet software to calculate ratios of Centrality to Count.

## Discussion

This systematic, configurative synthesis review (Newman & Gough, 2020)  showed connections within and between academic disciplines across 40 years  of research on student engagement. Some results were expected, such as central roles of Education & Educational Research and Psychology. On the other hand, some less expected Categories showed relatively high betweenness centrality scores, especially when considering their relatively low number of citations (e.g., Computer Science, Information Systems;  Business; Management;  etc.). The results suggest student engagement disciplines that might be underdeveloped, especially across multiple languages. Student engagement has remained relevant over 40-plus years, as its educational roots connect with a multitude of academic disciplines.  Though grades 13-20 education places student engagement at the level  of theory (largely in behavioral senses), more work is  needed between disciplines to develop theory for g rades K-12. By examining its past, scholars can forge and maintain interdisciplinary connections that improve the learning

and development of students across all ages, grades, languages, and career and civic endeavors.
