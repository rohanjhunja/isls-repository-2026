# Exploring the Effects of Integrating Online Knowledge Building Activities into Elementary Students’ Collaborative Scientific Inquiry Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores the integration of online knowledge-building (KB) activities into elementary students’ collaborative scientific inquiry learning through the “Inquiry Together” platform. Grounded in Knowledge Building Theory, the platform offers tools for collaborative idea refinement, progress monitoring, and dynamic scaffolding, addressing persistent challenges in elementary science education. 52 fifth-grade students participated in the study, divided into an experimental group using the platform and a control group engaged in conventional KB-based activities. Pre-and post-test analyses revealed improvements in both groups regarding science learning attitudes, scientific inquiry skills, and perceptions of KB-oriented learning environments. The experimental group showed significantly greater gains in science learning interest, recognition of science's value, scientific inquiry skills, and perceptions of a collaborative and agentive learning environment, attributed to the platform’s targeted scaffolding. These findings highlight the potential of "Inquiry Together" to enhance KB-based pedagogy by promoting engagement, critical thinking, and collaboration in elementary science learning.

## Introduction

Inquiry-based learning (IBL) has become a foundational appr oach in contemporary science education, fostering critical thinking, problem-solving, and the collaborative construction of knowledge. Globally, education systems have integrated inquiry into science curricula to prepare students for knowledge-based societies. Frameworks such as the U.S. Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) and the European Union’s Inquiry-Based Science Education (IBSE) initiatives highlight the essentia l role of inquiry, partic ularly through teacher professional development to improve classroom implementation. Ho wever, despite its recognized value, IBL often face s challenges in sustaining collaboration, critical thinking, and engagement. The effectiveness of IBL relies heavily on students’ ability to collaboratively generate, refine, and apply ideas, which not only deepen their understanding of  scientific concepts but also promote essential 21s t-century competencies. Knowledge Building Theory (KBT) (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 1994) provides a  robust framework for supporting these processes by encouraging students to collectively share, evaluate, and refine ideas. Although platforms such as Knowledge Forum (KF) have operationalized KBT, their application in elementary settings has been limited. Many younger learners perceive KF's i nterface as overly complex, limiting their ability to engage effectively in collaborative inquiry. Likewise, tea chers report difficulties with task management, gro up coordination, and timely scaffolding when using KF, which further constrains its implementation in elem entary education (Lai & Law, 2013).

To address these limitations, the “Inquiry Together” platform was developed, integrating KBT principles into a digital environment tailored to elementary science inquiry. Unlike traditional platforms, “Inquiry Together” features simplified operations, dyna mic visualizations of idea relationships, and intui tive tools for progress monitoring, thereby facilitating younger students’ engagement in collaborative knowledge building. For teachers, the platform offers real-time task assignment, activity monitoring, and scaffolding functions, enabling more effective management of collaborative learning.  This study investigates how such platform innovations ca n address enduring challenges in elementary inquiry-based science education. Specifically, it examines the platform’s impact on students’ science learning attitudes, scientific inquiry skills, and perceptions of collaborative learning processes. The research focuses on the following questions:

1. How does knowledge-building collaborative inquiry affect students' att itudes toward science learning

compared to traditional knowledge-based activities?

2. How does knowledge-building collaborative inquiry impact students' sci entific inquiry skills compared to

traditional knowledge-based activities?

3. How does knowledge-building collaborative inquiry influence students' awareness of collaborative and

knowledge-building-oriented learning environments compared to traditional knowledge-based activities?

## Research design and KB-based online learning platform

This study adopted a quasi-experimental research design. Two fifth-grade elementary classes in northern Taiwan were randomly assigned to either the experimental o r control group, each containing 26 students. Both groups participated in KB-based science inquiry learning activities. However, to enhance persuasive knowledge building, KB-based scientific inquiry learning activities suppor ted by the "Inquiry Together" online platform were implemented in the experimental group, while simila r activities without th e assistance of the platform were implemented in the control group.

The platform's user-friendly interface and dynamic visualizations facil itated enhanced engagement among younger learners in the collaborative inquiry  process. Additionally, teacher facilitation played  a crucial role in promoting student learning by guiding discu ssions and providing scaffolding for collaborative activities. Scientific inquiry learning emphasizes hands-on experiences and critical thinking. Although the "Knowledge Forum" was not specifically designed to support "scientific inquiry learning," it does enable learners to engage in individual reflection, generate ideas, and enhance community knowledge during collaborative scientific  inquiry processes. However, it presents challenges in suppo rting the hands-on aspects of scientific inquiry and the integration of practical activities with cognitive processes. Therefore, there is a necessity to design and develop a learning platform that specifically supports "scien tific inquiry learning." This study aims to reference the "Knowledge Forum" platform to create a learning environment that facilitates learners' engagement in "scientific inquiry learning" and assists teachers in conducting "scientific knowledge innovation inquiry instruction."

## Data collection and analysis

To examine the effects of integrating online knowledge building activities into elementary students’ collaborative scientific inquiry learning, the students’ science learning attitudes, scientific inquiry skills, and perceptions of the learning environments were evaluated before and aft er the KB-based instructions. Three instruments we re used in this study. The Science Learning Attitude Scale,  adapted from the TIMSS 2019 scale, measured studen ts’ interest, confidence, and perceived value in scienc e learning, with strong reliability (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.85). The Scientific Inquiry Skills Scale, based on Lin a nd Wu’s (2019) framework, evaluated scientific inqu iry skills across four aspects: observation, planning, analysis, and reasoning, with high reliability (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.88). Finally, the Knowledge Building Environment Scale (KBES), adapted from Hong et al.  (2019), assessed perceptions of the learning environment, focusing on engagement, agency, and community, with a reliability score 0.90. These validated instruments ensured alignment with the study’s objectives and provided comprehensive data on the targeted learning outcomes.

## Results and discussion

Impact on science learning attitude The ANCOVA results (Table 1) showed that the experi mental group scored significantly higher than the c ontrol group in “interest in science learning” and “value of science as a subject” (p <.05), demonstrating the efficacy of the “Inquiry Together” platform in enhancing studen t engagement and appreciation for science. This inc rease in interest aligns with Knowledge Building Theory (Sca rdamalia & Bereiter, 2006), which emphasizes motiva tion through collective knowledge construction and valid ation of individual contributions. Hong et al. (201 9) also stressed the importance of agency-rich environments in supporting motivation and value development. These results echo Hidi and Renninger’s (2006) four-phase interest model, where inquiry-based and autonomous learning can foster situational interest that develops into sustained individual interest. The platform’s scaffolding features—such as collaborative idea refinement and personalized inquiry tasks—likely contributed to this transition. Recent studies (Wang et al., 2023;  Li & Zhao, 2023) further support the role of inter active digital platforms in enhancing situational interest through feedback and interactivity. Improved perceptions of science’s value in the experimental group suggest that the platform also helped students link science concepts to real-world relevance, consistent with findings by Tan and Chai  (2023), who emphasize the importance of collaborat ive environments in developing students’ awareness of science's societal impact. However, no significant difference was found betwee n groups in “confidence in science learning” (p =.664), indicating that confidence may require longer-term interventions. Bandura’s (1997) Self-Efficacy Theory suggests that mastery experiences and sustained pos itive feedback are essential for developing confide nce. Supporting studies (Morales & Lee, 2022; Johnson et  al., 2023) show that short-term inquiry may boost engagement but not self-efficacy. Future design s could integrate self-reflection, regular feedback, and peer support—such as reflective journals or peer assessments —to help students recognize progress and build confidence (Zimmerman & Schunk, 2011).

## Conclusion and limitations

This study demonstrates the effectiveness of Knowle dge Building (KB) -based pedagogy supported by the “Inquiry Together” platform in enhancing elementary  students' scientific inquiry learning. While both groups showed learning gains, the experimental group exhib ited significantly greater improvements in science learning interest, perceived value of science, and inquiry skills. These outcomes underscore the platform’s ability to foster engagement, autonomy,  and scientific thinking through collaborative scaff olding, progress tracking, and interactive tools. Aligned with Knowledge Building Theory, the platform promoted student agency and ac tive participation, contributing to these improvements. However, no significant difference was found in stu dents' confidence in science learning, suggesting that con fidence development requires sustained engagement. According to Bandura’s (1997) Self-Efficacy Theory, confidence builds through repeated  mastery e xperiences and positive feedback, which short-term interventions may not sufficiently provide.  To address this, future instructional designs should incorporate strategies  such as iterative inquiry cycles, reflective activ ities, and peer feedback. Platform enhancements could include featu res that promote deeper idea-building and community engagement—such as structured self-reflection, peer assessment, and collaborative goal-setting—to cultivate a stronger sense of accomplishment and metacognitive awareness (Zimmerman & Schunk, 2011). Notably, the limited improvement in students’ perceptions of “Wo rking with Ideas” and “Fostering Community” highlig hts the need for features that explicitly support these dimensions. This study has limitations: the sample was restricted to 52 students from one school, and the short intervention period limited the assessment of long-term effects. Future research should adopt longitudinal designs with larger, more diverse samples, and incorporate qualitative data (e.g., observations, student reflections, teacher interviews) to better understand student engagement  with the platform.  Overall, this study offers important implications: educators can leverage digital platfo rms like “Inquiry Together” to enhance inquiry-based instruction, while researchers should further inves tigate how such tools support students’ confidence and collaborative knowledge-building over time.
