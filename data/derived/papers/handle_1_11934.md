# Appropriate Reliance in Human-AI Collaborative Problem-Solving: The Role of Critical Thinking

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Reliance behaviors describe the frequencies and ways that people use AI tools for tasks such as problem-solving. There is a rising concern that students are over-reliant on Generative AI so that their critical thinking are being compromised. The notion of strategy-graded reliance argues that reliance behaviors are affected by a cognitive process in which an individual evaluates AI’s performance against human performance. We use critical thinking to capture this cognitive process. By analyzing 808 survey responses, including students’ critical thinking disposition, critical thinking skills, and reliance behaviors during a collaborative problem-solving task among undergraduate students, we find that collaborative, reflective, and cautious use of Generative AI is positively related to critical thinking. Therefore, we further conclude that desirable reliance on human-AI collaborative problem-solving is characterized by collaborative, reflective, and cautious use of Generative AI.

## Introduction

Problem-based learning (PBL) equips students with essential skills such as problem-solving and collaboration by engaging them in complex, real-world tasks (Schmidt et al., 2009). The integration  of Generative AI tools into PBL enhances activities such as  brainstorming and solution refinement (Joksimovic e t al., 2023). This study conceptualizes human-AI collaboration as a cognitive partnership in whic h Generative AI supports learners in achieving diverse goals, and reliance refers to how students engage with these tools (Fok & Weld, 2024). Problemsolving is a dynamic process wherein some tasks can  be delegated to AI, while others require independe nt reasoning. The study adopts a strategy-graded reliance framework, which values the quality of thought processes leading to reliance behaviors (Fok & Weld, 2024; Va sconcelos et al., 2023). A self-report questionnaire by the authors was developed to identify four reliance types —reflective, cautious, collaborative, and thoughtles s— focusing on behavior rather than out comes (Hou et al., 2025). Critical thinking, encompassing both skills and dispositions (Ennis, 2011), is central to this proc ess, enabling users to assess AI outputs, override biases, and engage in deeper reasoning (Buçinca et al., 2021). Despite AI’s increasing role in educatio n, limited research explores how critical thinking is related to reliance behaviors in complex problem-solving. This study addresses this gap by examining reliance through the lens of strategy-graded reliance, emphasizing the role of  critical thinking in fostering appropriate human-AI collaboration.

## Participants and context

Participants were first-and second-year undergraduates enrolled in a digital literacy course at a large Asian university. Of the 3,030 students registered across  70 tutorials (20 –50 students per group), 1,498 completed the pre-survey and 1,406 completed the post-survey. Tutorials were designed to promote interdis ciplinary collaboration in groups of 5–6 students. One class activity was selected for this study due to its open-ended nature and strong alignment with problem-based learning (PBL). Students were encouraged to u se Generative AI tools, including ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, during all stages of the activity. The institutiona l ethics board approved this study and waived the requirement for consent forms.

## Measures and procedures

Two surveys were administered before and after the problem-solving task. The pre-survey measured students’ critical thinking disposition. The Critical Thinkin g Disposition Scale (Sosu, 2013) assessed students’  inclination toward critical thought on a 5-point Likert scale and included two subscales: Critica l Openness (7 items) and Reflective Skepticism (4 items). The post-survey focused on critical thinking skills and reliance behaviors during the problem-solving activity. The Critical Thinking Skills Scal e, adapted from the Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (Pintrich et al., 1993), measured students’ application of reasoning using a 7-point Likert scale. The Reliance Behavior Scale, developed by th e authors (Hou et al.,  2025), evaluated students’ frequency of AI usage across four subtypes: reflective, cauti ous, thoughtless, and collaborative use. Collaborat ive use included items such as “My peer(s) helped me modify  Generative AI’s output,” emphasizing how students coconstructed solutions using AI. The course followed a flipped classroom model. Stud ents watched pre-recorded lectures and completed quizzes before class. In the classroom, they worked in teams to draft a policy proposal o n AI ethics  over three stages of problem-solving, defining the concepts, information exploration and solution presentation (Liu & Pásztor, 2022). For each stage of problem-solving, we provided scaffolding questions to help students draft and evaluate the policy proposal, and there are allowed  to use Generative AI freely to assist in answering  the scaffolding questions.

Data analyses The collected survey responses were first screened for carelessness using the longstring; extreme outliers were removed (Kupffer et al., 2024). After merging pre-and post-survey responses, 808 matched datasets remained for analysis. Descriptive statistics and polychoric correlations were used to examine relationships among variables. Given the ordinal nature of Likert-scale data, polychoric correlation was selected for its accuracy in estimating associations between latent continuous variables (Choi et al., 2010).

## Discussion and conclusion

This study underscores the important role of critical thinking in shaping undergraduates’ reliance behaviors when using Generative AI in problem-based learning (PBL) contexts. Findings suggest tha t stronger critical thinking skills are associated with more reflective, cautious, and collaborative uses of AI. Thus, these patterns indicate thoughtful engagement with AI outputs rather than p assive acceptance (Gajos & Mamykina, 2022; Vasconce los et al., 2023). These results align with prior research in human-AI collaboration showing that cognitively engaged users are more likely to demonstrate appropriate reliance (Buçinca et al., 2021). This study has several limitations. The cross-sectional design cannot reveal within-person changes in AI reliance over time, even though the observed correl ation between thoughtless and reflective use sugges ts that multiple reliance behaviors may coexist during prob lem-solving. Future research using longitudinal, or processtracing methods could better capture how students a dapt their reliance in response to evolving task de mands and perceptions of AI reliability (Li & Little, 2023). Additionally, reliance and critical thinking were m easured through self-reports, which may be subject to recall bias or social desirability, especially when assessing internal cognitive processes.
