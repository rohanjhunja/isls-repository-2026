# Hissing Cockroaches and Micro-Moments of Justice: Youth and Adult Allies Engaging in Politicized Care and Rightful Presence

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper explores how care can make or reshape informal learning spaces through micro-moments of justice. Rooted in the work of politicized care and rightful presence, we trace moments of care by positioning students as carebrokers of the more than human. The findings demonstrate how micro-moments of justice can be incorporated into informal learning space design principles to create more just learning in STEM and beyond.

## Introduction and theoretical framework

Communities of color may experience a dismissal of the social and emotional disparities in learning environments, particularly in traditional school settings. Thus, attending to moments of care in informal learning spaces may act as a direct response to systemic inequities (Baldridge et al., 2017) that can  inform pedagogies across all learning spaces. Building on the work of educators who enact  politicized care (McKinney de Royston et al., 2017), we reposition youth as interactional brokers of care (Garcia-Sanchez, 2018) to account for youth’s invisibilized labor, and bids for rightful presence (Tan & Calabrese Barton, 2023). This paper explores how micro-moments of justice can make or reshape informal learning spaces and how ‘Care-centered’ practices help us reimagine/reshape STEM learning experiences as/to be more relational and t ransformative. Thus, further addressing systemic in equalities and supporting the rightful presence of marginalized students in science. Though every being may enact care, care practices, care sensemaking, and how we feel about/during care is multiple. Rooted in the work of Black and C hicana scholars, we see how care is shaped by our h istories, dominant ideologies and sociopolitical positioning (hooks, 2000; Anzaldúa, 1987).  Many practitioners come to learning spaces because they “care” about students,  yet they often cause further harm through deficit views (Valenzuela, 1998). Instead, we frame care to inclu de a political stance and communa l commitment to a child’s development beyond the current subject where educat ors are aware of their role, influence, and purpose (Beaubeouf-Lafontant, 2008). Enacting care demands we not igno re the realities of systemic inequalities, social hierarchies and  oppression but rather make them visible while holdi ng an unwavering belief that historically marginalized youth are brilliant and capable (McKin ney de Royston et al., 2017). Emerging from the fie ld of critical justice studies, we situate care through t he tenets of rightful presence (Tan & Calabrese Barton, 2023). These tenets guide our analysis and we begin to und erstand the underpinnings of care in an informal af terschool program that supports positive STEM experiences and  a sense of belonging. Elucidating the bids made to ward rightful presence in STEM, where young people and educators collectively co-create more just learning spaces, we expand current understandings of care that promotes rightful presence.

## Data and methods

The preliminary findings were collected in an infor mal afterschool science program, Daphnia, a non-profit organization offering inquiry-based, student-centered science programs targeting underserved youth populations. The organization aims to empower and give agency to  youth by developing youth voices and empowering th em to express their perspectives, a sense of belonging, and recognition by othe rs of their position as social actors (Gonsalves, 2014; Rahm et al., 2014). In the study' s second year, we conducted participant observation s, ethnographic field notes, and collected video data. Our study brings the iterative design of lessons and pedagogical strategies together with an analysis of learners’ a ctivity and their relationships within and across c ommunities to investigate how care legitimizes and (re)positions them as members of these spaces in dynamically unfo lding interaction. We traced for care through our cohesive framework, micro-moments of justice:

1. Building Genuine Relationships (BGR):

a. Foster deep, meaningful relationships with students that are authentic and caring b. Ensure students feel seen, heard, and valued for their full identities.

2. Addressing Systemic Inequities (ASI):

a. Recognize and address the systemic barriers affecting students. b. Advocate for social justice and equitable practices within the educational system.

3. Incorporating Cultural Knowledge (ICK):

a. Integrate students' cultural and community backgrounds into the curriculum. b. Value and leverage students’ diverse experiences and identities.

4. Creating a Supportive Community (CSC):

a. Promote an interdependent classroom culture based o n mutual respect and collective responsibility. b. Encourage collaboration and support among students and between students and educators.

## Findings and discussion

Our episode explored care through the more than hum an. Care for the roach is juxtaposed between a spec tacle and as an actual living breathing thing that needs to be carefully taken care of. Although care for th e roach is prioritized and avoids discourse of  the roach as a pest by humanizing the roach through  internal dialogue and attributing human-like features, the roach is still being held in captivity and centered around human needs. Despite being hesitant at the beginning of the session, youth engage the roach with care, partly attributed to the modeling of the community scientists. Our analysis illustrates how tracing a constellation of micro-moments of justice built on the tenets of politicized care and rightful pres ence, show how care is multidirectional, reposition ing youth as care receivers and carebrokers. These episodes are in conversation wit h each other showing us what micro moments of justice look like within an afterschool program with youth and in the designing of these le arning spaces. The contours of care are baked into the tenets of rightful presence and we illustrated this through examples of environmental injustice from the guest speaker, and through students' questions as bids for rightfu l presence that challenge and transform what participation in STEM entails (Tan & Calabrese Barton, 2023). Advocating for justice in knowledge production and communication and more equitable engagement that values the public’s and, in this case, the student’s perspectives and contri butions connects to rightful presence by challengin g the guest/host relationships. The students' bids and ed ucators' critical stance provided interactional exa mples of how rightful presence may emerge through care.

Activist and labor organizer Grace Lee Boggs said t hat critical change and movements are “born of critical connections rather than critical mass.” (2 011). Whether it is across our research team, the D aphnia organization, and informal learning spaces more broadly, we see our contribution to the movement in these micro moments of justice through care. By attuning for the small, tiny, most overlooked parts, we begin to highlight the critical connections — connections that move us towards a more just and equitable future.
