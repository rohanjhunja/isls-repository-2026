# Critical Perspectives on Technology Integration: Insights from Epistemic Network Analysis

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines how 46 in-service teachers address topics and strategies for fostering students’ critical perspectives on technology, using Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA). Findings indicate that teachers enrolled in elementary programs emphasized designer bias, while non-elementary program teachers focused more on data bias, critical data literacy, and employed critical inquiry strategies. These differences highlight the need for tailored professional development and further research on integrating critical technology education across grade levels

## Introduction

With the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence  (AI) and the rise of generative AI (GenAI) tools, teachers must become critical users of technology, integrating it s societal and individual impacts into their decisi on-making to prepare future generations to navigate both the benefits and harms of technology (Mouza et al., 2022). However, little is known about teachers’ perspectives on cri tical aspects of technology or how they address the se issues in practice. This study explores in-service teachers’ per ceptions of critical technology topics, such as alg orithmic bias and data surveillance, and how they incorporat e these issues into their classrooms. Specifically,  we propose the following research questions: ● Which topics and strategies do teachers adopt to address the potential harms of technology? ● How do the epistemic networks of elementary and non-elementary program teachers differ in terms of the topics selected and strategies employed to develop students’ critical perspectives on technology?

## Method

The participants included in-service teachers (n=46) who were enrolled in a grad uate-level course at a mid-sized university in the Southeastern United States. The data sources included online course discussions related to critical examination of the role of technology. Epistemic ne twork analysis (ENA) was used to model co-occurrences between strategies and what teachers want their stu dents to know, highlighting differences between ele mentary and non-elementary program teachers. Data preparation involved two cycles of coding (Saldaña & Omasta, 2016).

## Discussion

Overall, our findings showed that teachers recogniz e technology is not neutral, and creators often emb ed their biases in designs. Additionally, teachers addressing designer bias frequently expanded their focus to include data bias, critical data literacy, and critical consciousness. Various strategies were  recommended to integrate these topics in K-12. The comparison between elementary and non-elementary program teachers revealed distinct differences. While elementary program teachers primarily relied on single strategies, such as integrating or critical inquiry, non-elementary program teachers were more likely to combine multiple strategies. Future research could explore how teachers connect these topics to their subject areas and develop strategies for int egrating them into early grade levels.
