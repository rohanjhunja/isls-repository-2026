# Generative AI in Project-Based Learning for Sustainable Development

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Generative AI (genAI) offers potential to enhance learning scenarios. However, research on specific use-cases and roles within educational frameworks is limited. This paper focuses on UNESCO’s Education for Sustainable Development framework and its recommendation to use Project-Based Learning (PBL) to develop key-competences for addressing the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The research investigates expert teachers' views on the potential for human-AI collaboration, focusing on the role of genAI in use-cases designed to enhance individual and collaborative learning within PBLxSDG. A workshop involving 33 participants yielded 11 use-cases. Along with 10 use-cases derived from the literature and evaluations from participants, forming the foundation of our analysis. Insights from our analysis saw genAI assuming diverse roles for human-AI collaboration within PBLxSDG. However, concerns were raised about overreliance on AI, learning outcomes, and the need for teacher guidance. This work provides insights for educators and researchers exploring genAI implementation in collaborative, active learning scenarios.

## Introduction

Generative AI (genAI) offers promises for the provi sion of advanced computer-supported functions to enhance education, particularly through human-AI collaboration (Giannakos et al., 2024). However,  challenges like misinformation, ethical concerns, or cognitive impact necessitate careful consideration when implementing genAI in educational settings (Sabzalieva & Valentini, 20 23; Lee et al., 2025). By recognizing both its potential and limitations, educators have an opportunity to respo nsibly leverage AI to enhance learning. Achieving t his transformation requires designing environments where humans and AI collaborate effectively (Hernández-Leo et al., 2025). Yet, learning with AI, like any collabo ration, requires active engagement and does not dim inish individual effort, aiming for seamless student-AI collaboration (Salomon et al., 1991; Atchley et al., 2024). This collaboration frames educational interactions as social processes involving conversa tion and exploration, rather than as simple sequences of prompts and responses (Sharples, 2023). To leverage human-AI collaboration in education, learning design emerges as a crucial fra mework for navigating these challenges. This involv es strategically integrating genAI tools across pedago gical methods. While studies have explored the pote ntial of genAI in education (Lee & Khoo, 2023; Johnson, 2024), research on effective strategies within comprehe nsive pedagogical frameworks remains fragmented. Therefore,  there is a need for exploration of strategies with in cohesive pedagogical frameworks to maximize the benefits of genAI in education. A pedagogical framework providing a range of opport unities for genAI integration is Project Based Learning (PBL), a teaching and learning methodology that engages students in real-world projects (Zhang & Ma, 2023). By connecting classroom content to real-world applications, PBL enhances student motivation and prepares them for future challenges, including thos e related to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG s). Integrating SDGs into PBL is a promising approach t o cultivate sustainable development mindse ts in students (Nguyen et al., 2024; Carrió et al., 2024). Hence, this paper focuses on UNESCO's Education for Sustai nable Development (ESD) recommendation to use PBL for SDG competency development (UNESCO, 2020). The ESD framework structures PBL sequences  and defines key sustainability competencies (UNESCO, 2020; Redman & Wiek, 2021). While sequences vary, they typically f ollow four phases: proposing a project, developing skills, planning, and presenting. UNESCO's framework outlines eight competencies to enhance cognition, shift attitudes, and foster an action-oriented sustainability mindset (UNESCO, 2020; Joseph, 2023). The integration of GenAI in PBLxSDG processes could potentially see a human-AI collaboration where genAI assumes different roles with the aim to enhan ce the development of SDG key competencies. To expl ore which are these roles, this study was guided by the  following research question (RQ): How can genAI be conceptualized for integration into the PBLxSDG ped agogical framework to support the development of ke y competencies for sustainability? To answer the rese arch question, we analyze use cases (UCs) on genA I within PBLxSDGs, proposed by expert stakeholders and deriv ed from prior research. These UCs illustrate Human-AI collaboration, often supporting collaborative learn ing in PBL. Given its learning impact (Kusam, 2024;  Lee & Khoo, 2023), we focused on text-generating AI.

## Study design, participants and procedure

The study employed a stakeholders-involving workshop (Stitt-Gohdes & Crews, 2004). This workshop brought together a group of 33 Catalan education profession als, including 27 educators and experts from high s chool, vocational training and higher education, as well as 6 specialists in the concerned field. Al l had experience in PBLxSDG theory or practice and voluntarily chose to join the GenAI (for PBLxSDG) workshop. The workshop focused on the application of genAI in education in the framework of PBL phases aligned with the key competences for sustainability and the  SDGs (Carrió et al., 2024). Participants were divi ded into eight groups and worked together throughout the ses sion. The workshop began with a presentation focusi ng on using genAI to enhance SDG competencies while addre ssing ethical concerns. An example was presented to demonstrate a use case (UC) where genAI can be used  to foster the “systemic competence” within PBL phase 1. Following this, participants engaged in two rounds of hands-on activities to review and develop their own UCs. In the first round, participants reviewed and discussed 10 UCs, adapted from prior work on genAI in the classroom (Johnson, 2024; Kusam et al., 2024; Lee & Khoo, 202 3; Wu & Chang, 2023), to fit the four PBL phases wi th a sustainability focus. Participants rated the UCs on  a five-point Likert scale from "not adequate" to "totally adequate" and provided feedback. In the second roun d, participants were tasked with creating their own  UCs. Participants rated the new 111 UCs and provided feedback.

## Data analysis

To gain an understanding of how participants conceptualized genAI's role in PBLxSDG education, we categorized the proposed roles using Sharples' (2023) framework. Additionally, we identified twonew roles from the UCs: an information provider, where genAI offers topic-related information, and feedback provider, where g enAI gives constructive feedback to improve student work. Moreover, we categorized the UCs by genAI risks (R) to learning: the need for AI literacy and awareness of misinform ation, bias, and prompting techniques (R1); ethical concerns like age limitations and GDPR compliance (R2); and the risk of promoting academic dishonesty (R3). The authors independently coded the UCs based on the proposed c ategories and genAI roles, achieving a Cronbach’s A lpha of 0.81. Discrepancies were resolved through discus sion. Finally, a qualitative analysis was conducted  to categorize participants' feedback. This led to the creation of three thematic groups.

## Results

The analysis of the 21 UCs revealed a diverse range of roles that genAI can adopt within the context of PBLxSDG. GenAI frequently assumed the role of an exploratory  tool (14 UCs), enabling students to delve deeper i nto complex topics. Roles such as possibility engine (1 3 UCs) and information provider (12 UCs) were also prominent, empowering students to generate ideas and access relevant information. Roles such as co-designer (10 UCs) and feedback provider (8 UCs) followed up, uti lizing genAI as a guidance tool towards developing their final product. While roles such as collaboration coach (2 UCs), storyteller (2 UCs) and socratic opponent (2 UCs) received the least mention. Eight representative UCs were selected from the 21 collected to illustrate genAI’s role in PBL phases for SDG competencies (see Table 1).

Regarding the coding of risks, we identified that t he majority of the UCs, 19 out of 21, require stude nts to possess AI literacy skills (R1). Both raters agreed that all UCs should be categorized under risk R2, due to the UCs not specifying genAI tools. We have identified that 10 of the 21 proposed UCs could potentially lead to R3. Most R3 cases were linked to UCs where genAI was us ed as a possibility engine or exploratorium. In con trast, the storytelling role was the only role not flagged  under R3. We then proceeded to explore the diverse  roles that genAI can assume throughout the PBL phases (see fig ure 1). In the first phase, genAI's role as an expl oratorium and feedback provider was prominent. As students progressed to the second phase, genAI's roles as an information provider and possibility engine became more importa nt. In the third phase, the focus shifted to co-designer and possibility engine. In the final phase, the co-designer role remained prominent, while feedback pr ovider and possibility engine played secondary roles. Followin g this, we proceeded to explore if any dominant gen AI roles appeared within the collected UCs to support the development of specific SDG competencies (see figure 2). Only three genAI roles showed recurring patterns to allow for conclusions (more than 3 occurrences). Based on participants' UCs feedback, we have identi fied three recurring themes related to: overrelianc e on AI, learning outcomes, and teaching guidance. Under the 1st theme concerns were raised about students over reliance on AI. ## Discussion and conclusions

Our study examined PBLxSDGs use cases enhanced with  genAI tools, drawing on stakeholders insights and literature to identify genAI's  roles in human-AI collaboration. Based on participants' ratings and proposed UCs, our analysis showed that genAI was mainly seen as a n exploratorium and possibility engine, offering di verse perspectives on sustainability challenges but riski ng overreliance (Zhai et al., 2024). In contrast, less risky role s like storyteller avoid these concerns by encouragin g independent analysis and insight-gathering. The identified genAI roles for each PBL phase align with the typical tasks associated with each stage, providing a form of validity for the coding of the UCs. In phase 1, roles like e xploratorium and feedback provider helped explore d ata and refine research questions. In phase 2, exploratoriu m and information provider roles supported informat ion gathering and perspective exploration. In phase 3, co-designer and possibility engine roles emerged as students designed their final product. In phase 4, the co-designer role was key in the final project stages.  These roles also align with specific SDG competencies. Fo r example, roles like information provider and expl oratorium may support critical thinking skills by offering ideas and encouraging analysis and reflection (Yu & Mohamed, 2023). Similarly, exploratorium and possibility engine rol es may support systems thinking, by providing multiple resources and knowledge sharing opportunities to students (Arantes do Amaral & Fregni, 2021). Even tho ugh speculative and needing real-world validation, these conclusions offer valuable insights into genA I's role in supporting key SDG competencies. Finally, participants' feedbac k emphasized concerns about overreliance on AI, limitations in learning outcomes, and the need for teacher guidance. Future research should focus on conducting empirica l studies to evaluate the actual implementation of the use cases, to offer evidence about the impact o f genAI on student learning outcomes, identify spec ific UCs that maximize the benefits of genAI, and de velop strategies to mitigate potential risks. Human-AI collaboration within the educational framework of PBLxSDG has pot ential for diverse approaches, however it is necess ary to take in consideration that different risks can arise that can potentially affect learning outcomes.
