# Knowledge Building Through Video Dialogues

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In this study, we designed video mind maps in which preservice teachers could engage through video dialogues. The data analysis focuses on what characterizes the organization of the mind map and how it supports students’ knowledge-building practices by analyzing their interactions. Video-based mind maps afford students to produce video dialogues and build upon and negotiate knowledge introduced in peers’ video dialogues. As the mind map evolved, it became the interface for the students’ knowledge-building practices.

## Introduction and theoretical considerations

In higher education students need to construct, confront, and negotiate understanding. Facilitating collaborative knowledge-building practices  is fruitful for promoting such competences (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2014). Knowledge building is regarded the “collective production and improvement of ideas (i.e., conceptual artifacts) that contribute to a community’s knowledge advancem ents” (Damşa et al., 2010, p. 148). Such practices can center around students’ collaborative knowledge wor k and, to a large degree, be learner-centered and preconditioned by students’ persistence to move activities onwards  (Damşa et al., 2010). In this study, I adopt these perspectives to explore how collaborating groups create and engage with  video dialogues in a classroom community-level, video-based mind map activity.  Video dialogues  are conversations that develop between learners, mediated by video, and that hold the potential to traverse conversationa l settings across time and space (Beal & Steier, 2024). Learning is here perceived as a transformation occurring in and among learners interacting with others, resources, and tools available to them (Damşa et al., 2019). A prior study found that students learned more from dialogue videos than monologue videos, partly due to “content moves” that offered alternative angles to the topic that the monologue videos lacked  (Chi et al., 2017). In the current study, video  dialogues were used to facilitate learner-centered knowledge-building practices. The research question s are: What characterizes the social, temporal, and spatial organization of the video-based mind map  for shared knowledge development ? In what ways do video dialogues support knowledge building among preservice teachers?

## Research design: the video-based mind map project

In this  study (Puntambekar, 2018), two researchers and a teacher educator iteratively designed, develop ed, implemented and analyzed a theoretically informed video-based mind map pedagogical design (Beal & Hontvedt,

2023) to better understand how students could work together to enhance their collaborative unders tanding and

negotiation of course literature ahead of individual oral exams. The student groups were to create an overview of the curricula founded mainly upon co-constructed video dialogues. We used existing software to design an online mind map structure in which students could post, watch and respond to videos, sounds, images, and text. So far, we have performed three iterations and collected 80 hours of video recordings of whole class and group work, as three classes of preservice teachers (N = 75) worked in 15 groups (N = 2-5) to co-construct video-based mind maps. Fixed cameras were placed in separate group rooms to video  record the students’ work.  I use interaction analysis (Jordan & Henderson, 1995) to explore the students’ epistemic practice and how it unfolds in interaction. The analysis displays relevant interactional “hot spots” (Jordan & Henderson, p. 43) that illustrate characteristics of the activity and how it can support students’ knowledge-building practice at different stages of the design.

## Findings

For this paper, I analyze how students create, watch and negotiate through video dialogue. The video dialogues were distributed to peers and moved across group rooms as they were uploaded to the mind map (Fig. 1), advancing the students’ shared knowledge work. ## Discussion and concluding remarks

The video-based mind map activity support s student’s knowledge-building practice through how activities are built by and upon students’ co-construction of the knowledge they engage with. This activity differs f rom traditional constellations in higher educational pr actices in which educators mostly convey what is deemed relevant. T he findings illustrate how digital platforms that facilitat e distributed video dialogues can support students in building, confronting, and negotiating knowledge, in which students take epistemic responsibility for knowledge development in  terms of how the practice evolve d and deeper understandings develop ed. The video dialogues challenge traditional social, temporal, and spatial organizations in classrooms and hold great potential for educators working to support student learning as they engage in knowledge-building practices. I aim to further analyze these initial findings to seek traces of students developing shared epistemic agency in this practice.
