# Youth Collaborative Inquiry for Sports-based Computer Science Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Efforts in computer science (CS) education extend technology across diverse collaborative and interest-based learning environments. This study delves into the intersection of CS and sports. Particularly, we ask how youth collaboratively engage with an AI-embedded sports app to better understand the technology. Pulling from video recordings of youth in-game engagement, this poster explores youth’s collaborative inquiry processes of CS in a sports context. We analyze the interactions between youth and the AI embedded app, so that we can understand youth's sense making about how the technology works through tinkering and the subsequent claims. We discuss how new technologies enter sports related contexts and inspire collaborative youth inquiries about how the technology works. The mix of verbal and embodied experimentation with the technology is increasingly relevant for designing learning environments for learning CS in context.

## Introduction and background

Sports are a popular after school activity that involves embodied and collaborative inquiry through play. Youth’s passion for sports can be used to support interest-based learning within computer science (e.g., Jones et al., 2020; Nasir & Hand, 2008).  We explore an afterschool program, where youth play with an artificial intelligence (AI) - enabled soccer training application. The group takes turn playing a soccer dribbling game on the app, where they begin to co-view and co-participate within the single player design of the game. We consider how the youth collaboratively make sense of the app’s live-recording as a tool to understand how the technology works. The project has implications for extending inquiry-based learning in computer science education throug h embodied and collaborative learning environments.  Likewise, i ntroducing new technologies, tools, and ideas to th ese contexts can support connections to youth’s interests and cultural backgrounds in sports and CS. Through inquiry-based learning, learners explore what tools exist and how to use them along with the norms and collaborative processes that exist within  the group. There are three different stages of the  embodied learning within inquiry: tinkering, tuning, and tra cking (Spatz, 2017). Tinkering centers exploration in a more open-ended direction, such that the learner is unsure how something may work, but they are searching for a pattern in the randomness. Next, tuning implies that the le arner has some sense about how something works, and they can use feedback to refine their model of how it wo rks. Tracking suggests that they can predict and understand how it works. Within learning how to play soccer, learners tinker with the ball and how it can bounce, move, roll based on their body’s movement. They attune their bodies to moving with the ball, until they can track the ball as an extension of themselves.

## Methods and data

We examine the AI app recordings across a small group of  participants. The participants are three fifth grade boys, who are of Latinx descent. They are members of an afterschool program that is primarily attended by youth of similar ethnicities. All of them are active in sports, and they mentione d prior experience playing soccer. As part of longer partnership with the program, we sel ected this small group as an example of youth sense-making around sports and technology. We draw on interaction analysis (Jordan & Henderson, 1995) to generate  a transcript of moment-to-moment interactions that encapsulated the talk, ges ture, coordination between participants, and use of camera view. All youth are referenced by pseudonyms here. For th is poster, our analysis summarizes patterns of behavior in the youth’s sense making process.

## Conclusions and future directions

By diverging from the single player set up, the group found a different way to engage the technology, whi ch helped them understand what the app registered and ignored. From this project, the mix of verbal and embodied experimentation with the technology was essential for the youth to understand how it works. This may be relevant for designing learning environments in non-traditional CS contexts. Moreover, this project’s learning environment builds on inquiry-based learning literature. We provide another case of what inquiry can look like in this context outside of a classroom setting and highlighting how  it shows up in moment-to-moment interaction s between youth. We frame the inquiry process as a concerted collaboration amongst youth wherein they make sense  of technology together, and embodiment is one of the primary forms of their engagement (Spatz, 2017). This study was limited to one camera view of the environment since these video recordings were found as an artifact of the project after it was complete d. A future iteration of the project would involve a camera view external of the tablet, so that we could see body o rientation and co-viewing from all players point of vi ew. It would provide a continuous of the interactions that  occur between turns. Another direction would be fa cilitating youth re-viewing of their game play as another place for sen se making about the app and technology that is not limited to the game countdown clock. This could support youth attunement to similar types of technology.
