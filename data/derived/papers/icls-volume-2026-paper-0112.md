# The Effect of Collaborative Scripts in Engineering Design: Unraveling Neural Underpinnings using fNIRS Hyperscanning Study

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Engineering design within STEM education serves as a vital pathway for cultivating innovative talent, yet the effectiveness of group collaboration is often influenced by factors such as members' cognitive engagement and collaborative strategies. To unravel the behavioral and neural underpinnings of how scripts affect this process, this study employed functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) hyperscanning experiment to examine the within-group inter-brain synchrony (IBS) during the engineering design progress with and without the support of project-based collaborative script. Results revealed that the project-based script significantly enhanced IBS in the right temporoparietal junction (r-TPJ) across three key channels. This IBS positively predicted task performance, correlating with improvements in the integration, creativity, and technical quality of engineering design products. The findings demonstrate that structured scripts align cognitive-behavioral processes during collaboration and establish neural synchrony as a potential biomarker for interaction quality. This provides neurobehavioral evidence for the efficacy of collabo rative scripts in complex, multi-peer collaboration environments.

Keywords: Engineering design; fNIRS; collaboration; inter-brain synchronization; IBS; hyperscanning STEM education emphasizes the application of interdisciplinary knowledge and thinking in authentic contexts to cultivate students' higher-order skills, including critical thinking, creativity, and problem-solving. As a core component of STEM learning, collaborative engineering design involves dynamic information exchange and knowledge co-construction. It promotes active and social engagement, enabling students to adopt diverse roles such as designers, critical thinkers, and communicators  (Hurst et al., 2013).  Advances in neuroimaging, particularly fNIRS hyperscanning, now allow simultaneous measurement of brain activity across multiple individuals during social interaction. Recent educational neuroscience research indicates that inter-brain synchrony (IBS) serves as a neurophysiological marker of successful collaboration—observed in teacher-student, parent-child, and peer dyads —reflecting interaction quality  (Nguyen et al., 2020; Pan et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2018).

In collaborative learning, cognitive engagement —defined as students' psychological investment in learning tasks—is essential for effective collaboration (Fredricks et al., 2004). It encompasses the willingness to exceed task requirements and tackle challenging problems  (Connell & Wellborn, 1991). While Wang (2023) offers valuable insights into how two brains synchronize during collaboration, real-world educational settings often involve three or more learners. This group structure, common in interdisciplinary STEM projects, introduces greater diversity and complexity. Triadic collaboration significantly increases interaction complexity, bringing in group dynamics such as leadership, mediation, and subgroup formation—elements largely absent in dyads. These factors complicate communication, role allocation, and consensus-building, while decision-making requires more negotiation and compromise. Although prior studies have reported IBS in the temporoparietal junction during poetry learning and computer gaming in triads (Pan et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2020), no research has yet examined IBS in multidisciplinary STEM contexts that demand creativity and knowledge integration. In terms of collaborative strategies, structured scripting plays a vital role in STEM engineering education (Marra et al., 2016). Collaborative scripts are pre-designed interaction frameworks that guide communication and cooperation to improve learning outcomes (Arango et al., 2007). In multidisciplinary STEM settings, such scripts help students integrate specialized knowledge into group  projects, fostering collective problem-solving and innovation. Research shows that students in structured interactive environments not only acquire technical skills 999 more effectively but also demonstrate improved group work and communication abilities (Kollar et al., 2006). Project-based collaboration scripts, in particular, simulate real-world engineering practice by scaffolding the problem-solving process. These scripts typically include task decomposition, role assignment, interaction rules, and feedback mechanisms, enabling students to experience and reflect on authentic engineering processes (Lee & Robles, 2019).

While previous research has provided initial evidence on the role of neural synchrony and influencing factors in collaboration, such as group diversity (Lu et al., 2021), consensus reaching (Pan et al., 2021), and the gender of partners  (Zhang et al., 2017), there is currently a lack of neuroscientific evidence specifically highlighting the significance of collaborati ve script and identifying neural marks for collaborative engineering design activity.

Given the advantages of fNIRS-based hyperscanning techniques, the present study aimed to provide a neurophysiological testbed for the Interactive Brain Hypothesis and investigate the contribution of project-based script during the STEM engineer design process. The research questions were as follows:

1) Does a project-based collaboration script increase IBS during engineering design tasks?

2) Does project-based script lead to better task performance compared to unstructured collaboration?

3) Under the guidance of a project-based script, is emerging IBS correlated with the task performance?

Specifically, we focused on the l-TPJ and r-TPJ cortex as the region of interest (ROI) to explore the interpersonal neural models between individuals involved in engineering design. The r-TPJ, as a component of the mentalizing system (MS) and default-mode network (DMN), which are responsible for mentalizing, theory of mind, and other social cognition and creative ideas (Lu & Hao, 2019; Lu et al., 2019). Meanwhile, the PFC, as a component of the executive control network (ECN), is associated with flexible attentional switches (Monsell, 2003), the evaluation of creative ideas  (Liang et al., 2022), problem-solving (Lu et al., 2021; Lu, Xue, et al., 2019), and language processing (Negishi et al., 2020; Nozawa et al., 2019;). We hypothesized that compared to the independent mode, collaboration between dyads would result in better STEM design outcomes and increased IBS in the ROI. We did not make a specific hypothesis for the second research question but aimed to explore the effectiveness of different behaviors through a joint analysis of IBS, task performance, and behavior coding.

## Participants

We recruited 66 college students from Beijing Normal University (12 males and 54 females, mean age = 21.48± 2.26 years). All subjects were strangers and participated in 22 three-person groups. This is comparable to the sample sizes used in previous hyperscanning research on three-person interaction (Dai et al., 2022). The members of each group did not know each other before the experiment. All the participants were right-handed, had no brain disease or mental illness, and had normal or corrected-to-normal vision. Informed written consent was collected from all participants. The study procedure was approved by the University Committee on Human Research Protection of Beijing Normal University.

## Experimental procedure and tasks

During the experiment, each group was required to complete product design tasks—specifically, a multifunctional campus cat shelter and a multifunctional campus mobile book borrowing station —under two different experimental conditions: firstly, without collaborative scaffolding, and then with project-based scaffolding. The duration of both conditions was 15 minutes, with a total duration of the experiment was about 1 hour. The order of two STEM engineering design tasks and two conditions was counterbalanced. The similarity of the task difficulty and experimental materials used in the two tasks better reduced the diﬀerences in IBS due to excessive diﬀerences between experimental tasks (See Figure 1.A). 1000 Figure 1 Experimental design, fNIRS Data Acquisition, and WTC Analysis Note. Data acquisition fNIRS Data acquisition Brain activity during the task was measured using the Brite24 wireless fNIRS system (Artinis Medical Systems, Elst), which was used to record the oxyhemoglobin (HbO) and deoxyhemoglobin (HbR) concentrations for each dyad. The absorption of near-infrared light (wavelengths: 752 and 830 nm) was measured at a sampling rate of 10 Hz. Two optode probe sets  (22 channels) were placed over each participant’s l-TPJ and r-TPJ regions, based on previous  studies suggesting the involvement of the l-TPJ and r-TPJ regions in group creativity and social interaction (Cui et al., 2012; Lu et al., 2021; Mayseless et al., 2019; Pan et al., 2020). (see Fig. 1 B). Task performance The rubrics of the overall rating of task performance were modified from the study by Chonkaew et al. (2016). They consisted of integration, innovation, technical, organization, and aesthetics. Two trained raters independently rated the 5 dimensions of each STEM work generated by the participants on a 5-point scale ranging from 1 to 5, with 1 being the lowest and 5 the highest. Video coding of collaborative behavior To better understand which behavior best contributed to IBS during STEM  collaboration, two undergraduate students who were blind to the experiment were recruited to independently and manually transcribe and code each group's discussion using the video-recording data. The coding framework for the current study was derived from the cognitive engagement framework in Zhu (2006) research, which consists of four dimensions: (1) question (i.e., group members raise a question to others); (2) statement (i.e., response to a previous message or provide information relating to the task); (3) reflection (i.e., reflects on changes in personal opinions and behavior).(4) scaffold(i.e., offering suggestions for designing or manage the time) (Bloom et al., 1956).

## fNIRS data analysis

MATLAB (Mathworks, Natick, MA, version 2022a) was used to process the data, and statistical analyses were conducted by Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS). ●  fNIRS Data pre-processing. T he data were preprocessed with the built-in MATLAB application function (Homer 3) with an appropriate filtering function to apply the discrete wavelet transform filter method to correct motion artifacts. we mainly focused on the HbO signal, since it is  more sensitive to changes in cerebral blood flow  (Hoshi, 2007; Jiang et al., 2012; Lu & Hao, 2019). First, use the hmrIntensity2OD.m function to convert raw intensity data to optical density (OD) data. Next, use the hmrMotionArtifact.m function to correct for motion artifacts in the OD data. The parameters used for correction are tMotion  = 0.5, tMask  = 1, STDEVthreshold = 10, and AMPthreshold  = 1. Then, by applying MATLAB PCA's built-in function, we could remove global physiological noise. Remove the top 80% of the variance from the signals.

1001 ●  Frequency band of interest (FOI). To identify the frequency band of interest, a one-sample t-test was conducted to evaluate time-averaged IBS increments in each channel combination within dyads (11 channels in l-TPJ and 11channels in r-TPJ) along the full frequency range (0.01–0.7 Hz)(Nozawa et al., 2016; Xue et al., 2018; Zheng et al., 2018). Consistent with previous studies, we focus on the relative enhancement of IBS during the task session compared to the resting-state session (Dai et al., 2018; Jiang et al., 2012; Liang et al., 2022). Thus, we subtracted the coherence value of the resting-state session from that of the task session to obtain an index of time-aligned IBS increase (Grinsted et al., 2004; Liang et al., 2022). Previous studies reported that the data above 0.7 Hz suffered from aliasing of higher frequency physiological noise such as cardiac activity (0.8 –2.5 Hz) (Barrett, 2010; Guijt et al., 2007; Tong et al., 2011). Therefore, we excluded the data above 0.7 Hz from the study. Moreover, the data below 0.01 Hz were not considered as well, to avoid very low-frequency ﬂuctuations. The analysis was carried out in 2 separate conditions (i.e., collaborative and independent). To avoid bias, the IBS values in both interaction modes were averaged before t-tests in each condition. The t-test results were thresholded at P < 0.05 (Lu et al., 2019). Considering that this analysis was only used to identify the frequency band of interest rather than to obtain the ﬁnal results, no further correction was performed (Lu et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2018). For further analysis, IBS values in this band were converted to the Fisher z-statistics (Chang & Glover, 2010; Cui et al., 2012; Lu et al., 2021). A one-sample t-test with FDR correction across all channels (P < 0.05) was calculated for each condition and the three 5-min phases within each condition. ●  IBS Within l-TPJ or r-TPJ. The data collected from these ROIs during the two conditions were used for the analysis of IBS. Before calculating  IBS, we took the data of the 1 minute  before each task as the baseline and resampled the sampling rate to 1Hz. Next, wavelet  transform coherence (WTC) was used to assess IBS from the corresponding ROIs of the two participants in each group (Grinsted et al., 2004). It can calculate the cross-correlation between two-time series as a function of time and frequency, which can reveal a locally phase-locked behavior that may not be uncovered by traditional time series analysis such as Pearson’s correlation (Grinsted et al., 2004). In further analyses, the IBS increments were converted to Fisher z-statistics (Chang & Glover, 2010). Correlationship between IBS and task performance We tested whether IBS in the PBL and control conditions were associated with task performance and if there any factors affecting them. Hence, we performed a Pearson correlation analysis on overall IBS and the designing draft's score in two tasks of the experimental and control condition. Besides, to examine the relationship between IBS and cognitive load, emotional state, and task enjoyment,  Pearson correlation analysis was employed in the present study. Ratings for cognitive load, emotional state, and task enjoyment were averaged within each pair. We then used Pearson correlation analyses to examine whether perceived emotional valence, emotional arousal, and cognitive load were reflected in IBS in the collaborative condition and whether they could predict STEM task performance.

The IBS in the project-based collaboration and free collaboration conditions First, we identified frequency bands associated with task-related cognitive activity by performing independent samples t-tests on neural synchrony increments. The results showed that under the "free collaboration" condition, neural synchrony increased significantly in the frequency band of 0.0129–0.0258 Hz compared to the resting state. Under the "project-based collaboration" condition, significant increases in neural synchrony were observed in the frequency bands of 0.0517-0.0580 Hz and 0.0122-0.0172 Hz. Therefore, these three frequency bands were selected as the bands of interest for subsequent WTC analysis. We conducted WTC analyses based on three frequency bands of interest identified in the previous phase, and performed independent samples t-tests on the computed neural synchrony increments. The results revealed that, in the frequency band of 0.0122-0.0172 Hz, the “free collaboration” condition did not exhibit a significant increase in neural synchrony relative to the resting state. In contrast, under the “project-based collaboration” condition, significant increases in neural synchrony were observed in channels 13, 16, and 21, all located in the right temporoparietal junction. Accordingly, we averaged the neural synchrony values from these three channels to derive a mean value representing the neural synchrony of the covered region (see Fig. 2). To compare the differences in neural synchrony between the two experimental conditions, we conducted descriptive statistics and paired-sample t-tests on Channel 13, Channel 16, Channel 21, and the mean neural synchrony across these three channels. The results revealed that under the project-based collaboration condition, 1002 the neural synchrony increments in all channels were higher than those under the free collaboration condition. The paired-sample t-test indicated a significant difference in the mean neural synchrony across the three channels between the two experimental conditions (t = 2.780, p = 0.013, Cohen's d = 0.674). This finding suggests that a structured collaborative framework can promote behavioral and cognitive alignment among group members. Compared to unstructured collaboration, the projectbased script provided clear task assignments, role definitions, and expected outcomes, thereby reducing uncertainty in the collaborative process (King, 2007). Script-guided communication and interaction help synchronize participants’ actions and thought processes. Such structured exchanges facilitate the coordination of individual cognitive activities, leading to stronger neural synchrony (Fischer et al., 2013). Previous studies have also indicated that structured interaction patterns can enhance inter-brain neural coordination (Dumas et al., 2010). The r-TPJ, in particular, plays a central role in social cognition, empathy, and perspective-taking (Duan et al., 2020), and is critically involved in collaborative creative tasks and product improvement activities (Li et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2022). By clarifying task and role expectations, the project-based script improved participants’ ability to predict and interpret others’ behaviors, making social interactions more consistent and predictable. This may have enhanced neural activity in the r-TPJ and contributed to increased neural synchrony (Scholz et al., 2009). In other words, when collaboration is guided by an explicit script, individuals are more likely to engage in similar cognitive and socio-cognitive processes, leading to aligned neural activity in the r-TPJ. Furthermore, project-based learning theory emphasizes the value of practice, inquiry, and collaboration in solving complex problems (Condliffe, 2017; Guo et al., 2020). The project-based collaboration script used in this study was designed to support such interactions. According to collaboration script theory, by providing a shared cognitive framework, collaboration scripts can improve communication and information exchange among group members (Kobbe et al., 2007), thereby enhancing neural synchrony during effective collaboration. Collectively, our findings provide empirical evidence underscoring the importance of project-based collaboration scripts in facilitating complex social intera ctions, supporting the integration of collaboration script theory and project-based learning principles to foster effective group dynamics. This study establishes an experimental basis for further exploration of effective collaboration strategies in both classroom and online collaborative learning environments, and opens up new paradigms for investigating the dynamic neural mechanis ms underlying multiperson peer interactions. This study further demonstrates that the project-based collaboration script strategy significantly enhances the quality of engineering group design outcomes, particularly in STEM knowledge integration, creativity, aesthetics, and overall product quality. Pearson correlation analyses revealed positive relationships between neural synchrony in distinct subregions of the right temporoparietal junction and various performance dimensions of the three-person groups’ design outputs. Specifically, IBS in Channel 16 (inferior temporoparietal gyrus) was strongly correlated with STEM knowledge integration, creativity, aesthetics, and total score. Located within Wernicke’s 1004 area, this finding aligns with the established role of Wernicke's area in language comprehension, production, and information integration (Binder & Desai, 2011). In the context of engineering design tasks, this may reflect a high level of coordination among group members as they comprehend and integrate interdisciplinary knowledge. Furthermore, the inferior temporoparietal gyrus plays a key role in social cognition and emotional understanding (Van Overwalle & Baetens, 2009), which may facilitate better mutual understanding of ideas and reactions among group members, thereby contributing to enhanced creativity and aesthetic quality in the final product. The IBS in Channel 21 (located in the somatosensory association cortex) was significantly correlated with technical quality. The somatosensory association cortex is involved in processing tactile and proprioceptive information (Bremner & Spence, 2017), which may relate to a deeper understanding of physical component manipulation and perception in engineering design tasks. The project-based collaboration script likely guided members in task division and coordination, thereby improving the group’s focus on technical precision and detailed execution. Last but not the least, p revious research has shown that well-designed collaborative learning strategies can enhance efficient task-related communication in STEM classrooms by providing a structured interaction framework (Canelas et al., 2017), thereby increasing interpersonal behavioral coordination and neural synchrony. Such synchrony may reflect a shared understanding of the design task and aligned cognitive processing among group members. Moreover, this strategy may also strengthen the group's common perception of task goals and expectations, further channeling efforts toward collective objectives (Noroozi et al., 2013). These findings highlight the importance of project-based collaboration scripts in facilitating effective communication and knowledge integration within groups, and suggest neural synchrony as a potential biomarker for collaboration quality. Future studies may further explore how to optimize project-based collaboration scripts to maximize neural synchrony and group outcomes.

## Limitations

While our results suggest interesting avenues for future work, it is important to acknowledge several limitations in the current study. Firstly, gender may influence interpersonal interaction (Cheng et al., 2015; Liang et al., 2022), and the majority of pa rticipants in our study were female-female dyads. In future research, it would be valuable to explore the collaboration dynamics in groups with different gender compositions, as leadership and cooperation within groups may vary by gender (Lu et al., 2020a;  Liang et al., 2022).  Secondly, the consideration of task difficulty across different experimental tasks is important. The tasks in our study were adapted from STEM activities and were determined through discussions with experienced STEM teachers. In future studies, it would be beneficial to carefully consider the age, educational level, and disciplinary background of the participants when designing tasks, and to strive to maintain consistent task difficulty between the two conditions.  Thirdly, the duration of the tasks can impact task performance and neural activity. Some participants in our experiment may have experienced increased fatigue over time, leading to decreased collaborative efficiency in the later stages. Therefore, future research should optimize the duration of experimental tasks within the limits of equipment parameters to ensure data quality and participant well-being. Lastly, this study focused only on bilateral TPJ as ROIs. However, engineering design and collaboration involve neural activity beyond these two brain regions. Future research can explore the involvement of additional brain regions during these processes. These limitations provide opportunities for future studies to further investigate and enhance our understanding of IBS in engineering design groupwork.

To investigate the neural mechanisms underlying engineering design activities in STEM education and the effectiveness of collaborative scripting strategies, this study conducted a hyperscanning experiment involving triadic collaborative engineering design under controlled experimental conditions. The analytical results confirmed our hypothesis that project-based collaboration scripts effectively elicit neural synchrony in the r-TPJ among group  members. Compared to the control condition without scripted support, the project-based collaboration script served as an explicit scaffold that guided and managed the collaborative process. It not only aligned the timing of cognitive engagement and workflow rhythm among group members but also manifested this alignment at the neural level through significantly enhanced inter-brain synchrony. These findings demonstrate the efficacy of project-based collaboration scripts in promoting consistent cognitive activation during STEM engineering design tasks.Furthermore, while previous studies of dyadic collaboration reported neural synchrony in only one channel, this study observed synchrony across three channels within the r-TPJ under the project-based scripting intervention. This provides neuroscientific evidence supporting the value of multi-peer interaction in interdisciplinary STEM learning.  Lastly, the neural synchrony observed under the scripted condition positively predicted performance across all evaluated dimensions of the engineering design task, reaffirming the potential 1005 of neural synchrony as a biomarker for assessing the quality of multi-peer interactions in STEM educational contexts.
