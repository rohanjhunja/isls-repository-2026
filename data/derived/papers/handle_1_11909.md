# Process Features in the Initial Phases of Virtual Collaboration that Predict Ultimate Collaborative Problem-Solving Outcomes

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Collaborative problem-solving (CPS) is crucial for virtual team performance, yet the specific behavioral factors influencing team success, especially in the early stages, are under-explored. This study examines which behavioral characteristics in the early phases predict team performance through two representative tasks, the Mondrian Geometric Fantasy task and the Three-Resistor Activity. The research reveals that the skill of real-time monitoring and evaluation is the most important predictor of team performance in the early stages of CPS, followed by exploring collaborative goals. The former provides continuous evaluation through timely feedback and correction, while the latter sets a directional framework for interaction, reducing cognitive load, thereby improving performance. This study offers insights into the behaviors of student teams in the early stages of CPS and their relationship with team performance, especially in virtual settings. These behavioral characteristics can be utilized to identify key assessment and intervention points for developing CPS skills.

## Introduction

Collaborative problem-solving (CPS) skills are increasingly recognized as  essential for students to address complex challenges effectively. CPS is a multimodal, dynamic, and synergistic phenomenon in which interactive, cognitive, regulative, behavioral, and socio-emotional aspects of collaboration take place syner gistically, intertwine inseparably, and influence each other dynamically (Stahl & Hakkara inen, 2021; Vogler et al., 2017). Therefore, understanding and intervening in students’ CPS processes is a key issue facing researchers worldwide. Due to the dynamic nature of CPS, students may face different challenges and needs at different stages, especially in the initial phase when collaborators form a team and build consensus on the problem. As early as the last century, Tuckman’s (1965) five-stage team development model suggests that behaviors in the initial stages of team formation significantly influence later develo pment. In the Forming stage, if a team can effectiv ely resolve uncertainties among members and establish good comm unication and trust, it will transition smoothly to subsequent stages. In contrast, early conflicts, un clear roles, or poor communication can lead to more  challenges later, affecting overall team performance and task completion quality. Gersick (1988) has further developed this theory, emphasizing that teams do not transition linearly in the early stages but undergo cyclical adjustments and changes, but also agreed that at the start of a tas k, communication, role redefinition, and interactio n among members will critically impact later development. Duri ng initial formation, members work to create a task structure to guide their subsequent interactions  (Kozlowski & Ilgen, 2006). Similarly, some studies have highlighted the importance of early behavioral characteristics in the collaborative process. For instance, reflective interventions by team members in engineering projec t teams can strategically guide members to improve longterm outcomes in planning, responses, and attention to quality (Weger et al., 2022); creating an expanded problem definition led to creative, integrative solutions (Basadur et al., 2000). However, these studies mostly remain at the theoret ical and qualitative analysis level  and need more empirical evidence to validate their findings. Therefore, we adopted a data-driven approach by process data analysis to identify features that facilitate or hinder CPS performance in the early stages. Process data, including response time, eye tracking, keystroke, and problem-solving actions and sequences  gathered by the system or through external devices, can provide a wide range of data that has attracted increasing attention in educational research (Keehner et al., 2016). With process data, we can examine the dynamic and t emporal characteristics of the team and collaborators (Ouyang et al., 2023). Intervening in these feature s may help optimize collaboration efficiency and assist students in improving their c ollaboration and problem-solving abilities (Gu et al., 2015). Blatchford et al. (2007) emphasize the importance of adequate training for e ffective team dynamics, which lays the foundation for successful collaborative learning.

Additionally, this study includes two heterogeneous datasets collected in China and the US separate ly with different CPS tasks to achieve better generali zability. Existing CPS research tends to focus only on single tasks (Andrews-Todd et al., 2023), with most studies conducted in highly controlled experimental environments, limiting the generalizability of the conclusions. For instance, s ome studies relied on analyzing behavioral characteristics in a single dataset of the CPS process  and have found that prior knowledge, constructing team climate and mental models, being organized and goal-driven, proposing ideas appropriately, seeking suggestions, and providing praise and encouragement are signific ant predictors of problem-solving success  (De Boeck & Scalise, 2019; Han et al., 2023; C. Sun et al., 202 2; Zhu et al., 2023). Their conclusions often need cross-context applicability, especially in complex real-world tasks where the transferability of results is questionable.

To address this research gap, recent scholars have started exploring multi-dataset analyses of CPS skills across different task contexts (Hao et al., 2017). Based on this approach, this st udy adopts a data-driven quantitative method and analyzes two heterogeneous datasets to explore the following research questions. RQ1: Are any team behavioral characteristics related to coll aborative problem-solving performance in the initial virtual team collaboration stage?

RQ2: Which behavioral characteristics in the initial stage can predict the final performance?

## Method

The study is based on two representative collaborative tasks, the Mondrian Geometric Fantasy (MGF) (Xu et al.,

2024) and the Three-Resistor Activity(3R) (Andrews-Todd & Forsyth, 2020). These tasks represent two distinct

cognitive domains and provide a means to validate the cross-domain consistency of the research findings. Tasks design MGF is a puzzle-based online CPS task that requires three participa nts to complete within 40 minutes. The task is divided into two stages: the first involves assembling 18 puzzle pieces, and the second involves 27 puzzle pieces. Each team member can see the areas  other members must assemble but cannot see their own. Therefore, participants must communicate through an online anonymous chat interf ace to complete the task. The team only succeeds when all members have successfully assembled their pieces. Figure 1 Mondrian Geometric Fantasy Interface Diagram 3R focuses on series circuits in physics. Participants engaged with the interface shown in Figure 1, which included three variable resistors, a digital multim eter, a calculator, and a chat box. Three participa nts controlled one resistor each, adjusting their resistance value s to reach a target voltage. Effective communicatio n and collaboration were crucial for completing the task due to the interdependent nature of voltages in a series circuit. The task was divided into four increasingly complex levels (none, A, B, C), with team performance assessed based on the number of levels completed successfully within the specified time. ## Participants

For the MGF task, 840 students from 16 high school classes in Hefei City, Anhui Province, were volunta rily involved in the study. Data collection was conducte d in batches from September 5 to 7, 2023. All students were informed about the task content, data collection me thods, and purposes, and they could withdraw at any  time without reason. They also signed an informed consen t form. Upon completion of the task, all students received a set of cultural and creative souvenirs. The Collaborative Learning and Support System (CLASS) (Zhu et al., 2023) captures and records each participant’s operational and conversational data in log files, which were then translated into final process data, including Team ID, User ID, Time of Creation, User Name, Content of Conversat ion, Content of Operation, Virtual Student Name, and oth er fields, for human coding. During the task, 21 teams were excluded due to early completion or abnormal discon nection, and 30 were excluded for violating protoco ls or showing signs of passive participation. After these exclusions, 229 teams remained in the dataset. The final dataset includes a total of 241,906 valid process data entries. Analysis To extract early features of team collaboration, we  selected the first third of the encoded behavior s equences for each team and calculated the frequency of occurrenc e for twelve skills. To ensure the accuracy of subs equent analysis, we standardized these skill frequencies and then  calculated their correlations with team performanc e. Since MGF used continuous data (number of successfu lly merged fragments) as the performance indicator, Pearson correlation analysis was applied. For the 3R task, where team performance was measured by the number of successful challenges (an ordinal variable ranging from 0 to 4), Spearman correlation analysis was used. To identify which significantly correlated features  were the most important predictors of CPS performance, we conducted backward elimination regr ession analysis. Considering the team performance o f the MGF dataset was measured by scores, which are continuous variables, multiple linear regression was used for it. While ordered probit regression was for 3R since it s team performance was measured by the number of le vels completed, which are ordinal variables. This method  allowed us to iteratively remove less s ignificant predictors, retaining only those that most effectively contributed to explaining the variance in CPS performance.

## Results

For the MGF dataset, the results showed significant  positive correlations between team performance and  the frequencies of skill occurrence A2 (r=.199, p=.002), B2 (r=.237, p<.001), C1 (r=.279, p<.001), D2 (r=.296, p<.001), and D3 (r=.173, p=.009). However, the average occurrence of D3 is 1. 135 per team, and about 57.64% of teams have never used this skill. Therefore, D3 might be influenced by outliers and was not considered further. For the 3R dataset, Spearman rank correlation reveal ed significant positive correlations between team performance and the frequencies of skill occurrence  A2 (r=.236, p=.008), C2 (r=.233, p=.009), C3 (r=.181, p=.042), and D2 (r=.315, p<.001). Similarly, the average occurrence of D3 is 0.496 pe r team, and about 74.79% of teams never used this skill. The regression anal ysis of D3 frequency and team performance is correl ated in MGF but not in 3R, indicating that the relationship between D3 skill and team performance may not generalize to other contexts or requires further in-depth analysis. Thus, it will not be explored further. After confirming no multicollinearity issues in bot h datasets, we conducted  regression analysis. A multiple linear regression model for the MGF dataset (R²=0.112, p<.001) indicated that the frequency of D2 was the most important indicator of the ultimate performance (Estimate=0.147, p<.001). An ordered probit regression model for the 3R dataset  also indicated  that D2 was the most significant predictor of team performance (Estimate=0.501, p=.007), with the occurrence of A2 also being significant (Estimate=0.413, p=.027).

## Discussion

This study reveals that in the early stages of virt ual collaboration, D2 (monitoring the results of actions and evaluating success in solving the problem) and A2 (discovering the type of collaborative interaction required and establishing goals) are crucial predictors of team performance and strongly correlate with final team performance. Applying the D2 skill is crucial in early-stage collaboration as the most significant predict ive variable. In a virtual collaborative environment, where team members cannot observe each other’s reactions as in face-toface communication, D2 skill represents a team-level monitoring function. They allow the team to e valuate the effectiveness of solutions in real time, detect misunderstandings and deviations, and prevent the accumulation of erroneous cognition. Early-stage D2 skill could foster a positive feedback loo p of “rapid feedback-timely correction”, which enhances team synchronization and consistency, ensuring that the team stays on track toward solving the problem. This mechanism significantly improves final problem-solving outcomes. Moreover, overcoming “groupthink” and individual co gnitive biases enables more effective collaboration and improves intellectual performance (Atlee, 2008). Effective monitoring and assessment of team progress can help teams adjust strategies more accu rately, maintaining efficient collaboration. Partic ularly in remote team settings, monitoring is central to prob lem-solving and a key driver in keeping the team focuse d and motivated.

The A2 skill also demonstrates its importance by helping establish the team’s cognitive foundation by encouraging team members to communicate proactively, clarify task requirements, and identify available resources. This enables the formation of a cognitive map of the task environment, alleviating the cognitive burden in subsequent stages of collaboration and significa ntly enhancing  problem-solving efficiency. This finding is consistent with the conclusions of Weger et al. (2022), who argue that team reflection facilitates in-depth discussions of key issues and promotes frequent tra nsitions between reflection and action stages, ulti mately improving team performance. Additionally, Rojas et al. (2022) emphasize the importance of establishing rules regarding t ask characteristics, resources, and distribution, facilitating discussion, reflection, monitoring, and control of the problem-solving process, thereby fostering more effective collaboration and decision-making. Both A2 and D2 skills belong to the category of “tak ing appropriate actions to solve problems” within the cognitive dimension and are statistically significantly correlated with team performance. In the early stages of CPS in virtual environments, where face-to-face communication is absent, team members require more explicit collaboration frameworks and systematic monitoring mechanisms to reduce uncertainty. Thus, these skills may be more important than those in other categories  in the initial phase of coll aboration. It should be noted that the significance of A2 is not found in the regression model for the MGF dataset. This may be attributed to differences in task types. The MGF task involves spatial unders tanding and visualization abilities, which are more  intuitive, potentially making it easier for participants to grasp the problem structure and its solutions. The problem-solving path may be more open-ended and complex, and participants may require more dynamic adjustments and engage in trial-and-error pro cesses throughout the task, which may result in A2 having a lesser impact on the final performance. In contrast, the 3R task involves prio r physical knowledge and typically has a clearer st ructure and solution path. Compared to the MGF task, the explor ation and understanding of goals and division of la bor are more complex in the 3R task, demanding more frequen t monitoring and evaluation during collaboration, t hus giving A2 a greater guiding role in the problem-solving process. Additionally, differences in age, language background, and other factors among participants in the two datasets may also influence the results, which could be further explored and compared.

Moreover, two other skills in this dimension, B2 (i dentifying and describing tasks to be completed) and C2 (enacting plans), also show significant positive  correlations in one dataset, suggesting that furth er research could explore these two skills. Additionally, C1 (communicating with team members about the actions performed) and C3 (following rules of engagement) show signifi cant positive correlations in one dataset,  hinting at undiscovered relationships that need further research.

## Conclusion

This study explores the roles and interplay of D2 and A2 in CPS processes. The findings indicate that D2 and A2 are key predicting factors for team collaboration e fficiency and their interaction role in CPS tasks. D2 helps maintain focus and motivation within the team by pr omoting the monitoring and evaluation of task progr ess. While A2 drives the implementation of concrete actions, thereby accelerating the problem-solving process. The findings are important for understanding and intervening in the CPS proces s. Theoretically, this study extends existing CPS research. We provide mor e objective and reliable analysis results by examin ing two heterogeneous CPS task datasets. This further validates the predictive role of action monitoring and evaluation in CPS performance, reinforcing the conclusions of Haa taja et al. (2022) and enhancing the reliability and generalizability of our findings. Haataja et al. em phasized that valence (i.e., whether team members p erceive progress toward the goal) is a key predictor of team performance. Although similar research (De Boeck & Scalise, 2019; Han et al., 2023; C. Sun et al., 2022; Zhu et  al., 2023)  identified certain predictive features, due to the limitations of a single dataset and the lack of exp loration of intervention timing, the practical appl ication value and intervention effectiveness of the predictive fe atures remain unclear. Focusing on the early stages of the CPS process, this study provides specific timing and strategic guidance for  interventions. It offers more detailed and actionable insights for intervention design, enrich ing the theoretical understanding of behavioral fea tures within the CPS process and providing a new perspective on how to implement interventions in actual collaborat ive settings.

By identifying two early-phase key skills, action monitoring and evaluation  (D2) and  collaborative interaction identification and goal-setting (A2), and understanding their roles within the CPS fram ework, we can implement targeted, dynamic interventions early in the CPS process to optimize team collaboration and problemsolving outcomes. Once these two skills’ specific manifestations and interrelationships in CPS tasks are identified, interventions can be tailored based on these featur es. In the early stage s of CPS, teams often lose direction or focus. Encouraging more effective action monitoring  and evaluation can help refocus the team. I ntervention strategies can promote timely, explicit collaborati ve interaction identification and goal-setting actions if the collaboration goal becomes unclear. This could incl ude providing reminders or specific action steps to advance progress. Rather than waiting until task completion  to assess success, real-time feedback and early-stage intervention can improve both the process and outco mes. Facilitators or team leaders candynamically g uide the team, correct imbalances, or reinforce  effective behaviors, enhancing collaborative perfor mance. This approach aligns with the adaptive nature of the CPS process, better matching the participants’ behavioral characteristics and contextual needs. It also provides a new, effective method for fostering and improving students’ CPS skills. This study offers practical strategies for effectiv e teamwork for participants in virtual collaboratio n environments. To quickly form efficient CPS teams in anonymous and dispersed virtual settings, team members should set specific, measurable goals and define milestones to encourage more interaction identification and goalsetting behaviors. In addition, regular progress mo nitoring and task evaluation can also help team members identify potential issues and  promote ongoing monitoring and evaluation, ensuri ng high efficiency and coordination throughout task execution. In this way, teams can quickly establish effective collaborati on frameworks, maximize collaboration efficiency, and ultimately enhance CPS skills. However, the study does have certain limitations. While it identifies the occurrence frequency of various collaboration skills during the CPS process, it struggles to capture these skills’ temporal dynamics and interactions. Therefore, it does not fully model and detect the CPS process’s inherent nonlinear and dynamic characteristic s (Ouyang et al., 2023). Additionally, the study only identifies early behav ioral characteristics that influence ultimate performance, but the effectiveness of inte rventions targeting these characteristics has yet to be empirically validated.

Future research should further investigate the impact of specific intervention strategies on e nhancing team collaboration and evaluate their effectiveness and generalizability across different contexts. Moreover, given the multidimensionality and complexity of behavioral characteristics, a single characteristic may not fully capture the state of collaboration. Therefore, future resea rch should consider multiple behavioral characteris tics, their interactions, and dynamic changes to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the complexity and variability of collaboration.
