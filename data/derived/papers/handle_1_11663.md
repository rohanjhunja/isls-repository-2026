# Enhancing Educational Engagement: Evaluating the Potential of 360° Virtual Field Trips in Immersive Learning Environments

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Virtual Field Trips (VFTs) using 360° technologies represent a transformative approach in education, merging accessibility with immersive learning. This study examines a 360° virtual paleontology exhibition developed as a Web VR experience. It focuses on fostering learner engagement and enhancing educational motivation. A mixed-method evaluation involving 20 undergraduate students revealed that the virtual environment significantly exceeded expectations in terms of learner attention and acceptance. The results indicate that VFTs may offer engaging and motivating learning experiences that share similarities with those reported for physical museum visits, though direct comparisons were not part of this study.

## Introduction

This study investigates the affordances of 360° vir tual field trips (VFTs), focusing on their ability to immerse learners in authentic learning contexts while overc oming logistical constraints of physical excursions. A VFT is broadly defined as “a journey taken without actuall y making a trip to the site” (Woerner, 1999: 1237), enabling learners to experience a real or imagined location without physically visiting it. Using 360° media, V FTs create immersive experiences that allow learners to explor e and interact with environments captured from the physical world, often incorpo rating interactive elements such as navigation betw een different perspectives or clickable hotspots that integrate images, text, audio, or video (Garcia et al., 2023). Studies on the effectiveness of field trips in promo ting motivation and knowledge retention (Falk & Dierking, 2018; Makransky & Mayer, 2022; Zhao et al., 2021) indicate that VFTs can likewise enhance student engagement and academic achievement (Koçoğlu & Haidari, 2025). However, the unique affordances of VFTs in shaping engagement and motivation require further e xploration. Leveraging the concepts of “place” and “scale” (Klippel et al., 2019), 360° media-based VFTs foster presence and proportionality, aug menting the educational value of physical field trips. With these principle s in mind, we developed a Web VR paleontology e xhibition, enabling users to explore four prehistoric skeleton s from the Paleontological Museum in Munich, German y, enriched with audiovisual elements designed to spark interest and encourage self-directed inquiry (see Figure 1). ## Research design

Employing a mixed-methods approach, the research combined quantitativ e and qualitative data collection to provide a comprehensive understanding of learner responses. The participant group consisted of 20 undergraduate students enrolled in a BA program in educational sciences. Their prior experience with virtual reality (VR) varied, though most had limited familiarity with using VR f or educational purposes. The study was structured i n three phases. First, during the pre-test phase, participants completed a survey that as sessed their openness to digital learning tools, prior VR experience, and expectatio ns for the learning environment. This was followed by the intervention phase, in which participants explored the 360° learning environment on tablets. The envir onment offered hotspots containing auditory, visual, and t extual information about paleontological exhibits, such as the Giant Deer and Ancient Elephant, enabling participa nts to engage interactively with the content. Final ly, in the post-test phase, participants completed a second survey that evaluated their acceptance of the learning tool, their perceived attention during the session, and their o verall satisfaction. The pre-and post-test instruments included a four-item scale assessing media acceptance and a three-item scale evaluating the usage of the 360° VFT, bo th adapted from a revised version of the questionnaire by Burghard et al. (2008), as well as a 12-item attention scale (Loorbach et al., 2015) derived from the ARCS model. Open-ended questions gathered feedback and improvement suggestions, focusing on how interactive hotspots a nd self-paced multimodal content supported Attention, Relevance, Confidence, and Satisfaction.  By linking these post-test measures to the broader concepts of engagement and motivation, the study provides a str uctured framework for understanding how VFT design choices translate into learner experience and educational impact.

## Data analysis and discussion

Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive s tatistics to calculate mean scores and standard deviations for media acceptance, attention, and satisfaction. Correlation analysis was conducted to explore relationships between pre-test expectations and post-test evaluations, as well as between perceived atte ntion and overall satisfaction. Qualitative data, derived from participants' open-ended responses, were thematically analyzed to iden tify recurring themes related to usability, perceived be nefits, and suggestions for improvement.  The quantitative findings indicated high levels of media acceptance among participants, with a mean score of M = 4.10 (SD = 0.58) on a 5-point scale. Attention levels during the session were also strong (M = 4.15, SD = 0.62). Correlation analysis revealed significant positive relationships, notabl y between pre-test expectations and post-test satisfaction (r =.56; p <.01), as well as between attention and overall satisfaction (r =.64; p <.01). These results suggest that the virtual learning environment successfully engaged p articipants and met or exceeded their expectations. Qualitative data from participants' open-ended responses were thematically analyzed, identifying three main areas of focus: (1) Immersion and Navigation, (2) Interactivity and Engagement, and (3) Technical and Design Limitations. Participants highlighted the immersive design and  self-paced navigation as key strengths but expressed a desire for continuous navigation instea d of discrete teleportation. Engagement was closely  tied to interactivity, with suggestions for 3D models and a nimations to enrich the experience. Additionally, s ome participants noted that using tablets instead of VR headsets limited immersion, emphasizing the potential benefits of fully immersive VR.  These qualitative findings align with  the quantitative results, particularly regarding attention and satisfaction, and echo prior research on immersive learning (Makransky & Mayer, 2022). The results also resonate with Klippel et al.’s (2019) concepts of place and scale, as participants valued the ability to explore exhibits from different perspectives but sought gre ater interactivity and spatial freedom.  High media acceptance and attention suggest the VFT created a meaningful sense of place, allowing participants to connect wi th the Paleontological Collection in Munich in immersive a nd engaging ways. Participants' calls for continuou s navigation and 3D models also resonate with the con cept of scale, enabling learners to explore exhibit s from varying perspectives and sizes, deepening their und erstanding. These results highlight how integrating  place and scale can enhance engagement and exploratory learning in VFTs.

## Conclusions and future research

This study highlights the potential of 360° VFTs for enhancing learner engagement, motivation, and satisfaction, with promising parallels to physical museum visits.  However, limitations include the small sample size, which restricts the generalizability of the findings, and the use of tablets rather than fully immersive VR headsets, which constrained the level of immersion. Future research  should investigate the impact of dedicated VR hardware and the long-term effects of VFTs on knowledge retention and lea rner outcomes. Building on these findings, future discussions might consider integrating advanced interactive features, such as dynamic 3D models and continuous navigation, to enhance learners' sense of place and scale. Insights provided by Heuke genannt Jurgensmeier et al.

(2023) regarding the practical affordances and limi tations of existing virtual field trip software cou ld serve as a

useful starting point for guiding the development o f such immersive educational experiences.  Additionally, exploring how the ARCS model of motivation can be f urther utilized to design highly engaging and acces sible VFTs presents an exciting area for collaboration. These elements provide a foundation for refining the design and application of VFTs, positioning them as transformative tools in modern education.
