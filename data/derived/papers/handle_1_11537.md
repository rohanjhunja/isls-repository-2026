# Transformative Pedagogy in Action: Global Dialogues and Climate Change Education for Prospective Teachers in Hong Kong

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores how prospective teachers in Hong Kong engage in transformative pedagogy within climate change education through the Global Dialogue for Climate Change Education (pseudonym), a collaborative program involving online dialogues with UK peers. Using positioning theory, we examine how participants articulate their roles and identities in climate education. Key themes include self-awareness, ethical responsibility, admiration for global peers, collaborative engagement, and commitment to action planning, highlighting transformative practices that promote sustainability and equity in educational settings.

## Introduction

The urgency of climate change calls for innovative educational approaches that empower future teachers as agents of transformation (Field et al., 2023). This study examines how prospective teachers in H ong Kong engage with climate change education through the “Global Dialogue for Climate Change Education” program (pseudonym). This cross-institutional program facilitates interactions betw een 13 Hong Kong and 5 UK prospective teachers via two Padlet tasks (sharing local issues and curricula) and two Zoom meetings (discussing experiences and proposing curriculum improvements). Hong Kong p articipants further engage in focus group discussio ns to reflect on these interactions.

Emphasizing transformative pedagogy, the program en courages collaborative learning and critical engagement, empowering teachers to address climate crises while imagining sustainable futures  (Goodale et al., 2024; Leite, 2024). The study focuses on how prospective teachers per ceive their roles, construct identities, and integrate program insights into teaching practices.

## Theoretical framework

Anchored in transformative pedagogy, the program fo sters critical thinking about local and global clim ate curricula. Transformative learning reshapes deeply held beliefs through critical dialogue (Mezirow, 1991; 1997), enabling prospective teachers to embrace global citizenship and develop action plans for sustainability. The cross-cultural exchanges in the program encourage reflection on biases and promote deeper understandings of climate change. Creative methods like show-and-tell, concept mapping and drawing allow participants to challenge existing views and reimagine teaching practices. Positioning theory (Davies & Harré, 1990)  further examines how participants articulate their roles and identities in climate change education, highlightin g the transformative potential of global peer inter actions. This theory allows for a nuanced examination of the narr atives and language participants use to describe th eir experiences, particularly in relation to their global peers.

## Research questions

This study was guided by the following research questions:

1. How do prospective teachers in Hong Kong perceive their roles and construct their identities in

climate change education through the lens of positioning theory?

2. How does the “Global Dialogue for Climate Change Education” program, using transformative

pedagogy, affect prospective teaches’ action plans in climate change education?

## Method

Qualitative data were gathered from 13 Hong Kong pa rticipants through focus group discussions, Padlet posts, show-and-tell artefacts, concept mapping, and collaborative drawing, aimed to facilitate rich, reflective conversations where the participants could have ample opportunities to express their thoughts and feelings about climate change education and their roles as future teachers. Thematic analysis identified key insights into how global dialogues foster reflection and transformative learning.

## Discussion and conclusion

This study highlights the transformative potential of teacher education in climate change. Themes of s elfawareness, ethical responsibility, collaboration, a dmiration for global peers and action planning emph asize the importance of reflective and practice-oriented learning environments. By equipping prospective teachers with the tools to address climate issues, the program foster s a generation committed to sustainability  (Favier et al., 2021; Leichenko & O’Brien, 2020; Vandael & Stålhammar, 2022). Future research should explore diverse pedagogical strategies to enhance the impact of climate education on global challenges.
