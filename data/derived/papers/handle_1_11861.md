# Teachers Learning: Interaction and Discourse in a Virtual Community: A Design Model

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This practice-oriented paper presents a model for a virtual learning community for teachers and examines teacher learning within this framework. The community’s learning primarily occurs through discourse on the WhatsApp messaging platform. The paper begins by describing the community learning model, emphasizing key characteristics of online professional development. Using qualitative content analysis, we analyzed WhatsApp activity from the first month of the 2021 school year, encompassing 137 posts, and identified learning discourse topics, interaction frequency, and interaction types. The WhatsApp-based community addresses a major challenge in online learning: fostering frequent and spontaneous interactions among members. The paper demonstrates the model’s potential to promote an active and engaging approach to professional development.

## Introduction

This paper presents a model of a virtual learning c ommunity for teachers and examines teacher learning  within this framework. We identify learning discourse topics and the types and frequencies of interactions. Teacher communities are widely recognized as an eff ective professional development (PD) approach (Hadar & Brody, 2017). Recent technological advance ments and the extensive experience of online learni ng during COVID-19 have significantly influenced these communities,  leading to an increasing adoption of digital tools, with some communities operating entirely in online environments. The growing need for online te acher learning, along with the incorporation of diverse digital tools, has become an essential ele ment of teachers' PD (Bragg et al., 2021; OECD, 2020; Baroud & Dharamshi, 2020). Consequently, the integration of technolog y within teacher communities serves as a catalyst for  enhancing PD and fostering collaboration skills. T his shift toward digital and online learning platforms has fundamentally reshaped the way teachers engage in PD. Online learning and online professional development Online learning refers to learning that takes place  over the internet, either synchronously or asynchr onously, utilizing a variety of devices (e.g., tablets, laptops, desktops, smartphones). Online learning occurs through digital platforms, such as social networks or dedicated learning management systems, enabling a wide range of modalities, including video meetings, access to onl ine materials and content, interactive questions, q uizzes, and hands-on experiences within virtual environments (Wong, 2 023). Research has recognized online learning as a catalyst for transformative changes in learning processes and the creation of new learning channels. The literature identifies several key characteristics of online learning, all of which are evident in the virtual learning community for teachers discussed in this paper (e.g., Singh & Thurman, 2019; Wong, 2023; Bragg et al., 2021): ● Access to Current Resources: Online learning provides up-to-date resources readily accessible to all learners.

● Flexibility: Learners can engage at any time, from any locatio n, and in various formats, allowing for expanded choices and accommodating diverse learning needs. ● Autonomy and Personalization: Online learning offers learners the ability to cu stomize their learning level and pace, either by personal preference or through adaptive technological tools. ● Multiple Interactions: Online environments encourage participation, know ledge sharing, collaboration, consultation, and requests for support among participants. Online teachers’ PD, a branch of online learning, has emerged as a significant field in education over the past decade, broadening opportunities for flexible and personalized learning (Hao & Gu, 2024; Kohnke e t al., 2024). The effective design and implemen tation of online PD programs relies on several key characteristics contributing to enhanced learning outcomes and impr oved teaching practices (Bragg et al., 2021), inclu ding: (1) support for teachers (e.g., facilitators, technical  assistance); (2) developm ent of pedagogical content knowledge for effective instruction within subject areas; (3) eng agement and flexibility; (4) provision of learning activities that can be applied in classroom instruction; (5) community building; and (6) opportunities for self-reflection and alignment with teachers’ needs (Bragg et al., 2021). One feature of effective  online PD is microlearning, which delivers content in small, focused segments, design ed for rapid learning and easy application. Microle arning enhances retention and engagement by offering infor mation that can be easily applied. It often leverag es digital tools and platforms, allowing learners to access content at their convenience across various devices, thus catering to their needs (Kohnke et al., 2024).

This paper examines a model of a virtual learning community for teachers, with WhatsApp as its primary communication platform. By describing the design of  this community model, we illustrate the characteri stics of online learning it embodies. Through an analysis of  one month of community activities, we demonstrate the content and type of teacher interaction. In the following, we introduce the virtual learning community model and then explore several aspects of teacher learning within this model. The virtual learning community for teachers The Center for Educational Technology (CET) in Isra el established a virtual learning community for mid dle school teachers of Jewish-Israeli Culture Studies in state schools in Israel.  The community enables teachers to collaborate and develop professionally  in an online setting, using WhatsApp as the primary  communication platform. Active since 2017, the community operates based on social network principles and is supported by two facilitators. The community invites teachers to joi n voluntarily by completing  a form with their contact details, school affiliation, and expectations. This allows f acilitators to establish personal contact with each  teacher to ensure the community meets their needs. Facilitator s guide and support teachers in the PD process, add ress professional issues, foster interactions among teachers, and provide support within the platform or through direct communication—factors critical to the success of PD (Babinski et al., 2009; Dille & Rokenes, 2021). The community includes teachers, principals and pedagogical coordinators, some of whom have shifted roles yet remain involved in the community. Since its incepti on, the community has expanded from a few dozen to over 200 teachers by 2024.

Activities and interactions design The virtual community includes several key activities and types of interactions on the WhatsApp platform: ● Learning routines: Weekly routines consisting of short, "bite-sized" content, delivered in small, focused segments, designed for rapid learning and easy appl ication. Routines are delivered at specific times a nd on set days, with varying purposes, such as stimula ting discussion, fostering skills, or challenging assumptions to develop critical thinking. For example, "One concept first thing in the morning" – a short three minutes podcast, introducing a concept in Jew ish-Israeli Culture, or “Additional seasoni ng” – a short enrichment content on different relevant topi cs. Community facilitators design and share these routines, which are tailored to holidays, current e vents, curriculum sequence, and teachers' needs. Routines incorporate various media (e.g., short  videos, audio clips, and images). This microlearnin g approach provides accessible, flexible learning opp ortunities, allowing teachers to select content tha t aligns with their specific learning goals and time constraints. ● Collaborative discussions in synchronous WhatsApp m eetings: These are semi-structured learning sessions conducted in real-time on WhatsApp. Facilitators invite teachers to a designated discussion time on WhatsApp, centered around a specific topic of in terest. Unlike the platform's free-flowing activity, these sessions are prepared in advance with structured conten t, including supporting images, discussion questions, and summaries of each learning section. WhatsApp meetings allow participants to engage flexibly while multitasking. Facilitators often conduct a pre-survey to gauge interest in session topics. ● Community interactions: Learning within the community includes spontaneous,  ongoing communication, knowledge sharing, and mutual support. Members share content, learning materials, and pedagogical insights, engage in discussions, send g reetings, collaborate on initiatives, and c ontribute resources to a shared Google Drive. Spontaneous ini tiatives, such as collaborative lesson plans, frequently emerge from these discussions and are uploaded to a community website for collective use. Design features ● Community Network: The community's activities and learning occur on th e WhatsApp platform, providing both accessibility and ease of use. Although each teacher must register to join the community, once a member, there is noneed for a username or p assword, as is typical in ot her online professional development platforms. WhatsApp is an integral part  of the teachers' daily routines, enhancing its usability and familiarity.

● Time Flexibility: Central to the learning experience is time flexibil ity. Teachers can access information, learn, or participate in collaborative discussions at their convenience, while scheduled meetings provide structured engagement opportunities. This ongoing interaction supports both individual and collaborative learning, promoting lifelong learning and transforming the group into a community, much like a physical community in a shared space over a continuous timeframe. ● Community Facilitators: The community is led by two facilitators: a middle school teacher and an academic, one of whom is also a co-author of this paper. Both facilitators are experts  in content and pedagogy and are responsible for preparing and sharing weekly routines. They encourage collaboration, foster dialogue through routines, and initiate proa ctive conversations among members. Through WhatsApp, they promote knowledge sharing and cultivate a unique, recognizable community language, exemplified by the slogan: "Learn, Teach, Do," for example. Analysis of teacher learning in the community We analyzed the learning activity within the virtual learning community for teachers by examining 137 posts over a one-month period at the beginning of the 2021 school year. At the time of analysis, the community included 149 middle school teachers. The month selected for analysis was chosen randomly to en sure an objective assessment of the community's data. This sample illustrates some of the learning activities within the community. Data were collected to examine the discourse topics and types of learning interactions, including the frequency and sequential development of some interactions. We employed conte nt analysis to identify discourse topics and qualit atively categorized the interaction types (Scherz et al., 2023). To contextualize the discourse analysis, we also examined participation patterns, including the number of posts, conversations, and active participants. The primary questions guiding this analysis were:

1. What are the topics of discourse in the virtual professional learning community?

2. What types of teacher-learning interactions are evident within the discourse?

Types of teacher discourse During the one month, community activity occurred o n every school day, with no activity on weekends or holidays. Of 149 teachers, 50 (33.56%) were active on the platform-a participation rate notably higher than typical engagement metrics reported on many online social platforms (Babka & Gloeser, 2023). A transition in the cont ent of the posts signals the end of one conversatio n and the beginning of another, for example, several posts ad dressing one topic; or a single post that received no further responses. Analysis of the 137 posts revealed 24 conversations, which were categorized into five discourse topics: content-focused discourse, pedagogical content knowledge discourse, procedural discourse, social discourse, and other interactions (irrelevant communications such as mistake messages and indirect educational promotions). Types of teacher-learning interactions The 137 posts were analyzed for types of teacher in teractions. To achieve this, the posts in the Whats App group were categorized into five types. (1) Original posts initiated by a community member to start a conversation with a question, comment or an idea (17 posts, 12.4%). (2) Sharing posts,  which included materials, resources, or experiences (24 posts, 17.5%). (3) Responses, consisting of replies to questions or comments (44 posts, 32.1%).

(4) Emotional reactions, such as emojis, likes, stickers (29 posts, 21.2%). (5) Facilitators posts, made by the

community facilitators (19 posts, 13.9%). Among the  facilitator posts, three distinct categories were identified: announcements (12 posts, 8.8%), routines (5 posts, 3.7%), and greetings (2 posts, 1.5%). Four messages  were deleted during this period (4 posts, 2.9%).

The analysis revealed an active and dynamic discour se within the community. The combined total of responses and emotional reactions suggests a high l evel of interactivity among members. Emotional resp onses, including emojis, likes, and stickers, reflect mutu al support. The sharing of materials indicates an a ctive professional knowledge exchange among members. The facilitators’ level of involvement represents a bal anced approach typical of community interactions, providi ng space for teachers' activities whil e maintaining a supportive presence through announcements, routines, and greetings. We also analyzed the interactions emerging from the learning routines. Of the five routines sh ared by the facilitators during this month, three e ncouraged interaction that promoted two types of learning: sharing topic-related content (presentations/lesson plans) and reflecting on potential student perspectives regarding the presented topic. ## Conclusion and next steps

The analysis illustrates the role that virtual prof essional learning communities can play in fostering  teacher learning and collaboration. It focuses on the types of content that drives teacher activity and engagement, teacher interaction, and the potential of structured routin es to promote learning among community members. The  model creates an open and accessible space for teacher discussions, exchange of ideas and resources, and mutual support. The prominent pedagogical discourse and content-focused conversations suggests that the community f unctions as a catalyst for reflection on teaching practices. Structured routines further enrich these interactions by providing a scaffold for discussions, encouraging the exploration of new ideas, and supporting the application of innovative practices in the classroom. Thus, the community hel ps bridge the gap between theory and practice, enab ling teachers to translate shared knowledge into actiona ble strategies within their own teaching contexts. This model positions the community as a key mechanism for faci litating online learning. Future research will delv e into the interplay between discourse and interaction types over time to identify patterns that maximize the impact of these virtual communities. Additionally, evaluating the quality and depth of discussions will provide insights into how such platforms can be refined to support more robus t professional growth. These studies will contribut e to a broader understanding of how virtual professional l earning communities c an transform teacher professional development and elevate educational practices across diverse contexts.
