# A Preliminary Network Analysis of Theory and Methods in ICLS

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper examines its thirty-year history as a complex adaptive system using network analysis to map the changing relationships among theories and methods from a small sample of 177 manually coded papers from the International Conference of the Learning Sciences. Findings suggest a possible shift from early separation between quantitative and qualitative paradigms toward a more complex multi-method theoretically diverse landscape. These preliminary findings offer a new perspective on the field's developing intellectual identity.

## A Preliminary Network Analysis of Theory and Methods in ICLS

Jonan Phillip Donaldson, Mahan Liaqat, John Bedingfield jonandonaldson@uab.edu, mliaqat@uab.edu, bedingjd@uab.edu The University of Alabama at Birmingham Abstract: This paper examines its thirty-year history as a complex adaptive system  using network analysis to map the changing relationships among theories and methods  from a small sample of 177 manually coded papers from the International Conference of the Learning Sciences. Findings suggest a possible shift from early separation between quantitative and qualitative paradigms toward a more complex multi-method theoretically diverse landscape. These preliminary findings offer a new perspective on the field's developing intellectual identity.

Scientific fields can be understood as complex adaptive systems where theories, methods, and practices interact dynamically, generating emergent intellectual structures that evolve over time (Coccia, 2020). The learning sciences represents a particularly rich site for examining such evolution, as it explicitly positioned itself as interdisciplinary, drawing from cognitive science, education, anthropology, and computer science (Evans et al., 2016). From a complex systems perspective, a field's intellectual landscape emerges not from centralized planning but from countless local interactions of researchers citing theories, adopting methods, and forming communities around shared approaches.  Network analysis offers a window into this emergent structure, revealing how theoretical frameworks and methodological approaches cluster into coherent paradigms and how these relationships shift across developmental phases (Hilpert & Merchand, 2018). This study applies network mapping to examine potential patterns in the learning sciences over its thirty-year history. Understanding these patterns may illuminate not merely what the field has studied, but how its fundamental epistemological structure has evolved.

This proof-of-concept study examines 177 manually coded papers (3.3% of 5,315 total papers) from the three temporal periods: early years (1996-2008; n=54), middle years (2010-2018; n=53), and recent years (20202025; n=70). We developed a comprehensive codebook containing 58 codes across four  categories: Learning Theories, Other Theories, Research Data, and Research Methodology. The first author coded half of the papers, and the second author coded the remaining documents, yielding 921 coded segments.  The research team met regularly tonegotiate the coding, but since this was a pilot study we did not conduct inter-rater reliability testing. For each period, we constructed correlation matrices calculating Pearson correlation coefficients between code pairs based on co-occurrence patterns within each paper. These matrices generated network maps using force-directed layout algorithms, where codes appear as nodes and significant correlations (p <.05) as edges. We applied Girvan-Newman cluster detection algorithms to identify intellectual communities (Girvan & Newman, 2002). tentative and exploratory. Patterns described below are initial hypotheses that we anticipate will change dramatically when we conduct comprehensive analysis.

Network analysis resulted in three maps with 4 clusters in early years (p <.01; Q =.551), 4 clusters in middle years (p <.001; Q =.424), and 5 clusters in recent years (p <.05; Q =.490). T he early years (1996-2008) map might be interpreted as reflecting a field with several distinct research communities. It  has four clusters, each potentially representing a different set of methodological and epistemological leanings. One cluster corresponds to a quantitative, experimental approach informed by cognitivist theory. Another large cluster highlight s work grounded in socially-oriented theories like situated learning and using qualitative methods. A third, smaller cluster suggests the emerging importance of design-based research (DBR), possibly linked to ecological and complex systems theories. The map for the middle years (2010-2018) hints at a move toward consolidation within the field's core, alongside a structural shift. An interesting feature in is the disconnection of the quantitative-2764 experimental cluster from the main network. The main network itself seems more densely connected, indicating a strengthening of a core community centered on qualitative, interpretive, and collaborative methods. N odes explicitly related to a critical turn, such as critical pedagogy, appear to be centrally located. The intellectual landscape suggested by the sample of recent years (2020-2025) could be seen as one of increasing methodological and theoretical complexity. The relatively clear boundaries that may have existed in the earlier maps seem to be replaced by a more integrated and multifaceted network. There is a large multi-methods cluster, where quantitative-experimental work appears to be linked with qualitative methods and critical theories. This could indicate the development of more pluralistic research programs, although different paradigms are potentially incommensurate (Wise & Schwarz, 2017). T he map suggests the formation of a cluster characterized by the application of diverse theories from outside the traditional learning sciences such as complex systems theory and cognitive load theory.

The three network maps suggest a potentially intriguing evolutionary trajectory in learning sciences research dataset. The early years appear to show separation between quantitative-experimental and qualitative-interpretive paradigms. The middle years hint at a pattern of marginalizing experimental approaches and information processing views of learning. The recent years may demonstrate emerging methodological complexity, where experimental designs, qualitative methods, and critical theories begin interacting within research programs. This pattern could indicate movement away from paradigmatic separation. Observing these three maps sequentially allows for the formulation of tentative hypotheses about the field's evolution. One potential pattern, which again would need deeper exploration with a larger dataset, is a move from methodological separation toward multi-method approaches. The maps could suggest a trajectory where distinct quantitative and qualitative camps eventually give way to a more complex approach. The apparent marginalization and later re-integration of experimental methods within this specific sample is a particularly interesting potential storyline that warrants further investigation. This might reflect a dynamic process of methodological negotiation and maturation within the ICLS community over the decades. A second potential pattern suggested by this preliminary analysis is a significant broadening of the field's theoretical foundations. The sample from the early years seems focused on developing a core set of learning theories, while the middle years' sample hints at the integration of critical perspectives. The recent years' map, in turn, suggests a marked increase in theoretical diversity, with the inclusion of numerous frameworks from adjacent disciplines. If this pattern holds in the larger population of  papers, it could signify that the field's focus has expanded from studying learning processes in narrowly-defined contexts to analyzing the complex, intersecting systems in which learning occurs (Jacobson, 2020). From a complex systems viewpoint, such a development might be interpreted as the maturation of a scientific field, characterized by an increase in both internal differentiation and integration. However, these remain preliminary observations from a proof-of-concept study. This pilot study suggested that our method of analyzing ICLS papers can yield fruitful insights. In the next stage of our study we will expand our analysis. We are currently developing and applying a larger codebook of over 300 codes in 19 categories. We will analyze the full text of the complete corpus of over 5,000 ICLS papers. However, manually coding this many papers with such an extensive codebook would be logistically impossible. Therefore, we are currently working with an interdisciplinary team to develop a means by which to train and leverage generative AI tools to autocode the corpus with minimal human-in-the-loop requirements. Rapid advancements in Large Language Model (LLM) size, computational power, and training, combined with improvements in AI prompt engineering, now enable a return to the Artificial Intelligence foundations that originally inspired the learning sciences field (Schank, 2016). The latest LLMs increased performance raises the probability of successfully conducting automated thematic coding across a large corpus of papers using an extensive codebook. We are currently exploring publicly available LLMs that support the OpenAI Application Programming Interface (API), such as ChatGPT, Google’s Gemini, Hangzhou’s DeepSeek, Meta’s Llama, and OpenAI’s GPT-OSS models, with the goal of achieving human-equivalent coding of the larger dataset. To further reduce human workload the team developed software that translates LLM coding results into an industry-standard exchange format, specifically, the Rotterdam Exchange Format Initiative’s Qualitative Data Analysis (REFI; Evers et al., 2020) standard. Converting the thematic codings produced by the LMM into the REFI standard enables seamless integration of the data into statistical analysis tools su ch as MaxQDA. The team expects that combining these tools with the research findings will allow them to complete the analysis, provide deeper insights into the past 30 years of research, and perhaps spark the beginning of a new pattern of inquiry, propelling the learning sciences field into the next three decades.
