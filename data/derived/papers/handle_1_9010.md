# Technological Repurposing: Extending Pedagogical Design Capacity to Include Technological Adaptations

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This report builds upon Brown’s (2009) Pedagogical Design Capacity Framework, to describe instances of “technological repurposing” of technologically-enhanced curriculum developed with prospective mathematics teachers (PMTs). The presentation will highlight the progression of one PMT’s understanding of compactness and how this contributed to making an innovative technological adaptation in a set of lesson resources developed to help middle school students make sense of Gerrymandering.

## Introduction

Given the omnipresence of technology in today’s mathematics classes, it is important to better understand what enables teachers to adapt their curriculum resource s to meet their instructional goals. A number of in fluential policy documents in the U.S. have advocated for Mathematical Action Technology (MAT) (Dick & Hollebrands,

2011) to play a major role in mathematics classroom s due the its ability to present content in novel, more

accessible ways (e.g., NCTM, 2018). The purpose of the study was to examine the effect of the instructional unit on the PMTs’ abilities to revise technology-based curriculum materials to incorporate their own knowledge. My research question is, “In what ways do PMTs adapt technologically-enhanced curricular resources that they have engaged with as learners when they are tasked with thinking about how their future students might engage with the same or similar content?”

## Theoretical framework

In Brown’s (2009) pedagogical design capacity (PDC) framework, the constructs of offloading, adaptation, and improvisation are discussed. Brown describes offloading as the practice of utilizing curriculum as it is written and improvising as the work teachers engage in when utilize their knowledge to build new curricular resources. In this paper, I described certain acts of adaptation as acts of technological repurposing due to the way that much of the original technological components remained intact. I define technological repurposing as an acknowledgement of or adjustment made to an existing piece of technolo gy that is meant to develop an understanding of a p articular content goal which may differ from the original intent of the technology.

## Methods

My analysis utilizing the PDC framework and my conc eptualization of technological repurposing, include d classroom video data, discussion transcriptions, and the PMTs’ documentational work (e.g., their submitted lesson revision assignment). For the analysis at hand, I f ocus only on identifying technological repurposing in my students’ lesson revision assignment. When the technological capabilities along with the questions prompts and informative content appeared to be copied, but then  modified to support a new goal, I referred to this  as repurposing.

## Conclusions and discussion

The role of technological repurposing could be an important area of future research due t o the necessity of technology integration in mathematics classrooms, b ut also because it is often necessary to adapt curr icular resources to meet teachers’ varying instructional goals. Though this study included, but did not focus on the role of sociopolitical context in the intervention lesso n, this is an ongoing project to better understand how teachers utilize a wide variety of knowledge sources in their practice of planning.
