# Categorization and Framing Teaching Discussions in an Online Physics Classroom

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We analyze written discussions of in-service high school physics teachers as part of the coursework of an online Master in Physics with Teaching Emphasis program. We categorize the form and scope of teaching talks made in response to course structure using the theories of community of practice and framing: Strategies for Teaching Math in Physics; Integration of MPTE Course Content into High School Courses; Pedagogical Strategies for Teaching Physics. We discuss the instructional implications.

## Introduction

Physics Education research (PER) has led to the dev elopment of research-based curricula such as tutori als that can be directly implemented in physics classrooms b y instructors. However, many of these efforts histo rically do not include pedagogical insights of instructors in the development process. (Elby & Yerdelen-Damar, 2020). When teachers dialogue around teaching practices, t heir classroom experience could provide a valuable perspective in considering how curriculum is developed and implemented. This provides a venue for teachers to form a community and discuss the shared goal of tea ching practices in high school physics curriculum a ligned with the definition of the community of practice (Wenger, 1999). An online Master in Physics with Teaching Emphasis  (MPTE) program has been developed to strengthen high school physics teachers’ content and pedagogical knowledge. The course work includes 6  core courses. A central part of each course design is pa rticipation in weekly online discussion boards whic h gives rise to teaching discussions. We categorize teachin g talks and contribute examples of teachers’ discus sions by exploring how teachers share about their approach t o teaching physics concepts in their own high schoo l classrooms, the mathematical concepts that is used in teaching physics, pedagogy, and how to connect advanced math and physics topics that they learn as part of the MPTE program into high school classrooms. Structural factors can promote classroom community formation and participation in practices (Irving & Sayre, 2014). In our community, we observed that the course structure (content of lectures and prompts from the course instructor embedded in the video lectures) t ends to inspire different forms of teacher talk. Fo r instance, physics lectures focused on the ‘advanced math content’ tend to fuel more discussion related to Teaching Math in Physics, while prompts presented in the video that asked ‘How do you teach this physics topic in your class?’ prompted more discussions related to Pedagogical Strategies for Teaching Physics. The theme related to Integration was represented in most of the weekly discussions,  as the MPTE course content design is intended to be relevant to the teachers’ teaching topics (See Table 1).

## Methods and Context

In this study, we focus only on teaching discussion s that form as part of the online problem solving discussion forums of a 12-week Electricity & Magnet ism for Educators course in the MPTE program. Each student is required to make at least two posts weekly. Students in this program are mostly in-service high school teachers. Of the 38 teachers from Spring 2018, 22 p articipated in the study. Two independent researchers first read the posts followed by discussions. Analysis of  the first 5 weeks with existing teaching talks rev ealed commonalities among posts, which led to development  of an initial coding scheme. Another round of analysis of further weeks revealed that some of the posts that overlap in the discussion theme are different in scope. Framing provides a window to better see the scope o f the practices in a situation based on the expectation of ‘What is going on here?’ (Tannen, 19 93). Prior study has applied framing to problem sol ving situations, where a student in a narrow frame tends to focus on a task at hand, whereas a student in an expansive frame is more open to take a general approach (Irving et al., 2013). Taking the guide from the study by Irving et al. (2013), we characterized expansive vs. narrow d iscussions in the teaching context. Narrow teaching discussions describe activities that are more focused and implemented to the ‘in the moment’ of the cl assroom, such as when teaching a math skill in a specific wa y to make it understandable for the students. On th e other hand, expansive teaching discussions describe teaching practices that can be applied across multiple c lassroom contexts (e.g. other physics classes), such as teaching students to use mathematical equations in physics. ## Findings and examples

For the theme of Strategies for Teaching Math in Physics, in narrow discussions, teachers tend to identify specific mathematical skills and concepts that students must utilize while studying physics. Teacher 1 addresses student difficulty with a particular math ematical concept (dot product) used in physics. The  teacher suggests that teaching students to use the relevant  trigonometric function rather thandefining the ‘d ot product’ has avoided their student confusion. We classified this comment as a narrow scope as it is focused on teaching a specific math skill. The teacher is not discussing a broad teaching strategy of how to address a mathe matical approach but instead focusing on a small topic of how to teach ‘dot product’ to students. The teacher has chosen to ‘neglect’ addressing the comparison of ‘dot product’ to ‘cross product’, which reinforces the limited scope of the teaching practice, as making this connection for students would have been an expansive approach. Teacher 1: I have so far neglected mentioning ‘dot product’ versus ‘cross product’… and I’ve never really had a whole lot of confusion from the students based on that idea. In an expansive discussion, teachers request for or  discuss with each other general strategies for teaching math in physics. Teacher 2 discusses a gen eral approach for teaching math skills in physics p roblem solving with variables (e.g. alphabet soup) vs. num bers. The teacher suggests a flexible teaching meth od of providing some numerical examples initially and the n transitioning to more symbolic examples. We class ified this comment as an expansive scope as it can be applied to different physics problem-solving situations. Teacher 2: I too prefer the alphabet soup, but my students are not always with me. I generally start with problems with numbers and angles, so tha t my students can get comfortable with the process and then do some with just the alphabet.

## Conclusions and implications

The categorization scheme presented in this study c an be useful in analyzing discussions among teacher s in a learning community. Additionally, the examples suggest that teachers make intentional decisions about utilizing a particular scope of their instruction in the clas sroom to support their students’ learning process. The reasons behind these choices can inform and aid in the deve lopment of physics curriculum for high school students that which portions of it might benefit from a narrow or an expansive perspective.
