# Cultural Humility Through Dance

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Students, faculty, and staff at American universities are currently grappling with policies that seek to limit or bandiversity, equity, and inclusion measures. Higher education is a place where people from diverse backgrounds converge, creating opportunities for learning about oneself and others. To explore how people can learn collaboratively about each other's backgrounds and cultures, we created a Cultural Humility Immersion program centered on dance. The four-week program culminated in a workshop where participants created the learning artifact of dances that represented their cultures. We will analyze this workshop through interviews with participants. Additionally, we will interpret observations of the dance workshop using embodied practice as our framework. The novelty of this work, in which dance is combined with the theoretical framework of Cultural Humility, will lead to productive conversations about the design of future workshops as well as ways that this kind of activity can promote increased cultural understanding.

## Introduction

Individual experiences vary widely across the globe, with each person contributing their unique perspe ctive to everyday interactions, especially in educational en vironments. The convergence of people from diverse backgrounds in these settings creates a val uable opportunity for learning about both oneself a nd others. Of particular significance is the concept of cultural humility, which fosters an understanding of one's own culture as well as the cultures of others (Tervalon & Murray-Garcia, 1998). This learning process supports the development of intrapersonal skills, effective interpersonal communication, and a deeper interest in contributing to meaningful societal and cultural transformation (Coppola & Tay lor, 2022; Gottlieb, 2021; Melchior, 2011). Notably, the landscape of American universities’ diversity, equi ty, and inclusion policies and practices is undergo ing a shift towards the removal of such policies and practices as they have been labeled as “[promoting] different ial treatment” (KUER, 2023). It is within this broader context that our research aims to promote lifelong learning of self and others as a means to generate more inclusi ve and equitable worldviews and commitments for cam pus community members. To work towards this goal, we, a s a team of univ ersity researchers, designed a Cultural Humility Immersion that integrates online learning and embodied practice for graduate students. We car ried out the Cultural Humility Immersion over four weeks, which included three online modules and an in-person cultural dance workshop. Situated in a constructivist paradi gm (Davis & Sumara, 2002; Lincoln & Guba, 2011), ou r research question asks: What aspects of the Cultura l Humility Immersion design support or hinder parti cipant engagement? We will collect a variety  of qualitative data in the form of in-depth interviews, member checks, video analysis, analysis of open-ended responses in the online modules, and research er reflective journal entries to address our question.

## Theoretical framework

In educational settings, the mix of diverse backgrounds and unique experiences each individual brings to everyday situations creates valuable opportunities for learn ing about ourselves and others. All interactions in volve some form of cross-cultural exchan ge, and a way to understand the self-and other-awareness of learners is to look through the lens of cultural humility theory (Tervalon & Murray-Garcia, 1998). Cultural humility is a theoretical lens for conceptualizing how time and space dedicat ed to learners’ inquiry into their own cultures and those of others is connected to transformative commitments t owards greater inclusion and acceptance (Tervalon & Murray-Garcia, 1998). Further, embodied practice is a rich, nascent area of research to explore cultur al humility learning (Danso, 2018; Darivemula et al., 2021). Specifically, dance is an embodied practice and form of learning that engages the body and connects learners to othe rs and their surroundings, which has been shown to be a valuable tool in in the  learning design of cultural humility in settings su ch as K-12 schools (Darivemula et al., 2021; Siljamäki & Anttila, 2019). Embodied practice s, such as dance, help learners explore and appreci ate different cultures as well as their own experiences  as a wa y to begin to formulate rich, transformative commitments to diversity, equity, and inclusion.

## Methods

For the Cultural Immersion Experience, the first portion of the online modules took place via Google Classrooms and the second part was located in person at a univ ersity in the western United States. Nine graduate students from varied fields of study participated in the study. Participants were recruited by sending out invitational posters via email listservs of departments and graduate stu dent groups. We align particular data sources to gr ound interpretations and findings in our theoretical fra mework and the ongoing academic discussion. We plan  to use the data sources of in-depth interviews, member checks, video analysis, an alysis of open-ended responses in the online modules, and researcher reflective journal e ntries. We will inquire into interconnected elements such a s learning objectives, activities, topics, delivery f ormat (whether online or in-person), physical environment, accessibility, inclusivity, and teaching methods to  determine the strengths and areas needing improvem ent throughout the immersion.

The online modules were situated in cultural humility theory and thus included materials on intrapersonal awareness, interpersonal communication, and transfo rmative commitments (Coppola & Taylor, 2022; Terval on & Murray-Garcia, 1998). The in-person dance workshop included warm-up and community building introductory exercises and sharing of cultural dance forms by th e two workshop instructors. For example, a short se quence in Indian traditional dance with gestures and meaning was shared by an instructor. The  workshop was designed to be a place for the learners to come to together to strengthen the self-and other-awareness they engaged with during online activities. The focus of the in-person dance workshop was the final activity where the students were broken into small groups to create a learning artifact i n the form of a dance which they created all aspect s of and shared with the class. The online modules and in-person dance workshop were completed during October  and November 2024. We are currently in the i n-depth interviewing stage. We expect to complete int erviews by December 2024. From there, we will move onto coding  the data in the forms of interview transcripts, re searcher reflections, and online course module submissions. We will do so over multiple stages coding and analy sis followed by interpreting themes to be weaved together into our findings.

## Significance

Cultural humility training is a growing area of wor k across many public-serving settings, such as education, that can facilitate more equitable practices as learners gain new perspectives. There is a need for increased training in cultural humility as educ ation professionals frequently work with students f rom other cultures (Jenkins, 2018). Cultural training provides learners with a deeper u nderstanding of the social dimensions of education,  as well as one’s own personal experience of privilege and marginalization. This proposed research study aims to contribute to the practice of ongoing education and awareness of an individual’s cultural identity, the experiences of others, such as how systems marginalize or privilege commun ities based on perceived identit ies, and skills in cross-cultural settings. Research in this unique context would allow for greater understanding of how graduate students learn through cultural humility training in a uniqu e interdisciplinary educational setting. It has the  potential to contribute to research in the areas of culturally re sponsive pedagogy and teaching strategies, cultural  dance immersion practices as a pathway for enhanced inter cultural skills, and promotion of self-awareness and understanding of others in education settings and more broadly.
