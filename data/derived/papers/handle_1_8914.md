# Biology Teachers’ Re-Designed eLearning Units: The Relationships Between Knowledge Types and Scientific Practices

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Lately, eLearning has substituted traditional forms of teaching and learning. However, little is known about the possible link between eLearning, addressing epistemic knowledge and scientific practices. We aimed to analyze teachers’ re-designed eLearning units and examine the extent to which high school biology teachers address epistemic knowledge and scientific practices in the Personalized Teaching and Learning, PeTeL platform. We qualitatively mapped the re-designed ecology and evolution eLearning units according to knowledge types and scientific practices. We found that a few teachers addressed epistemic knowledge in their re-designed eLearning units, and that questions that include only content knowledge, mostly lacked scientific practices. Thus, it seems that most of the teachers find epistemic knowledge difficult to address.

## Introduction

In the past 25 years, eLearning has substituted tra ditional forms of teaching and learning in schools,  higher education and companies (Herden, 2011). During the COVID-19 pandemic, when educational institutions were closed all over the world, eLearning has become nec essary, facing the challenges of preserving the pro cess of learning while ensuring that it is still valuable (Mailizar et al., 2020). Designing eLearning units and incorporating them into the students' curriculum might increase student motivation and the use of these techniques will probably be needed in the future as well (Mystakidis et al., 2021). Teachers report an increase in ownership, agency and professional development when they re-design eLearning units (Penuel et al., 2007). In this study, as part of a 30 hours professional development (PD) course of in-service teachers, teachers re-designed eLearning units in an eLearning platform, called PeTeL (PeTeL, 2022). The main aim of this study was to analyze teachers’  re-designed eLearning units and trace the epistemi c knowledge and scientific practices that the teachers had implemented into their units.

## Theoretical background

Scientific literacy and types of knowledge A central goal of science education is preparing students for their roles as future scientifically literate people, as reflected in many policy documents worldwide (National Research Council., 2012). In this paper, we follow the definition suggested by the OECD PISA Framework (PISA 2018 Science Framework, 2019), which defines three components of scientific literacy: (a) content knowledge-knowledge of the theoretical concepts and ideas of the discipline; (b) procedural knowledge-knowledge of  the standard procedures that underlie the methods and practices used to establish scientific knowledge; a nd (c) epistemic knowledge-understanding the rati onale of scientific inquiry, how scientific knowledge has been derived; and the degree to which such knowledge is justified by evidence or theoretical explanations. We shall concentrate specifically on this type of knowledge and the way it is reflected in the eLearning units that teachers had re-designed since it has been found that students’ interest in science learning was positively correlated to their  understanding of the epistemic knowledge of scienc e (Ho & Liang, 2015). Additionally, when students actively engage in the  practices of science, it means that teachers believe that their students are capable of thinking in an epistemic manner (Stroupe, 2014). Students who view that scientific knowledge changes and that experiments a re needed to prove it, are more likely to hold cons tructive conceptions of learning science (Ho & Liang, 2015).

The PeTeL platform and teachers as re-designers The PeTeL platform, developed by the Weizmann Insti tute Department of Science Teaching, is an interact ive online Learning Management System (LMS) that allows  educators to manage their classroom materials and individual curricula in a single platform. The vision of PeTeL is to enable science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) teachers in Israel to teach science accompanied by a single, collaborative, digital platform, rich in interactive units that enable diagnostic and evaluation processes integrated into the LMS. When designers design learning units, the content is usually developed from scratch. Yet, in many cases, it is possible to use existing course materials suc h as written books, exercise sheets, instructions f or laboratory lessons and so on, as a basis for developing eLearning units (Herden, 2011). In these cases the teachers are called re-designers and we shall use this term when relating to the teachers who participated in the PD course. Research goal and research question The link between usage of epistemic knowledge, scie ntific practices and re-designing of eLearning unit s in the context of high school biology in general and in ec ology and evolution in particular, has not been pre viously investigated. In this study, we attempt to analyze biology teachers’ re-design of eLearning units duri ng a PD course and trace the epistemic knowledge and scient ific practices that the teachers had implemented in to these units. Accordingly, we aim to answer the following research question: To what extent do high school bi ology teachers address epistemic knowledge and scientific practices in the eLearning unit they re-designed?

## Research design

During summer 2021, a 30 hours PD course "Ecology, evolution and datasets-from the researcher’s desk  to classroom teaching" was offered to high school biology teachers in Israel. The participants of this study were 20 experienced high school biology teachers. The teachers voluntarily chose to participate in the PD cour se and in the accompanying research.

PD course description The course was designed to help high-school biology  teachers incorporate eLearning to their instructio n in the context of ecology and evolution. The PD course was carried out as a hybrid course due to the COVID-19 outbreak restrictions. The 30 teaching hours, included online sessions, a guided tour and asynchronous meetings dedicated to two tasks (Figure 1). The first day of the course was dedicated to introducing an ecology eLearning unit in the PeTeL platform from a student's point of view, a re view of techno-pedagogy in science education and collaboratively establishing criteria for good eLea rning instruction units. Following this session, a task was assigned to the participants, in which they were asked to re-design an instruction unit based on either a textbook, a simulation or a dataset (Figure 1, 1st task). Later, they received personal feedback from the authors based on the pre-established criteria. The second day of the course was dedicated to a tour at the National Natural History Collections with an emphasis on the fish, reptiles and the paleontological collections. The third day included a scientific lecture on evolution, and a further look into the interactive PeTeL platform. On the last day, following a lecture on ecology, the teachers presented their eLearning units (Figure 1, 2nd task), received feedback from their peers and reflected on the process of re-designing their unit. ## Data collection and analysis

During the PD course, each teacher or small group of teachers re-designed an eLearning unit. The data sources of this study were 14 eLearning units in the PeTeL platform. Each question of each unit was coded according to the knowledge components of scientific literacy (PISA 2018 Science Framework, 2019) and NGSS science practices (Next Generation Science Standards, 2013) using ATLAS.ti Scientific Software. Each question received multiple codes. This paper refers to the analysis of a total of 176 questions included in the 14 eLearning units. The data sources were coded by the two first authors. First, two units were coded together. Then, each author coded half of the units herself and the unit s were swapped. The degree of consent was 95%. The differences were discussed until a 100% agreement on all encodings was reached.

## Results

Distribution of knowledge types in the teachers’ eLearning units The distribution of the scientific knowledge needed to answer the questions that were included in the eLearning units that the teachers re-designed is shown in Figure 2. Out of 176 questions, 131 questions required the use of content knowledge (blue), 92 procedural knowledge (orange) and 16 epistemic knowledge (purple). Thirty-nine questions required both content and procedural know ledge. All 16 epistemic knowledge questions include d content knowledge, and eight of them included all three knowledge types (Figure 2). While all 14 units included content and procedural questions, only four units included epistemic knowledge questions. ## Discussion

In this study we present an analysis of biology tea chers’ re-design of eLearning units through which w e investigated the teachers’ use of knowledge types i n these units, dealing with ecology and evolution. Our main findings are that teachers use mainly content knowl edge in eLearning units they re-design, then proced ural and the least epistemic knowledge. This finding is in line with PISA’s target distribution of score points by knowledge type, which states that the proportions of content,  procedural and epistemic knowledge would be 55-66%, 1931% and 11-22% respectively (PISA 2018 Science Framework, 2019 table 4.9). However, our results meet this distribution only in the overall analysis, since we found that only a third of the teachers contributed to the epistemic components and most of the teachers did not relate to epistemic knowledge at all. Although it is known that epistemic knowledge is important for students to be come future educated citizens that can judge misinformation, understand scientific claims and make informed decisions (Hofer, 2020), usually students engage in a rather small amount of epistemic knowledge during their science lessons (Erduran & ICLS2022 Proceedings 995 © ISLS Garcia-Mila, 2015). Despite the importance of this issue, some teachers  do not refer to it during their teaching, maybe since some teachers do not have enough confidence with epistemic knowledge, and find it difficult to deal with NOS in class (Leach et al., 2003). This might explain our finding that only a relatively small percentage of teachers dealt with epistemic knowledge in their units. It was recently shown that there is a hierarchy between knowledge types, when high-school students are using a molecular viewer for answering questions about protein structure and function. It was shown that the use of epistemic knowledge is not possible without the use of content knowledge and procedural knowledge (Levkovich & Yarden, 2021). The analysis of the units described here has also shown this pattern. Thus, epistemic knowledge is difficult for teachers to include in t heir units not only because they might not fully un derstand it, but also because prior content and procedural knowledge, are needed as a basis for students to engage in epistemic knowledge.
