# Teacher Sensemaking in Knowledge Building Professional Development

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This is a work-in-progress case study aiming to understand university instructors’ sensemaking and learning in Knowledge Building professional development. We examined the sensemaking process of two instructors: one with the highest learning gains and one with the least learning gains in a PD program. We found that the instructors with greater learning gains adopted more assimilation and accommodations, while the instructor with the least gains displayed more toleration and distantiation while interacting with others in the classroom.

## Introduction

Teacher professional development (PD) plays a crucial role in enhancing the quality of te aching in higher education institutions (Borko, 2004). Extensive research has examined teachers’ conceptions and practices in PD (Hong & Lin, 2010; Light et al., 2009; Stenberg et al., 2016), and some also examined the focus (Popp & Goldman,

2016) and needs (MacPhail et al., 2018) of teacher PD. However, limited research has examined how teac hers

learn from PD. In this study, we try to examine teacher learning during PD from the sensemaking perspective. Teacher sensemaking refers to teachers’ search for meaning and coherence between their own personal frames of reference and the perceptions of situatio nal demands (Luttenberg et al., 2013; Stollman et a l., 2020). Teachers’ personal frames of reference coul d influence how they interpret new information (All en & Penuel,

2014) and therefore how they learn from PD.  Luttenberg et al. (2013) delineate d four patterns of sensemaking:

assimilation, accommodation, toleration, and distantiation. Specifically, assimilation involves teachers reshaping external ideas to fit their own thinking without changing their own views or pr actices; accommodation entails teachers becoming agents of change by adapting their own perspectives to fit new contexts; Toleration arises when teachers acknowledge alternative views but maintain their original thinking. Distantiation occurs when teachers reject external ideas and persist with their initial perspectives. Much previous research has  focused on teachers’ sensemaking in the context of educational reforms (e.g., Allen & Penuel, 2014; Stollman et al., 2020). Our study aims to extend previous research by exploring the relationship between  teachers’ sensemaking and learning in a PD focused on  learning a new pedagogy. Employing a case study approach, we selected two instructors who demonstrated different levels of learning gains in PD. We try to explore how they differed in their  sensemaking while interacting with others during the PD. It will provide insights into effective means of su pporting teacher learning in PD.

## Participants

Ten instructors from a public university in Singapore participated in a PD training on Knowledge Building (KBPD) (Lin et al., 2023). We examined their conceptions of teaching and learning before and after the PD with Chan and Elliott's (2004) Teaching and Learning Con ceptions Questionnaire. For comparison, w e selected two instructors who demonstrated contrasting learning gains in constructivist views, including one (Aaron) with the highest change (pre-test = 3.75; post-test = 4.25; Change =.05) and one  (Chris) with the least change (pre-test = 4.08; post-test = 4; change = -.08). Pseudonym was used here to ensure the anonymity of the teachers.

## PD context

The KBPD consists of three three-hour synchronous sessions, interspersed with asynchronous learning. The first session introduces KB theory, principles, and technology. The second session emphasized practical applications. Participants were asked to collaboratively redesign a lesson plan with KB pedagogy. Between sessions two and three, participants applied KB in their classes. In session three, participants worked in small groups to refine their KB designs. Throughout, participants were encouraged to actively engage in  Knowledge Forum  (KF) in and outside of the classrooms (Lin et al., 2023).

Data sources and analysis We analyzed Aaron and Ch ris’ transcripts from their whole class and group discus sions to understand their sensemaking process.  Lutternberg’  et al. (2013)  search for meaning framework  was adapted to examine how instructors interpret and treat the new information/knowledge in PD. The focus of the analysis is turns of talk. We categorized four sensemaking patterns: Assimilation involves linking new information to existing knowledge and experiences for understanding. Accommodation involves connecting new information to one's context  for new solutions, necessitating adjustments to existing knowledge or beliefs. Toleration involves linking new information to one’s personal experiences while acknowledging potential limitations in applying it. Distantiation involves rejecting new information based on past experiences, denying or ignoring information conflicting with established beliefs.

## Results

We first counted the number of turns each participant took during a session and calculated the average number of turns per session. Aaron’s average turn of talk per  session is 2 6.33 (8.9%) and Chris is 2 8 (9.4%). It suggested that both participants have a similar amount of talk turns  in the classroom. Then we coded and counted  their sensemaking patterns. We found both Aaron and Chris displayed varying levels of assimilation, accommodation, toleration, and distantiation during their discussi ons with their others in the classroom. Specifically, Aaron exhibited 10 instances of assimilation, 13 instances of accommodation, 3 instances of toleration, and no instances of distantiation. In contrast, Chris displayed 3 instances of assimilation, 1 instance of accommodation, 16 instances of toleration, and 6 instances of distantiation.  This suggests that Aaron exhibited a higher degree of assimilation and accommodation, indicating he has been actively integrating new information into existing mental frameworks for understanding or modifying his current understanding for new solutio ns. However, Chris demonstrated more tendency toward toleration and distantiation, displaying less willingness to adjust existing beliefs and maintaining some distance from the new knowledge.

## Discussion

This is a work-in-progress study aiming to examine university instructors’ sensemaking process in PD. Our initial findings offer a glimpse into why instructors, despite being part of the same program, experienced different levels of learning when viewed through the lens of sensemaking. Due to space constraints, we can't detail the qualitative data linked to each observed pattern. However, our next steps involve enriching these cases by analyzi ng their participation in knowledge building and tracing the ir learning trajectory. Furthermore, we plan to bro aden our scope by including additional cases from the progra m, aiming for more comprehensive insights into the  possible association between teacher learning and sensemaking.
