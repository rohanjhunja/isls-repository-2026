# Promoting STEM Careers: Preliminary Analysis of the Young Scholars Program

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper examines the Young Scholars Program (YSP), a STEM enrichment initiative for middle and high school students in the U.S. that was funded by the National Science Foundation from 1988 to 1996. In particular, the analysis focuses on participation-level and long-term impacts of the YSP on the academic and professional trajectories of students. Guided by social cognitive career theory (SCCT), this paper applies epistemic network analysis (ENA) to examine the data collected from in-depth interviews with 8 YSP participants. It was found that YSP participation was linked to the development of motivation and self-efficacy and subsequent decisions to pursue STEM fields.

## Introduction

Maintaining an adequate workforce in the science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) fields has remained an issue of significant national interest in the U.S. To this end, numerous policies and prog rams have been implemented over the past decades to support the development of STEM-related skills among students and to encourage their pursuit of academic and career p aths in STEM-related sectors. Such initiatives are diverse in format, scope and size and can range from scholarsh ips and fellowships to school curricula and enrichm ent programs. This retrospective study examines the Young Scholars Program (YSP), a STEM enrichment initiative for middle and high school students in the U.S. tha t was funded by the National Science Foundation fro m 1988 to 1996. The YSP was intended to: (1) improve parti cipants’ knowledge of and exposure to STEM fields; (2) foster interest in STEM education and research; (3) increase awareness of academic for STEM careers; (4) gain familiarity with universities and research institut ions; and (5) enhance confidence in making career-r elated decisions (National Science Foundation, 1993). This paper explores the impacts of the YSP on the academic and professional trajectories of student participants. Guided by social cognitive career theory (SCCT), th is paper applies epistemic network analysis (ENA) to examine the data collected from interviews with 8 YSP participants. Social cognitive career theory (SCCT) Social cognitive career theory (SCCT) provides a framework for understanding the relationship and interactions among constructs that affect an individual’s interests, goals and choice behavior in their own career development (Lent, Brown, & Hackett, 1994). SCCT builds on the assumptions of Bandura’s (1986) social cognitive theory, which focused on the connections between variables associated with the person, their behavior and thei r environment (Lent, Brown, & Hackett, 2000). In addition to the personal-cognitive constructs of self-efficacy and outcome expectation, SCCT therefore incorporates personal attributes, such as predispositions and demographic information, as well as environmental factors and specific learning experiences (Lent et al., 2000). In this manner, SCCT emphasizes the role of self-efficacy and outcome expectation in mediating interest and goals, which lead to choice behavior. Also characterized within the SCCT is the notion of an iterative and dynamic feedback loop, whereby the outcomes of prior career-related action influence subsequent decisions (Lent et al., 1994). SCCT has been used to study career trajectories in STEM fiel ds, both in analyzing academic choices of students (Wang,

2013) and career decisions-making processes. It has  also been applied in examining the career paths of  groups

that are underrepresented in the STEM fields (Fouad & Santana, 2017).

## Methods

This paper provides a preliminary examination of th e impact of participation in the YSP on the academi c and professional trajectories. It analyzes data collected from semi-structured interviews of 8 female YSP participants. Convenience sampling was utilized, as comprehensive lists of participant names and contact information were not retained for many of the YSP projects carried out in the 80s and 90s. As presented in Table 1, the YSP participants interviewed for this paper consist of women from di verse demographic and educational backgrounds, who are engaged in a broad range of professions.

ENA is a tool in quantitative ethnography that utilizes stat istical and ICLS2022 Proceedings 1674 © ISLS visualization techniques to identify patterns in discourse (Shaffer, 2017). In particular, ENA models connections between codes in the data by quantifying their co-occurrences in the recent temporal context (Siebert-Evenstone et al., 2017). For this analysis, a unit of analysis (line of data) was defined as a single sentence contained within a turn of talk taken by the participant. A conversation was specified to be the set of lines that were related to the same topical question. A moving window of size 5 wa s used to model the co-occurrences of codes between  a given line and four preceding lines in the same conversation. ENA networks were created at two levels to examine the participation-level and long-term impacts, respectively.

## Results

Participation-level impact The first analysis focused on the participation-lev el impact of the YSP. Figure 1a presents the ENA mo del reflecting the discourse pattern of the responses r elated to the YSP. Strong connections are visible a mong the codes of STEM KNOWLEDGE, EXPOSURE and INTERPERSONAL SKILLS. This suggests that enhancing knowledge in STEM-related subjects while having opportunities to  make friends and gain new experiences was a signif icant feature of the YSP for the participants. As shown i n the strong connections between S TEM KNOWLEDGE  and MOTIVATION as well as S TEM KNOWLEDGE  and S ELF-EFFICACY, learning more about STEM topics was a key motivator for participants and allowed them to bolster their sense of confidence and achievement. The prominent association between I NTERPERSONAL SKILLS  and S ATISFACTION indicates that social relationships contributed significantly to their overall positive experience during the program. Long-term impact In the second analysis, attention was given to the long-term influence of the YSP experience, particularly on the career-related choices made by the participants. Figure 1b displays the discourse pattern of participants’ responses to questions focusing on their academic and professional pathways and the role that the YSP may have played in them. A strong relationship between TOWARD STEM and YSP RELATED can be seen, confirming that participation in the YSP had a positive impact for participants p ursuing STEM fields academically or professionally.  The prominent connections between CAREER OPPORTUNITIES and both TOWARD STEM and AWAY FROM STEM suggest that various opportunities that present themselves during the course of a life can be a double-edged s word for developing Stem workers. For some interviewees, educational and job opportunities allowed them to remain in a STEM-related field, while for others they led them away to other disciplines and sectors. It is interesting to note that PERSONAL DIFFICULTIES and CAREER CHALLENGES were found to have a much lower strength of connection to AWAY FROM STEM, indicating that they did not exert a high level of influence on the decisions of participants to move into other fields of study or work.

## Discussion

This paper examined the participation-level and long-term impacts of the YSP on the academic and professional trajectories of young participants. Based on an ENA  analysis of in-depth interviews of YSP participant s, it was found that a combination of STEM learning, exposure to a diverse set of activities and connections to like-minded peers provided an experience that not only allowed them to develop interest and confidence but was als o quite ICLS2022 Proceedings 1675 © ISLS enjoyable and memorable. From an SCCT perspective, these findings point to the self-reinforcing nature  of interest-driven learning processes. Given that the YSP projects were generally intended for high achie ving students, it is likely that the additional learning  experiences provided through the YSP often validat ed and expanded their interest in pursuing a particular ST EM area academically or professionally. Participant  #7 described this dynamic in the following way: And so being able to be among peers who also were academically engaged or at least engaged in learning new things, interested in learning how things work, just gaining knowledge for knowledge sake. That was the first time that that ever happened, and that gave me permission to really encourage that part of myself and not be ashamed of that and to fully embrace pushing myself to learn as much as I could and be okay with that. The analysis of the long-term impact found that participation in the YSP was influential in some of the decisions made by interviewees toward STEM careers. However, it was also observed that educational and professio nal opportunities may not only help individuals to pursue STEM-related fields but also steer them away fro m these disciplines. An example of this was articulated by Participant #3: And I think…having that initial exposure to the col lege campus [through the YSP] made me more confident to apply for the apprenticeship prog ram that I got into the next summer. So I felt pretty confident coming out of high school tha t I was going to be an engineer. And so I applied to a bunch of different schools. I thought and I was probably going to … [a] School of Mines here… and I got a scholarship and I thought that's what I was going to do. But then I got a music scholarship to [another university], and it was a better scholarship. And my mom was really concerned about debt and money, things like that. So that's the school that I ended up going to. And I started off in the honors program t here, and the focus there was not so much science and math.

While SCCT stresses the role of internal factors su ch as self-efficacy and interest in career-related choices, this finding points to the importance of also accounting  for external influences that may significantly imp act the academic and professional trajectories of individuals.
