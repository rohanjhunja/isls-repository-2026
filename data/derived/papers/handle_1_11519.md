# Undergraduate Formation and Digital Technologies: The Case of the Philosophy Subject

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines the urgent need and the exciting possibilities of integrating digital technologies into the undergraduate formation of philosophy teachers. Its objective is an integration that does not instrumentalize technology. It proposes using the Design-Based Research in three cycles: developing training solutions that value technologies as mediators of critical thinking, promoting pedagogical innovation, and a critical philosophical practice that impacts teaching and reflection on the role of technologies in society.

## Introduction

The discussion about integrating digital technologi es is familiar in Brazil's education field. However, the topic has gained greater expression with the publication of the National Common Core Curriculum (BNCC) in 2017 (Brasil, 2018) and its complement focused on Computing in 2022 (Brasil, 2022). These documents guide a series of competencies focused on digital technologies, which are distributed along three axes: Computational Thinking, Digital World, and Digital Culture, which aim to tr ain students to, respectively, think with digital r esources, interact with their interfaces, and participate in the social arrangement that digitalization helps to  produce. With this, Brazil indicates an intention to promote training for an i ntegrated approach to digital technologies, working from a reflective process on the use of technology,  not reducing the interaction of subjects with it t o a merely instrumental approach.

With the BNCC in mind, the Common National Basis fo r the Undergraduate formation of  Basic Education Teachers (BNC -Formação) was also published (Brazil, 2019), which includes a set of ten general professional competencies, as well as a set of 12 specific competencies divided into three dimensions (professional knowledge, professional practice, and professional engagement), which are broken down into the various  skills required for teaching. Of these competences, only two mention digital technologies, and of the skills, only six are related to them, most of which take an instrumental approach. Therefore, we see that this set of documents guides deep technological integration, although it does no t emphasize how this process should take place in t eacher training since there is an instrumental tendency in training. In the contemporary technological context, marked b y cyberculture as a new universal  (Lévy, 2000), there is a pressing need for teaching and learning that explicitly address this situation. Critical and reflective thinking is fundamental for living in digital envir onments, as unthinking actions and swarming behavior can potentially damage the entire political and social structure (Han, 2022). Therefore, there is a need to instill a philosophical attitude for the new digital times, w hich allows subjects to critically reflect on their  ways of being and acting. This understanding of philosophy, as the creation of concepts, starts precisely from problematization to creation (Deleuze & Guattari, 2010), rescuing this critical attitude towards the reali ty of the subject's surroundings. As a result, the complete set of skills is re-signified, impacting the subject's relationship wit h the world.

Based on this situation, this research aims to inve stigate the undergraduate formation of  philosophy teachers at a Brazilian university. The research question guiding this project is: how candigital technologies be integrated into the training of philosophy teachers, so that they are able to integrate digital technologies into their teaching processes, favoring learning through digit al languages? To answer this question, it considers teacher training from a broad perspective, not just technical or didactic aspects, but understanding it, with Nóvoa (1992), from three dimensions: the production of the teache r's life, the production of the teaching profession  and the production of the school. This training is based on  socialization and professional configuration (Nóvo a, 2019), which allows technological integration to be thought of from a broad, non-instrumental perspective. The choice to consider the relationship between digital technologies in this professional training process is based on the understanding that these technologi es can play a profound role in establishing thought  processes in subjects. Thus, more than just tools to be used in everyday pedagogical practice, digital resources can serve as intelligence technologies, guiding how thinking is organized and systematized. Historically, various technologies of intelligence have been employed, such as orality  and textuality, resulting in profound impacts on t he tradition of thought (Lévy, 1995). With the emergence and spr ead of hyper textuality  and artificial intelligence, a new horizon is opening up for cognitive processes, virt ualizing, and decentralizing knowledge, making it accessible everywhere and simultaneously. However, we see that  in the area of Humanity (where Philosophy is inclu ded), an instrumental perspective persists (Freitas, 2019; Marfim & Pesce, 2019; Oliveira, 2020; Lasakoswitsck, 2021; Alves, 2022), which reflects an approach to pedagogical processes based on traditional logic, based on orality and textuality. The digital technologies used in this c ontext serve to replace the text, without a clear c hange in the purpose of their use. In this way, there is no reflection on the use of technology, which can lead to practices guided by a purely technical, decontextualized reason that  objectifies the subject's relationship with knowle dge. Therefore, it is necessary to reframe this use, whi ch involves a change in the teacher's mentality, wh o must integrate these emerging thinking technologies into  their own thinking, encompassing professional perf ormance as a whole.

Because of this scope of use, we have chosen to app roach the issue from the undergraduate formation stage, when there is the first reflective process on the school experience (Tardif, 2014). As this is a question about teachers' thinking, which is part of an ongoing tra ining process, we believe that training in technolo gies that encourages these ways of thinking should begin in initial training. Interventions can help bridge the gap between the knowledge built up in introductory courses and the knowledge built up in practice (Nóvoa, 2019). We believe that teachers who have their undergraduate formation in different technological languages will be able to initiate processes of innovation more easily  in their professional practice, as well as take mor e significant advantage of the continuing training offered to them.

## Methodology

Design-Based Research (DBR), based on Hoadley and Campos (2022), will be used as the methodological approach for this research. Therefore, the understanding of DBR that will be assumed will operate in four stages: grounding, conjecture, iteration and reflection, wh ich are not absolute and linear, being revisited th roughout the process. As a result, design principles will be drawn up to enable training projects that integrate a systemic vision of the use of digital technologies in the teaching of Philosophy, favoring the development of pedagogical practices that take into account the contemporary virtual context and make it possible to show the importance of Philosophy as an ethical basis for critically reflecting on th e impact of digital technologies on society. The in itial proposal is for three cycles:

(1) Exploratory cycle: stage in which the context i n which the design and implementation process will

take place will be known. This cycle will include a cartographic analysis based on Passos & Barros (2015), who, inspired by Deleuze & Guattari (2011), think of the  act of research as mapping processes of creation a nd transformation, rather than representing fixed stru ctures. These maps are open and can be produced in the most diverse languages (texts and images), expressing tensions of the most varied orders that manifest themselves in the researched event, such as politics, aesthetics,  and ethics. The researcher is implicated in the ca rtographic approach since maps can express but hide relationships. The cartographic approach is chosen because the research locus is unknown to the researcher, implying the inadequacy of any previous approach. Mapping gives more space for the context to speak for itself, exposing the c rossings that make up the meaning given to the peda gogical processes in the institution. At the end of the cycle, maps of regulatory documents, lesson and teaching plans, onsite observations, and interviews with teachers and students will be produced.

(2) and (3) Implementation cycles: Based on the results produced, designing the training proposal will

begin. This will occur through meetings with univer sity philosophy professors, teachers who work in ba sic education, and students on the philosophy degree co urse. Participants will be recruited by open invitation, counting on their adherence and interest in the pro ject proposal. In the first meetings, the researcher will present a first set of theoretical conjectures, which will be confronted with the experience of each co-creator and then refined and implemented into an artifact. The defin ition of the artifact, which will be a training pro posal, will be linked to the needs mapped out in the first cycle, and can  take a variety of forms, from the design of a disc ipline to cross-cutting interventions in the approach to digital technologies, for example. After the design, there wi ll be two stages of implementation in real contexts, gathering data from the researcher's notes, the class teacher's notes, questionnaires and student interviews. As an analysis tool, a case study will be conducted at the end of each cycle (Yin, 2015), which will organize the information in order to understand the particularities of the event, offering a detailed and contextualized view. A qualitative analysis will be conducted, combining the various data sources to capture the experience of integrating digital techn ologies into philosophy teacher training. The approach will include data triangulation and interpretation in th e light of the  theoretical framework, allowing us to identify patterns and challenges in the subjects' relationsh ip with technologies. This strategy will enable an in-depth and contextualized understanding, respecting the complexity of the phenomenon under investigation. It is hoped that this formative approach will enabl e future philosophy teachers to explore digital technologies as mediators of thought and critical reflection, rather than as mere tools.
