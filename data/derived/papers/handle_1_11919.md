# Understanding Teacher’s Practicum and Implication of Knowledge Building Principles: From Theory to Lesson Design

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines how 55 Taiwanese teachers understood and applied knowledge-building principles in their lesson design after a teacher training program. Using qualitative and quantitative methods, including theme-based open-coding and paired t-tests, the study found that the teachers' understanding of KBPs improved significantly, enabling them to effectively implement KB theory in their lesson design.

## Background

Lesson design is becoming increasingly complex in t he AI-driven era. Even experienced teachers must reassess their expertise and embrace new design paradigms. With AI tools like ChatGPT now capable of delivering much of the traditional knowledge, future lessons must f ocus on meaningful content that is directly connect ed to students' lives. This shift calls for teachers to present ideas, engage in brainstorming, and develop well-structured lesson plans. Teachers must learn to generate, appl y, and interact wi th ideas to inspire innovative teaching and foster deeper learning. Likewise, students must lea rn to generate ideas and apply knowledge, rather th an merely absorbing information. The Knowledge Building (KB) theory (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2006) emphasizes three core elements: "agency," "ideas," and "community" (Nasir & Lee, 2014; Lin et.al,2011). In Taiwan, while teachers are beginning to value ideas within the 108-curriculum reform, fully integrating this philosophy into practice remains challenging. Many classrooms still overlook "ideas" as a central focu s, despite the growing need for innovative thinking to address societal challenges.  This study centers on four KB principles: (1) real  ideas, authentic problems, (2) idea diversity, (3) idea improvement, and (4) rise-above. The research questions focus on:

(1) How do participants deepen their understanding of KB principles related to ideas? (2) Did participants integrate

their understanding of KB theory into their lesson design process? (3) How did participants apply the KB principles in designing lessons over time?

## Context and method

This study involved 55 teachers in the “Internation al Knowledge Building Teacher Training Programme” (IKBTTP) to improve their professional development in idea-centered education through knowledge building pedagogy. Prior to training, a 7-point Likert-scale and open-ended questionnaire assessed participants' understanding of KBPs. The 10-week program (2 hours/week) introduced KBPs through  theory and hands-on practice. Participants used the “Knowledge Forum (K F)” platform to interact with peers, materialize id eas, and refine lesson designs. After training, a post-test using the same instruments was conducted, and participants selfassessed their KBP application, which was also evaluated by the researcher. Quantitative data was analyzed with paired t-tests, and qualitative data was analyzed using open coding. Rating criteria for evaluating participants’ understanding of KB principles can be found in Seet, et.al (2024). The 1-7 evaluation scores, for lesson design outcomes focusing on the connection to KBPs, was presented as follows: 1:  The lesson plan is textbook-based (such as following the sample provided by textbook editors), with little reallife relevance, offering only a single perspective and no opportunities for idea improvement or higher-level thinking.

2:  Problems have more knowledge-based material but minimal real-life connection, with few perspectives provided. Limited improvement opportunities are pre sent, and there’s little support for going beyond c urrent knowledge 3:  Include some real-life relevance activities and diversity of perspectives, but exploration remains at the surface level. There are basic opportunities for learners t o improve ideas, with limited mechanisms for higher-level synthesis.

4:  Learning materials have good connections to real li fe, with diverse perspectives enriching the learnin g. Structured opportunities exist for idea improvement, and mechanisms encourage moving beyond simple solutions.

5:  Learning contents strongly connected to real-life contexts, actively promoting diverse perspectives. The lesson emphasizes continuous idea improvement and supports  students in integrating higher-level knowledge or ability.

6:  In-depth real-life exploration, fostering active diversity in per spectives and collaboration. Idea improvement is ongoing and systematic, with guidance for tackling complex problems creatively. 7:  Deeply integrates real-world challenges (such as solving SDG issues), enco uraging creative synthesis of diverse ideas. Central focus on continuous improvement and guiding students to transcend knowledge through interdisciplinary innovation.

## Results

To answer RQ1, Table 1 indicates that participants significantly improved their understanding of ideas-related knowledge-building principles after the training program. In terms of qualitative questionnaires that require participants to explain the theory of ideas related  KBPs, they also demonstrated deeper understanding by means of their own words and real-life experience as compared to the pre-test. Overall, both qualitative and quantitative data showed that participants significantly gained knowledge and could apply the theory to acti on. For RQ2, to assess whether participants effectively integrated their theoretical understanding of KBPs into the le sson design process, the self-reported ratings indicated a high level of alignmen t with  ideas-related KBPs. Participants explicitly identified specific elements of their ac tivities that directly corresponded to these princi ples (M = 5.32, SD = 0.32). Similarly, the researcher’s evaluation revealed that participants seamlessly incorporated the practical application of KBPs into their lesson plans (M = 4.32, SD = 0.07). Further comparative analysis of the self-ratings and researcher evaluations showed a statistically s ignificant difference (t = 4.46, p <.001); however,  both reinforced the concl usion that ideas-related KBPs were present and effectively integrate d within the lesson designs.

## Conclusion and discussion

Overall, regarding idea-related knowledge-building principles (KBPs), the participants gained  deeper understanding of the meaning of KB theory, enabling them to put KB theory into practical application. In terms of practicum, teachers fundamentally incorporate th e principle of "real ideas, authentic problems" to design and develop activities. Applying KBPs is a gradual proc ess that requires sustained interaction and contrib ution. This study represents the beginning of valuing ideas in classroom practices within Taiwan context, as teach ers also seek to design lessons (activities) that strongly emphasize idea application for their target learners. Future studies will focus more on the process of lesson design and  development, e.g., how exactly does the idea development help shape a lesson with consideration given to using AI as an assistant to support and triangulate data.
