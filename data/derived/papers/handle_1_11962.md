# Exploring Shifts in Adolescent Identity Development in a Global, Virtual, STEM-focused Collaborative Learning Setting

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Underpinned by identity-based motivation, this study examines identity development among adolescents after one year of participation in a global, virtual, STEM-focused project-based learning community. Pre-and post-assessment interviews with 29 students from the US, Brazil, Ghana, Kenya, and Mexico reveal shifts in constructs such as encountering challenges, deficiency awareness, and collaborative openness. Visualizations using epistemic network analysis (ENA) highlight differences between students in the US and outside the US. Findings indicate that US students became more open to sharing their deficiencies with math and science, and both students in and outside of the US were able to address challenges in their experiences by demonstrating more consideration in interacting with others.
