# Lowering Barriers for Designing AI-Embodied Robots for Learning

**Conference:** ISLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper presents RoboHearts AI, a web-based platform that enables educators, students, and researchers to easily design and deploy large-language-model (LLM)–powered characters on embodied robots such as Pepper, NAO, and LEGO-based extensions. By combining LLM-driven conversational capabilities with robots’ multimodal features, including gesture, animated movement, gaze, and visual display, RoboHearts AI supports naturalistic, adaptive, and contextualized interactions that extend beyond traditional screen-based chatbots. The paper introduces practical applications across educational and non-educational settings, and proposes two pedagogical scenarios: learning with AI, where robots act as tutors or learning companions, and learning about AI, where students gain AI literacy and deepen their understanding of AI by designing and iteratively refining robot characters. This hands-on demonstration guides participants in character creation and multimodal behavior design. This work lowers barriers to embodied AI adoption and highlights new opportunities for research, instructional innovation, and human-centered learning with AI-embodied robots.

The rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI), especially generative AI, has made the integration of AI into a variety of applications significantly easier than before. AI-embedded robots with conversational capabilities can provide learning opportunities for learners through communication whenever necessary, as well as intelligent pathways to broader educational and societal needs. These systems combine the multimodal and social presence of robots with the conversational capabilities powered with AI, creating new opportunities for personalized, engaging, and contextualized learning experiences in classrooms and informal settings (Cardona et al., 2023; Følstad et al., 2021; Łukasik & Gut, 2025; Martin, 2025). Recent advances in natural language processing and large language models (LLMs), which power today’s conversational agents, have enabled AI chatbots to engage in increasingly natural, contextualized, multiturn dialogue. These capabilities have accelerated their adoption across diverse domains, including education, health, and customer service. In education specifically, a growing number of educators recognize that conversational AI can provide tutoring, on-demand and personalized feedback, and opportunities for learner autonomy at scale. These emerging possibilities, in turn, underscore the need to investigate responsible, humancentered approaches for integrating AI into teaching and learning ecosystems (Cardona et al., 2023; Følstad et al., 2021; Łukasik & Gut, 2025; Martin, 2025).

Embedding AI-driven conversational capabilities into physical robots has transformed them from purely task-oriented tools into interactive social partners capable of listening, responding, and adapting to learners’ needs. Unlike chatbots on computers or mobile devices, AI-enabled robots candraw on embodiment, gesture, gaze, and other nonverbal behaviors to enhance social presence and rapport, which can shape learners’ affective responses and perceptions of the interaction. These embodied interactions have the potential to reduce learner anxiety, increase motivation, and sustain engagement, particularly in domains such as language learning, communication skills, and other activities where relational interactions play a critical role (Park & Neumann, 2024). AI-embodied robots can act as interfaces to curriculum-aligned content, analytics dashboards, and adaptive learning services, creating collaborative learning opportunities for teachers and students. Such platforms can enable individualized support, including just-in-time explanations and formative feedback, while also giving educators new ways to orchestrate classroom activities and monitor student progress (Cardona et al., 2023; Martin, 2025).

Chatbots in education have evolved from simple rule-based systems to advanced conversational agents capable of supporting complex teaching and learning processes across K-12, higher education, and lifelong learning contexts. The literature suggests potential benefits for learning support, motivation, and scalability; however, it also highlights methodological, ethical, and pedagogical challenges that remain as a concern (Aguilar, 2024; Eteng-Uket & Ezeoguine, 2025; Okonkwo & Ade-Ibijola, 2021; Thi Thuy Mai et al., 2024; Wollny et al., 2021). Research generally reports positive or mixed effects of chatbots on learning outcomes, engagement, and motivation, though effect sizes and robustness vary. Studies show that AI chatbots can improve comprehension, retention, and application of knowledge, while highlighting the need for grounded pedagogical principles to support learning (Aguilar, 2024; Eteng-Uket & Ezeoguine, 2025; Körner et al., 2025, 2025; Wollny et al., 2021). T he literature indicates that chatbots in educational settings commonly assume roles such as tutor, teaching assistant, coach, or peer. Pedagogical conversational agents, in particular, are often designed to deliver step-bystep instructions, formative feedback, and/or socio-emotional support. Research highlights the importance of empathetic, adaptive, and feedback-rich interactions, such as metacognitive prompts or affective feedback, in enhancing learner retention, metacognitive sensitivity, and overall engagement in chatbot-based learning environments (Aguilar, 2024; Körner et al., 2025; Wollny et al., 2021; Yin et al., 2025). However, reviews also highlight several critical challenges, including the need to align chatbot evaluation with pedagogical objectives, design for mentoring and sustained use, ensure data privacy and transparency, and mitigate risks related to bias and misinformation. Research on LLM-based tools, such as ChatGPT, identifies notable strengths, including personalization, immediate feedback, and support for idea generation, while also documenting significant limitations. These include generating inaccurate information, fabricated citations, hallucinated content, and persistent uncertainty regarding their impact on learners’ motivation, overreliance, self-regulation, and long-term learning behaviors (Eteng-Uket & Ezeoguine, 2025; Thi Thuy Mai et al., 2024; Wollny et al., 2021). The literature underscores the ongoing need for research on the integration of chatbots in both education and broader societal contexts. At the same time, scholarship on AI-embodied partner or companion robots remains in its early stages, with many questions still unexplored. In response to these gaps, this paper introduces an AIpowered tool that enables educators, students, and other users to easily create AI-embodied robots tailored to support diverse learning goals and practical needs. This work aims to lower barriers to adoption while expanding opportunities for human-centered, embodied AI applications in educational settings. RoboHearts AI – designing robot personality for AI-embodied robots RoboHearts AI is an online system initially designed for Pepper and NAO humanoid robots (see Figure 1), originally developed by Aldebaran, providing users with an accessible interface for creating and deploying LLMdriven characters within embodied robotic platforms. Since the system is a web-based interface, there is no installation requirement, which could make it difficult to implement in school environments where the school IT department’s permission is required to install additional applications. AI-embodied robots created with RoboHearts AI are multimodal, extending far beyond the capabilities of traditional chatbots by integrating visual displays with images and/or text, animated movements, and expressive facial behaviors. Each character can speak a specific language or switch between multiple languages. RoboHearts AI is currently in development to expand its capabilities to other robotic platforms.

Figure 1 From the left, Pepper Robot, Nao Robot, and LEGO extension with Android Phone Functionality – How it works With RoboHearts AI, a multilingual conversational platform equipped with a user-friendly Character Editor, users can create and refine multiple AI characters (see Figure 2). The Character Editor enables users to define each character through customizable instructions that specify personality, role, and scenario. Within the interface, users can adjust a range of parameters, including prompts, which can be typed and edited in the textbox on the platform, voice settings, and capabilities, such as animated movements, movements, and display behaviors. Once developed, characters can be deployed to robots by installing the corresponding application on each device. RoboHearts AI can orchestrate those multimodal robot actions, including animated movements and gestures (wave, point, dance, and express emotions), tablet display (only with Pepper, show images, text, floor plans, or conversation prompts), and interactive suggestions (offer touch-ready responses on Pepper’s tablet to h elp conversations flow). The RoboHearts AI decides when to use these functions based on context and the character’s instructions.

Using the Character Editor, users can also specify the animated movements the robot will perform. These movements can be defined through special commands or by embedding emojis within the text editor of the instruction window, where users create the prompt that defines a robot’s behavior (see Figure 3). This allows users to seamlessly integrate expressive, context-appropriate animated movements into the robot’s conversational responses.

Figure 2 RobotHearts AI Characters Figure 3 RobotHearts AI Characters Instruction Window for Prompting Practical applications RoboHearts AI offers a wide range of applications across educational and non-educational contexts. Current use cases demonstrate its versatility. It has been used in human-robot interaction research, deployed as a conversational agent for customer support in settings such as museums and retail environments, and integrated into educational contexts, including vocational training with nursing students (see Figure 4). These examples illustrate the system’s capacity to support research, enhance user engagement, and facilitate skill development across diverse domains.

In educational contexts, we propose two primary scenarios: learning with AI and learning about AI. In the learning with AI scenario, teachers candesign AI characters that align with a lesson’s learning goals. AIembodied robots can serve as learning companions for small groups in the classroom or as individualized tutors outside of class, providing rich learning experiences supported by on-demand responses and adaptive interactions, promoting sustained engagement. In the learning about AI scenario, students become the creators of robot characters using the RoboHeart AI platform. Students may already be familiar with generative AI tools, such as ChatGPT, on their personal devices. Although these tools are interactive, traditional generative AI chatbots do not inherently support students in learning about AI, as they offer limited visibility into how AI systems work, make decisions, or process information. Becoming a creator of robot characters provides students with valuable learning opportunities, allowing them to experiment with prompting and immediately observe the results through their interactions with AI-embodied robots. They may need to refine their prompts or adjust the robot’s animated movements to make the human-robot interaction feel more natural. This iterative process not only deepens their u nderstanding of AI but also enriches their overall learning experience. Through the process of creating robot characters, students will learn 1) how prompting contributes to desired outcomes; 2) how LLMs control not only speech but also physical behaviors, including robot actions and movements; and 3) how to explore and test different use cases by deploying their robot characters with users and iteratively refining their designs. To explore student learning experience, the pre-/post-survey or interviews, and recording of student dialogue during the development and test process will shed light on how students deepen their understanding and curiosity towards AI.

Figure 4 AI-Embodied Robots in a Museum (left) and nursing training session (right) Demonstration-Test and explore the potential of AI-embodied robots This demonstration will offer a hands-on exploration of the RoboHearts AI platform. During the demonstration, participants will explore how prompting influences the AI-embodied robot’s outcome-speech and physical behaviors by co-designing robot characters and deploying it in an AI-embodied robot. First, we will demonstrate how RoboHearts AI works by showcasing a variety of pre-created characters aligned with the use cases described in the previous section, helping participants understand the platform’s key functionalities. This demonstration will include advanced features such as speech customization, animated movements and gestures, and tablet display options (for Pepper robots only). Next, we will engage participants in a co-design activity, collaboratively creating a robot character using their input to illustrate how the platform’s functionalities can be edited, customized, and deployed on the robot in real time. In addition, we will demonstrate the prescribed conversation option using QiChat, the scripting language for creating structured, interactive dialogues on NAOqi-enabled robots, such as NAO and Pepper. This feature allows users to design controlled and purpose-specific conversational flows, ensuring that the robot stays within predefined dialogue boundaries while still enabling meaningful interactions.

We will also introduce additional robot options, such as a LEGO Spike robot paired with an Android phone to integrate a RoboHearts AI character. This alternative is especially important for broadening access, as many classrooms and schools already have LEGO Spike kits and can adopt RoboHearts AI without purchasing new hardware.

Finally, we will invite participants to engage in discussions about potential use cases and learning activities beyond those we have identified, as well as explore possible research agendas that can advance our understanding of how AI-embodied robots can be integrated into teaching and learning environments and enhance student learning.

The rapid development of AI, particularly generative AI powered by LLMs, has made it possible for platforms, like RoboHearts AI, to support robots that interact and communicate more naturally with human users while leveraging the rich multimodal functionalities these robots already possess. RoboHearts AI broadens access to such powerful learning tools by offering affordable, easy-to-use features designed for non-technical users, including educators and students. This significantly expands opportunities for hands-on, experiential learning in both formal and informal educational settings. As AI-embodied robots become increasingly feasible and accessible, we can expect to see broader adoption in classrooms in the near future. This demonstration provides an important opportunity to experience and explore effective use cases, identify ways to enhance students’ learning experiences, and ensure that AI-embodied robots have a positive impact on student learning, daily life, and society more broadly.
