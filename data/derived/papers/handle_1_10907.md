# Teacher’s Understanding and Attitude about “Ideas” Related Principles in a Knowledge Building Pedagogy Training Course

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The study explores teachers’ understanding of four knowledge-building principles. The four principles include (1) Real ideas, authentic problems, (2) Improvable ideas, (3) Idea diversity, and (4) Rise above. Data were gathered from 55 teachers. An open coding process was conducted, paired-t-test and ANOVA were performed. Results revealed gaps between teachers’ self-rated understanding and an expert’s assessment of the participants’ understanding of these four principles. Some suggestions were proposed for practical implications.

## Background

In the 21st century, an important educational goal is to help address global SDGs (Oberman & Sainz, 20 21). To this end, education needs to help students nurture innovative thinking. Thus, it is  crucial to inspire students to work creatively with ideas via teamwork to foster creative thinking and knowledge building (KB) as an innovative pedagogy (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2006), which provides 12 principles about agency, idea, and community, suits this educational purpose. In Taiwan, teachers mainly teach by following a set curriculum, with student’s creativity being limited. The new education policy, called the 108 curriculum guidelines education policy, aims to  foster ideas and develop capacity among students. This intention is appreciated but most teachers need help transitioning from the traditional lecture-based teaching model.

To enable a change in teachers from a firm teacher-centered teaching belief to teaching in a more flexible approach, we conducted a professional development training course using principle-based guidance, to gather some insights from pre-service teachers. The main research questions were as follows: (1) To what extent do teachers und erstand the four idea-focused KB principles based on either self-assessment or expert  assessment? (2) What do they think of the significance and fe asibility of these four principles? (3) Are there any differences among the 4 KB principles ? (4) Do teachers with different teaching experiences see these 4 KB principles differently?

## Context and method

Participants in t his exploratory study included 55 teachers. They participated in a teacher professional development (PD) training course to help these teachers develop an idea-centered mindset for knowledge-building (KB) pedagogy. Before the start of the training course, an open-ended survey was employed. Using a 7-point Likert scale (from low to high), the survey asked the participants (1) the extent of their understanding of the four KB principles is mainly related to ideas as conceptual artifacts for sustained knowledge advancement; (2) to what extent do they perceive the four principles necessary for today’s education ?; and (3) to what extent do they perceive the four principles as feasible for today’s education? In addition, the participants were also asked to write down their qualitative comments  regarding their level of understanding of the four KB principles. The four KB principles include “Principle 1-Real idea, authentic problems ”; “Principle 2-Improvable Ideas”; “Principle 3Idea Diversity”; and “Principle 4-Rise Above”. A coding rubric emerged from the collected data (see Table 1), and an external reviewer was invited to rate the participants’ responses, with the internal consistency reliability computed as Cronbach alpha=0.70. All data was analyzed by using SPSS to perform a paired t-test and ANOVA. ## Result

Table 2 shows that to answer RQ1, it was found that  the teachers perceived their understanding of the four KBP as around medium (M=3.86), but the expert rated their understanding as low (M=2.00). For RQ2, they perceived the four KBPs as important (M=5.78) but quite difficult for actual implementation in an authentic learning context (M=2.45). To answer RQ3, comparisons among the four KB principles were conducted, and it was found that relatively the participants felt difficulty understanding the meaning of the “P4-Rise Above” principle (t=8.309, p <.000), and it was also considered as very challenging in terms of its feasibility. As for the “P3-Idea Diversity” principle (t=11.180, p <.000), participants perceived it as easy to  understand, but a discrepancy between the participants’ rating and the researcher’s rating was also identified. Lastly, to answer RQ4, it was found that there is marginal significan ce in terms of understanding these principles between novice teachers (0-2years), and experienced teachers (more than 2 years), and between those who only know about these principles and those who have experience of practicing KB in the classroom.

## Conclusion and discussion

In today’s era, both novice and expert teachers agreed  that working with “ideas” represents an essential skill in learning. The four KBPs discussed in this study seem to serve as  a suitable approach. However, it was found in this study that the participants only had a medium understanding of the four KBPs. Nevertheless, the result shows that “idea diversity” has the highest feasibility. This could be because encouraging students to generate diverse ideas seems than other KBPs, such as “idea improvement.” Many teacher s think that improving ideas means revising or overthrowing existing theories and concepts. As such, these teachers do not believe that young students cando so. Moreover, the “real idea, authentic problem” principle, has lower feasibility, and the reason could be that most teachers tend to focus on textbook knowledge in teaching, rather than on authentic problems in real life. In Taiwan, students usually do not have time left after studying in cram schools or due to a full schedule in school. Their curiosity and mental effort are already exhausted. Overall, KBPs represent design ideas; thus, understanding them may not be easy for newcomers. So, it is suggested that future KBP training course s have more guiding activities on difficult KBPs such as “ rise-above.” More time is alsoneeded for both teacher s and students to gradually change their deep-rooted beliefs or mindsets in traditional teaching.
