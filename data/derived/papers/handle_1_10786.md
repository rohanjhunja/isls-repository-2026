# A Review of AI-Enhanced Personalized Learning Systems: Implications for the Learning Sciences

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This research focuses on recent studies of AI-Enhanced Personalized Learning, organized into three main sections: understanding key aspects, investigating practical methodologies, and elucidating motivations for AI integration into personalized learning to provide insights for future research in learning science. The methodology involves a rapid literature review, emphasizing eligibility criteria and a precise study selection process. The conclusion underscores the importance of seamlessly integrating AI analytics with human-centric approaches in personalized learning, enriching data, and training algorithms for efficiency, alongside emphasizing the role of human oversight.

## Introduction

In the rapidly changing world, education will be we ll-equipped to leverage the latest initiatives in tech nological support, notably artificial intelligence (AI). This research will delve into recent studies to explore the advantages of AI-supported personalized learning in optimizing the overall learning e xperience, organized into three major sections. First, the focus will be on understanding the key aspects that AI has facilitated in realizing personalized learning. Second, the study will also aim to comp rehend the tools and methodologies that can be prac ticed to address existing challenges and advance AI-supported personalized learning. Lastly, the resear ch will elucidate the motivations propelling the integration of AI into the realm of personalized learning. Purpose The primary purpose of this review is to critically evaluate and integrate established theories and methods in AIsupported personalized learning research. In genera l, we aim to assist learning scientists in being we ll-prepared and focused on identifying existing obstacles, dete rmining where and in what direction subsequent barr iers may arise, and devising strategies to create systems wi th humanistic values.  The following research question guided our study:

1. What directions is AI in education taking to inform personalized learning experiences?

## Methods

Eligibility criteria aand study selection process To ensure relevance and quality, we prioritized rec ent and highly-rated articles within the post-secondary education sector, spanning diverse disciplines and originating from international settings. Eligible l iterature needed to be at most eight years old. Searches were made on the Education Resources Information Center (ERIC) database to generate results with connections to education. These guidelines were applied to support a process of abstract screening. At the same time, the final results were synthesized predominantly as a narrative review (Green et al., 2006) to present an overview of available findings in a field. We utilized ERIC to find papers and screen related papers, employing a systematic approach that ultimately narrowed the selection to approximately 30 papers. This method ensured a focused and comprehensive review of the most relevant literature in alignment  with our research objectives. During the initial s creening, search results were imported on a spreadsheet to sc reen titles and abstracts independently. A full-text article review was performed during level 2 screening. We e nsure inter-reliability between  reviewers during level 1 screening; we established a list of key terms (including synonyms and derivatives). Further terms were negotiated as we discovered relevant and deserving key terms. In this process, two raters independently reviewed titles and abstracts, followed by conflict resolution and negotia tion in which both raters were involved. A single r eviewer reviewed and excluded the studies during the full-text review unless a reviewer needed a second opinion. Data items and extraction process The authors carefully looked at all the papers with  specific keywords to answer the main questions in our paper. All the keywords were associated with our study foc us, including "Personalized learning pathways," "Technology-supported evidence," "Tailored c ontent for students and instructors," "Adaptive lea rning environments," "Facilitating Goal Setting," "Contex tual Learning," "Recommender Module," "well-designed long-term curriculum," and "AI-enabled personalized education”. Results of full-text extraction will be available in a later report.

## Results and discussion

Search results resulted in 52 studies from the ERIC database. After removing six duplicate studies, the remaining 46 went through title and abstract review. After th e first screening, 30 studies were eligible for full-text review. Based on the content, eligible studies were coded using the questions as follows: Q.1 What aspects of personalized learning are important to consider in developing AI-supported learning analytics?

In recent research, the focus on AI in personalized learning has emphasized automation in assessment, feedback, and quiz generation (vander Vorst & Jelicic, 2019;  Pataranutaporn et al., 2021; Maghsudi et al., 2021; Murtaza et al., 2022; Fake, 2023). These  initiatives significantly impact real-time processes, including generation, assessment, scoring, and feedback provision based on evolving data. Kokku et al. (2018) highlight the elimination of predefined assessments, advocating for dynamical ly adjusting questions based on learners' responses. Conversely, Maghsudi et al. (2021) underscore the l imitations of AI analytics systems in interpretabil ity and predictive power, particularly regarding meaningful  feedback. Other studies stress the importance of c onstant nuanced feedback and incorporating student reflections to understand individual actions (Hsia et al., 2023). Research (Garrido & Onaindia, 2013; Kokku et al., 2018; Rad et al., 2018; vander Vorst & Jelicic, 2019; Magomadov, 2020; Maghsudi et al., 2021; Pataranutap orn et al., 2021; Chaipidech et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023) underscores that AI significantly supports teaching, learning, and social aspects. Regarding learning support, AI can compel and generate learning content, define and recommend diverse learning materials, suggest lea rning styles, and accommodate all learning modalities.

In teaching support, AI excels as a digital teachin g assistant for routine tasks, supported by knowled ge tracing capabilities (Murtaza et al., 2022). The auto-generation of visual and digital assets enhances effective knowledge acquisition (Kokku et al., 2018; Rad et al., 2018; Pataranutaporn et al., 2021). Nevertheless, human e ducators remain indispensable for guidance, relationship-building, creating high-quality learning materials, and fostering creativity (Kokku et al., 2018; Magomadov, 2020; Pr atama et al., 2023). Research shows that natural language programming tools, such as ChatGPT, excel in handli ng challenging tasks (Adiguzel et al., 2023), and A I-based personalized suggestions effectively detect and address learning errors (Liu & Quan, 2022). One key debate in personalized learning centers on its social aspect, including learning communities, networks, and learner motivations (Maghsudi et al., 2021). Mo tivating learners poses a challenge for AI due to i ts complex nature (Maghsudi et al., 2021).  Strategies suggested include using AI-generated characters as virtual instructors for motivation (Pataranutaporn et al., 2021; Al-Badi & Khan, 2022), leveraging AI for personalized feedback and support (Chang & LU, 2019; Somasundaram et al., 202 0), and adopting a user-centred approach, using digital twins as sources (Furini et al., 2022), learner-machine interaction (Ogata et al., 2023) to motivate students. Q.2 What methodologies are used to train and model AI-supported personalized learning systems?

In the era of AI, the crucial role of data and data analysis cannot be overstated — it is integral to training machines, feeding algorithms, and presenting information effectively. The depth and quality of data generation and analysis are paramount in this  context. Concerning big data in personalized learni ng, researchers advocate for enriching datasets by identifying standard features and patterns. They recommend expanding data sources to encompass not only students historical and current student data a nd future but also enrich data with employment outc omes, academic background, individual interviews, focus g roups, and the learner's notes and reflections (Kok ku et al., 2018; Maghsudi et al., 2021; Furini et al., 2022; F ake, 2023). Further recommendations encompass diver se unstructured data types, including images, audio, t ext, and database data, and involve linking example s with learning objectives (Kokku et al., 2018; Rad et al., 2018; Hsia et al., 2023). These various forms of data contribute to the holist ic understanding and personalization of the learnin g experience. The emphasis is also on regularly feeding AI with data to optimize its performance, p articularly with auto-tagging capabilities driven by AI, highlighting the dynamic nature of personalized lea rning (Magomadov, 2020). This multifaceted approach underscores the intricate interplay between data utilization and the enhancement of AI capabilities in personalized learning contexts. Following the analysis of big da ta, diverse data analytics modules like Natural Lan guage Processing, Emotion Recognition,  Network Security, and Object Detection (Rad et al.,  2018), classification, clustering, neural networks, and deep learning (Fur ini et al., 2022) will contribute to training machi nes on algorithms such as the Adaptive Learning Module (Murtaza et al., 2022). To validate these algorithms, a Humanin-the-loop or Human Wizard approach is recommended (Kokku et al., 2018; Maghsudi et al., 2021). In subsequent applications, AI and machine learning techniques have been extended to augmented reality and virtual reality in personalized learning. This includes the use of AI-generated characters as digital representations (Pataranutaporn et al., 2021), technology-driven intelligent tutoring systems acting as scala ble augmentation aids (Kokku et al., 2018), and teachable robots fostering personalized understanding (vander Vorst & Jelicic, 2019), using a digital-twin to analyze data (Furini et al., 2022) and epistemic games to evaluate learners' creativity (Lin et al., 2013).

Q.3 What are the fundamental underpinnings and motivations for developing these AI-supported personalized learning systems? What are we trying to understand at a deeper level that is difficult to achieve through traditional learning systems? Several reasons guide and motivate the development of AI-supported personalized systems. At the fundamental level, researchers are keen to understand how symbi otic learning systems can be meaningfully realized in educational settings (Ogata et al.,2023). In additi on, the role of AI-supported personalized systems needs to be clarified for better development, such as defining roles as an advisor, coach, and mentor (Fake, 2023). Hence, to provide better and equitable support to learners, A I-supported personalized systems need to be trained to detect nuances and errors in learners' abilities to provide helpful feedback and valuable learning pathways (Liu & Quan, 2022).

In this review, we found researchers are trying to establish frameworks that understand how 21st-century competencies are developed and supported. A key asp ect of personalized learning is encouraging learner s to embark and engage in the proposed learning pathway.  For instance, when learners learn through game-based scenarios, AI-supported systems can evaluate technical capabilities and gauge creativity levels by how the learner interacts with the system to solve problems (Lin et  al., 2013). Similarly, AI-supported personalized systems aim not just to understand the individual learner but also to understand how humans learn various skills. Such machine learning algorithms require analysis of relationshi ps between learning performances and behaviours and  learner reflections to provide holistic feedback (Hsia et a l., 2023; Maghsudi, et al., 2021). These personaliz ed pathways offer memorable, inclusive, and encouraging learning experiences, enhancing learning outcomes (Pataranutaporn et al., 2021; Somasundaram, et al., 2020).

## Conclusion

Addressing trust and ethical  concerns in AI-supported personalized learning involves enhancing data insights with diverse unstructured data types; training AI i n-depth on human language, continuous data collection, and ongoing assessment. Challenges persist in comparing  students with diverse educational backgrounds, esp ecially in competitive contexts like medical school entranc e exams. Conversely, AI applications prove effectiv e in student-centred approaches, providing autonomy in choosing options, learning at their pace, and managing their schedule. Therefore, there is a need to centre learners’ voices in designing AI-supported personalized systems to understand student motivation and meaningful learni ng scaffolds. These directions should inform learni ng sciences research in building AI-supported personalized learning systems to emphasize humanistic interactions.

In conclusion, our research contributes to the ongo ing discourse on AI in education by highlighting it s potential and addressing challenges. Seamless integ ration of AI analytics with human-centric approaches is essential for personalized learning. To entir ely rely on AI-driven personalized learning, extensive research is required to train machines with enriched data and t ackle the challenges in designing learning experien ces, which often demand human logic and contributions. Therefo re, our emphasis on AI-supported approaches underscores the crucial importance of human supervision in steering these advancements.
