# Self-Regulated Learning With Task-Centered Microlearning

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The need for self-regulated learning (SRL) skills was intensified during COVID-19, with schools shifting to online-learning. Using microlearning (short videos with self-evaluation) may promote SRL while an organizing-task may help assemble learning-units into a wider concept. We propose a task-centered microlearning framework for promoting SRL and other 21st century skills. Our pilot with middle school students revealed practicing aspects of SRL such as controlling the learning-environment, self-reflecting, and overcoming obstacles by using peers’ support.

## Introduction

As educators, we need our instructional methods to consider skills and competencies that are required for our students’ career mobility as grownups. These skills, often referred to as 21st-century skills, include SelfRegulated Learning (SRL), critical thinking, collaboration, and more (Ananiadou & Claro, 2009).  The need for SRL was intensified during COVID-19 worldwide spread, which drove school systems to online-learning and required students to learn by themselves.

Microlearning is a set of small chunks (5-10 min of length) of digital media learning units (microunits) that target small learning objectives and are usually delivered online. Microunits include short videos accommodated by short tasks for self-evaluation (Hug, 2007). Using the video operating keys and the embedded tasks, the student controls his learning environment and monitors his progress, both are pillars of self-regulated learning (Zimmerman, 2002). Furthermore, microlearning may reduce some of the cognitive load involved as the units are structured in learner-paced segments (Mayer, 2002). Microlearning by itself may not be sufficient when there is a need to acquire complex concepts since a single microunit covers only a fragment of a curriculum concept and the student needs to relate between sets of microunits and put them into the appropriate larger context. An organizing-task may help the student cognitively assemble different learning units and engage him in the learning activity by providing meaning and motivation (Ahlfeldt et al., 2005; Krajcik & Blumenfeld, 2006). In addition, timely teacher interventions and facilitation (Goodyear & Dudley, 2015) are crucial to monitor the student’s progress. We developed and piloted a task-centered microlearning framework with 18 microunits centered around a team’s task of creating a digital escape room. We intend to further assess how this framework promotes self-regulated learning & 21st-century skills in general.

Research Goal and Questions Our research intends to evaluate the promotion of SRL skills and additional 21st-century skills with the proposed task-centered microlearning framework. The research questions are: (1) Which SRL skills are promoted through Task-Centered Microlearning? And how? (2) Which other 21st-century skills are promoted through TaskCentered Microlearning?

## Participants

Our research includes four science teachers and 23 8th grade students from one middle school. The participants signed a consent form approved by the institutional ethics committee. Materials The program lasted four weeks.  During the first week, the teacher explained the program’s collective task (building a digital escape room), introduced three digital tools for the digital game creation, and exposed the microlearning platform.  During the following three weeks, the students individually learned environmental concepts by watching 18 microunits and completing the unit’s microtasks. In parallel, the students, working in teams, developed a digital escape room. The final week is devoted to the projects’ presentation and a reflection process. See figure 1 below.

We used the adjusted MSLQ questionnaire (Pintrich & Others, 1991) to measure students applying aspects of SRL related to controlling their learning environment and managing time/goals. We used open-ended questionnaire to collect aspects of SRL that the students were applying while facing obstacles during the program., In addition, we collected qualitative data regarding applying aspects of SRL and other 21st-century skills by interviewing the teachers.

## Preliminary Findings and Discussion

As part of this pilot study, twenty-three 8th grade students used the framework for learning about the human impact on earth resources. We found, following the preliminary analysis of the data, that the students were practicing one or more aspects of SRL, such as controlling and replaying the videos to ensure self-learning ("I replayed the video over and over again until I understood all of it"), self-balancing between learning and offlearning, and overcoming obstacles by using their colleagues’, teacher’s, or parent’s help. The teachers reported that the students were actively engaged through self-controlling their microlearning environment, demonstrated ownership of the learning outcomes, and applied critical thinking. Next, we plan to use a Learning Management System (LMS) based environment, to automaticallycollect data related to the students’ control of the microlearning environment, including usage of the video control buttons, partial watching, rate of completion unit’s tasks, etc. We also plan to explore the promotion of additional SRL aspects with this task-centered microlearning framework, such as evaluating personal progress and motivation to learn.    Since the proposed framework fits remote and face-to-face learning, this research may contribute to a worldwide need for effective tools and methodologies for online and blended learning. It may also extend the knowledge about Microlearning applicability in K-12 education.
