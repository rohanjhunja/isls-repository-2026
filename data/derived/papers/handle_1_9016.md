# Reframing Design for Transformative Change: Supporting Pre-Service Teachers in Adopting Designerly Stances Towards Inquiry

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We need transformative change in K-12 education to address the myriad challenges and inequities facing the world today. Traditional approaches to design in education as taught to pre-service teachers, however, support incremental improvements to efficacy and efficiency rather than transformative change. Guided by contemporary theory and research from design fields, we propose a framework for conceptualizing K-12 educators’ stances towards design organized along a continuum from technical rational to designerly. We use this framework as a lens to analyze data drawn from a case study examining 28 pre-service teachers' conceptualization and engagement in design within the context of a design course taken by over 400 pre-service teachers. Our analysis of a range of data sources demonstrated that most participants adopted either a technical rational or exploratory stance towards design by the end of the course. We conclude by explicating why certain shifts took place and proposing ways pre-service teachers could be supported in adopting more human-centered approaches to design.

## Theoretical framework

Insights from design fields suggest that a designerly stance towards design for K-12 educators focused on problem finding and framing – in contrast to the more tradi tional focus on solution finding – would emphasize four key themes as follows: (a) view of the problem space (e.g., Buchanan, 1992; Norman, 2013; Schön, 1984), (b) approach to stakeholders and inquiry (e.g., Bang & Vossoughi, 2016; Christensen et al., 2016; Krippend orff, 2004), (c) framing and frame creation (e.g., Buchanan, 1992; Dorst, 2011), and (d) conceptualization of the design process (e.g., Norman, 2013; Owen, 2006). These elements overlap and interrelate with one another and share, in many instances, common themes. We created a detaile d rubric based on these four areas that we used to guide this study and data analysis. Space precludes including the full rubric here, but we will share the fu ll version at the conference.

## Research Questions

1. When viewed through the theoretical framing outlined above, how do pre-service teachers’ understandings

of design evolve in a course that includes an emphasis on design-based thinking from the perspective of the design fields as well as perspectives on design from the field of education?

2. Which aspects of design as defined in the theoretical framing come most naturally to pre-service teachers and

which aspects require more support, revisions, or emphasis? Building on these two core questions, our discussio n also considers how articulating a design framewor k based in the broader literature from the design fields might provide insight into avenues for improvement in pre-service teacher education to improve these outcomes.

## Methods, Data Sources, and Coding Schema

We employed case study research (Stake, 2005; Yin, 2014) to consider how, and the extent to which, pre-service teachers engaged in designerly stance t owards design within the context of a mandatory fiv e-week course taken by 400 students at a university in western Canada focused on a range of design processes in the field of education including perspectives from design fields (Design Council, 2007; Norman, 2013) as well as design perspectives in education including design thinking (IDEO, 2012). The bracketing of the boundaries for this case involved limiting the focus of the research to data  gained from this course. In total, the team analyz ed the work of 28 pre-service teachers across the 18 sections o f the course who consented to allow their work to b e used as data in this study. Data in this regard were drawn from three course pre and post reflections and design projects.

## Findings

Framing and frame creation. Our analysis of the data set suggested the pre-service teachers started at a more exploratory stance towards framing and frame creation but made little progress beyond that during the course. They overwhelmingly associated design with the crea tion of more active and engaging learning environme nts involving a shift away from traditional approaches to education rooted in teacher-centric pedagogies, passive learning, and content coverage.

Conceptualization of the design process.  An area where participants demonstrated substantia l growth occurred in relation to process. Pre-service teache rs initially displayed a range of technical rationa l and early exploratory perspectives that shifted towards primarily exploratory stances by the end of the course. In particular, pre-service teachers became much more attuned to th e iterative nature of design. Given these shifts, t here was little evidence of a fully designerly stance in how participants conceptualized and engaged in the design process.

## Scholarly significance

The significance of this study is threefold. Our st udy offers one of the few fully articulated framewo rks for conceptualizing K-12 educators’ stances towards des ign. This framework provides both a lens for pre-se rvice teachers to reflect on how they engage in design, as well as a tool for research, critique, and re-envisioning of preservice and in-service programs. Insights gained from this study additionally offer ways educators might be more intentionally and purposefully supported in moving towards more designerly stances that could position them as agents of change in service of preferable education al futures. Ultimately, helping educators move towa rd more designerly stances will support them in becoming more comfortable with the ill-defined nature of problems and their societal and cultural roots as well as the cr itical role of moving beyond traditional teacher-centered frames in educational discourse that have limited educational possibilities for transformative change.
