# Improving the implementation of Knowledge Building through Pedagogical Scaffolds

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigates teacher scaffolding practices during Knowledge Building implementation. This study, with a mixed design, involved 59 students enrolled in Education program. Data on the quality of discourse were collected from entries or notes on the Knowledge Forum platform, while information on teacher scaffolding was obtained through interviews from students. The results lead to a discussion on the value of teacher scaffolding in facilitating the implementation of Knowledge Building in the classroom.

Indroduction The work of Scardamalia and Bereiter is pivotal in the Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (CSCL) field. They introduced the Knowledge Building framework, an educational theoretical, pedagogical, and technological framework that aims to cultivate student communities engaged in collaborative knowledge creation (Bereiter & Scardamalia, 2021). Thus, students engage in iterative reflective sessions that run parallel to the inquiry process, fostering collaborative improvement ideas (Cacciamani et al., 2021). Knowledge Building pedagogy proposes that teachers should gradually entrust students with responsibilities linked to knowledge construction. The teacher should provide scaffolds that encourages students to share, question, and refine  ideas about authentic problems (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2021). In this context, Scaffolds are tools that provide support to complete a specific zone of proximal knowledge  (Gutiérrez-Braojos et al., in press). Zhu and Lin (2023) focus on introducing scaffolding strategies to encourage student collaboration, such as initiating an inquiry, motivating students to develop and deepen their ideas, promoting building upon community ideas, stimulating new research direc tions, establishing community norms, and providing direct instructions.  Based on these previous studies, our  goal are:

i) To e xamine the overall effects and impacts of implementing Knowledge Building pedagogy in educational

research; ii) To  understand the value that students attribute to the actions of the teacher in facilitating the collective construction of knowledge.

## Method

This study employed a mixed-methods design (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2018). A total of 59 students enrolled in a 16-week course focused on educational research and based on Knowledge Building Pedagogy and Knowledge Forum platform. A sequence of modules was conducted for implementing Knowledge Building in the classroom, encompassing: i) Getting familiar with Knowledge Building and Knowledge Forum in Module 1; ii) Identifying significant questions to address throughout the course in Module 2; iii) Following cycles of inquiry on Knowledge Forum to resolve the major questions established in Module 2 in Module 3; iv) Engaging in reflective assessment about improving ideas collaboratively in Module 4.  During the initial weeks, the teacher provided essential feedback to help students improve their participation in the Knowledge Forum, in line with its principles. As part of this guidance, he provided concrete examples of how to properly use various discursive moves and write highquality notes, and he pointed out practices to avoid. To achieve the first goal, we extracted notes written by students from the records in Knowledge Forum. Each note was considered a unit of analysis and organized chronologically.  Each note was encoded according to a coding scheme: questioning, theorizing, sophistication, and authorized sources (e.g., Zhu et al. 2021, GutiérrezBraojos, in press). Subsequently, a diachronic analysis is provided for the average quality of student contributions per week. To achieve the second objective, a content analysis of interviews with 10 postgraduate students was conducted to identify instructor strategies perceived positively by learners in fostering collaboration among students and enhancing their understanding of the subject (3 students with a low level, 3 students with a medium level, and 3 students with a high level).

The results show that it took students a few weeks to improve their grades, and the importance of teaching scaffolds during the initial weeks was evident. A significant improvement in the quality of grades is observed 2341 star ting from the sixth or seventh week (Figure 1). This suggests that students needed a minimum of about six weeks to understand the expectations in Knowledge Building (KB), but they are entirely capable of improving within a reasonably short time frame. Students acknowledged the effectiveness of specific teaching scaffolds in fostering collaborative efforts to refine their ideas during the first weeks (Figure 2). The most relevant scaffolds were "explaining how to make good discourse moves", "explaining how to write a note according to scientific criteria", and "encouraging students to take ideas from the Kowledge Forum and expand them further". Figure 1 Diachronic Analysis: Quality of Contributions Figure 2 Pedagogical Scaffolds The Knowledge Building pedagogy has positive effects on learning outcomes and the quality of discourse among participants, even with novice students in this pedagogy. It has also been noted that students need an adaptation period to this pedagogy, and in this process, teacher support is crucial. Thus, the role of the teacher in Knowledge Building aims to provide scaffolds that help students empower themselves to collaboratively construct knowledge. We believe that new studies should investigate the effects of these teaching strategies in promoting collective cognitive responsibility.
