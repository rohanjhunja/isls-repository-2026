# Cognitive Flexibility in the Acceptance of AI Tools in Mathematics Education within the Framework of the Technology Acceptance Model: The Mediating Role of Technostress

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines the role of cognitive flexibility in mathematics teachers’ acceptance of AI-based tools and the mediating effect of technostress. Using a correlational design with 127 teachers, findings revealed that cognitive flexibility reduces technostress and increases technology acceptance. Mediation analysis showed that technostress partially mediates this relationship. Results suggest that cognitive flexibility facilitates adaptation to technological innovations, while technostress acts as a barrier, highlighting the importance of supporting teachers’ psychological readiness for AI integration.

The rapid integration of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies into mathematics education has fundamentally transformed instructional processes by enabling personalized learning environments, automated feedback systems, and data-driven decision-making practices (Hu, 2022; Yıldız Durak & Onan, 2025). However, the effective and sustainable use of these technologies depends not only on their technical affordances but also on teachers’ willingness to adopt and integrate them into their pedagogical practices. The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) has been widely used to explain individuals’ adoption of new technologies through perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use (Davis, 1989). Meta-analytic findings also confirm that TAM has strong explanatory power in educational contexts (Scherer et al., 2019). However, recent studies emphasize that technology acceptance is not solely a cognitive evaluation process but is also shaped by psychological and emotional factors such as stress, anxiety, and individual adaptability (Chou et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2025).

In this context, cognitive flexibility and technostress emerge as two critical variables. Cognitive flexibility refers to an individual’s ability to adapt to changing conditions, generate alternative solutions, and shift perspectives (Martin & Rubin, 1995), whereas technostress reflects the cognitive and emotional strain experienced due to the use of digital technologies (Tarafdar et al., 2007). These two variables represent opposing psychological mechanisms in technology integration processes.  Previous studies indicate that teachers with higher cognitive flexibility are more capable of coping with uncertainty and are more likely to perceive technological innovations as opportunities rather than threats (Zhai & Chu, 2023; Kourou et al., 2024). Conversely, hi gh levels of technostress can reduce teachers’ motivation, perceived control, and willingness to use technology in instructional settings (Kundu & Bej, 2021; Zhao & Xu, 2022).

Despite the increasing body of literature on AI integration in education, limited research has examined the interaction between cognitive flexibility and technostress within the framework of TAM, particularly among in-service mathematics teachers. Addressi ng this gap, the present study investigates the mediating role of technostress in the relationship between cognitive flexibility and teachers’ acceptance of AI-based tools.

## Method

This study employed a quantitative correlational design within the framework of TAM. The sample consisted of 127 elementary mathematics teachers working in public schools in the Marmara region. Participants were selected through voluntary participation. Data were collected using three validated instruments: the Cognitive Flexibility Inventory (Martin & Rubin, 1995), the Technostress Scale (Çoklar et al., 2017), and the Technology Acceptance Scale for AI-based tools in mathematics education (Demir et al., 2024). These instruments measure teachers’ adaptability, stress levels related to technology use, and their acceptance of AI-based instructional tools, respectively. The data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics and the PROCESS Macro (Model 4) developed by Hayes (2018). A bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples was applied to test the mediating role of technostress and to estimate indirect effects.

Technostress was found to have a significant negative effect on technology acceptance (β = –0.583, p <.001). In addition, cognitive flexibility significantly and positively predicted technology acceptance (β = 0.339, p <.001).  Furthermore, the mediation analysis revealed a significant indirect effect of cognitive flexibility on technology acceptance through technostress (β = 0.142, 95% CI [0.069, 0.230]). Since the direct effect remained significant, these findings indicate a partial mediation effect. Consistent with previous research, cognitive flexibility appears to function as a protective factor that reduces stress and facilitates adaptation to technological innovations (Lazarus & Folkman, 1984; Park & Jo, 2023). Teachers who can think flexibly are more likely to reinterpret challenges, develop alternative strategies, and engage more positively with new technologies. This supports the idea that cognitive flexibility enhances individuals’ coping mechanisms in complex and uncertain environments. On the other hand, technostress acts as a barrier to technology acceptance by negatively affecting motivation, perceived usefulness, and perceived ease of use —core components of TAM (Tarafdar et al., 2007; Salanova et al., 2013). High levels of technostress may lead to resistance to technological change, particularly in the context of complex AI-based systems.

Importantly, the mediation findings suggest that cognitive flexibility not only directly enhances technology acceptance but also indirectly contributes by lowering technostress. This finding is consistent with recent studies emphasizing the role of psychol ogical variables such as stress, self-efficacy, and emotional states in shaping teachers’ technology adoption behaviors (Chou et al., 2024; Du et al., 2025). From a practical perspective, these results emphasize the importance of supporting teachers’ psychological readiness alongside their technical competencies. Professional development programs that focus on enhancing cognitive flexibility—such as problem-solving activities and reflective practices —may help teachers better cope with technological change. Additionally, institutional support mechanisms aimed at reducing technostress may further facilitate the integration of AI tools into mathematics education (C hen et al., 2023). Overall, this study contributes to the literature by extending TAM through the inclusion of psychological variables, offering a more comprehensive framework for understanding teachers’ acceptance of AI-based technologies.
