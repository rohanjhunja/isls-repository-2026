# Exploring eSports as a Learning Space for Identity, Community, and Ethical Engagement

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study is about how eSports, a global phenomenon in organized, competitive video gaming, serves as a dynamic learning platform for university students. Through identity exploration, community building, and ethical engagement, students developed critical skills such as teamwork, resilience, and digital citizenship. Over six weeks, 109 students participated in gameplay, written reflections, class discussions, and follow-up interviews that offered deeper insight into their experiences. The analysis is grounded in sociocultural learning theory, affinity spaces, and digital self-representation. Findings position eSports as a valuable educational tool that bridges formal education and digital culture, supporting personal growth and meaningful social connections.

## Introduction

eSports, defined as organized, competitive video ga ming, has emerged as a global phenomenon with signi ficant educational potential. Scholars have noticed its ca pacity to foster informal learning, collaboration, and problemsolving skills (Gee, 2003; Squire, 2013). As an aff inity space, eSports encourages natural learning th rough participation and shared goals, making it an ideal platform for exploring teamwork, leadership, and digital identity. Digital identity is a key theme in eSports, where p layers use a vatars and usernames to experiment with self-representation, reflecting the fluidity of identity in virtual spaces (Goffman, 2023; Turkle, 1997). eSports provides a platform for ethical engagement, allowing players  to navigate complex social norms and develop trans ferable skills such as strategic thinking and adaptability.  To investigate these intersections systematically,  this study explores how eSports fosters identity development, community dynamics, and ethical learning. The study  draws upon sociocultural perspectives (Vygotsky, 1978) to  understand how learners co-construct knowledge through mediated activity, as well as Jenkin’s (2006) framework of participatory culture and digital affinity spaces.

The study This study involved 109 university students enrolle d in a second-year digital media course at the University of Prince Edward Island. As the instructor, I designed  the course to explore meaningful lessons about ide ntity, community, and ethical engagement through students’  reflections and discussions. Over the six weeks, f rom September 6th, 2024, to October 18th, 2024, each cl ass included a 20-minute discussion session dedicated to exploring themes such as connection, collaboration,  social values, and ethics  in digital media. Students participated in a single mandatory eSports gameplay  session to provide a shared experience for reflect ion, with the option to engage in additional gameplay if desired. This shared activity served as a common experiential basis to support thematic reflection and analysis. Student s documented their reflections through written assi gnments and engaged in both small and large group discussio ns to deepen their understanding of these themes. T he qualitative data corpus includes 109 written reflections, two full-class discussions (recorded and transcribed), and 16 follow-up semi-structured interviews. Data were thematically analy zed using an inductive approach. Initial codes were generated through open coding of a sampl e of reflections and discussion transcripts. Codes were refined iteratively, and emergent themes were ident ified through constant comparative analysis. To ens ure rigor, we used peer debriefing, triangulated data sources, and selected Fortnite and Apex Legends for their accessibility, team play, and relevance to identity and collaboration, as shown in Figure 1. Emergent themes From the analysis of data collected, four key theme s emerged: identity exploration, community building, ethical engagement, and transferable skills. Identity explo ration emerged as students used avatar customizatio n and gameplay to express aspects of their  identity. Yixing said, “Choosing my avatar felt lik e picking a version of myself that I wanted to show the world. It was libe rating” (Personal communication, October 19, 2024),  which aligns with Turkle’s (1997) discussion of self-representation in digital spaces. Collaborative gameplay supported community building, as students described shared goals and teamwork. Dawson shared, “When we won as a team, it felt like a family victory. Everyone had a role,  and it mattered” (Personal communication, November 1, 2024), consistent with Squire’s (2013) concept of affinity  spaces. Ethical engagement was reflected in how students navigated dilemmas around fairness and respect. Car olina remarked, “Even though it’s just a game, ther e’s an unspoken rule of respect. Losing is okay, but being  rude isn’t” (Personal communication, September 19,  2024), connecting to principles of digital citizenship (Ribble, 2015). Finally, students discussed transferable skills, such as resilience and adaptability, with Joshua stating, “After handling intense moments in the game, I feel more prepared to face things in real life” (Personal communication, September 19, 2024). These t hemes signify the connections between eSports and student learning. These findings reflect the dynamic interplay between learners’ digital identities, peer collaboration, and moral decision-making within participatory media environments. ## Discussion and conclusion

This study demonstrates the potential of eSports as  a platform for informal learning, supporting identity development, community building, ethical engagement, and the cultivation of transferable skills. Stude nts expressed how activities like avatar customization allowed them to experiment with aspects of thei r identity, reflecting Turkle’s (1997) perspective on self-representation in digital spaces. Furthermore, coll aborative gameplay fostered a sense of belonging and shared p urpose, echoing Gee’s (2003) argument that video ga mes encourage natural learning through participation and shared goals. These findings suggest that identit y performance, moral negotiation, and social cohesion  in eSports mirror the types of collaborative and s ituated learning emphasized in sociocultural theory. For ex ample, designing classroom discussions or reflectiv e assignments can help students articulate the social  and ethical dimensions of their gameplay experienc es. Incorporating principles of digital citizenship (Ribble et al., 2004) can help students engage critically with issues of fairness and respect, preparing them for ethical participation in both digital and physical environments. Educators interested in incorporating eSports into their teaching can adopt several approaches. Facilitated reflection activities, for instance, can provide students with a structured way to connect gameplay ex periences to social, ethical, and personal themes. These reflections might include written assignments or group discussions that encourage students to think critically about their actions and decisions in the game. Creating inclusive group tasks can further enhance the sense of community, enablin g students to work together and build meaningful connections. Designing activities that promote coll aboration within diverse teams ensures that all stu dents feel supported and valued, fostering a culture of inclusivity. Workshops on ethical engagement can address topics such as fairness, respect, and digital citizenship, equi pping students with the skills needed to navigate c omplex interactions. These workshops can also serve as a p latform for discussing how the skills developed in eSports, such as strategic thinking and adaptability, apply to broader contexts, both academic and professional. The poster presentation will showcase the thematic findings of this study, including identity exploration, community building, ethical engagement, and transfe rable skills. It will feature selected student refl ections, examples of gameplay activities, and p ractical recommendations for integrating eSports in to education. Future research should explore how these findings might ge neralize across different sociocultural contexts and examine longitudinal impacts of eSports integration on lear ner agency and ethical reasoning. By recognizing th e educational potential of eSports, institutions can better prepare students to navigate and contribute to increasingly interconnected and complex worlds.
