# Project-Based Learning in Digital Pedagogies and Assistive Technologies Face-to-Face Course for Undergraduate Pre-Service Teachers

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The design and implementation of a project-based undergraduate course, Digital Pedagogies and Assistive Technologies, highlights effective approaches to preparing pre-service teachers for contemporary K-12 classrooms. The course combines instructional design theories with a variety of emerging digital tools embedded in Project-Based Learning (PBL) principles to enable learners to engage in weekly mini-projects, fostering critical thinking, collaboration, problem-solving, and technology integration. The mini-projects emphasized the creation of instructional activities and materials applicable to classrooms, bridging theory with practice. A clear outline of the course structure and design selected mini-projects, final project, and scaffolding strategies used to support learning and collaboration are provided. Challenges faced during implementation, reflection, and recommendations provide actionable insights for educators aiming to integrate PBL into their courses.

## Introduction

Today’s rapidly evolving educational technologies (i.e., Virtual Reality, Augmented Reality, Mixed Reality or Artificial Intelligence) and the challenges associated with their adoption in the K-12 classroom (Guo et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2023) make it imperative for pre-service teachers to acquire relevant knowledge, skills, and abilities to coherently integrate technologies into their teaching and learning practices. Among these advancements, digital pedagogies and assistive technologies have emerged as fundamental tools for fostering inclusive, equitable, and engaging learning environments (Alam & Mohanty, 2023). Despite the widespread recognition of the potential of these technologies, their integration into classroo m practice remains inconsistent  (Hew & Brush, 2007). Many teachers, specifically those in the early stage of their careers, report challenges in selecting, adap ting, and implementing technology to align with pedagogical g oals and meet the diverse needs of the learners (Sepula & Simuja, 2024). This gap highlights the critical importance of  preparing pre-service teachers to navigate the complexities of technology integration effectively (Johnson et al., 2016). To meet this need, an innovative instructional approach that combines theory with practice was introduced through the IDE405: Digital Pedagogies and Assistive Technologies, an undergraduate course offered at the School of Education in a private university in the Northeastern USA. This course provides pre-service teachers with the opportunity to develop a comprehensive understanding of the transformative role of pedagogical approaches and content delivery  using technologies that best support the learning process  (Admiraal et al., 2017). By incorporating PBL, this course emphasizes real classroom applications, empowering students to enga ge with instructional design deeply and actively  in a collaborative manner (Han & Bhattacharya, 2001).

Integrating foundational principles of PBL into course design The course design is grounded in PBL, informed by  constructivist educational theories, which emphasize experiential learning and social interaction as cri tical components of knowledge construction. PBL prioritizes inquiry and collaboration, promoting a student-centered learning environment through active partic ipation and practical engagement (Loyens et al., 2010). Project s in PBL are based on authentic situations that fos ter meaningful inquiry aligned with learning objectives, encouraging students to solve real-world challenges and take ownership of their teaching practices  (Loyens et al., 2010; Tsybulsky & Sinai, 2022). This method enhances initiative, critical thinking, teamwork, creativity, and professional skills (Balleisen et al., 2023),  shifting the classroom from a teacher-led to a student-driven setting. PBL is a fitting instructional strategy for the course Digital Pedagogies and Assistive Technologies. Firstly, PBL aligns well with the course’s practical focus, whic h allows pre-service teachers to apply theoretical knowledge through hands-on mini-projects and a final project. Through individual, pair, or small group projects, students can independently explore various digital tools and ass istive technologies, boosting their digital profici ency and promoting self-directed learning – key skills for future e ducators. The collaborative final project mirrors r ealworld teaching environments, allowing students to collaborate in designing and implementing instructional design theory-driven solutions using relevant technologies to support diverse learners. PBL encourages critical thinking, adaptability, and problem-solving, which helps prepare pre-service teachers to use digital tools creatively and inclusively in their future classrooms.

According to Figure 1, the inner circles represent the original components of the model, while the out er rings illustrate the customized strategies applied in our course for pre-service teachers. The left circle highlights the Seven Essential Project Design Elements, tailored t hrough strategies such as addressing real-world classroom challenges with emerging technologies, fostering su stained inquiry via weekly mini-projects, ensuring authenticity through the simulation of K-12 teaching scenarios, promoting student agency by offering voice and choice, embedding reflective practices to enhance critical thinking, incorporating peer and instructor feedback for iterative refinement, and culminating in public presentations of final projects to foster professional readiness. The right circle outlines the Seven Project-Based Teaching Practices, which were customized to include alignment with instructional design principles and relevant s tandards, fostering a collaborative learning cultur e, managing activities with cl ear timelines and milestones, scaffolding learning through structured resources and templates, employing a combination of formative and summative assessments to monitor progress, and actively engag ing students through coaching and mentoring.

Course development The IDE405 course was intentionally designed to provide  pre-service teachers with the knowledge, skills, and experiences necessary to incorporate various digital and assistive technologies into everyday teaching and learning practices and to inspire changes in practice. This course is designed to guide students toward the constructive use of technology by building meaningful connections be tween subject matter, pedagogical knowledge, and technological knowledge. Each module within this course is grounded in PBL principles to deeply engage learners in meaningful, hands-on experiences of working on multiple mini-projects using digital technologies for teaching and learning in K-12 classroom settings. The resulting 11 modules engage students in the following areas: website evaluation, multi-media design, CANVA for educator s, game-based learning,  game-based assessment, asynchronous learning with google classroom, assistive technologies (part 1 & 2), artificial intelligence, and cyber security. The weekly module topic and corresponding mini-projects were intentionally crafted to foster a str ong foundation for practical application. The selection  and progression of topics are strategically ground ed in PBL, ensuring that pre-service teachers gain e xperiential, reflective, and actionable insights th at bridge theory with classroom realities. The student experience is the result of a pre-defined course design and structure that supports distributed practice, including relevant mini-projects such as website evaluation, video editing, etc., a pre-defined rubric to evaluate each of the mini-projects, feedback mechanisms that provide consiste nt scaffolding, learner progress throughout the course with sustained suppo rt, and meaningful learning outcomes.  Each course module was structured to integrate  relevant content, activities, and assignments nested within a single module. Within each module, mini-projects focused on specific content were incorporated to en hance learner engagement and deepen their understanding of digital pedagogies and assistive technologies. All information relevant to the mini project was shared on the Blackboard Ultra, ensurin g a logical sequence that facilitated easy navigati on on the LMS. The course emphasized the use of technology to develop instructional strategies f or teaching subjects that align with New York State Learning Standards. Analyze various case studies that demonstrate the impact of digital tools in inclusive education, design digital learning experiences that are accessible and  inclusive for all learners and reflect on the ethical and legal considerations of using assistive technology in K-12 classroom settings. The course design was grounded in a constructivist appr oach drawing on foundational instructional design t heories and principles like the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning, Cognitive Load Theory, and Universal Design of Learning (UDL).

Course implementation The sessions combine instructional theory with hands-on practice of designing effective learning experiences for different subject areas such as Sciences, Mathemati cs, Social Studies, and English Literature within the K-12 settings. Students were guided through key instructional desi gn elements such as needs assessment, learning objectives, content organization, and assessment st rategies. Students utilized various digital tools, including Google Classroom, Padlet, MS PowerPoint, menti-meter, and Bitmoji, while platforms like Camtasia and Apple iMovie were employed for video creation and editing.

Weekly sessions combined lectures with practical workshops, ensuring students gained proficiency in digital tools and instructional strategies. Mini-projects were introduced during the session, with students initiating work independently or collaboratively in pairs or teams.  Each mini-project was supported by a detailed instructional guide and rubric to ensure clarity in expectations.  Students were allotted one week to complete and su bmit their projects, with scheduled office hours available for additional assistance. The course modules demonstrated diverse instructional strategies; for instance, asynchronous learning was delivered in a synchronous format, accompanied by a project designed for application in K-12 classrooms. Topics were sequenced progressively,  advancing from foundational concepts like website evaluation to mo re complex topics such as game-based assessment. This structured approach enabled students to build their  knowledge and skills systematica lly. The course integrates structured reflection activities, requiring student s to critically evaluate their learning experiences  after every two modules. Using the reflection worksheet, students e ngage in three stages of analysis: the mirror to reflect on personal growth and conceptual understanding, the m icroscope to delve into specific challenges and pro blemsolving strategies, and the binoculars to project the applicability o f acquired knowledge to future tasks. These reflections foster deeper self-awareness and prepare students for coming academic and real-world applications (McCarthy, 2013). The course culminates in a collaborative final project that involves group presentation, and an individual report designed to integrate instructi onal design  and technological application. In teams of three, students create and deliver a 15-minute lesson aligned with curriculum standards, emphasizing active technology use. The project includes lesson planning, material  creation, and a final presentation, with individual reflections evaluating team dynamics and contributions.

Challenges One of the primary challenges observed during the c ourse was the varying technological skill levels among students. Some students demonstrated ease in utilizing digita l tools for designing and developing mini-projects, while others struggled due to unfamiliarity with th e technology or difficulties adapting to assistive technologies. This disparity made it challenging to ensure effect ive collaborative learning in a classroom with vari ed levels of comfort with technology. The need for personalized support for students became clear, which highlight ed the importance of tailored guidance in facilitating inc lusive, technology-rich learning environments. Notably, while most students demonstrated strong digital competencies, they often required additional time and support to grasp how learning theories, instructional design theories, or models could be constructively integrated into the design. As a response to these challenges, several adaptati ons and refinements were made to the course. Feedba ck from students' periodical reflection journal and instructor observations revealed a need for more additional scaffolding and structured support. As a result, the course was adjusted to include more hands-on workshops and extended time after sessions for project work.  Weekly office hours were also established to help s tudents address any questions related to the mini-projects or underlying concepts. The implementation of PBL in this setting highlighted its demands for significant time, resources, and real-world context connections. Effective facilitation of PBL requires educators to possess specific skills in pr oject management, scaffolding, and inqui ry-based learning. Additionally, educators must be adept at monitoring  the learning process and providing adequate support to both individual students and teams (Lewis et al., 2019). These insights led to further course adaptations aimed at enhancing the learning experience and ensuring that all students could benefit from the innovative approach of this course design. Relevance for practitioners This paper offers valuable insights for educators s eeking to implement similar project-based courses into their teaching practices. One of the key takeaways is the importance of designing inclusive PBL environments that not only incorporate digital tools but also foster accessibility for all learners. Educators must cons ider the varying technological competencies of their students and ensure that sufficient support and resources are available to help all students succeed. Practitioners benefit from strategi es to address diverse learner needs, especially in scaffolding support for varying digital skill level s, while promoting collaboration and critical think ing via teamwork in both mini-projects and final projects. This course also emphasized the role of iterative f eedback in refining students’ projects and emphasizes the prac tical application of assistive technologies, equipp ing practitioners to tackle inclusivity challenges effectively.
