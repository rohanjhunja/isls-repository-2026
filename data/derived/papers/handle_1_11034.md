# Using Biterm Topic Modeling to Explore Gender Differences in Secondary Students’ Wonderings about AI Concepts

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The study reveals gender-based differences in how secondary students express curiosity in an AI curriculum. The study used biterm topic modeling to explore questions posed by male and female learners (n=239) in Grades 8-12. The findings revealed that keywords related to model accuracy and prediction such as Words and Weights were prevalent in both groups. Key variations were observed in technical terms, such as Machines. The discussions highlight the need for socially relevant AI curriculums.

## Introduction

The rapid growth of Artificial Intelligence (AI) as  a field has imposed a critical need for delivering  educational programs that would foster the development of knowledge and skills in the area. Empirical research has indicated that designing and developing socially relevant and student-centered AI curriculums comes from understanding how students learn through the contextualization of  their needs (Alvarez et al., 2023; Eguchi et al., 2021). This study is framed by the formative assessment approac h, which also has foundations in design-research theory (Sandoval, 2014). It is fundamental to assess stude nt learning and development of interests throughout  an instructional unit and adapt subsequent instruction  to align with the student’s attainment and interes ts. Students’ responses reveal information about their thought processes, interests, identities, and conceptions about the learning topics. The study's main purpose is to identify themes in questions that Grades 8-12 students ask as they learn AI concepts and how these themes differ across female and male gend er groups. The following research question s are addressed in this study: RQ1: What keywords and their associations were pres ent in students’ responses to a prompt about text classification models? RQ2: How do these keywords and their associations differ across gender groups?

## Methods

This study is based on an AI curriculum module desi gned for secondary students to learn AI concepts in  their English language Arts (ELA). The participants inclu ded female (n=133) and male (n=106) students in Gra des 812 who took part in a 7-hour module designed to explore AI concepts and pra ctices in the context of text classification models. The data collection involved  extracting students’ responses from A ctivity 2’s open-ended question labeled “What questions or wonderings do y ou have about the model?”. The  study deployed Biterm Topic Modeling (BTM) (Yan et al., 2013) to predict emerging themes in the students' questions from a corpus of open-ended responses. For this case, we went for clustering occurrences of nouns “NOUN”, “PROPN” and “ADJ” with hyperparameter K set at 4.

## Results

The results of the analysis provide valuable insigh ts into the context and scope of responses that bot h male and female students posed after completing a series of activities on text classification models. In the fr equency distribution charts (see Figure 1), it can be observed that keywords such as Words, Model, Negative, and Positive had the highest occurrence numbers in the analyzed responses from both student groups. The questions posed by male students were more technical and included fiel d-specific terms such as Machines and Accurate. The results of female students indicate that the majority of th eir questions were interested in understanding how the model worked and how it assigned weights to specific word s. The four-topic BTM model validated the observations from the keywords and showed an association of comm on words in both male and female questions (see Fig ure 2). For instance, in the green theme, male response s had associated the model with classification, tex t, and accuracy, while in female respons es, the model was associated with words. Another un ique observation is that female responses used more verbs (such as pick, imp rove, and determine) compared to male responses tha t used human action words.

## Conclusion

AI Education for K-12 presents ample opportunities for young learners to nurture their curiosity and develop their skills in AI concepts. The identified themes can inform the efficiency of the AI module’s cohesiveness and guide the future design of advanced and socially relevant AI curriculums.
