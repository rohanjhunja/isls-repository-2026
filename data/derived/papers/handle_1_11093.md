# Digging the Abandoned Feelings out of the Wasteland: Raising Trouble Makers through A Dystopian Critical Action Game

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study advances an innovative approach using a dystopian game in a classroom setting to empower youth to be critically involved with complex societal issues within a dystopian fictional world. This design-based research endeavors to develop a critical action game that challenges students to adopt the role of 'troublemakers' (i.e., proactive change agents) in critically addressing divergent social values and beliefs. Engaging in the game provides personal reflection and a secure environment for vulnerable groups to co-create new narratives and restore the public sphere for open discussions and collective decision-making.

Theoretical perspective This study reports on a co-design partnership with a Grade 11 visual art teacher, focusing on nurturing resilience and critical thinking in diverse cultural values and complex social dynamics. Drawing upon concepts of  critical pedagogy (Freire, 2020), this  project allows students to communicate and work wit h people they disagree with, which is crucial for thriving in a world with incre asingly divergent cultural and political views. By rooting our game design within a dystopian universe called "The Fall of Artica: A Way Back Home" (FoA), we aim to foster communicative action (Habermas,1990) as an implementation of critical action education a nd collective inquiry (Carvalho et al., 2021). This approach aligns with Gutierrez’s (2019) Social Design-based Experiment, advocacy for pedagogies that cultivate a critical stance in literacy education. The game-based curriculum in the Grade 11 visual art class is inspired by Vasquez's (2017) insights on critical literacy, which encourages students to question and problematize norms. This approach is also suppo rted by Giroux's (2020) discussions on critical ped agogy, which emphasize the importance of student agency in learning and decision-making. The game's framework aims to build global citizenship, which challenges stude nts to address and dismantle social-political conflicts and imagine “what ifs.”  Our research question is: How can a serious classroom game with dystopian scenarios provide a secure environment for students to openly discuss and configure unresolved societal tensions and foster students' identity as global citizens who embrace diverse perspectives and values?

## Methodology and data collection

This study utilizes a participatory action research methodology that incorporates a co-design approach in a Grade 11 visual art class. We designed a game-based curriculum that aligns with critical pedagogy  and participatory design (Cummins, 2009), integrating systemic societal issues into the learning environment. Students in this visual art class collaborate in groups of 4-5, transcending from passive receivers of informati on to become active contributors and co-designers. They delve into various social problems that are contextualized within the dystopian world of Artica. This setting provided student with a safe space for open discussion on some  of the most controversial topics in public spaces such as formal classrooms. The FoA game was designed as a r esearch tool and a curriculum, situating the heated social dilemmas within its fictional narrative. Students e ngaged with AI-powered NPCs (Zhou et al., 2023) to co-create game elements (e.g., settings, characters, a rtifacts). The game thus serves as an immer sive platform where artistic expression, critical t hinking, and social issues converge, fostering a rich, interactive learning experience in the visual art class.

## Design and procedure

Students were included in a participatory design ap proach, designing their characters, settings, and a rtifacts that complemented the game.  Because this was a visual arts class, the teacher emphasized student creations, including an artist’s statement, as part of the summative evaluation.  We implemented a  five-phase collaborative design approach:  1) Initial Engagement:  Familiarize students with the FoA world through gro up interactions with AI NPCs. 2) Thematic Exploration: Dividing the class into five gro ups, each focused on a unique dystopian theme, reflecting social issues (e.g., surveillance vs. security; development vs. sustainability) through different lenses. 3) Collaborative Learning Methods:  Employing a mix of whole-class discussions, group inquiries, and individual creative projects. This approach facilitated a deep er understanding and exploration of the themes. 4) Student Contributions and Narrative Development: Students actively contributed to the game's narra tive and artifacts by creating hand sketches, digital drawings, and paint ings. 5) Synthesis and Real-World Application: Students are encouraged to practice collective storytelling to deconstruct the systematic issues in the FoA world. This activity helps them draw parallels with real-world issues of the 21st century. Figure 1 Grade 11 Visual Art Students Contributed Item Cards.

Students developed artifacts such as Item Cards (Figure. 1), environment drawings, and character sketches, drawing inspiration from their interactions with the AI NPC that reflected their specific regional contexts. Following this, they were tasked with composing a brief artist statement to articulate their concept.  Their various products were submitted as a summative assessment for their visual arts course.

## Findings and discussion

The project aims to address several critical areas in its future iterations: 1) Narrative Expansion: U pdating and interconnecting the storylines to encompass six dystopian themes that closely align with contemporary real-world issues. This enhancement is e xpected to deepen the relevance and engagement of t he students with the subject matter. 2) Framework for Collaboration: Developing a robust framework for co-design, collaboration, and version management of game assets. This framework will main tain coherence and quality as the project grows and involves more participants. 3) Open-Source Community Development: Designing user experi ences tailored to platforms such as Github. This community will be a hub for sharing teaching materials, game assets, st orylines, and programming codes related to the FoA game. Such  a community will enrich student learning with dive rse inputs and foster collaborations extending beyond f ormal classroom learning. This project represents a  forwardthinking approach to education that integrates art,  storytelling, and technology to equip students wit h the skills and mindset necessary to navigate and influence the 21st-century world.
