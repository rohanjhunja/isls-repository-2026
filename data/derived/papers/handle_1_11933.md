# A Multi-Agent System (MAS)-Based Tool to Support Novice Teachers in Knowledge Building Pedagogy

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study introduces a Multi-Agent System (MAS)-based tool to support novice teachers in Knowledge Building (KB) pedagogy. By addressing challenges in lesson planning and student-driven learning, the tool enhances teaching confidence and KB alignment. Empirical findings confirm its effectiveness while identifying areas for improvement, such as interface design and resource integration.

Research background and questions Knowledge Building (KB) pedagogy promotes creative thinking and collaborative learning, grounded in principles like “real ideas, authentic problems” and “epistemic agency” (Scardamalia, 2006). However, these abstract principles pose challenges for novice teachers, including understanding KB theory, applying it in practice, and managing uncertainties in KB classrooms (Teo, 2 014; Chan, 2011). While tools like Knowledge Forum facilitate student collaboration (Chan, 2011), there is a lack of adaptive, user-friendly tools to support novice KB teachers, especially in resource-constrained, high-stakes public school environments (Tan, 2023). Gene rative AI (GenAI) and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) show potential in personalized learning and feedback (Chen et al., 2023), yet their applications in supporting teaching practices remain underexplored. This study introduces a MASbased tool to support novice KB teachers in overcom ing challenges related to understanding KB principl es and designing effective lessons. The research investigates:

• Does the MAS tool enhance novice teachers' self-efficacy in implementing KB pedagogy?

• Which specific design features of the MAS tool are most effective in supporting teachers?

This study contributes by proposing and validating a MAS tool that simplifies KB implementation for novice teachers and identifying key design features that enhance teacher support through interviews. • Content Generation. This module utilizes a collection of autonomous a gents. Each agent operates

independently while cooperating to achieve shared g oals. After teachers provide course objectives and key inputs, a virtual mentor agent generates output s such as problem scenarios, contextual background knowledge, and structured activities. Virtual stude nt agents simulate group discussion, generating diverse perspectives and scaffolding strategies to support collaborative processes. Additionally, a product expert agent offers guidance on creating tangible learning artifacts.

• Evaluation. Agents trained on KB 12 principles will evaluate lesson plans against each KB principle,

offering targeted, constructive feedback to refine designs. Teachers can upload existing lesson plans for evaluation and receive tailored recommendations to enhance quality.

• Support Modules. This module combines retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and large language

models (e.g., GPT-4, Llama3) to access public repositories (e.g., cur riculum standards) and localized resources (e.g., school-specific materials). These technologies ensure plans are contextually relevant and authoritative, while also enabling tailored outputs for diverse teaching needs.

To address the dynamic and emergent nature of KB classrooms, the tool integrates a dynamic adjustment mechanism and an iterative interaction mechanism. The former mechanism allows teachers to input real-time classroom feedback, enabling agents to modify lesso n plans accordingly. The latter mechanism allows teachers to engage in multiple rounds of dialogue with agents to refine outputs progressively, minimizing potential biases and ensuring precise alignment with instructional needs.

## Empirical study design and results

Since September, the MAS-based tool has been piloted in a large public schoo l, with six teachers (four novice and two experienced) participating in semi-structured interviews. Interview questions, adapted  from Woolfolk and Hoy’s (1990) Teacher Efficacy Scale and the KB 12 principles, explored teacher self-efficacy, tool usability, and alignment with KB pedagogy.  Findings revealed that this tool enhanced novice teachers’ confidence in adopting KB pedagogy by addressing critical challenges, such as guiding student-driven discussions and aligning lessons with KB principles. Teachers highlighted the tool's dynamic adjustments, which they found alleviated planning stress and enhanced the quality of discuss ions. Additionally, the framework generation streamlined planning, enhancing focus and efficiency.  However, teachers identified several areas for impr ovement. They emphasized the need for version control to support iterative lesson design, better integration with te xtbooks and external platforms, and a more intuitive interface to facilitate transitions between different planning stages. On conclusion, d espite these limitations, the tool demonstrated its  potential to alleviate common challenges faced by novice KB teachers, providing p ractical and innovative support for implementing KB pedagogy.
