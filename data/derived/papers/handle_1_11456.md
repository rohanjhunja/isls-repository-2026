# Enhancing Student Metacognitive Thinking and Content Learning in Chinese and Science Classrooms with Generative AI Chatbots

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study explores the practical application of a chatbot driven in Chinese and Science classes, focusing on cultivating students' metacognitive thinking and guiding students' subject learning. The chatbot embedded prompts are designed to stimulate students' inquiry skills, guide subjective learning, and metacognitive thinking. At the same time, teachers have designed supplementary prompts to provide scaffolding support for students' interactions. This two-tiered design approach ensures that Fairbot can both serve as an effective learning assistant and align with subject-specific teaching frameworks. The study involved 94 fifth-grade primary school students and two teachers, and the data sources included classroom observations, teacher reflections and student feedback. The findings show that Fairbot demonstrates its potential in supporting reflective thinking, teaching guidance, and text comprehension. This study highlights the potential of AI tools in classroom while pointing out the importance of integrating AI capabilities with instructional strategies through collaborative instructional design.

## Introduction

Implementing generative artificial intelligence (AI) in education brings en ormous transformative potential for customized instructional designs. Customized teachi ng chatbots, as emerging technology tools, have att racted instructors' attention to providing students instan t feedback, supporting differentiated learning need s, and facilitating deep investigation (Kuhail et al., 202 3; Tan et al., 2023). However, their effective application in solving domain-specific challenges remains underexplored. In parti cular, there is insufficient understanding of designing generative AI-driven chatbots to cultivate vital capacities such as critical thinking, creativity, and metacognitive abilities. Furthermore, introducing c hatbots into a classroom setting is still challengi ng, students often attempt to get answers directly and struggle to use effecti ve prompts to interact with chatbots effectively, and let alone evaluate chatbot responses. Teachers also face difficulties integrating AI tools into existing courses while maintaining teaching goals. Thus, this paper attempts t o address these challenges by presenting insights from teachers designing and implementing a Generative AI-driven chatbot, namely Fairbot, into domain-specific lectures to help students understand content and de velop metacognitive skills.  Knowledge Building is the theoretical framework that guides the design of the chatbot and lesson plan of this study (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 1991; Teo & Tan, 2023). The prompts engineering for the  Fairbot overall flow and reflective section focus on integrating key principles of Knowledge Building to  promote inquiry learning and metacognitive thinkin g (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 2006). Three key principle s guide the d esign process: Epistemic Agency, Improvable Ideas, and Rise Above (Scardamalia & Bereiter, 1991; 2006). Epistemic agency reinforces student-driven inquiry so that students' activity explores opportunities to advance the knowledge building work in the classroom (Miller et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2022). The Improvable Ideas stress that ideas can be improved through iteration, encouraging students to constantly revie w and revise their understanding based on feedback and new information input. Rise Above encourages students to actively reflect, monitor, a nd evaluate their understanding of the current state, identifying cognitive gaps and refining inquiry directions or strategies.

## Study design

Fairbot is a generative artificial intelligence chatbot based on based on the ChatGPT-3.5 large language model. It was developed with three primary functions, each fo cusing on a core subject area —Chinese, Math, and Science (Figure 1). Over a year of design, stakeholders from a local university, AI platform technicians, and teachers from a primary school collaborated to co-design Fairbot’s structure and prompt flow. The ove rall pedagogical design consists of two layers: the first layer is the prom pt engineering embedded in Fairbot, and the second layer is the prompts designed by the teacher to guide students t o interact with the Chatbot efficiently. Each subje ct area’s chatbot interactions were aligned with a specific i nstructional framework and unique teaching context.  For instance, in the subject of Chinese, the chatbot in teraction design was combined with the framework of  Feeling, Action, Speech, and thoughts (FAST) to guide studen ts in their Chinese writing and added the metacogni tive sections to facilitate students’ metacognitive reflection at the end of dialogue flow. The science subject was mainly guided by knowledge-building principles structure the science module. F ollowing multiple rounds of testing and design sections, teachers learned about Fairbot’s m ain features. They collaborated with researchers t o co-design lesson plans aligned with their curriculum goals and the integration of Chatbot into the curriculum. T he Fairbot prompt was crafted to match the unique subject and cognitive demands of the subject area, aiming to st imulate diverse ideas and foster cognitively active learnin g interactions. For instance, instead of giving a d irect answer, the Fairbot was designed to ask students understanding and questions to provoke deeper thinking before providing direct instant answers. Moreover, teachers designed preset prompts to support student interactions with the Fairbot at the early stage, such as “Based on the article p rovided, where did the Inuit live?” Due to the spac e and time constraints, this study will focus on designing and implementing Chinese and Science subjects. ## Participants

The participants involved 94 fifth-grade primary school students and two subject teachers. The students were from science class (42 students) and Chinese class (52 students). Each student has a laptop to use to interact with Fairbot in class. The Chinese language class students were selected based on their high readiness to learn and explored in depth through reading comprehension activities. All  participating students have sufficient information  and communication technology (ICT) skills to use Fairbo t for learning activities. The two subject teachers are experienced in their respective fields and have dem onstrated innovative abilities in integrating ICT a nd new technologies into teaching practice. The course des ign was further refined through collaboration with six other senior subject teachers, who worked together to design and revise teaching plans. Data and analysis The data collected for this study includes classroo m observations, video recordings, student chatbot l og data, teacher teaching plandocuments, and students' pre-and post-class questionnaires. 1) Teachers' teaching plandocuments and post-lesson reflectio ns were analyzed to examine how teachers designed t he class and implemented chatbot in their curriculum. The coding  process of teacher reflections started by inviting  Chinese and science teachers to record their reflections after teaching the lessons. The reflection includes aspects of lesson design, teaching strategies, and challenges faced d uring the design implementation process. The resear chers cleaned the data and conducted a thematic coding an alysis (Gibbs, 2008). Through multiple iterative re views of these reflections, salient ideas and patterns were identified and systematically coded based on shared  conceptual and design considerations. 2) To investigate the le arning experiences of students with Fairbot, their post-class questionnaire feedback was collected to analyze their learning experience using Fairbot. The feedback focused on questions about students' learning experiences with Fairbot to gather their opinions on its support and effectiveness during the learning process.

## Findings

RQ1: How do teachers reflect on the design and implementation of Fairbot-supported lessons and perceive its benefits for students’ learning during the planning process? The analysis of teacher reflections identified five  key themes illustrating how Fairbot supported stud ent learning (Table 1). Teachers emphasized that pre-designed prompts helped students effectively interact with Fairbot at the beginning, while critical e valuation of the chatbot responses fostered self-assessment and critical thinking. Moreover, collaboration was enhanced through peer feedback on Padlet, supported by Fairbot's iterative guidance, and its scaffolding promoted metacognitive skills b y encouraging reflective inquiry with provoking gui ding questions to expand the conversation. Intending to design Fairbot as a learning assistant, teacher ref lections indicate that Fairbot meets expectatio ns in providing subject-specific support —helping Chinese students with reading comprehension tasks and science students wi th experiment project planning, research question formulation, and data interpretation.

RQ2: How do students reflect on and perceive their experience using Fairbot for learning? Qualitative analysis of Chinese student feedback ab out using Fairbot to learn Inuit texts revealed fou r main patterns: gaining cultural knowledge  (76%), improving text comprehension and summary skills (12%), and supporting vocabulary and language learning (12%). Some feedback was multi-coded during the coding process; one piece of feedback may apply to multiple coding categories simultaneously. Some students reflected that using Fairbot increased their understanding of Inuit lifestyles. Moreover, other students shared unique insights that they gained through the interaction, which is beyond the  curriculum requirements met, such as the practical ity of hut entrance designs. Other students emphasized that Fa irbot helped them summarize the text and understand  it s structure through guided frameworks, such as analyz ing the content from different perspectives. A few students mentioned that Fairbot supported their vocabulary l earning and helped them master and understand new vocabulary in the text. However, it is sti ll worth mentioning that a small percentage of resp onses (6%) indicated that Fairbot did not help them learn much new information but only "helped with understanding the vocabulary in the passage," highlighting the variability in its effects.

## Discussion and conclusion

This study reported an innovative Gen AI-supported Fairbot and its pedagogical implementatio n in Science and Chinese. The results demonstrate its potential in p romoting students' content learning, idea explorati on, and reflection. The Fairbot prompt engineering focuses on embedding structured prompts so that they can effectively function as a learning assistant to guide subject-specific learning. Results of the analysis show tha t chatbots can provide guidance, inspire critical thinking, and pr omote deep learning. With the guidance of the speci fic pedagogical design with improvable ideas, rather th an answering questions directly, Fairbot encourages  a more interactive and reflective learning process by prompting students to reflect, clarify, and expand their understanding. The teacher-designed customized prompts played an important rol e in helping students interact with Fairbot effectively, especially when students were new to F airbot. The results of teacher reflections highligh ted the effectiveness of the current pedagogical design in improving student engagement and subject learning. However, Fairbot was designed to use guiding questions to in crease students' engagement and avoid giving direct  answers immediately, some students felt challenged when the answers were not immediately pr ovided. This insight provides an opportunity to further improve Fairbot with adaptive features, such as tailored hints, to balance guidance and independence. These insights provide p otential innovative design and support for student engagement in meaningful ways. In sum, this innovat ive pedagogy paved the way for using Gen AI with pedagogical designs and lesson implementation.
