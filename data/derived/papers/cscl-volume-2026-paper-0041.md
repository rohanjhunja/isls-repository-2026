# Teachers’ Evolving Sentiments and Competencies about Generative AI in Social Media Discourse: A Case Study on X

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines teachers’ evolving sentiments and competencies regarding Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) by analyzing teacher-authored posts on X (formerly Twitter) in 2023 and 2025. Combining qualitative content analysis with automated and manual sentiment analysis, the research is guided by the UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Teachers (AI CFT) and Zhai’s teacher agency framework. The study contributes to the CSCL discourse by framing X as a space for informal, collaborative learning. Within this environment, educators share experiential insights and articulate evolving visions of GenAI as a collaborator in their pedagogical practice and professional devel opment. Findings indicate a gradual shift toward more positive and practice-oriented engagement, though concerns about agency, accountability, and professional responsibility remain. A significant link between sentiment and competency suggests that emotional stance shapes readiness to adopt GenAI. The study informs professional development strategies aligned with AI CFT curricular goals, promoting ethical and human-centered GenAI integration in education.

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) refers to deep learning models that generate original content based on patterns learned from large datasets (Sengar et al., 2025). Since the release of ChatGPT in 2022, its use in education has sparked both enthusiasm and concern. Teachers highlight its potential for personalized learning and efficiency, while raising issues related to critical thinking, problem-solving, and academic integrity (Ogurlu & Mossholder, 2023; Söderström et al., 2024). These developments are also reshaping teachers’ roles and increasing demand for professional development, though how teachers’ perspectives and human–AI collaboration practices have evolved remains unclear (Alkhasawneh et al., 2025; Cress & Kimmerle, 2023). In this context, as during the COVID-19 pandemic (Beardsley et al., 2021), some teachers have turned to social media as  CSCL spaces for informal, collaborative learning about GenAI. While survey studies show mixed attitudes toward GenAI, they are often limited in scope (DeVito et al., 2025). Platforms such as X (formerly Twitter) provide a large-scale and relatively unfiltered  view of how teachers discuss and negotiate these technologies (Bruguera et al., 2019), building on its established role in peer learning and resource sharing (Orlanda-Ventayen & Ventayen, 2017; van Bommel et al., 2020). Recent studies indicate that teachers express both optimism and concern about ChatGPT, particularly regarding creativity, efficiency, plagiarism, and institutional pressures, with discourse becoming more nuanced over time (DeVito et al., 2025; Mamo et al., 2024; Naing & Udomwong, 2024).

This study analyzes teachers’ evolving discourse on X in 2023 and 2025, combining sentiment analysis with two complementary theoretical perspectives: the UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Teachers  (AI CFT; UNESCO, 2024) and Zhai's (2024) teacher agency  framework. The AI CFT  defines three progression levels—Acquire  (awareness), Deepen (application and reflection), and Create  (innovation and leadership) — operationalized through structured learning objectives (LOs)  that capture teachers’ development in knowledge, skills, and values. Although the framework includes five dimensions, this study focuses on the Human-Centered Mindset, as its LOs  most directly reflect teachers’ beliefs, attitudes, and values toward GenAI as expressed in discourse. Zhai’s framework conceptualizes teacher agency through four roles—Observer, Adopter, Collaborator, and Innovator—highlighting evolving engagement and pedagogical integration while emphasizing that agency is dynamic and shaped by contextual factors.

Together, these frameworks provide a multidimensional lens to examine how teachers’ sentiments, competencies, and roles evolve. From a CSCL perspective, GenAI is not only a tool but also a potential collaborator that reshapes agency and expertise in hybrid learning environments (Hernández -Leo et al., 2025). Accordingly, this study addresses  the following research questions: (1) How have teachers’ sentiments toward GenAI shifted in 2023 and 2025, as expressed in their discourse on X (formerly Twitter)?; (2) How do teachers’ competencies, particularly within the Human-Centered Mindset dimension of the AI CFT, emerge and evolve in their social media discourse?; (3) How do these evolving sentiments and competencies reflect teachers’ agency as conceptualized in Zhai’s (2024) framework (Observer, Adopter, Collaborator, and Innovator)?; and (4) What 299 professional development orientations emerge from aligning AI CFT learning objectives  with Zhai’s teacher roles?

This study draws on two datasets of social media posts retrieved from the Kaggle platform (Quaranta et al., 2021). To capture educators’ discourse on GenAI, posts were filtered to include: (a) English-language content; (b) educator accounts identified through biography keywords (e.g., teacher, educator, professor); (c) education-related terms (e.g., student, class, school); and (d) references to ChatGPT as a proxy for GenAI, reflecting its prominence as the most widely recognized tool in public discourse. W hile this anchors the dataset in widely recognized discourse, it also limits the analysis to ChatGPT-related discussions. After filtering and deduplication, the dataset comprised 130 posts from 100 teachers in 2023 and 261 posts from 219 teachers in 2025, totaling 20,979 associated comments.

Teacher comments were coded using the Human-Centered Mindset dimension of the UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Teachers (AI CFT; UNESCO, 2024). A coding scheme based on its learning objectives (LOs) across the three progression levels—Acquire, Deepen, and Create—was applied. Each comment was independently coded by two trained coders in ATLAS.ti, a qualitative data analysis software (Hwang, 2007), using the framework’s hierarchical notation (LO.X.Y.Z), where X denotes the dimension, Y the progression level, and Z the specific learning objective. Inter-coder reliability was ensured through independent coding followed by reconciliation discussions until consensus was reached. To capture the affective dimension of teachers’ engagement with GenAI, a triangulated sentiment Reasoner), a rule-based model optimized for analyzing the informal and concise language typical of social media (Hutto, 2025; C. Hutto & Gilbert, 2014), and RoBERTa (Robustly Optimized BERT Pretraining Approach), a transformer-based deep learning model capable of capturing more nuanced semantic and contextual patterns in text (Y. Liu et al., 2019), alongside manual classification. All comments were categorized as positive, neutral, or negative. Manual coding was prioritized in cases of disagreement between automated methods to capture contextual nuances such as sarcasm and irony.

Teachers’ sentiments were analyzed alongside their AI-related competencies by linking each comment to relevant UNESCO AI CFT learning objectives (e.g., LO.1.1.1) and labeling it as positive, neutral, or negative. The datasets were merged in Python to produce a contingency table showing the distribution of learning objectives across sentiment categories. A Pearson’s  chi-square test of independence (α = 0.05), implemented using the scipy.stats.chi2_contingency function in Python, was conducted to assess whether sentiment and competency levels were significantly associated with one another. To examine teacher agency, Zhai’s (2024) framework (Observer, Adopter, Collaborator, and Innovator) was applied to a filtered subset of 2025 comments previously coded at AI CFT Level 2 (Deepen). From this subset, 64 comments containing explicit references to teachers’ practices, attitudes, or use of GenAI were purposively selected and manually classified according to Zhai’s  roles. This process linked competency development with role progression and enabled a more nuanced understanding of teachers’ engagement. By integrating the AI CFT’s competency structure (Acquire, Deepen, Create) with Zhai’s  socio-psychological perspective, the analysis captures both competency progression and evolving teacher roles in practice. Finally, comments were coded according to AI CFT learning objectives, which were then linked to their corresponding curricular goals and aligned with Zhai’s roles, forming a learning objective–curricular goal–role framework. This alignment captures competency progression alongside role-specific support needs and informs training implications. Coding was conducted independently by two researchers and subsequently reconciled through discussion to ensure reliability.

Comments reflecting on the benefits and limitations of GenAI persisted, indicating sustained foundational awareness among teachers. References to limitations increased slightly (9.2% to 10%), suggesting a shift toward more critical evaluation of tool functionalities. Meanwhile, references to human agency and the notion that AI is human-led declined markedly (10% to 3%), and discussions of human rights implications nearly disappeared. This pattern indicates a shift at this introductory level from foundational ethical awareness toward more practical, tool-oriented considerations.

However, engagement with institutional and polic y-level dimensions remains limited. Although references to human supervision (LO.1.2.1) appear, broader considerations of regulatory frameworks and teacher accountability are largely absent, indicating a persistent gap in governancerelated discourse. At the third level of the AI CFT  framework (Create, LO.1.3), the data reveal  a shift from general reflection toward more analytical engagement. While 50% of teachers’ comments focused on reflecting on GenAI’s educational implications in 2023, this proportion declined to 27.2% in 2025, as attention to critically evaluating those implications increased to 14.9%. Despite a slight rise in attention to mitigating educational risks, broader dimensions—such as GenAI’s role in the workplace and its impact on citizenship development —remain limited. This pattern suggests that while teachers are moving toward higher-order engagement, important gaps persist in addressing social responsibility and policy-related aspects of GenAI adoption. Sentiment analysis by AI CFT subcodes The sentiment distribution across AI CFT  subcodes reveals a clear association between pedagogical application and emotional stance. Subcodes focused on concrete practice, such as methodology design (LO.1.2.4) and risk mitigation (LO.1.3.2), were predominantly positive, suggesting increased teacher confidence in responsible integration. In contrast, LO.1.2.3 (encroachment and resistance) remained a primary source of negative sentiment, reflecting persistent concerns about the displacement of human functions. More abstract subcodes, such as AI authorship (LO.1.1.2) and general educational implications (LO.1.3.1), showed higher levels of neutral sentiment, 301 indicating a more descriptive or cautious stance.  Pearson’s chi-square tests confirmed a significant association between sentiment and subcodes in both 2023 (χ² = 55.02, p <.001) and 2025 (χ² = 98.74, p <.001). This finding suggests that teacher attitudes vary by competency type, with stronger emotional responses linked to practical applications than to abstract considerations.

Observers are primarily linked to LO.1.2.3, reflecting limited trust and apprehension regarding AI encroachment. Adopters align with human supervision (LO.1.2.1) and tool accountability (LO.1.2.4), demonstrating a cautious, exploratory approach. In contrast, Collaborators are strongly associated with the design of AI-enhanced methodologies (LO.1.2.4), exhibiting high confidence and positive sentiment. The absence of Innovators  suggests that transformative co-creation has not yet materialized. This integrated framework suggests that emotional responses influence role progression, highlighting the need for differentiated professional development strategies tailored to the specifi c curricular and attitudinal needs of each teacher profile.

## Implications for teachers’ training orientation

Aligning AI CFT learning objectives, curricular goals, and Zhai’s roles (2025 Deepen level) reveals differentiated training needs. Comments linked to LO.1.2.3  were associated with Observers, who display caution and limited trust, indicating a need for foundational literacy, ethical framing, and peer exploration. LO.1.2.1 aligned with Adopters, who engage in cautious experimentation and benefit from practical use cases, mentoring, and supportive infrastructure. LO.1.2.4 was most often linked to Collaborators, who show readiness to integrate GenAI and benefit from co-design opportunities and dialogue on student agency.  Overall, the findings highlight three key priorities for professional development: (a) the irreplaceable human role in education, (b) the importance of active supervision in GenAI adoption, and (c) the risks of over-reliance on GenAI tools without a deep understanding of their limitations. This alignment of AI CFT curricular goals with role-based development provides a structured, evidence-informed approach to designing differentiated training that is responsive to teachers’ evolving needs. This study examines the evolution of teacher discourse on X from 2023 to 2025, revealing a pedagogical shift from initial skepticism toward more practice-oriented engagement with GenAI (DeVito et al., 2025; Ogurlu & Mossholder, 2023). While year-to-year sentiment trends show modest growth in positive  attitudes and a decline in neutral and negative stances, educators appear to be shifting  from novelty toward more situated pedagogical uses (Mamo et al., 2024; Naing & Udomwong, 2024). Mapping these shifts to the UNESCO AI CFT  and Zhai’s teacher agency framework shows that while the Acquire level discussions remain stable, the Deepen  and Create levels increasingly emphasize critical evaluation and risk-aware design. However, a persistent gap remains in policy and institutional accountability, reflecting prior findings that tool adoption often outpaces engagement with ethical frameworks (DeVito et al., 2025; UNESCO, 2024). The integration of these frameworks clarifies how knowledge and attitudes evolve: Observers  express resistance related to human replacement, Adopters  engage in cautious experimentation focused on supervision, and Collaborators prioritize human-centered methodologies. The absence of Innovators  suggests that transformative co-creation remains emergent and context-dependent (Hernández-Leo et al., 2025). Furthermore, the statistically significant association between sentiment and AI CFT subcodes indicates that positive affect aligns with confident, human-centered design, while negative sentiment reflects concerns about professional displacement.

These findings have important implications for teacher training. Following UNESCO’s curricular goals for the Human-Centered Mindset dimension, professional development should: (a) reinforce the non-substitutable role of humans in educational processes, (b) model “human-in-the-loop” supervision, and (c) address the potential risks of relying heavily on GenAI tools without critically understanding their limitations  (Ogurlu & Mossholder, 2023). Support should be role-sensitive: Observers require foundational l iteracy and ethical framing; Adopters benefit from practice-based mentoring and infrastructure; and Collaborators benefit from co-design opportunities and dialogue on student agency. This differentiated approach supports the balance between opportunities and risks while strengthening teacher agency (Hernández-Leo et al., 2025). Limitations include the potential overrepresentation of vocal users on X, a narrow focus on ChatGPT, and the challenges of modeling sentiment in short texts.
