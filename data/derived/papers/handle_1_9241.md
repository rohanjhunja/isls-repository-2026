# Inside the Black Box of Student Interaction in Collaborative Source-Based Writing: The Development of a Coding Scheme

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Collaborative source-based writing has been shown to be an often used and effective way for university students to acquire content knowledge, collaboration skills, and writing competencies. The outcomes of collaborative source-based writing processes depend on the interactions between the group members. However, these interactions remain underexplored. As a first step to uncover interactions, the development of a coding scheme to map interactions among collaborative source-based writing students is presented in this poster.

## Introduction

University students are often required to engage in collaborative source-base d writing for three reasons. First, they learn about a topic by reading information from sources, select the relevant pieces, and integrate them into a new, meaningful text (Spivey & King, 1989). Second, they learn from each other, in terms of collaboration skills, writing strategies, and content, as they can pool information (Damşa, 2014; Storch, 2005). This leads to the third reason: due to sharing insights, collaborating students may write higher quality texts than individual students (Storch, 2005).

A collaborative writing process leads to various outcomes, the most straightfor ward one being the collaboratively written text. All outcomes are highly determined by what happens during the process, and more in specific the interactions between students. Not all interaction promotes collaborative learning (Damşa, 2014). This is, however, often a black box. Future studies should investigate the inter actions that occur during collaborative source-based writing processes. The first step should be answering the research question “How can interactions between collaboratively source-based writing university students be mapped?” We answer this question by means of the development of a data and theory based coding scheme, as elaborated below.

## Methods: Coding scheme development process

A coding scheme was developed based on both the literature on productive interactions in collaborative learning and writing instruction as well as authentic interactions between collaboratively source-based writing university students. The coding scheme de veloped by Damşa (2014) served as a starting point but was adapted to map interactions during a collaborative source-based writing process. The coding scheme consequently went through five developmental steps.

First, data from a previous study (Putzeys & De Wever, 2021) in which two groups of three university students synchronously and collaboratively wrote a source-based text during three hours, using Google Docs and MS Teams, was used. Students’ interactions were recorded and transcribed. Second, we demarcated units of meaning (UoM) in these transcripts, which w ere used as the units of analysis, and are to be seen as a set of consecutive expressions by one or more students aimed at the same goal. Third, using the coding scheme based on Damşa (2014), each UoM was assigned one code at the lowest level of the coding scheme (i.e., statement). This coding scheme has a th ree-tiered hierarchical structure, which will be detailed on the poster. Theory on writing strategies and collaborative writing was integrated into the coding scheme in the form of new statements. Also, when the existing codes did not capture what was happening within the UoM, a new code was added to the scheme. In this way, both theory and da ta were used to create our adaptations. After iterative coding, nonew codes emerged from the data. This led to coding scheme version 1. Fourth, version 1 was adapted to another data collection in a similar way. This time, four groups of three university students synchronously wrote a longer source-based text over the course of eight sessions of 2,5 hours each using Google Docs and MS Teams. The entire process was recorded and transcribed. The researcher demarcated UoM in 10% of the transcriptions and coded these using version 1. Due to the different nature and length of the task, some new codes emerged and were added to version 1, resulting in the final coding scheme. Fifth, a trained coder segmented and coded all transcriptions using the final coding scheme, which was supplemented with an extra column containing descriptions of each statement. ## Implications, limitations and suggestions for further research

This coding scheme is a data and theory based tool that helps to decode the black box of student interactions. The development of this scheme impacts further research, as it allows for  interactions among collaboratively writing students to be analyzed. The coding scheme is comprehensive and versatile: re searchers candecide how finegrained their data should be coded. This coding scheme, like all others, is not free of limitations. Coders must be trained before they can use the coding scheme. Furthermore, the coding scheme was designed for coding source-based writing processes, and may therefore not be applicable to all text types. However, changes can easily be made. The coding scheme can be used in further research in many ways. For e xample, patterns within interaction processes could be explored, and the relationship with other variable s, such as students’ perceptions of collaborative writing and task quality, could be investigated.
