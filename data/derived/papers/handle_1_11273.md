Abstract: This study explores the potential of eye-tracking for evaluating learning experiences in video-based online courses. Eye movements from 26 student s were recorded using a Tobii T120 device, yielding fixation, saccade, and pupil-diameter features. Six machine learning models, including Random Forest (RF) and Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA), were trained via five-fold cross-validation. RF achieved the highest accuracy, demon strating robust predictive capabilities. Correlation analyses indic ated th at focusing on core content regions, maintaining stable visual distances, and exhibiting  moderate pupil dilation were positively associated with improved learning experiences, whil e peripheral distractions and frequent attention shifts negatively impacted engagement. These findings underscore the feasibili ty of integrating eye-tracking data into online learning platforms to enable personalized feedback and optimize interface design.

## Introduction

As technology advances, online learning platforms have become an integral part of higher education, with videobased learning playing a critical role (Abad et al., 2020). However, significant disparities in studen ts' learning experiences in video courses have led to challenges  such as low completion rates and insufficient enga gement (Chakraborty & Muyia Nafukho, 2014; Shapiro et al.,  2017). Online learning experience refers to studen ts' psychological perceptions and emotional responses t o the learning environment and outcomes (Davis & Wo ng, 2007; Katuk et al., 2013). Traditional assessment m ethods, such as surveys and interviews, exhibit not able limitations (Sharpe et al., 2006). These approaches  fail to provide real-time monitoring of video-based learning, necessitating intelligent assessment techniques bas ed on objective data. Eye-tracking technology, with its real-time, non-intrusive, and high-precision capabilities, has emerged as a promising tool for evaluating learning experiences (Wang et al., 2020; Ke et al., 2024). This study focuses on video-based online learning environments and aims to address two key research questions: RQ1: To what extent can machine learning models based on eyetracking data evaluate students' learning experienc es? RQ2: Which ey e-tracking features are significantly correlated with learners' learning experiences?

## Measures

A framework was designed in this study to enable in telligent evaluation of online video learning exper iences through eye-tracking data. The framework is composed of four es sential components: data collection, feature extraction, dataset creation, and machine learning model construction (see Figure 1).

## Data collection

A total of 26 students from Shanghai, China, were recruited for data collection. The data collection lasted for one week in March 2024 and involved six 10-minute video lessons. Eye movements were captured t hroughout the experiment using a Tobii T120 eye tracker with a sampling frequency of 60 Hz, ensuring the accuracy and stability of the data. After viewing the videos, participants  completed the NPS and UEQ-S questionnaires (Reichheld, 2003). In total, 739,826 eye-tracking data points were collected, providing a co mprehensive dataset for the development and refinement of the analytical model.

Feature extraction To analyze learners' visual engagement, we employed  an Areas of Interest (AOI) segmentation method, in spired by Zhang et al. (2018) and Chen et al. (2023), using a two-tier approach. First, based on the layout of the learning materials, the screen was divided into four quadrants: top-left (x < W/2, y < H/2), top-right (x ≥ W/2, y < H/2), bottom-left (x < W/2, y ≥ H/2), and bottom-right (x ≥ W/2, y ≥ H/2). Here, (x) and (y) represent the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the gaze point, respect ively, while (W) and (H) denote the screen's width and height. Building on this global quadrant segmentation, the screen was further divided into central and peripheral regions (Faraji et al., 2023) to enable a more detailed ana lysis of learning behaviors. The central regions (l abeled (m3, m4, m5, m6)) were defined as the areas closest to the center of each quadrant, while the peripheral regions (labeled (r1, r2, r7, r8)) were identified as the areas near  the edges of each quadrant. Finally, 30 eye-tracking features related to fixation, saccade, and pupil data were extracted from these segmented regions (see Table 1). Machine learning model development Six commonly used machine learning algorithms were employed to build the learning experience assessmen t models: decision tree (DT), support vector machine (SVC), random forest (RF), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), linear discriminant analysis  (LDA), and logistic regression  (LR). Model performance was evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score metrics (Yacouby & Axman, 2020; Belavagi & Mu niyal, 2016). To mitigate overfitting, a 5-fold stratified cross-validation protocol was applied, ensuring the train ing set remai ned representative of the overall dataset. Performance metrics were averaged over five iterations for cons istency and reliability (Tsamardinos et al., 2015).

## Results

RQ1: To what extent can machine learning models, bas ed on eye-tracking data, effectively assess students' learning experiences?

To address this question, several commonly used mac hine learning models were employed to evaluate thei r performance in predicting students' learning experiences based on eye-tracking data (see Table 2). Among these models, RF demonstrated the best overall performance, achieving an accuracy of 0.9000, a precision of 0.8500, a recall of 0.9000, and an F1-score of 0.8667. This indicates that the RF model exhibits strong predictive capabilities and a well-balanced performance in assessing learning experiences using eye-tracking data. LDA also performed well, with precision (0.8148) and F1-score (0.8444) closely approaching those of the RF model. In contrast, SVC and KNN showed relatively weaker performance, with lower precision and recall scores. Overall, machine learning models based on eye-tracking data effectively assessed students' learning experiences, with RF emerging as the most robust model in terms of predictive accuracy and balanced performance. This study examined the correlation between eye-tracking features and learning experience (see Figure 2). The findings revealed significant positive and negative relationships between learning experience and features such as fixation count, switch count, pupil diameter, eye p osition, and visual distance. Specifically, learnin g experience declined when learners focused on peripheral areas devoid of teacher presence or learning content (e.g., r1_ratio, r2_ratio, r7_ratio), whereas attention to core cont ent and  teacher interaction zones (e.g., m3_ratio, m5_ratio, m6_ratio, r8_ratio) significantly enhanced their ex perience. Notably, m6_ratio and r8_ratio exhibited strong positive correlations, suggesting that prolonged fo cus on teacher-interaction visuals contrib utes to improved learning experiences(Desai & Kulkarni, 2022).Switch  count demonstrated a negative correlation with lea rning experience, indicating that frequent switching betw een regions may reflect unstable attention and nega tively impact learning outcomes. Variations in pupil diame ter (mean_pupil, std_pupil) were positively associa ted with learning experience, highlighting the role of focus ed attention and task engagement in fostering posit ive experiences. Conversely, vertical eye position shif ts (e.g., eye_pos_left_y_mean) negatively correlate d with learning experience, suggesting that frequent verti cal eye movements increase cognitive load and reduc e efficiency(Bonnet et al., 2013). Regarding visual distance, e xcessive screen distance (distance_right_me an) was found to hinder the learning experience, while stab le distances (distance_left_std) appeared to enhanc e learning outcomes.Finally, no significant correlations were observed between learning experience and fixation duration or saccade count, suggesting that attention span and information acquisition frequency have limited influence on the overall learning experience.

## Discussion

This study explores the potential of eye-tracking data for the intelligent evaluation of learning experiences. Among the six models developed, the RF model demonstrated the best performance, as reflected in its high precision and recall metrics. In addition to its predictive accu racy, RF provides feature importance analysis, offe ring valuable insights for optimizing instructional content and l earning platforms. This model can be integrated int o online learning platforms to enable personalized recommend ations, real-time performance  monitoring, and interface optimization, ultimately enhancing learning outcomes and user experience. The analysis of eye-tracking features revealed deep insights into learn ers’ cognitive processes and engagement. For instance, the strong positive correlation between m6_ratio and r8_ratio with learning experience indicates that directing learners’ attention toward teacher interaction zones or key content holds significant value. This suggests that designing more interactive and engaging learni ng materials can effectively boost learner engageme nt and satisfaction. Conversely, the negative correlations  associ ated with peripheral areas like r1_ratio and r7_rat io underscore the disruptive effects of visual distrac tions, emphasizing the importance of minimizing irr elevant elements in interface design.The positive correlation between pupil diameter changes (mean_pupil and std_pupil) and learning experience further underscores the imp ortance of attentional focus and task engagement. P upil dilation, often linked to increased cognitive effort and interest, suggests that learning environments that stimulate such engagement may yield better outcomes.In contrast, excessi ve visual distances (distance_right_mean) and frequent vertical eye movements (eye_left_y_mean) n egatively impacted learning experiences, likely due  to increased cognitive load and reduced efficiency.

The study offers practical recommendations for inst ructional design: incorporating teacher interaction  elements, reducing visual distractions, optimizing visual distances, and maintaining key in formation within a consistent horizontal level. These strategies can significantly enhance learning outcomes and provide valuable guidance for the design and optimization of future intelligent learning platforms.
