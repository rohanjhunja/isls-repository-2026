# Educating for Worldmaking and Equitable Engagement in Makerspaces: A Case Study of Ukrainian Students Collaborative Knowledge Creation and Sharing in a Norwegian School

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines how collaborative hands-on educational practices in makerspaces can foster equitable engagement among immigrant students. We investigated students’ knowledge creation learning in makerspaces with a particular focus on; collaboration, communication, equal access to resources and opportunities among students of various backgrounds and culture. We found that Makerspaces as educational environments have a good potential to teach for equitable engagement.

Introduction: As urban schools become increasingly diverse, educa tion for equitable engagement is essential to ensur e that students from various socioeconomic and cultural ba ckgrounds can participate meaningfully in learning. Makerspaces provide a unique environment where communication can transcend language barriers, making them particularly valuable for immigrant students adapti ng tonew educational settings (Kumpulainen & Kajam aa, 2020; Heredia & Tan, 2021). In such spaces, technic al knowledge can be demonstrated rath er than explained verbally, allowing students with different linguistic backgrounds to collaborate effectively. This study highlights how Norwegian and Ukrainian students, despite not s haring a common language, engaged in productive knowledge exchange through hands-on making.

Maker-centered learning fosters collaboration in technolo gy-rich environments, where ideas and knowledge are constructed through iterative prototy ping. In this process, epistemic artifacts not only  drive learning but also serve as tools for communication,  enabling students to share knowledge through gestu res, material interactions, and joint problem-solving. This demonstrates that makerspaces are not  just learning environments but also social spaces that facilitate adaptation and integration for immigrant students. By providing an alternative mode of communication, these spaces help students develop a sense of belonging in new educational settings (Tan & Barton, 2018; Seo & Richard, 2021).   It can offer ways of breaking the ice between mem bers of groups that have formed outside the classroom.

Furthermore, hands-on collaboration in makerspaces enables students tonegotiate roles, build identities, and establish community connections beyond the clas sroom (Malin, 2012). As learners engage in making a nd interacting with artifacts, they develop crit ical and reflective dispositions through dynamic so cial interactions (Peppler et al., 2023). Catterall and Peppler (2007) emphasize that creating together fosters self-efficacy, particularly for marginalized groups, by reinforcing a positive sense of capability and achievement. In our study, we observed that the nature of hands-on collaboration in a makerspace not only facilitated knowledge sharing but also played a crucial role in helping immigrant students adapt to a new educational and social environment.

The study: The study took place in a Norwegian 7th-grade makerspace classroom (12-years old students) where twenty-five students worked on a project to create a board game using 3D printers and vinyl cutters. The focus of our research was on five Ukrainian students who, prior to this project, did not frequently collaborate with one another nor with their Norwegian classmates. We were particularly interested in how the relationships between the boys developed through the course of the project period of three weeks. We observed and documented the children while they were working on  the project. W e especially focused on their instances of collaboration, verbal  and physical interactions, and noted several types of other engagement levels.  We also conducted interviews with the five Ukranian students. The interview questions  focused on the students' reflections on their makerspace experience, perceived changes in peer relationships, and comparisons with their prior schooling in Ukraine. Discussion: The makerspace project provided Ukrainian students with equal access to resources, enabling them to ac tively participate in collaborative activities. Initially,  cultural differences in resource management were e vident: Ukrainian students sought to maximize their use of 3D printers, reflecting survival-oriented values shaped by their prior experiences. Norwegian peers, accustomed to shared resource use, adapted without conflict. Over time, the Ukrainian students began to share resources more eq uitably, demonstrating the makerspace's role in fos tering a democratic learning environment where diverse cultural practices can harmonize. The makerspace encouraged the Ukrainian students to engage in meaningful collabor ation, both within their group and with Norwegian peers. This enhanced  peer communication bridged social and cultural div ides, fostering a dynamic space where ideas and skills we re exchanged freely. Throug h hands-on activities, students integrated their prior knowledge with new technical skills, aligning with research on knowledge creation learning (Kajamaa et al., 2018). One student became particul arly adept at using the 3D pr inter, evolving into becoming a resource for his classmates and demonstrating the reciprocal nature of collaborative learning. By bringing their unique perspectives into the makerspace, Ukrainian students contributed to innovative and creative solutions, enriching the project outco mes. The process allowed them to see themselves as capable creators and innovators in STEAM, supporting their positive identity development. The makerspace evolved into a participatory space w here students from different cultural and linguisti c backgrounds could engage as equals. Ukrainian students, who initially refrained from initiating interactions, began collaborating openly with their Norwegian peers, gradually establishing cross-cultural connections. As noted by Nasir and Vakil (2017), such inclusive spaces candisrupt inequities in urban classrooms, fostering understanding and mutual respect.

Integrating digital tools, such as 3D printers and vinyl cutters, played a critical role in facilitati ng the collaborative learning process. These technologies supported the students in linking their existing knowledge with new skills, enabling innovative approaches to the p roject. The findings align with research emphasizin g the transformative potential of computer-supported collaborative learning in enriching educa tional experiences and promoting epistemic practices (Kajamaa & Kumpulaine n, 2020; Oswald & Zhao, 2021). Through equitable resource access, collaborative design, and the inte gration of technology, the makerspace served as a m icrocosm of participatory democracy, fostering not only know ledge creation but also social integration and mutu al respect among diverse student groups.

## Conclusion and future directions

Makerspaces present a powerful opportunity to foste r equitable engagement and positive identity develo pment among immigrant students, helping bridge cultural g aps and supporting democratic, inclusive learning s paces. The integration of equitable engagement principles i n urban education, particularly through the lens of makerspaces, not only enhances the learning experie nce for all students but also fosters a culture of inclusivity and innovation. By leveraging the diver se perspectives of students and creati ng environments that encourage collaboration and creativity, educators can cultiva te a more equitable educational landscape.  Further research is needed to explore specific design features of maker spaces that maximize equitable engagement and to ex amine long-term impacts on immigrant students’ identity development and STEAM interest.
