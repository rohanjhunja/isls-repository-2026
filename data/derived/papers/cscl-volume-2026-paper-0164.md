# Dropout and Success in Online Higher Education: A Comparative Analysis of Students in Brazil

**Conference:** CSCL 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Despite the significant growth of online higher education, challenges persist regarding how to foster engagement and address dropout rates. Therefore, personalized learning has been identified as bridges to success. This paper examines whether the design of reasonable accommodations based on personalized learning affects the success or dropout rates of students in undergraduate courses at an online higher education university in Brazil. By tabulating and analyzing the public database of student enrollment status before and after the design changes, we observed that personalized learning was associated with higher success rates and lower dropout rates, suggesting that Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and Personalized Learning (PL) can mutually benefit a diverse student population.

In many Global South nations, democratizing access to higher education has become a political priority, with online higher education (OHEs) institutions increasingly seen as a scalable way to expand enrollment (Flores et al., 2010; Chiroleu & Marquina, 2017). However, these institutions continue to face significant challenges, particularly high dropout rates in large-scale distance learning programs (Shaik & Asif, 2022; Yang et al., 2025) and, consequently, low graduation rates (Smaili et al., 2022). To address these issues, researchers and educators have explored personalized design approaches (Lin et al., 2024) to prevent at-risk students from dropping out (Smaili et al., 2022) and to improve educational outcomes (Heiman, 2006). In this context, Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and Personalized Learning (PL) frameworks have been applied to create environments that accommodate variability and provide multiple forms of engagement, gaining wide policy adoption in equityfocused education (Meyer et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2020). The integration of these modalities offers a strategy that benefits students with and without  Specific Educational Needs  (SEN) (Zhang et al., 2022), shifting focus from individual deficits to systemic barriers to learning (Sewell et al., 2022).  This perspective aligns with the Brazilian concept of SEN, which defines the target group for specialized educational services and guides the design of resources that remove barriers to student participation (Mantoan, 2003). Building on this, the study examines success and dropout rates among undergraduates at a public, tuitionfree OHE university in Brazil. Using data from the public database covering 2014-2023, we assessed whether Personalized Learning (PL) interventions introduced in 2018 influenced outcomes for students with and without SEN. With this, we ask: RQ1: What are the success and dropout rates of  students with (G1) and without (G2) SEN? RQ2: Did the adoption of PL-based design affect the success and dropout rates of students with SEN (G2) before and after 2018 ? Enrollment data from students, combined with historical events at the university, were analyzed to explain disparities.  Results show significant changes after PL implementation for both groups, especially for non-SEN students, who experienced a marked reduction in dropout rates alongside an increase in success rates. Nevertheless, the dropout levels observed warrant further specific studies, so that structural inequalities that continue to constrain equitable access, retention, and graduation in OHE institutions in Brazil can be more effectively addressed and contextualized.

## Context, collection and data analysis

The university, founded in Brazil in 2014 as a public and tuition-free OHE institution, has ensured since its inception that its Virtual Learning Environment (VLE) and test system comply with WCAG standards. Beginning in 2018, increasing accommodation requests from SEN students prompted system redesigns and ultimately led to the creation of an institutional committee dedicated to accessibility and in clusion. Data were obtained from the university’s public dashboard, which provides annual institutional information covering the period from 2014 to

2023. The enrollment status of 134,501 students was tabulated and categorized into two groups: G1 (N = 1,582),

referring to students with SEN, and G2 (N = 132,919), referring to students without SEN. Subsequently, the data 812 were proportionally compared using a two-proportion z-test. Next, applying two-proportion z-test, we compared data from both groups before and after the implementation of reasonable accommodations grounded in personalized learning, introduced in response to increasing student diversity following the expansion of university access. The personalized learning strategies requested by students and adopted by the university included granting extended exam time, early release of content, conducting remote exams (i nstead of in-person exams at the university), developing activities individually that would originally be collective, and improving captioning, sign language interpretation, audio description, and compatibility of the entire system with screen reader softw are. In this view, the interpretive process was interactive and involved correlating the findings with the institution’s historical context.

A first analysis was conducted on the existing data regarding students with SEN. Between 2014 and 2023, a total of 1,582 enrolments were recorded. The years  2018 and 2022 registered the highest number of SEN students, whereas the lowest numbers were observed between 2014 and 2016. Overall, 898 students were classified as having achieved academic success, while 684 were unable to continue their studies.  In the second phase of the among SEN students, the highest enrolment numbers were recorded in the years 2018 and 2022, while the lowest occurred in 2014, 2015, and 2016. Of the total non-SEN student population, 60,026 were classified as academically successful, whereas 72,893 were unable to complete their studies. Following the individual analysis of both groups, a proportional comparison of success and failure rates was conducted.  A two-proportion z-test showed statistically significant difference in enrollment success rates between SEN and non-SEN students (p = 0,0001), demonstrating distinct academic performance across the two groups. In Brazil, several policies were implemented to democratize access to public higher education, leading to an increase in enrollment from 6.7 million in 2011 tonearly 8.9 million students in 2021 (Brasil, 2021). The online university examined in this study was established in the early 2010s, although these policies could only be effectively implemented beginning in 2018. This timeline helps explain the rise in the number of applications and, consequently, the increase in students with SEN.

Considering RQ1 (What are the success and dropout rates of students with (G1) and without (G2) SEN?), the data show that 898 out of 1,582 SEN students with SEN achieved academic success (approximately 56,76%), while 684 (approximately 43,23%) dropped out. Among non-SEN students, 60,026 out of 132,919 (approximately 45,16%) were successful, and 72,893 (approximately 54,84%) did not complete their studies. Regarding RQ2 (Did the adoption of PL-based design affect the success and dropout rates of students with SEN (G2) before and after 2018 ?), our findings indicate t hat success rates in both groups increased. Following the adoption of PL-based design, the success rates of SEN students (G2) rose from 51.75% to 58.46%, representing an increase of 6.71%. In contrast, the success rates of non-SEN students (G1) improved much more significantly, increasing from 29.28% to 54.74%, a gain of 25.46%. Before the adoption of PL-based design, the success rate of G1 was 22.47% lower than that of G2. However, after the implementation of the changes, this difference fell to 3.72%. These f indings reinforce the results reported by Zhang et al. (2022), who predict that Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and Personalized Learning (PL) mutually benefit students with and without SEN.

In turn, dropout rates decreased from 48.25% to 41.54% for G2 (SEN) and showed a sharp reduction from 70.72% to 45.26% for G2 (non-SEN) following the implementation of the PL-based design approach. Despite these improvements, the rates remain high. We therefore emphasize the need for further studies that analyze the enrollment cancellation forms completed by students themselves to identify the potential reasons associated with the high failure rates observed, so that indicators for addressing them within this university can be delineated. In addition, future research should consider correlations with factors that may contribute to dropout in a post-pandemic period (Kaisara et al., 2024) We conclude that inclusive pedagogy, grounded in personalized learning, is inherently nuanced and context-dependent. At this university, such resources have shown strong potential in improving student success rates and reducing dropout rates across different  groups. However, current data do not yet allow us to establish a direct causal link between the observed outcomes and the PL measures adopted, underscoring the need for more targeted studies. In this regard, it is important to emphasize that in regions of the Global South, where structural inequalities persist, this approach must be complemented by broader systemic support to effectively address barriers to equitable access, retention, and graduation in online higher education, particularly given the significant dropout rates observed.
