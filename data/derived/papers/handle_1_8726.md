# Designing to Support Teacher Mediated Strategies for Student Learning in a Remote Kindergarten Music Class

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Using a single-setting, iterative DBR approach to program design, along with the multimodal affordances of music learning, I consider resource design to support teacher mediated student learning in remote contexts. Throughout the KinderMus program, teachers adapted their teaching in remote spaces, finding ways to mediate learning through language, social interaction, and active learning, and considering limitations around access to externally oriented artifacts. Findings have implications for design in both remote and in-person settings.

## Introduction

As K-12 education moved almost entirely online in the 2020-2021 school year, many of the strategies regularly employed by teachers to scaffold teaching had to be  modified for the unique context of remote learning. This move was especially challenging for early childhood teachers. The change in environment physically sep arated learners from their peers, reducing opportunity for  collaboration, and challenging teachers to rethink  active teaching strategies for students seated in front of a computer monitor at home. Many of the strategies like group and partner work, games and movement-based activities regularly employed by teachers required modification. While challenging, this new context offered an exciting opportunity for designing to support teacher development around teaching in remote learning contexts for kin dergarten students while simultaneously supporting student learning. Using design principles rooted in design based research (DBR) (Design-Based Research Collect ive,

2003) along with the multimodal affordances of making music, I consider how teachers adapted and codesigned

resources to develop new strategies for mediating learning in remote kindergarten classrooms, with implications for design of both remote and in-person learning environments beyond the present context. KinderMus Program Design The objective of the KinderMus program was to both support teacher development around mediating learning in remote contexts and provide opportunity for student learning in music. The design is based on understanding that students learn best when they are actively engaged, when instruction is multimodal, and within a socia l context along with their peers (e.g., Vygotsky, 1978) and I chose music as a disciplinary focus because it engages students in listening, singing, moving, and playing. As both  researcher and music teacher, I grounded the progr am in general music theory with opportunities for teacher s to practice music teaching techniques and engage with students in their music learning, and for students to engage actively with the songs and activities. L essons were responsive to weekly teacher feedback and reflectio n. The design of the resource-driven program is bas ed on principles of DBR (Design-Based Research Collective, 2003) and elements of effective teacher PD (Darli ngHammond et al., 2017; Desimone & Garet, 2015). The program is based on four main design elements. First, KinderMus is content focused on music, incorporating singing, playing, moving, modelling, and peer interaction. Multimodal teaching strategies engage students through active learning and can also be applied in other academic areas. Second, the program is centered on resources and artifacts (Kazemi & Hubbard, 2008). Components include prerecorded, interactive video lessons, written lesson plans, weekly student assessment rub rics, and weekly teacher reflections. Teachers used  video lessons during synchronous instruction, directly de livering music content and vocabulary and modelling movements and games. Third, KinderMus is designed in and for real world settings, as interactive resources allow for practice in the classroom context with students (Design-Based Research Collective, 2003; Desimone & Garet, 2015). Students learn both directly from the video resource and from their teacher’s interactions with the videos. Fourth, the program employs responsive design strat egies (Design-Based Research Collective, 2003). Teachers inform the initial design by providing weekly assessments and reflections. Feedback is directly applied to lessons and resources are constantly updated for student interests, engagement, and learning pace.

## Methods

For this exploratory study, I took a single-setting, iterative DBR approach to program design. The stu dy was conducted from fall of 2020 through winter of 2021 at an elementary school site in south Los Angeles C ounty that delivered synchronous lessons remotely during the COVID-19 pandemic. I analyzed weekly teacher survey responses to open-ended questions asking how teache rs were using the lessons and their perceptions of student learning as the program progressed, as well as three semi-structured post program interviews conducted with each ICLS2022 Proceedings 2084 © ISLS teacher participant. With an iterative approach to data analysis, themes around mediated learning emerged, with descriptive codes emphasizing the verbal, social, a nd instructional moves individual teachers made to interact with the videos and their students in support of student learning.

## Findings

## Conclusion

The Design-Based Research Collective (2003) emphasizes the importance considering how context “can lead to improved theoretical accounts of teaching and learning” (p. 7). Through this DBR project, teachers adapted their teaching in remote spaces, finding ways to mediate learning through language, social interaction, and play-based instructional strategies, and considering limitatio ns around using externally oriented artifacts. Whil e this exploratory study was conducted under somewhat extr aordinary circumstances, the idea of designing reso urces that simultaneously support teacher development and student learning may be adapted for in-person learning and useful in subjects where teachers may have less personal content knowledge and expertise.
