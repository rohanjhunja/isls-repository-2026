# Online Platforms as Sites for Learning: Exploring the Use of Online Resources in Computer and Software Engineering Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigates how online platforms are used as sites for learning by undergraduate students in computer and software engineering (CSE). The study combined different methods and data types to explore the resource infrastructure available to 73 CSE students as they used several online platforms to learn software development over a period of three months. Our findings reveal that students combined multiple platforms in different ways, providing access to a wide variety of resources, and across learning contexts that extend beyond the boundaries of the formal curriculum. The study contributes empirical evidence about learning with platform-based resources and suggests that future research should focus on how platforms can be considered as resources for learning in undergraduate education.

## Introduction

Students in undergraduate education engage increasi ngly with online platforms such as Google or Udemy to support their learning activities, included or not in the design of their curricular tasks (Zlatkin-Troitschanskaia et al., 2021; Henderson et al., 2017). Research shows that students use such platforms also to learn stud y-related content outside the context of curricular tasks (Brown et al., 2016). Online platforms can offer support for student learning in multiple ways (Manca, 2020), but resear ch has mostly focused on examining learning in curr icular contexts and through social networking platforms (e.g. Facebook or Twitter) or in instant messaging pl atforms (e.g. WhatsApp or WeChat). Other platforms, many of  them domain-specific, have received less attention. In domains such as computer and software engineering (CSE), where professional work has become rich in online resources and practices (Storey et al., 2017), plat forms are especially relevant. CSE professionals us e a wide variety of platforms for their work (e.g. GitHub or Stack Overflow), and some studies have shown CSE students using those same platforms to support their learning during their studies, both formally (Tushev et al., 2020) and informally (Damşa & Nerland, 2016). Nonetheless, th e understanding of how students learn using platfor ms in specific disciplinary domains is still limited (Zlatkin-Troitschanskaia et al., 2021), constraining teachers’ efforts to support students during their studies. This stud y sheds light on this matter, by exploring the infr astructure of resources provided by online platforms as CSE stude nts learn software development during their studies. To address this aim, we used a mixed methods research design, in order to answer the following research questions: RQ1: How do CSE students combine online platforms a cross curricular and online, non-curricular contexts to learn software development?

RQ2: What learning resources are made available to students when online platforms are used?

## Conceptual Framework

To examine students’ use of online platforms, this study builds on two conceptual underlays. Firstly, the use of platforms is seen as contextual, as actions student s carry out involving the use of platforms are not isolated but bound to specific situations and circumstances taking place over time (Zheng & Yano, 2007). Secondly, platforms are conceptualized as online software infrastructures that are characterized by the resources they provide access to, and which play a mediational function by linkin g students with different people and types of infor mation (Bruce & Levin, 1997). Analytically, we focus on th e performance of students’ actions over time involv ing the use of platforms. These actions are represented by the access to different platforms over time, charac terized by different configurations of platforms used. Configurations determine which resources are at students' disposal as they move across boundaries of curricular and onlin e, non-curricular contexts. Configuration is a vari able consisting of different combinations of platforms that are assembled in specific situations and provide access to different types of resources, shaping the infrastru cture of resources students have access to as they use online platforms.

## Methods

The study was conducted by inviting undergraduate students from CSE programs from four major universities in Norway to participate voluntarily in the Fall 2020 and Spring 2021 semesters through their course teachers, limited to courses related to software development. The identity of the participants was protected following national and ICLS2022 Proceedings 1097 © ISLS GDPR regulations. In total, 73 students participated, 33 in the Fall 2020 and 40 in the Spring 2021. T he dataset collected consists of students’ web-browsing history and self-reports, as well as 27 individual stimul ated recall interviews (Dempsey, 2010). A custom-made software was used to collect self-reports and web-browsing history, which surveyed students about actions directed at learning software development and the platforms used to carry them out. The software also retrieved information about every time those platforms were accessed during a threemonth period back from the moment the survey was submitted. During the interviews, carried out using an online video conference service, students were shown the p latforms they selected in the survey and the monthl y frequency of access to each platform, to prompt reflections about when, how and why online platforms were used. The interviews were audio recorded and later transcribed verbatim. The dataset was analyzed, firstly, through a sessionization, in which the events in the web-browsing history were aggregated into smaller aggregated units (i.e. sessions), defined based on a maximum separation be tween events (Kapusta et al., 2014). A subset of se ssions was selected for analysis based on the self-reports. Secondly, a descriptive mapping was carried out t o identify and group the resources within each platform, in order to define distinct types of platforms. Thirdly, a latent class analysis (LCA) (Muthén, 2001) of the types of platforms used during the sessions was performed, with the best model selected based on the lowest BIC criteria. Finally, a thematic analysis of the interview transcripts (Braun & Clarke, 2012) identified learning contexts involved in the use of platforms.

## Findings

The sessionization resulted in 12,799 sessions characterized by platforms accessed, of which 3,336 sessions were selected based on the actions carried out with the platforms involved. The descriptive mapping resulted in eight different types of platforms being used by the stud ents, with search engine (SE) and video repository (VR) platforms being the most frequent in the sessions, identified in more than half of the sessions. Tutorials and courses (T&C), questions and answers (Q&A), source code rep ository (SCR) and communication (COMM) platforms were present in about 30-40% of the sessions, while text library (TL) and SN platforms were the least used, present in less than 30% of the sessions. The analysis of t he interview data revealed that the sessions took place mainly in the context of curriculum-based tasks, although some of the students also reported using them to de velop software outside their study-related work. The curr iculum-based tasks were mostly recurrent software development tasks part of specific courses, and stu dents used platforms both during these tasks as wel l as to prepare in advance for them. In a few cases, studen ts used platforms also to prepare in advance for le ctures. Outside the context of their studies, some of the i nterviewees reported using online platforms to mast er and implement new programming methods, either as leisure or for entrepreneurial purposes. The LCA resulted in a five-class model being chosen  after following the minimum BIC criteria, and assessing results from likelihood ratio tests and t he entropy of the models, which reached 0.906, sugg esting a good class assignment (See Table 1).

## Discussion and Conclusions

With regard to how do CSE students combine online platforms across contexts to learn software development, the findings revealed that students’ use of online platforms took place across both curricular (Manca, 2020; Henderson et al., 2017) and non-curricular contexts (Brown et al., 2016), primarily during performing software development tasks. Our findings add to previous studies by show ing that, in both types of contexts, students used multiple platforms that were combined in different ways, presumably to address different aspects of the tasks they engage in. Students constantly crossed curricular boundaries to informally use resources they deemed relevant for their learning, and even used platforms to work in their own projects. Moreover, the platforms accessed by s tudents were, in some cases, domain-specific, highlighting the importance of taking into account disciplinary contexts to understand how students learn using online platforms (Damşa & Nerland, 2016). With regard to what learning resources are made available for students when online platforms are used, the findings show that, although the resources available to the students varied significantly across sessions, clear patterns emerged related to the configurations of platforms used by them to support their learning. For instance, while some of the resources infrastructures observe d are similar, important differences emerged with r egard to what specific resources were available across these  configurations. Moreover, the infrastructures acce ssed not ICLS2022 Proceedings 1099 © ISLS only involved resources related to the use of infor mation, but also those that connected students with  different people and that enabled different ways of relating with them. Such differences, found as a result of the LCA and the descriptive mapping, suggest students act purposefully to address different aspects of the tasks they engaged in, suggesting that the use of platforms not only is situated in nature but also relational. These findings are in line with previous studies that argue that students purposely shape the resource infrastructure they use to support their learning by moving across multiple contexts and that this infrastructure is domain specific (Damşa & Nerland, 2016; Tushev, 2020). Furthermore, the current study adds by providing empirical evid ence of such processes, showing the specific resources and contexts shaping such infrastructure and that students use multiple platforms in combination to shape it.

This study makes an empirical contribution, by shedding light on students’ learning that involves the use of online platforms. Students act purposely to asse mble different resource infrastructures as a respon se to the learning context in which platforms are used, but a lso enclosed in larger learning contexts that involve both the curriculum as well as specific disciplinary domains. The current study also makes a methodological contribution, by showing how different types of data and new anal ytical techniques, such as the sessionization proce ss in combination with the LCA, can provide valuable insights into students’ learning practices. Future research should focus on how online platforms can respond to specific disciplinary domains, and examine in-depth how students combine multiple platforms purposefully in order to  access the resources they deem as necessary for sp ecific learning needs. This research should further explor e how the resources within platforms are used by students, what relevant learning affordances these provide and what purposes drive students to use platforms. Hi gher education institutions should also take such considerations into account, as the informal use of platf orms seems to play a determinant role in what learning resources shape student learning.
