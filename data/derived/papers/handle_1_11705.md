# Preparing Future Workforce in AI-driven World: A Systematic Review

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
A systematic review analyzed empirical studies published between 2019 and 2024 regarding AI literacy focusing on workforce development in higher education. A final selection of 8 studies focusing on integrating AI literacy into higher education curricula was made to adequately prepare students for AI-driven roles. The review highlights the critical need for curricula that foster technical proficiency and essential skills such as critical thinking and collaborative problem-solving.

## Introduction

Despite the rise of AI and the demand for skilled professionals, employers struggle to recruit adequately prepared graduates for AI-driven workplaces (Cengage Group, 2023). This disconnect threatens upward career mobility, exacerbates socio-economic disparities, and impedes overall economic productivity—critical areas for exploring equitable, technology-enhanced education (Bühler et al., 2022).  While existing research highlights  the transformative potential of AI literacy ’s transformative potential in education —demonstrating benefits such as increased student engagement and personalized feedback (Ding et al., 2024; Long & Magerko, 2023)—the reality is that students' competencies in AI literacy remai n significantly underdeveloped. Additionally, persi stent concerns regarding academic integrity, depende nce on AI-generated content, and the associated ethical ramifications continue to challenge the effective integration of AI in educational paradigms (Orduño-Osuna et al., 2024). Thus, this systematic review aims to critically address th ese issues by identifying discipline-specific AI literacy and ex ploring definitions and frameworks for integrating AI liter acy into higher education. We believe learning can improve through student collaboration and AI systems (Greeno, 1998). Our review will address the following questions:

1. How is AI literacy defined, implemented, and evaluated within higher education frameworks?

2. What cognitive and collaborative skills are essential for fostering AI literacy in workplaces increasingly

dominated by AI?

3. How can higher education institutions effectively embed AI literacy into their curricula, with a focus on

enhanced human-AI collaboration?

## Method

We conducted this review following PRISMA guidelines (Page et al., 2021). We focused on peer-reviewed studies about AI literacy in higher education published between 2019 and 2024. We searched various databases like Web of Science, EBSCOHost, and Semantic Scholar using k eywords such as “AI literacy,” “higher education,” and “workforce.” Eight of 60 eligible studies  were selected based on predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Data extraction was systematically organized. We or ganized and analyzed the data in a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet, identifying AI literacy definitions, r equisite skills, and integration strategies. Two researchers conducted the analysis, refining findings through discussion until they reached 100% interrater agreement.

## Findings

AI literacy definitions and frameworks (RQ1) Only two studies (Al-Abudllatif & Alsubaie, 2024; Delcker et al., 2024) clearly defined AI literacy based on existing models: Long & Magerko’s (2020) model and The EuropeandigComp Framework 2.2. Another study operationally defined AI literacy based on the authors’ previous research (Kong et al., 2021). The reviewed papers used AI literacy to assess students’ knowledge, competency, attitudes, ethical considerations, and confidence in using AI within higher education settings. These studies were conducted with students from diverse disciplines, including health sciences, computer science,  non-STEM fields, education, and unspecified majors. Methodologically, the studies used mixed methods (n =4), quantitative (n=3), and qualitative (n=1) approaches. They analyzed different data types, such as surveys, questionnaires, reflective writing, discussions, and focus groups.

Cognitive and collaborative skills (RQ2) All reviewed studie s measured students’ understanding of AI and their ability to use it eff ectively. They also looked at the perceived benefits of AI capabilities in fostering cognitive and collaborative skills. For higher-order cognitive thinking, two studies (Walter, 2024; Tzirides et al., 2024) highlighted the importance of computational thinking, critical thinking and problem-solving abilities as essential components of AI literacy. Only one study (Tzirides et al., 2024) examined how human collaboration contributes to AI literacy. The study investigat ed the role of human collabora tion in embracing AI literacy. Additionally, two studies (Kong et al., 2022; Subaveerapandiyan et al.,2024) emphasized the need to develop ethical reasoning alongside AI literacy to mitigate ethical concerns in AI use, advocating for ethics training initiatives. Curriculum integration (RQ3) All the reviewed articles underscored the critical role of integrating AI into the curriculum, including the need for comprehensive educator training to align with AI’s societal impacts. Key elements of this curriculum integration include non-coding foundational courses that delve into the history of AI and ethical considerations (Kong et al., 2022), as well as utilizing disciplinary projects (e.g., prompt engineering) that are aligned with disciplinary goals and interdisciplinary projects that simulate real-world challenges  (Walter, 2024), thereby bridging the gap between theory and practice. Collectively, these co mponents emphasize the synthesis of theoretical fra meworks with experiential learning, effectively equipping students for success in AI-centric environments.

## Discussion

Considering that the primary goals of higher educat ion include future workforce development, t he review highlights the importance of designing and developi ng an AI-integrated curriculum and assesses its impact on student learning. The review results suggest active integrations of AI literacy development by refining pedagogical frameworks and incorporating collaborative pedagogical strategies. This analysis elucidates the transformative capacity of human-AI partnerships in educational contexts by synthesi zing existing frameworks and proposing AI-enhanced collaborative learning modalities—such as project-based learning. For example, both technical and societal perspectives on AI literacy fall short of capturing human-AI collaboration as an evolving learning process. To bridge this gap, we propose enhancing existing f rameworks by integrating situative learning theory (Greeno, 1998). By conceptualizing AI as a collaborative par tner in knowledge construction, we aim to reconcile  the technical and societal dimensions, thereby fostering a more holistic understanding of AI literacy. Future research must explore AI’s potential in co-constructing knowledge frameworks, ensuring that AI literacy paradigms remain adaptable to the evolving requirements of technology-enhanced learning environments. Furthermore, the review’s focus over a limited five-year timeframe illuminates the pressing need for further research to address geographic disparities,  validate competency assessment methods, and evalua te longitudinal workforce outcomes.  Policymakers are encouraged to prioritize funding f or AI literacy initiatives, while educators are urged to embed AI literacy into  their programs through collaborative human-AI projects. Additionally, researchers should investigate AI ’s potential in co-constructing knowledge and enhancing workforce readiness, ensuring that AI literacy fram eworks adapt and evolve alongside the rapidly chang ing technological landscape.

## Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations affecting generalizability. The small sample of eight studies restricts the ability to draw broader conclusions, especially since 75% o f these studies are based in the U.S. institutions,  creating geographic bias. Excluding non-English publications may omit valuable insights, and the five-year timeframe restricts analysis depth. Future research should expand the sample, extend the timeframe, and include more diverse geographic contexts for a more comprehensive understanding.

## Conclusion and implications

This review highlights the essential collaboration among educators, students, administrations, IT professionals and policymakers  to equip the  future workforce with AI integration competencies. Furthermore, This review aligns with the mission of the International Society of Learning Sciences to innovate educational practices, thereby empowering both learners and educators in a progressively AI-embedded landscape. Future research should focus on practical challenges in integrating AI into higher education and  addressing these challenges using research-validated learning and instructional strategies based on empirical studies.
