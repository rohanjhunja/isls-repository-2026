# Integrated STEM: Student Sensemaking Across Time

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Many integrated STEM frameworks focus on disciplinary ideas that students learn through integrated STEM activities. They may hide how students experience and coordinate disciplinary ideas and practices in schools as they make sense of STEM problems. This poster introduces a theoretical framework that conceptualizes integrated STEM from a perspective of student sensemaking as students experience disciplinary thinking at different time scales and illustrates how it could be used to explore teacher questioning in integrated STEM.

## Introduction

Many frameworks for integrated STEM describe the disciplinary concepts and practices students are expected to learn and use during an integrated STEM activity (Moore et al., 2020). T hese discipline-centered frameworks focus on the disciplinary knowledge and practices that are used in integrated STEM activities, either  through the general interactions of STEM domains, the more specific interactions of cross-disciplinary concepts and practices (e.g., Chalmers et al., 2017; Kelley & Knowles, 201 6), or the use of STEM problems as mediators for th e interaction of STEM domains (e.g., Leung, 2020). Th ese framings address what students should know, and they are useful for describing and planning the concepts, practices, and tasks targeted in integrated STEM activities. These frameworks, however, do not indicate when and how students coordinate knowledge and skills of different disciplines as they make sense of integra ted STEM activities (Tytler et al., 2021). Since th ey only represent disciplinary boundaries or ideas, the y may hide the ways in which students use disciplinary ideas and practices as they make sense of STEM problems, how they draw upon prior knowledge and skills, or how they engage in the creation of approximations of discipl inary practice. This is problematic because knowled ge of student thinking and the experi ences of students as they engage in integrated STEM  activities are necessary to develop effective integrated STEM instructional strategies. Framing integrated STEM as sensemaking across time This poster introduces a framework  conceptualizing integrated STEM from the perspective of student sensemaking (Li & Schoenfeld, 2019; Odden & Russ, 2019; Zhao & S chuchardt, 2021) as students experience disciplinary thinking at different time scales (Tyt ler et al., 2021). There is a temporal aspect to ho w students experience school disciplinary thinking in integrat ed STEM (Tytler et al., 2021).  At a micro-level time scale, students recognize  they need multiple disciplinary ideas or practices  to grapple with a STEM problem. These ideas may be learned or newly generated through the  course of the STEM unit at a meso-level time scale, or disciplinary ideas could have been learned months or years earlier at a macro-level time scale. Across these different time scales, students reach for disciplinary ideas in different ways as they ma ke sense of integrated STEM problems  (see Figure 1).  D uring micro-level integration, students use disciplinary thinking to make sense of STEM problems in the mome nt of the lesson. They engage in sensemaking as they try to “figure something out” and build explanations to make sense of their gaps in knowledge (Odden & Russ, 2019, p. 199). Students generate new knowledge or make new connections with their prior knowledge as they construct their sensemaking explanations (Odden & Russ, 2019). These explanations occur within disciplinary boundaries, or students may also blend disciplinary ideas (Zhao & Schuchardt, 2021) as they construct and revise them. During meso-level integration, students use disciplinary thinking to build on new knowledge and make connections as the project or problem unfolds over the course of the unit. Meso-level integration occurs as students use new knowledge generated from micro-level integration as a resource to develop subsequent explanations. As they engage in sensemaking throughout the unit, they connect this new knowledge to other disciplinary ideas and practices (Benedict-Chambers et al., 2017).

Students experience the ideas and practices of mult iple disciplines over a school year or across grade levels (Tytler et al., 2021). During macro-level integration, students’ sensemaking explanations become more sophisticated over time as students draw on more disciplinary ideas and practices as explanatory tools and develop their abilities to engage in sensemaking as a problem-solving strategy. Exploring teacher questioning Framing integrated STEM from this sensemaking perspective allows us to make conjectures about how teachers’ instructional strategies may support students’ thinking as students make sense of STEM problems. Teachers’ questioning is one instructional strategy that plays a key role in supporting students’ sensemaking (Colley & Windschitl, 2016). This poster describes preliminary findings on how middle school teachers ask questions to support students’ sensemaking and use of disciplinary thinking at these different time scales.
