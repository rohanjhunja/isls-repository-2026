# Curriculum Routines and a Community Builder AI Agent: Synergy to Mediate the Convergent Tendencies of AI

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
AI technologies, while powerful and the subject of much innovation, have inherent bias towards convergent thinking: reproducing and mimicking patterns already seen in its training data. Learning sciences and CSCL research focus on studying tools, including AI, in authentic spaces where diversity of thought and contributions are highly valued. Focusing on an AI agent designed for supporting socio-collaborative learning in classroom spaces, we explore how these design tensions can be mediated through boundary-spanning curriculum routines. This analysis provides a roadmap from theory to practice for AI powered tools in classroom spaces that aim to leverage diversity in collaborative learning while mitigating the harms of over-attending to convergent thinking.

## Introduction and theoretical framing

Recent youth-engaged critical co-design spaces have pushed researchers to consider expansive metaphors for AIsupported learning, particularly around the issues of resilient and community-focused collaborative learning that position students as transformative agents and world makers of the future (Chang et al., 2022). These new topics bring new dynamics around educational equity. One of these AI specific challenges centers on how AI tools tend to reinforce dominant and common forms of behavior,  termed convergent behavior, while sociocultural learning theories promote using uniqueness and diversity, termed divergent behavior, as resource and asset for learning (Moll et al., 2006). Machine learning models inherently both reproduce and mimic patterns in their training data, hence valuing convergence towards those observed patterns and the patterns most represented in the training data. Building on other work that has positioned AI as a thought partner to support divergent (and thus more  human) thinking (Simeone et al. 2022), we explore the tens ion of ideational convergence and diversity  in the context of co-designing an AI collaboration support partner and a ssociated curriculum routines for K-12 classroom spaces. This AI collaboration partner, CoBi, is designed to  support students in establishing and revisiting community agreements for collaboration in classroom learning communities (Breideband et al., 2023). In this work, we explore the research question: In the context of expansive co-design that has illuminated the possibilities of collaborative AI agents, how c an curriculum routines mediate the tension between AI’s convergent tendency and the affordances of divergen t thinking for equity?  By curriculum routines, we mean a generalizable and repeatable sequence of teacher mo ves that promote a curricular student learning goal. While routines have been discussed as a strategy for 1) a ligning good teaching practices and classroom techn ology, 2) guide classroom usage of technology and 3) guide in structional design (DeBarger et al., 2011), their integration with AI technologies has not yet been explored.

## Methods

A set of routines, co-designed within the context of storylined units (Penuel et al., 2022), were designed to engage small groups of students in rich, collaborative lea rning discussions supported by a collaborative AI a gent called CoBi. The routines presented have two distinct forms. The first is the establishing community agreements routine where students 1) Brainstorm Community Agreements around four categories (Respectful, Equitable, Committed to our Community, & Pushing Our Thinking Forward) a nd 2) Develop a set of class wide agreements through a consensus building discussion.

Students 1) reflect on agreements in action, celebrate successes, and engage with new ideas to u phold to support our learning; and 2) investigate and discuss the AI concepts used in the agent.

## Findings and discussion

During the establishing our community agreements routine, we see a balance between convergent use of AI and divergent promotion of student thought. This part o f the routine positions students as the unique and creative thinkers while setting up the AI to natural languag e processing and semantic analysis in novel ways during the convergence stage of agreeing on a class contract f or collaboration. This also mediates the tension be tween innovation and convergence because AI researchers can focus on innovative strategies for categorizing and sorting the agreements (with the goal of ease of display) w ithout creating a tool that has consequential impli cations for inevitable model bias, a key feature that can lead to AI tools struggling in classrooms. Figure 1 The Co-Designed Community Agreement Routines, Establishing and Revisiting During the revisiting routine, the AI engages in convergent analysis of student noticings. This part of the routine helps mediate the possible implications of decentering and devaluing diverse ideas by providin g opportunities for critiquing the AI and its bias, o mitting evaluative uses of the AI agent and having students discuss the prompt, “what was an example of one of these agreements that you heard but think maybe the AI did not hear?” This also mediates design tensions by allowing researchers to focus on novel categorizations of student talk while practitioners can integrate these routines into existing classroom practices. The absurd idea protocol (see Warm Up in Figure 1) at the beginning of the routines promotes divergent thinking in the context of convergent AI.  During this protocol,  students are first asked to think of the most ridiculous and absurd idea for how technology could  help them to collaborate with others Since there a re no “wrong ideas”, only different versions of bad ideas, the exercise gets students and teachers to think  outside the box, valuing heterogeneous thinking  but also working towards convergence abou t what the class views as good collaboration.

## Conclusion

The community agreement routines offer resolution to th e seemingly contradictory tensions between feasibil ity and aspirational design as well as between promotin g diversity and equity through heterogeneity and AI ’s fundamental default tendency toward convergence and similarity. We saw how design components in the routines and their revision by learning scientists, teachers and technologists offered a roadmap for using conv ergent tools as touchpoints for conversations about valuing diversity and non-normative collaboration. While this case of curriculum routines mediating th ese tensions was contextualized in a collaboration support-focused AI technology, we believe it offers a road map for other novel and innovative applications of AI technologies to be integrated into classrooms to pr omote and uplift valuable and heterogeneous contrib utions, identities and ways of knowing. In addition to mediating implementation, the routines can mediate design, helping developers and stakeholders keep the needs of users  and authentic classroom implementation at the cent er. The next steps in this work are to study the routine’s enactment; teachers’ novel and creative application s of the routines to use the AI tools to support divergent and inclusive socio-collaborative learning communities will seed the next frontier of this work.
