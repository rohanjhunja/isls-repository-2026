# Electrodermal Activity in Learning Sciences Research: A Systematic Literature Review

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Electrodermal activity (EDA) is a widely used psychophysiological measure given its sensitivity to cognitive and affective processes via sympathetic arousal. Although interest in this data modality for studying learning processes has been boosted by the advances in biosensors, its research tradition spans over a century. In this work, we systematically review the empirical literature using EDA in the learning sciences from both theoretical (e.g., frameworks and constructs) and methodological (e.g., collection and analysis) perspectives.

## Introduction

In the quest towards understanding learning as a pr ocess rather than an outcome, data modalities able to capture learning as it happens have gained increasing atten tion. Examples of those modalities could be log data from virtual learning environments, fixations and saccad es from eye-trackers, and physiological data from biosensors (Noroozi et al., 2020). The later has a long tradition especially in the multidisciplinary field of psychophysiology, but the growing quality and  reliability of wearable biosensors has spread the interest and potential in those measures to other fields, and the learning sciences have been no exception (Järvelä et al., 2021). One of the most widely used physiological responses  in psychophysiology is electrodermal activity (EDA; Boucsein, 2012), owing to its sensitivity to cognitive and affecti ve processes via the activation of the sympathetic nervous system (i.e., sympathetic arous al; Dawson et al., 2017). Together with heart rate, EDA has been the most used signal for detecting emotions (Ba & Hu, 2023). This could be explained for example by arousal being a central dimension  to emotions in general (Bakker et al., 2014), of which academic emotions a re no exception (Pekrun et al., 2002). Accordingly, a previous review on EDA, considering studies from 2009 to 2021, has exclusively focused on emotions (Horvers et al., 2021). Nonetheless, it is important to consider also the wide spectrum of constructs studied through the lens of physiological arousal, which are not necessarily re lated to emotions.

In addition, over more than a century of EDA research since the pioneering studies of Vigouroux (1879) and Féré (1888), important progress was made before the advent of wearable sensors  (Boucsein et al., 2012). Therefore, an evaluation of the progress of the field leading to (and even informing) the state of the art, should not overlook that extant research. Having a clear picture of what has been done and wh ere we are at the moment is crucial to inform the research agenda of enriching and strengthening traditional learning sciences research with the physiological arousal dimension that is an integral part of learning. Aims This systematic literature review aims to synthesize the current state of the art of empirical learning sciences research leveraging EDA, as well as identifying gaps in the literature that could serve as a research agenda in the coming years. The review pursues both theoretical and methodological perspectives. On the  theoretical side of empirical research, we aim to synthesize the theoretical frameworks that have been used to ground EDA and link it to the learning process, as well as the constructs that have been studied using EDA as a proxy. On the methodological side, we aim to understand th e different ways in which EDA data has been collected (e.g., the sensors used and their charact eristics), as well as how the EDA data has been pro cessed and analyzed, and which features have been extracted for use in statistical analysis and in triangulation with other data modalities.

## Method

The database query for the literature review was defined as follows: “(("Elect rodermal Activity") OR ("galvanic skin*") OR ("skin conductance") OR ("sympathetic ar ousal")) AND (education OR pedago* OR teaching OR teacher OR learner OR student)”. As can be seen, the query contains two conditions linked by the Boolean operator AND, which requires that both are met. The first co ndition or subgroup refers to the presence of EDA o r related keywords. Specifically, EDA is sometimes referred to as “skin conductance ” or “galvanic skin response”, “galvanic skin reaction”, or “galvanic skin reflex”. The last three ones we re captured via the wildcard operator * next to the “galvanic skin” keyword. The second con dition or group targets the educational or learning related content, with keywords such as “education”, “teaching”, and agents such as “teacher”, “learner”, and “students”. The query was used with seven databases, namely, Scopus, SpringerLink, IEEE Explore, ScienceDirect, ERIC, the ACM Digital Library, and the Web of Science. The combined search produced 944 results, which were inspected for duplicates, whether EDA was actually measured (some papers mentioned but not measure d EDA), whether the participants in the studies were studen ts or teachers, and whether the study was actually related to learning. After this exclusion criteria was applied, 689 reco rds were excluded, and 256 remained for detailed screening in the review. The search was not constrained to any time period, and in the final sample of 256 there were papers from the years 1960 to 2023.

## Preliminary findings

Preliminary findings showed that most of the studie s (202; 79%) focused on higher education, while 54 studies (21%) used EDA in K-12. The theoretical approaches used varied substantially, with 41 studies (16%) providing no theoretical grounding for using EDA, and for the rest different theories were used in a small number of studies such as the circumplex model of affect (4 studies),  control-value theory (4 studies), and cognitive load theory  (2 studies). The studies were conducted in more than 20 countrie s, and of the studies already screened, about 50% focused on emotion, 30% on cognition, 10% on motiva tion, and 10% on behavior. Among the main construct s studied with EDA we find emotional arousal, anxiety, stress, cognitive load, attention, and engagement. Methodologically, tens of different sensors were used to measure EDA in learning related contexts, being the most used sensors Empatica (26 studies), Shimme r (20 studies), and BIOPAC (15 studies). The sample  of students and/or teachers on which EDA was measured also varied significantly from a single participant to 272 participants. The recording duration per participan ts ranged from a couple of minutes to a couple of h ours, the latter ones leveraging the capabilities of these sensors for continuous measures. About 20% of the studies reported discarding some amount of data due to low quality r ecordings, whether based on visual inspection, statistical, or physiological criteria (e.g., minimum logical number of skin conductance responses). In terms of body parts where the EDA signal was mea sured from, most of the studies took the measurement from the palm, fingers, or wrist. The p alm and the fingers are among the best places for m easuring EDA due to the concentration of sweat glands in tho se areas, as sweat glands are not homogeneously distri buted across the human body. About 50% of the studies measured other physiological measures together with EDA, and 44% collected other data modalities (e.g., questionnaires and performance measures). We are exploring other theoretical and methodological aspe cts of the study as well as ways of meta-analyzing the results quantitatively and qualitativ ely wherever possible, as the studies have differen t characteristics. We would like to determine for what kind of educa tional and research purposes EDA has been used for so far in the learning sciences. Prelimina rily, it seems that the primary purposes have been to group students into different categories, to find patterns relating to their learning process es, and to study relationships between different constructs more strongly by triangulating different data modalities.

## Implications

This timely and ambitious systematic literature rev iew of EDA in learning sciences research, which is currently in progress and most likely to be finished by the t ime of the ISLS conference, will provide a comprehe nsive picture of the use, strength s, weaknesses, methodological approaches, and theore tical advances concerning this physiological data modality in our field. This holistic view will enable us to identify best practices, successful and unsuccessful approach es, gaps in the literature, as well as  to establish a systematic and coordinated research agenda. Although this systematic review was not limited to a specific theory to capt ure the breadth of studies using EDA in learning sciences, we are considering analyzing the findings in the light of self-regulated learning (SRL; Järvelä et al., 2021)  theory, which provides a solid umbrella for cognitive, af fective, metacognitive and motivational (CAMM) processes. Thereby, the role of arousal in CAMM processes can be better understood. From the preliminary results, it became evident tha t learning processes in K-12 have been understudied in comparison to higher education, where the majori ty of the studies have focused. Similarly, as a lim itation of many previous studies, it is important that future studies explicitly and thoughtfully ground the use of EDA, and by extension of other physiological measures, in learning sciences theor ies (e.g., SRL). This will ensure that the potential of this data modality for continuous and real-time measures of learning processes is capitalized and the interpretation of such measures is facilitated.
