# Estimating Action-Specific Expertise in AR-Guided Learning and Training by Modeling Visual Attention

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper explores a new method for estimating expertise in Augmented Reality (AR) learning and training, emphasizing the learner’s familiarity with action-relevant Areas of Interest (AoI) and their decision-making processes. By examining both gaze behavior and object tracking based on egocentric video data from a group of 15 participants, we investigate the interaction dynamics of learners within AR environments to distinguish between expert and novice behaviors. Key findings highlight notable variations in visual attention and engagement strategies, reflecting different degrees of task familiarity and decision-making capabilities among participants with varying self-reported expertise levels. This approach advances the understanding of visual attention behavior and its relation with expertise in the context of AR learning, offering new insights for the design of adaptive, learner-centered AR education systems. The study contributes to the field of learning sciences by enhancing the effectiveness and personalization of AR learning and training models.

## Introduction

This paper studies a new approach for estimating ex pertise in AR-guided learning and training environments through modeling visual attention. Emphasis is plac ed on task familiarity and decision-making, specifically on the amount of time spent fixating on Areas of Interest (AoI) relevant to the current action and on the frequency of saccades between action-relevant AoIs. To objectively analyze visual attention in an AR learning system (Figure 1-a) and measure these metrics, a new approach is prop osed, which utilizes gaze data in conjunction with egocentric video data captured from AR apparatus (Figure 1-b) to enable framewise analysis of gaze interactions with AoI. This approach directly addresses the gap in the literature concerning the assessment of task  familiarity and its impact on decision-making (Akçayır and Akçayır, 2017). Our study involved 15 grad uate students, categorized into experts and novices based on their previous experience with two cooking tasks (Figure 1-c). This classification was essential for understanding how different levels of action-relevant objects in the environment influence learners’ decision-making. This setup is inspired by the studies conducted by Seeliger et al. (2021), who explored the effects of visual cues on eye gaze in AR environments, and by Yoo et al. (2023), who focused on modeling gaze behavior for estimating visual attention and expertise levels in AR. Our research builds upon the work of Wilson et al. (2023), who investigated the role of extended reality in scaffolding psychomotor-intensive tasks. This study provides a context for our focus on expertise modeling in AR learning environments. We anticipate providing clearer insights into the relationship between task familiarity, decision-making, and learning efficacy in AR settings, contributing significantly to the design and implementation of AR learning systems capable of dynamically measuring the expertise level of learners and adapting the learning content and interventions accordingly. This research is expected to addres s the gap highlighted by Wu (2010) regarding expert and novice understanding in comple x systems, specifically within AR learning and  training environments. The remainder of this paper provides an overview of the study design and findings along with directions for future research in this domain.

Experts displayed a higher score, especially for mo re difficult actions, indicating more targeted and focused fixations on relevant AoIs. This suggests better fa miliarity with the task environment and AoI. Furthe r, although novices exhibited a higher rate of saccade between AoIs, which underscored their relatively more explo ratory approach due to less familiarity, those saccades were not necessarily always between action-relevant AoI. Further analysis of four subjects (two experts and two novices) using Python OpenCV and Tkinter Video tools highlighted experts’ higher precision and smoother transitions with relevant AoIs, while novices showed tentative movements and longer fixations on irrelevant AoIs. These find ings validate the hypothesis about correlation s between “task familiarity” and visual attention. The expertise sc ore, emphasizing fixation and saccade patterns, pro vides a nuanced understanding of learner engagement in AR, contributing to educational technology and AR learn ing. This empirical measure of expertise informs the design of adaptive, person alized AR learning and training experiences, tailored to individual learners’ needs and expertise levels in real-time.

## Conclusions

The analysis of gaze behavior and egocentric video data revealed distinct engagement patterns in learn ers performing tasks in AR settings. The proposed data-driven expertise modeling approach presented here builds on evidence that experts demonstrate relatively more focused gaze patterns, signifying a deeper familiarity with tasks and relevant AOIs, as opposed to novices who exhibit broader, more  exploratory gaze behaviors (Yoo et al., 2023). These insights not only validate our approach for distinguishing expertise levels but also advances our understanding of visual attention in AR learning and training. The implications of this research are substantial for the design of personalized AR educational content, as it allows educators to tailor learning experie nces based on the learners’ specific actions and expertise levels. The findings presented bring the potential of AR technology for immersive learning and expertise modeling, sett ing a foundation for future research. This study em phasizes the growing need for personalized and adaptive learning approaches in educational technology and highlights the potential of AR in reshaping learning and training methodologies.
