# Personalized Learning Analytics Dashboards in Higher Education: Fostering Self -Regulated Learning and Student Agency

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This rapid scoping review examines how personalized learning analytics dashboards (LADs) foster self-regulated learning (SRL) and student agency in higher education. Following the PRISMA guidelines, it synthesizes findings from twelve articles (2023-2025) grounded in SRL theory and student agency theory. Key f indings indicate: (1) personalized feedback in LADs promote metacognitive reflection and goal setting, and (2) data visualization enhanc e metacognitive awareness. This review highlight s the benefits while also acknowledg ing limitations in overlooking potential challenges.

In today’s digital age of higher education, the interdependence of learning activities requires students to balance peer collaboration with  individual autonomy to achieve  successful learning outcomes. Achieving this balance demands self-regulated learning (SRL)  strategies and student agency, enabling proactive goal setting, progress monitoring, and adaptation in collaborative learning settings (Zimmerman, 2002). Personalized learning analytics dashboards (LADs), which  customize visualizations of interaction data, show strong potential to scaffold these processes (Lee, 2024; Matcha et al., 201 9). Despite growing research interest, few studies synthes ize how personalized LADs influence SRL and student agency. This gap leaves educators without consolidated guidance for how to use personalized LADs to support students’ SRL and student agency. To address this research gap, the research question guiding this study is: In what way s do personalized LADs support self-regulated learning in collaborative higher education settings, and how do they shape student agency? This study employs an integrate d theoretical framework combining SRL and student agency theory. According to SRL theory (Zimmerman, 2002), self-regulation is viewed as a dynamic, self-directive process by employing goal setting, self-monitoring, and adaptive strategies, and learners can convert their inherent abilities into academic proficiencies. On the other hand, s tudent agency theory conceptualizes agency as the capacity for deliberate, self-reflective action, and this capacity influences how  students engage in their learning (Klemenčič, 2015). By linking the dual lenses of SRL and student agency theory, this study aims to examine how personalized LADs can serve as a mediating tool that not only facilitates SRL but also empower student agency.

## Method

This study adopts a rapid scoping review method. Rapid scoping review  is an accelerated form of a traditional scoping review that simplifies methodological procedures to generate timely insights into emerging research areas (Tricco et al., 2015). This method is appropriate in the rapidly evolving field of educational technology, where technological innovations demand up-to-date syntheses without the extensive rigor of full systematic reviews. This study followed the four steps: (1) Identification. The Boolean string " learning analytics dashboards" AND "self-regulated learning" AND “student agency ” AND “higher education” was used to identify literature in the selected f our databases: Scopus, IEEE Xplore,  Web of S cience, and EBSCOhost. (2) Screening. Literature was independently screened by all researchers against the following criteria: (a) published in English; (b) published between 2023 to 2025; (c) published in top-tier Q1 academic journals ranked by the Scimago Journal Rank (SJR); and (d) study must focus on personalized LADs in higher education. The PRISMA flowcharts were followed to ensure transparency. (3)  Coding. Data were analyzed by using Nvivo software. A hybrid deductive-inductive coding approach was adopted; the codebook was grounded in SRL and student agency theory. (4) Analysis. Following Braun and Clarke’s (2006) reflexive thematic analysis, c odes were col lated through thematic synthesis to identify patterns. All four researchers independently coded and analyzed the data to synthesize patterns. Consensus was reached through discussion. The functions of personalized LADs, together with their implications for SRL and student agency, are outlined with supporting studies (see Table 1).

2904 Personalized feedback and scaffolding for self-reflection and goal setting

• Promoting metacognitive reflection through tailored feedback. By leveraging natural language

processing (NLP) techniques, personalized LADs scrutinize learner-generated content (reflections, responses, or interactions) and deliver adaptive feedback (e.g., Chen et al.,2023; Giorgashvili et al., 2025). This attuned feedback can promote students’ SRL by enabling them to reflect on their learning and adjust their learning strategies.

• Grouping learners for customized support. Personalized LADs offer educators a tool for categorizing

students based on their interaction patterns. Using technologies  like advanced clustering and profiling, students are segmented based on their SRL levels  (e.g., Abouelenein et al., 2025; Bauer et al., 2025). This approach facilitates the delivery of targeted interventions that empower student agency without disrupting group dynamics in collaborative environments. Data visualization for enhancing metacognitive awareness

• Fostering self-awareness and behavioral adjustments through visual tools. Personalized LADs

provide data visualization to help students sharpen self-awareness and make quick changes accordingly in teamwork, which strengthens SRL and keeps everyone motivated (e.g., Hatala & Nazeri, 2024; Maier & Klotz, 2025; Weidlich et al., 2025).

• Promoting reflective action through predictive cues. Personalized LADs promote student reflection

and action by using predictive indicators that integrate diverse data sources into concise prompts, encouraging behavioral adjustments without imposing direct instructions  (e.g., Jung & Wise, 2025; Tretow-Fish & Khalid, 2023). These tools gather various inputs into simple overviews or hints that gently guide changes without direct instructions.

• Tailoring self-guided learning support through peer networks. Personalized LADs facilitate SRL by

leveraging peer network data to group students with similar traits, aligning tailored feedback with their learning stages, bridging skill gaps to build confidence and motivation in collaborative networks  (e.g., Alam et al.,2023; Kaveri et al., 2023; Wu & Chiu, 2025). ## Significance and future research

The findings from this rapid scoping review shed light on understanding how personalized LADs foster SRL and student agency (Klemenčič, 2015; Lee & Park, 2024) within collaborative higher education settings. By integrating the theoretical lenses of SRL and student agency, this study highlights the potential of personalized LADs as a mediating mechanism that both supports SRL processes and strengthens students’ sense of agency. This study specifically discusses the benefits of personalized LADs in fostering SRL findings, but the challenges of LADs, such as data privacy risks, algorithmic prejudices, or equity issues have not been addressed. Futu re research should examine both benefits and challenges of personalized LADs to provide  a balanced and comprehensive perspective on this topic.
