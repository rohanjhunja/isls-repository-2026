# An Investigation of Promising Pedagogical Practices for Supporting (Emergent) Bi/multilingual Learners in Mathematics—Including Practitioner Voices

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
There is growing scholarship researching emergent bi/multilingual learners (MLs) in STEM contexts. However, there have been calls for more research on how students engage in content-specific work, such as mathematics. We present an in-process literature review that will contribute to discussions about supporting MLs in mathematics by identifying effective pedagogical practices, especially around two strands: 1) using translanguaging approaches, and 2) engaging in disciplinary practices, such as reasoning abstractly and quantitatively.

## Background and objectives

Although a considerable amount of research on supporting (emergent) bi/multilingual learners in mathematics has been accomplished in recent years (de Araujo et al., 2018; Erath et al., 2021), more investigations and summaries of effective pedagogical practices are still needed. We present an ongoing literature review project that builds on the existing scholarship by adapting strategies used in previous work. Specifically, our literature review expands the one by de Araujo and colleagues (2018) by broadening the scope of the search and inclusion criteria to include research-based and practitioner journal articles and chapters. This approach is significant because it will identify promising pedagogical practices that have been (co)developed and used by teachers and combine translanguaging pedagogy with disciplinary moves to support MLs’ understanding of mathematics. This literature review is guided by the following question: What are the different pedagogical approaches that support ML students in engaging in disciplinary practices, such as reasoning abstractly and quantitatively? This report describes the theory underpinning our work and the steps we have accomplished thus far: (i) searching and identifying articles and chapters and (ii) screening and selecting. The actual poster will present the analysis, coding scheme, and emerging findings.

Translanguaging and engaging students in mathematical practices As teacher educators and scholar-activists dedicated to equity and justice in resear ch and practice-based application, we are informed by pedagogical frameworks that challenge deficit narratives of linguistically diverse communities and account for histori cal power dynamics in the education of multilingual  students. Translanguaging pedagogy (Creese & Blackledge, 2010; García, Johnson, & Seltz er, 2017) is one such framework. In mathematics, translanguaging pedagogy values and  supports the natural way bi/mult ilingual learners “use their multiple languages with their one language system… combining two (or more) languag es to communicate as meaningfully as possible” (I & Marti nez, 2020, pg. 48). Translanguaging rejects the premise of Language A versus Language B, as two separate langu ages in the brain, and also rejects the social boundaries of language—“those can be the perceived boundaries of named languages such as English or Spanish (Garcia et al.,

2017) but also the boundaries that tend to limit wh en and how c hildren are welcomed to speak in classrooms”

(Adams-Correll, 2024, pp. 166–167). Grounded in pluralist and culturally sustaining theories of education (Paris & Alim, 2017), t ranslanguaging seeks to foster students’ complex linguistic repertoires (García, Johnson, & Seltzer, 2017), recognizing their cognitive and sociopolitical value. We also recognize the importance of providing opportunities  for MLs to engage in mathematical practices (Moschkovich, 2012).  Some practices viewed as fundamental to mathematics  thinking and learning include sustained engagement in problem-solving, reasoning abstractly and quantitatively, pattern finding and generalizing, attending to precision, and developing and communicating mathematical ideas  using multiple representations (symbols, tables, graphs, etc.) (Moschkovich, 2012, pp. 19–20). A focus on disciplinary practices is a necessary, but not sufficient, step toward equ ity and academic excellence for MLs in mathematics.  To th at end, our review seeks to capture moves that support these practices in the context of translanguaging, emphasizing the key role teachers and other practitioners play in developing these crucial pedagogies.

## Methodology

This review is part of a larger project supporting a variety of educators serving bi/multilingual lear ners in mathematics, including pre-service and in-service K-12 teachers. This means that we must broaden the sc ope of our inclusion criteria to search for  relevant articles discussing strategies for support ing multilingual learner s in developing their mathematical understandings. Moreo ver, by including educators’ voices, we challenge d eficit assumptions regarding the rigor of practitioner jou rnals. We consider narratives and pedagogical guida nce developed by teachers as worthy of inclusion  and powerful sources of knowledge about how to support MLs in classrooms and other educational settings (Zavala & Aguirre, 2024). We started our search for research-based literature by adapting de Araujo and colleagu es’ (2018) list of individual journals searched and keywords.  This involved three main outlets: mathematics educa tion journals, general education journals, and bilingual education  journals. We looked for recent articles from 2016 to 2023, which picks up where their review left off, resulting in over 1,300 articles. Next, we included additional research journals focused on teaching and teacher education,  critical and cultur al studies-focused journals, and bilingual education journals they did not have in their origi nal lists. Lastly, we expanded the search to include practitioner articles and chapters. In total, these additional outlets yielded another 1,340 articles, bringing the list to over 2,640. By replicating the de Araujo et al. (2018) process and adding our expanded search, we i dentified new articles that could potentially be analyzed and included in the study. Full lists of all journals searched and detailed sorting procedure s will be provided in  the actual poster. We are currently sorting through  article s/chapters, removing duplicates, and ensuring the integrity of the dataset. Exclusion criteria include: other literature reviews, not mathematics, not K-12, and not specifically focused on the teaching and learning of mathematics.

## Emerging findings

We have preliminarily identified several pedagogical strategies that combine translanguaging with moves to support engagement with mathematical practices. We will provide details on these practices during  our poster presentation. Due to space constraints, we highligh t just a few her e. First, establishing the math classroom as a translanguaging space is emerging as a promising strategy (e.g., Adams-Correll, 2024). The literature highlights the importance of teachers demonstrating powerful t ranslanguaging practices by modeling for students h ow one can flexibly move across language boundaries and use multiple linguistic repertoires. Another emerging strategy is establishing the classroom as an expansive multimod al space that recognizes and encourages students’ embodied repertoires to make sense of and communicate mathematical ideas (Liu & Takeuchi, 2023). Revoicing students’ contributions in multiple languages is sh own to increase language access and may encourage students to repeat/revoice their classmates in other languages, thereby enhancing both mathematical and language learning. Lastly, we may be observing a significant uptick in scholarship discussing the importance of translanguaging practices in mathematics (Bailey et al., 2018) and culturally sustaining instructional practices  (I, Martinez, & Jackson, 2021) that connect to MLs’ communication practices at home and in local communities.
