# The Role of Entry Points in Defining, Cultivating, and Sustaining Community Learning with Data Artists

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster proposal focuses on entry points as a lens for analyzing learning in community learning events (CLEs), which involve youth ‘data artists’ and community members. Thematic analysis of qualitative data is used to understand how entry points influence learning at CLEs and inform future iterations of the current project. Early analysis shows three emergent themes: geography and physicality; relationality, and communication.

## Background

This proposal is part of an ongoing design-based research project aimed at developing and test ing a model for youth-driven, community-centered data science through artistic media. Youth in the program (called data artists) participate in learning sessions which engage them in thinking about, collecting, and visualizing data. They also participate in Community Learning Events (CLEs) by sharing their work  with community members. A central goal of CLEs is to allow data artists and the commu nity to (re)claim agency over aspects of their comm unity which haven’t (sufficiently) been addressed through data and/or data visualization. An emergent idea of the overall research project  is how community is being defined, cultivated, and sustained. The project is run by a team mostly base d out of a single university and, while there are c ommunitybased partners for the program, many of the decisio ns are made by people on the project teams  (e.g. curriculum team, events team, research team). In this proposal, entry points are used as a lens to consider how those decisions are being made, who is involved, and how that influences learning that takes place at CLEs. Research focus This proposal takes up the research question What is the role of entry points in cultivating com munity learning during CLEs? Entry points are defined here as design goals which can facilita te interaction between community members and data artists at CLEs. Examples include physical spaces, event flyers, and correspondences. By better understanding, analyzing, and (re)structuring entry points, we influence how community is defined and shaped at CLEs. This is important because “…developing arguments around agency and justice requires additional layer s of analytical responsibility, including the need to  specify our definitions of ‘community’” (Vossoughi  and Shea, 2019, p. 330). This relates to MVP’s focus on agency and the sociocultural theoretical framework taken up in this analysis. We emphasize learning as a dynamic phenomenon connec ted to the relationships, identities, goals, and practices of individuals and their communities (Gutiérrez & Rogoff, 2003; Nasir and Hand, 2006). Gi ven this framing, the way that community is cultivated through entry points to CLEs will significantly shape how learning takes place. Some of the guiding questions of this proposal are: ● What are the processes for inviting community members to the CLEs? ● How are relationships created and sustained with community members before, during, and after CLEs? ● What design processes of MVP are used to create and/or modify entry points for future CLEs?

## Methods, data collection, and data analysis

Qualitative data is used to understand connections with and across communities. Text-based data (e.g. emails, event flyers) will support analysis of discussions of entry points, ho w decisions are made, and who is involved. Internal document data (e.g. meeting notes, checklists, analytic memos) will provide insight into how decisions are being made by the project team as well as how entry points have c hanged (and/or will change) over time. Additionally, interviews with members of the project and members of the community (in progress) will enhance understanding of how entry points are created, accessed, and sustained. For example, members of the project team may be asked questions about how they chose (and/or  prioritized) which community members to contact ab out CLEs. Conversely, community members may be asked qu estions to understand what kind of information they were given about the CLEs and how partnerships were  navigated. Both types of interviewees may be asked questions about the cultivation of community to better analyze perceptions of community representation at CLEs (e.g. do community members at the CLEs feel representative of the community? Why or why not?). Bowen’s (2009) document analysis is used for text-based data, which includes content analysis and thematic analysis. Content analysis examines and or ganizes the data with respect to the research quest ion and guiding questions. Thematic analysis aims to describe themes, trends, and gaps in documents. A similar an alytic approach will be taken for interview data, with the focus being identifying case-based themes (Creswell, 2013).

## Preliminary findings

Preliminary document analysis has included  planning documents, reflections, and meeting minute s. This has enabled the identification of emergent codes that will inform (1) which types of existing data may be supplemented by interviews and (2) what kinds of data can be added through interviews (topics or themes not captured through written records). Three main themes have been identified, listed below: Geography and physicality: This refers to physical aspects of CLEs such as the geographic location, setup of the physical space, and design/layout of t he building. Many elements within this theme relate  to accessibility and access. Planning documents may sh ow the location, layout, and accessibility consider ations of the space without fully capturing  how the setup influenced the experiences of participants. Interview data may provide richer perspectives on these physical entry points and how they may be improved for future CLEs. Relationality: This theme refers to how relationships are formed, navigated, sustained, and negotiated. Each relationship serves, in its own unique way, as  an entry point to the CLEs. Primary data analysis and exploration shows that, often, discussions of partn erships begin in informal ways (e.g. casual convers ations that may not be documented through meeting minutes). The se ‘early entry points’ can be important to underst and further (especially through interviews), as they can set the tone for future partnership engagement. Communication: This study takes up the position that each commun ication decision effectively creates, enhances, and/or limits entry points. One type of promotional artifact in the project are flyers used for each CLE. These flyers contain basic information about the ev ent; however, preliminary document analysis suggest s that it is less clear how many people these flyers are reac hing. Further investigation would enable richer dat a about the reach of these flyers and how to create more visibility for future CLEs.

## Discussion points and visuals

This project integrates elements of youth agency, d ata visualization, art, and community collaboration. One goal is to facilitate rich discussion around such elements, prompting attendees to consider how they impact learning (for the MVP project and other contexts). More specifically, considering these elements through the lens of entry points provides a unique perspective to examine how sociocultural learning is shaped through CLEs. Through this discourse, we hope to learn from and with participants in ways that push MVP in new directions. Useful visuals for this poster may include event fl yers or planning schematics of CLEs. These could serve as examples of document analysis data and eng age conference attendees in discussion of other ana lytic approaches. Another possible visual  is flow charts to map networks (of people, organizations, etc.) and streams of information (between individuals, through the MVP team, etc.). Discussions of networks and flow charts could elicit ideas around power dynamics, potential oppor tunities for new directions in information flow, and how to conceptualize expanding or enhancing networks. Through ‘painting a map’ of these entry points, new possibilities can be traced that can enhance how we understand, analyze, and design for learning in this project.
