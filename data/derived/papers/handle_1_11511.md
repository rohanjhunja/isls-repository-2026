# Enhancing Elementary Teachers’ AI-TPACK through a Professional Development Workshop

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The rapid advancement of generative AI presents new opportunities for personalized learning and adaptive instruction in elementary education. However, many teachers lack the necessary AI-TPACK competencies to integrate AI effectively. This study examines the impact of a two-day professional development workshop designed to enhance elementary educators' AI knowledge, pedagogical applications, and ethical awareness. Findings from pre-and post-workshop assessments indicate significant improvements in AI proficiency, instructional integration, and ethical considerations. Educators reported increased confidence in using AI for lesson planning and student engagement, yet highlighted the need for ongoing training in ethical AI use, privacy, and fairness. These results underscore the importance of structured, hands-on AI training to support responsible and effective AI integration in classrooms. Future research should explore long-term impacts on teacher engagement and student learning outcomes.

## Introduction

The swift progression of generative artificial inte lligence (GEN-AI) is significantly reshaping elementary education by facilitating personalized learning exp eriences, adaptive assessments, and interactive ins tructional methods. Nevertheless, numerous educators encounter difficulties in the integration of AI technologies, primarily due to insufficient training and ethical dilemmas, underscoring the necessity for the development of A I-TPACK competencies-comprising technological, pedagogical, and content knowledge essential for effective AI integration. This research examines the effects of a professional development workshop aimed at enhanc ing elementary educators' AI-TPACK skills, with a particular emphasis on the fun ctionalities of AI, pedagogical applications, and ethical considerations. The results reveal  that educators reported increased confidence in utilizing AI for tailored instruction and inquiry-based learning; however, they also recognized chall enges, including ethical issues, resource constraints, and the necessity for age-appropriate AI applications. While the workshop succeeded in improving AI literacy among t eachers, they also indicated a continued need for s upport regarding ethical AI usage, lesson planning, and th e safeguarding of student data privacy. This study contributes to the understanding of how structured training can boost AI-TPACK, thereby promoting responsible and effective integration of AI technologies in elementary educat ional settings. The investigation is framed by two central research questions:

1. How has the workshop impacted elementary teachers' knowledge of AI tools, and how does this affect

their ability to integrate AI into classroom instruction?

2. What new challenges and opportunities do teachers p erceive in AI integration after the workshop, and

what additional skills do they seek to enhance learning outcomes?

## Methods

This study involved 25 elementary school educators (ages 26-62, with an average age of 41 years) who had an average of 16 years of teaching experience and 10 y ears of technology-assisted instruction. However, their exposure to AI was minimal, averaging only 0.66 years. In September 2024, a two-day professional development workshop was conducted to enhance their AI-TPACK competencies through structured phases: (1) Understanding AI Tools, Teachers explored various AI applications, including ChatGPT for simplifying science reading s, an eTutoring system for facilitating science inquiry, and Suno for creating lesson-related music; (2) Experiencing and Reflecting, engaging in AI-enhanced science experiments and discussions; and (3) Co-designing and Refining, where teachers collaboratively developed and refined AI-integrated lesson plans. A comprehensive questionnaire, adapted from Celik’s (2023) Intelligent-TPACK scale, assessed four key AI-TPACK constructs: AI interaction skills, AI applications in teaching, AI tool select ion, and ethical considerations (measured on a five-point Likert scale). Quantitative data from pre-and post-workshop respons es were analyzed using paired-sample t-tests, revealing significant improvements in all construct s. Qualitative reflections were analyzed through th ematic and content analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006), highlighti ng increased confidence, ethical awareness, and ins tructional adaptability.

## Results

Impact of the professional development workshop The workshop significantly enhanced elementary educ ators' AI competencies across four key constructs (Table 1). Teachers showed notable improvements in AI prof iciency, with mean scores increasing from 3.47 to 4.18 (p<0.001), indicating that hands-on training boosted their confidence and technical skills. Their understanding of AI applications in teaching also improved (3.17 to 4.12, p<0.001), enabling them to better leverage AI for personalized learning and adaptive instruction. Additionally, AI tool selection and integration skills rose (3.14 to 4.13, p<0.001), empowering educators to critically evaluat e and apply AI in student-centered learning environments. Finally, ethical awareness increased (3.82 to 4.18, p<0.01), as participants gained insights into AI fairness, bias, and responsible use. These findings highlight the effectiveness of structured AI-TPACK training in preparing teachers for ethical and practical AI integration in elementary education. ## Discussion and conclusion

A key outcome was the improvement in ethical awaren ess, particularly regarding fairness, inclusivity, and data privacy, highlighting the need for AI training beyo nd technical skills. However, teachers expressed a need for ongoing development in advanced AI  applications and ethical considerations, suggesting  that AI-TPACK is an evolving framework requiring continuous support. Fu ture research should examine the long-term impact of AI training, exploring its effects on teacher engageme nt, student outcomes, and  equity in education through larger sample sizes and longitudinal studies. This study u nderscores the potential of AI to transform classro oms, provided that educators receive sustained professional development to navigate both its opportunities and ethical challenges effectively.
