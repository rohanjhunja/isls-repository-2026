# Interdisciplinary Learning in K-12 Humanities and Its Outcomes

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper introduces a novel assessment tool designed to measure Interdisciplinary Competencies within K-12 Humanities Education, emphasizing Interdisciplinary Learning (IL-Humanities). The tool's conceptual framework posits that IL-Humanities has a dual capacity to facilitate cross-disciplinary knowledge building and transdisciplinary identity work among students. We operationalize these concepts into three key competencies: comprehension, integration, and synthesis, each demonstrated through diverse actions. We used the Interdisciplinary Competences Assessment (ICA) to evaluate the effectiveness of IL-Humanities units designed by teachers in ten Israeli middle schools, using a quasi-experimental pre-post intervention/comparison design. While IL-Humanities units positively influenced learners' interdisciplinary competencies, our findings indicate these competencies require further development for optimal impact.

## Introduction

Interdisciplinary pedagogies integrate school subje cts and disciplines together in various ways and have become increasingly popular worldwide in recent years (OECD, 2019; UNESCO, 2021). While most scholarship on the topic focuses on higher education (Kidron & Kali, 2015) and/or on STEM subjects (Takeuchi et al., 2020), this paper focuses on interdisciplinary learning in the humanities (IL-Humanities). IL-Humanities is defined  as any sort of integrative learning activity that meaningfully connects the learner to the broader human condition, culture, history, thought, and products, while drawing from multiple fields within the humanities. Most existing studies in this field form "an advocacy literature, arguing pro or con ba sed on the experiences of individual teachers or schools" (Applebee et al. 2007, p. 1003)  rather than testing their efficacy systematically. This may be partly due to the fact that, in contrast to  STEM (Linn & Eylon, 2011) and higher education  contexts (Boix Mansilla et al.,

2009) where interdisciplinary performance assessment rubrics are used, no such performance measures exist for

IL-Humanities.

## Conceptual framework and literature review

Previous studies have highlighted two distinct learning processes tied IL-Humanities in K-12 settings: Cross-disciplinary knowledge building  involves integrating and advancing learners’ unders tanding by drawing from various subjects, aiming to enhance cognitive abilities by intertwining core concepts and cogniti ve skills from multiple disciplines for new insights (Boix Mansilla, 2017) This process requires an emphasis on knowledge use, a careful treatment of each discipline involved and  appropriate interaction between disciplines. Studies find that IL-Humanities help learners advance their understanding of complex concepts, develop sophisticated higherorder thinking skills and enhance their integration abilities (Kuisma and Ratinen, 2021; Schuitema et al., 2009). Transdisciplinary identity work  catalyzes change by shaping learners’ identities, values, and  worldviews, thus advancing their personal development. The process of authoring one's narrative identity (McAdams et al., 2013) through learning is the focus of transdisciplinary humanities p rograms. The humanities enhance identity ork through cultural and social immersion (Tay, 2018), and studies support this: Applebee et al. (2007) found that transdisciplinary curricula generated significantly more student disc ussions, position-taking, and autonomous learning thandid cross-disciplinary curricula. Other identity related impacts of  of IL-Humanities include deepening identity (Beames & Ross, 2010); enhancing spiritual awareness (Clark & Button, 2011) and equitable multicultural identity building (Ferreira et al., 2022). Understanding the relationship between cross-disciplinary knowledge building and transdisciplinary identity work is key to designing powerful learning environments that capitalize on the unique affordances of IL-Humanities in K-12 settings. One way to do so is to consider these two processes as levels of progressi on, (Applebee et al., 2007). Another is to consider them as co-constructive (Freire, 1996/1970). We suggest evaluating both processes through the concept of interdisciplinary learning competences. Proposed schema of interdisciplinary competences Competences are multi-dimensional capabilities involve skills, knowledge,  attitudes and values (Rychen, 2016). Since they combine knowledge and identity building, they are a w ell-suited concept to assess both. Our novel schema introduces three pivotal interdisciplinary competencies tailored for IL-Humanities: Comprehension encompasses learners' understanding of the connections between text, context, prior knowledge, and related concepts. Comprehending texts across multiple disciplines invo lves identifying different contextual disciplinary frameworks and being able to compare t hem (Bråten al., 2020). We posit that students who are competent in interdisciplinary comprehension can (1) identify and discuss similarities between sources f rom different disciplines, and (2) identify and expound  upon differences between them.

Integration involves incorporating different elements into a joint schema. (Boix-Mansilla, 2017) Students who are competent at interdisciplinary integration will be able to (1)

a source from another; (2) integrate sources across disciplinary boundaries to reach new and shared insights. Synthesis entails the application of integrated knowledge to reflect and connect, by bringing the integration into conversation with oneself. The ability to synthesize can involve self-reflection, identity formation, moral development and/or creative pursuits (Sill, 2001). Students competent in synthesizing can (2) express their ide ntity creatively through artistic expressions, social act ion or dialogue; (1) leverage integrative insights to reflect on their self, identity, values and beliefs. ## Research questions

To assess the efficacy of IL-Humanities we collaborated with educational practit ioners and policymakers in the design and implementation of a study on interdisciplinary learning in the humanities in ten Israeli middle schools. We asked two questions:

(1) What is the effectiveness of  a tool developed for evaluating interdisciplinary competences in the

humanities?

(2) What are the outcomes of IL-Humanities in terms of interdisciplinary competences?

## Methodology

The study included a series of professional development (PD) courses that provided teachers with background on IL-Humanities and its underlying competences and invited teacher teams to design their own teaching units. The courses lasted two months and spanned 30 hours.  Teams of teachers designed 1–3-month IL units based on the humanities subject matter that they taught.  IL programs developed included: Eco-Poetics, Women Path Blazers, and Person and Environment. Institutional Review Board approvals were obtained The time-lag between the preand post-intervention assessments ranged between 4.2-6.3 months. Students were administered the ICA to test for changes in their interdisciplinary competencies after studying the units.

## Participants

Participants were 586 students who were administered the ICA, 297 of whom completed the measure at both time points. 198 were the intervention group and 99 were a control group, who didn't learn IL-Humanities units. Materials: Tool development and analysis The Interdisciplinary Competences Assessment (ICA) mobilized the classification scheme described above. Student received a pair of unfamiliar texts loosely linked by a common theme (e.g., women's rights) but diverging in context, style, and disciplinary origin (e.g., one text typically associated with history class and  the other with Bible class). Six essay questions were designed to elicit various applications of interdisciplinary competences:

Inter-rater reliability, calculated using Krippendorff's Alpha, ranged from.74 to.94 per question. Principal-components analysis with varimax rotation of the pretest and posttest scores suggested a single factor solution that explained 40.1%-43.1% of the variance. We cautiously interpret this to indicate a single construct behind all questions—interdisciplinary competences. Reliability was good: Cronbach’s alpha scores were 0.70 in the pretest and 0.74 in the posttest.

## Discussion

This study examined the outcomes of two processes of interdisciplinary learning in K-12 humanities units—crossdisciplinary knowledge building and transdisciplinary identity work—in terms of learner competences tested with the Interdisciplinary Competencies Assessmen t (ICA). Our findings offer preliminary affirmation  of its validity and reliability for assessing IL-Humanities outcomes while indicating the need for a dditional work on the instrument and construct. Interdisciplinary compete nces seem amenable to improveme nt following IL-Humanities. The modest improvements across the comp etencies suggest a need for further pedagogical innovation.

In light of contemporary societal challenges, such as information overload, fake news, identity politi cs, and political violence, schools should consider emb racing discourses intentionally designed to sustain  liberalism and value pluralism (Novis Deutsch, 2018). Our findings highlight the central role that IL-Humanities can play in realizing this goal, by mediating meaningful identi ty work and cultivating students’ competences to en gage with multiple perspectives fruitfully. One of the promis ing venues of this study was a focus on competences  that undergird IL-Humanities. Further research is recommended, includ ing testing relationships between the two processes of interdisciplinary learning and assessi ng the effects of change in each learning activity on interdisciplinary competences.
