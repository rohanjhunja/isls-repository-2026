# Problems of Practice in Scaling Computational Thinking and Digital Literacies in Pre-service Teacher Education Efforts

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster presents a holistic characterization of the problems of practice that emerge as faculty in colleges of education work to scale institution-wide integration of computational and digital literacies across teacher education programs. By identifying the thorny issues inherent to institutional change this analysis supports teacher educators working to transform teacher preparation programs to center teacher candidates’ computing integration.

## Introduction

As interest in preparing teacher candidates to equitably and meaningfully integrate computing-related instruction (e.g. computer science, computational thinking, dig ital literacies) into their future teaching grows, scholars have shared rationales articulating the why of computing integration, have developed frameworks  specifying the what of computing integration, and designed curricular s equences mapping out the where of computing integration across course work and field experiences (Falloon, 2020; Foulger  et al., 2020). Scholars further argue that translating these visions of integrated computing into initiatives to transform teacher education programs at scale requires models of coherent institutional change (Warr et al., 2023). However, given the long-standing difficulties of achieving coherent institutional change at scale  in education (Cuban, 1984), it is important to the orize the challenges surrounding scale in order to provide pr actitioners with more realistic guidance and schola rs with theoretically robust analytic frames.

Cynthia Coburn’s (2003) expansive theorization of scale–comprising issues of ownership, depth, spread, and sustainability–helps to explain dynamic complexities that so often pull systems change efforts up short. Yet, practical guidance for educators on how to achieve ownership, depth, spread, and sustainability, and h ow to navigate tensions within and between these, is spar se. This is especially true for the context of the current study: faculty and administrators working to transform 15 colleges of educati on (in a major University system in the Northeast) to support future teachers to equitably integrate computational thinking and digital literacies into K12 classrooms. The current study aims to learn alongsi de practitioners (Safir & Dugan, 2021) to illumin ate the problems of practice they encounter, and creative s olutions they develop, in their efforts to achieve ownership, depth, spread, and sustainability of their computing-integrated teacher education initiatives.

## Methods

Within the context of a larger research-practice partnership, longitudinal ethnographic data was collected on each participating college team’s strategic planning pro cesses, and triangulated across field observations and memos, video-recorded interviews, and organizational documentation. Informed by the scholarly literature on scaling and grounded immersion in the study data (Strauss & Corbin, 1997) an analytic framework was developed (see Figure

1) to account for the issues teams contended with in their institutional change processes.

## Findings

Using Coburn’s (2003) scale framework as an organiz ing guide, Figure 1 summarizes the resulting 12 ana lytic constructs within the study context of computing-integrated teacher education (CITE). Results show h ow these analytic constructs, in relation to a c ollege team’s unique context, hold explanatory powe r to characterize (a) the different ways and points at which college teams became stuck in their efforts to scale CITE, and (b) the shifts in focus of strategic planning as problems of practice  became salient. For example, to support ownership via autonomy, a college team encouraged faculty to creatively design CITE learning opportunities without constraint. This lack of constraint, however, came into tension  with the issue of depth via coherency as the desig ns did not congeal around a shared vision of an instructional core. Once salient, the team shifted their strategi c planning to address this tension.

## Conclusion

As computational technologies create new needs, and opportunities, across all areas of teaching and learning, it's critical that colleges of education transform their  pre-service education efforts. The framework shared her e attempts to provide practical guidance around the challenges of institutionalizing and scaling these efforts.
