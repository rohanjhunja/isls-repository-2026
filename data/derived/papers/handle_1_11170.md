# Fostering K-12 Students’ Computational Thinking Skills Using Collaborative Technology-rich Learning Environments: A Systematic Review

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In today’s technology-driven society, mastering Computational Thinking (CT) skills is crucial for students. To develop these skills, leveraging collaborative technology-rich environments (TREs) can be an effective approach. Examples of those TREs include block-based programming learning environments, immersive digital games, and robotic toolkits. In this systematic review, following PRISMA, we identified 56 studies from the literature. We investigated how collaboration within TREs influences the development of CT skills among K-12 students. The findings underscore the value of collaborative learning environments within TREs for effectively fostering students’ CT skills, emphasizing the importance of creating impactful learning environments that prioritize collaboration in CT skill development.

## Introduction

Computational thinking (CT) has been defined in various ways, with some emphasizing cognitive skills and others focusing on more tangible skills. For instan ce, Shute et al. (2017) and Wing (2008) relate CT t o a range of problem-solving and analytical skills that enable individuals to break down complex problems into smaller, more manageable parts. Similarly, Wing (2006, p. 33) describes CT as “solving problems, designing systems, and understanding human behavior by drawing on the concepts fundamental to computer science.” These definitions highlight the cognitive aspects of CT, such as problem-solving and analytical thinking. On the other hand, some researchers describe CT as encompassing more concrete skills. Grover and Pea (2013) propose that CT includes various sub-skills, such as algorithmic thinking, pattern recog nition, abstraction, and decomposition. These skills are more closely tied to the practical application of computational concepts. Furthermore, Denner et al. (2012) suggest that CT i s a comprehe nsive skill set composed of three fundamental elements: programming, documenting and understanding software, and designing for usability. This definition emphasizes the importance of hands-on experience with programming and software development in developing CT skills.

In this paper, we refer to CT as a combination of c ognitive and practical skills. We recognize the importance of problem-solving and analytical abilities in breaking down c omplex problems, as well as the value of hands-on experience with programming and software development in fostering CT. By considering both the cognitive and practical aspects of CT, we aim to provide a more comprehensive understanding o f this critical skill set and its potential applications in various domains. In the modern era of technology,  CT competency is an essential life skill that should be taught in early education. (Wing, 2006; Yadav et al., 2017). Studies indicate that proficiency in CT correlates with both academic achievement and career advancement (Hurt et al., 2023; Israel-Fishelson et al., 2021). Various interventions have been implemented to enhance CT skills among learners (Tang et al., 2020). For instance, robotic tools like Lego robotics (Leonard et al., 2 023), online games (Sun et al., 2023; Cheng et al.,  2022), unplugged activities such as card games (Guimarães et al., 2020), and storytelling (Chen et al., 2023)  have been employed to enhance CT skills.

Collaboration during learning activities is effecti ve in promoting CT skills among K-12 students (e.g., Lin et al, 2022; Standl, 2016). Collaborative learning involves a group of learners working together to achieve a mutual goal and through that collaboratio n improve their knowledge and skills (Hmelo-Silver, 2004). Collaborative learning enables students to e xchange ideas and ask questions, fostering a dynami c environment for knowledge sharing and inquiry (Laal  & Laal, 2012). The effectiveness of collabora tive learning in promoting CT skills could be due to pro viding opportunities for peer learning and receivin g feedback (Shahin et al., 2022), and engaging studen ts in problem-solving, critical thinking, and effective communication (Ardito et al., 2020).

Advances in technology have created new opportuniti es for collaborative learning in technology-rich environments (TREs) (Barfurth, 1995). TREs refer to classrooms or digital learning environments where technology is extensively used to support and enhan ce the learning process (Shute et al., 2016). TREs that are designed to promote CT skills do that through i nteractive and immersive learning experiences (Zhao  & Shute, 2019). For example, digital educational game s and other virtual learning environments that prom ote CT skills provide a safe and supportive space to pr actice and experiment with CT skills (e.g., De Jesú s & Silveira, 2021; Galdo et al., 2022; Zhao & Shute, 2 019). Therefore, integrating collaborative learning  in TREs can be a powerful way to promote CT skills among K-12 students (Turchi et al., 2019). Multiple empirical, design-based studies have been conducted to examine the ef fectiveness of collaborative learning in TREs (e.g., Wen et al., 2 02; Latulipe & De Jaeger, 2022). However, to our knowledge, there is no systematic review of the stu dies focusing on TREs that facilitate K-12 students’ CT through collaboration In this systematic review, we  synthesize the existing literature on collaborativ e learning of CT skills in TREs, identify gaps in the  current literature, and provide suggestions for fu ture studies. The findings will be valuable for educators  and researchers interested in promoting CT skills in collaborative TREs. The research questions guiding this review are as follows:

1.  What types of collaborative TREs are used to promote students’ CT skills in K-12?

2. What CT topics are being taught and promoted in those TREs?

3.  What are the effects of collaboration in TREs on K-12 students’ CT skills?

4.  What are the learning theories implemented in the c ollaborative TREs to promote students’ CT

skills in K-12?

## Method

To address our research questions, we conducted a s ystematic review using the updated PRISMA (Page et al., 2021) guidelines. A comprehensive search of relevant databases including ScienceDirect, ERIC, Scopus, ProQuest, Google Scholar, and Web of Science was co nducted to identify studies on collaborative TREs and their effectiveness in promoting K-12 students’ CT skills. Using several search string s (e.g., (Collaboration OR collaborative learning OR collabo rative) AND (“computational thinking” OR game OR digital OR coding OR programming OR “blocked-based programming”) AND (K-12)), we searched in the mentioned databases. To avoid publication bias and ensure a comprehensive review of the literature, ou r inclusion criteria included all types of manuscript s (e.g., peer-reviewed or non-peer-reviewed) meeting the specified criteria: studies (1) published between 2 006-2022 (inclusive). We chose this timeframe because following Janette Wing’s seminal paper in 2006, int erest in CT began to grow in academic settings; (2) written in English; (3) focused on enhancing CT in collaborative TREs; and (4) in K-12 education context. The exclusion criteria were: (1) studies before 2006; (2) not in English; (3) students with disabilities or adult learners; and (4) low-quality articles. Following the inclusion/exclusion criteria, we included 56 studies for review (see Figure 1).

To extract the data, we created  a spreadsheet to collect and summarize the key featur es per study (e.g., title, manuscript type, field of st udy, research method, name of the learning environment, target audience, sample size, and learning theories). Subsequently, both researchers individually extract ed data from each study through several roun ds and reconciled any differences. This process yielded 56 studies, with 11 studies included through hand search.

## Results

The 56 studies were from a diverse set of manuscrip t types (38 journal articles; 6 conference papers; 9 proceedings; 2 symposiums; 1 dissertations). These studies included participants ranging from 3rd to 1 2th grade. Moreover, the studies selected in this review originated from various fields of study such as computer science (n = 27), educational technology (n = 11), learning sciences (n = 17), and mathematics education (n = 1). Furthermore, among the studies in educational  technology (n = 7) and learning sciences (n = 6), there was a notable focus on primary students, indicating  a growing interest in understanding their learning dynamics. In contrast, within the field of computer  science, only 4 studies specifically targeted prim ary education, suggesting a relatively lesser emphasis on this demographic. Regarding RQ1, we encountered a rich diversity of e nvironments used within the scope of these studies. Among the various TREs, digital games (n =  6), block-based programming environments (n = 22), robotics toolkits (n = 6), and Programming tools (n  = 5) stood out prominently. Each of these settings contributed uniquely to the educational landscape, offering distinctive learning opportunities and experiences. Notably,  block-based programming learning environments (e.g., Scra tch [13 studies used Scratch]) emerged as a leading example. The prevale nce and influence of Scratch in facilitating learni ng experiences highlight the significance and impact of block-based programming environments in educational research (see Figure 2).

## Discussion

In this systematic review, we investigated 56 studi es that leveraged collaboration within TREs to foster the development of computational thinking skills among K-12 students. Our analysis revealed that these studies targeted a broad range of audiences, and originated from different academic disciplines. The findings suggest the use of various digital platforms to facilitate collaborative learning of CT skills, including digital games, block-based programming environments, programming tools, and robotic toolkits. Each of these platforms offers unique and interactive opportunities for students to engage collaboratively with CT concepts. Notably, the prevalence of block-based programming platforms indicates their widespr ead utilization by researchers as effective tools for collaborative CT skill devel opment. The prevalence of block-based programming platforms may be attributed to their user-friendly nature, capacity to reduce entry barriers to programming, visually appealing interface, and their facilitation of collaborative learning, creativity, and skill development in computational thinking for K-12 students (João et al., 2019). The majority of papers did not deeply explore the C T skills or specific concepts within CT, given its multifaceted nature. This finding highlights a need for more explicit definitions and focus on par ticular areas of CT skills. Addressing this gap by specifying targeted concepts could improve pedagogical strategies within TREs, enabling more effective integration of CT skills into educational frameworks. Additionally, including a diverse age range of part icipants demonstrates the adaptability and scalability of collaborative TREs as a platform for fostering CT skills. The studies’ origins spanned multiple academic disciplines, including computer science, e ducational technology, and learning sciences. This diversity emphasizes the pivotal role of collaborative digital environments in promoting CT skills, fostering cross-disciplinary collaborations, and contributing to co mprehensive educational advancements. The prominence of computer science in this context creates exciting opportunities for interdisciplinary research, allowing for the integration of pedagogical approac hes, learning theories, and technological innovatio ns to optimize the enhancement of computational thinking skills among K-12 students. Our investigation into the learning theories utiliz ed by researchers has uncovered a noticeable gap in the adoption of a theoretical framework across n umerous studies, 42 out of the total did not explic itly mention or incorporate any learning theory. The a bsence of a clear learning theory within these stud ies can give rise to several challenges in educational rese arch, including the lack of a theoretical framework, difficulties in interpretation, and limited generalizability. Moreover, it is worth highlighting that a significant proportion of papers in the computer science field did not mention any specific learning theory. This indicates a potential area of improvement for resea rchers in this domain. It is worth noting that constructivism was the most domin ant theoretical perspective within those limited st udies that explicitly employ a learning theory. Our analysis has shed lig ht on various learning approaches aligned with constructivism, including game-based learning, collaborative learning, and problem-based learning. Careful examination of these approaches can provide valuable insights into the pedagogical principles that contribute to the effectiveness of collaborative CT skill development.

## Limitations

We faced several limitations in conducting this review. First, the review was limited to studies published in English, potentially excluding relevant research co nducted in other languages. Second, the lack of a universally accepted definition for CT poses a chal lenge in comparing and synthesizing findings across studies. Third, the included studies employed vario us assessment methods, which may lead to different interpretations of results and make it difficult to  draw definitive conclusions. And, future res earch would benefit from a more standardized approach to defining and assessing CT skills.

## Future directions and conclusion

This research aims to synthesize findings on foster ing K-12 students’ computational thinking skills using collaborative TREs. As our review continues, we wil l address our last research question related to the specific design elements that enhance students’  CT skills in collaborative TREs. This upcoming rese arch intends to deepen our understanding of how particul ar collaborative aspects within digital settings continuously influence and improve the development of CT skills among K-12 students. Additionally, we plan to refine our focus to emphasize conceptual in sights over empirical findings, thereby enhancing t he overall coherence and relevance of our work.
