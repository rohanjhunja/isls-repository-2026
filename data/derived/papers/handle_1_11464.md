# Curation As Design: Co-designing Learning Experiences for Public School Education System

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This practice-oriented paper shares insights from a research-practice partnership for designing learning experiences through curation of open educational resources (OERs). The collaboration aimed to improve classroom interactions, inclusivity, and learning outcomes in secondary schools by maximizing the pedagogical affordances of Interactive Flat Panels (IFP). This paper identifies the curation process as a complex exercise entailing design iterations while negotiating stakeholder expectations and outlines the intricacies and challenges faced during this process. It highlights how the curation team navigated these challenges to co-design learning experiences, emphasizing that pedagogically informed curation is not just about identifying relevant educational resources and mapping to curricular requirements – it is very much a design process. Exploring the practice of curation as design, the paper reveals emergence of negotiated space in a research-practice partnership and shares insights about the considerations involved in OER curation for large-scale implementation in a state education system.

## The context

Interactive Flat Panels (IFPs), also known as ‘smar t boards’, are large digital displays (typically 75  inches or larger) equipped with touchscreens, built-in digital computer processors and operating system s. In recent years, IFP devices have been increasingly installed in schools through various governmental and educational initiatives. The research-practice partnership – Telangana IFP OER Project  – intended to harness the pedagogical affordances of IFP devices in the classrooms to enh ance the quality of interactions, inclusivity and o verall pedagogical practice in classrooms. To achieve this, textbook-mapped digital Open Educational Resources (OERs) were curated for all seven subjects (Biology, Physics, Mathematics and Social Sciences as well as English, Telugu and Urdu languages) taught in grades 8, 9 and 10. During the pilot phase a research-backed, comprehensive framework was developed known as the RAP Curation R ubric to assess the quality of digital resources ac ross three parameters: Relevance, Affordance and Pedagogical practice (CETE, 2023a). In the main project phase subject-specific design principles were identified based on  the RAP rubric, which guided the curation process in each subject. A template was designed to organize curation which included classroom enactment activity with the OER and assessment instructions. Detailed notes for teachers were provided to facilitate integration of the OERs in the classroom.

The scope of this collaborative project between the State Council for Education Research and Training (the nodal state agency for preparing school curriculum and te acher training) and the Tata Institute of Social Sciences (University as research partner), involved extensive collaboration between practice and research faculty from both institutions, school teachers and external subject matter experts (SMEs). The curated resources were v etted by a quality control (QC) panel to ensure compliance wit h quality  standards drawn from learning and technology design. The project spanned over six months (June t o December 2023) and curated/created over 80 7 OERs, organized into 303 concept-wise templates across seven subjects, aimed to be i ntegrated into more than 14,000 classrooms in 4,000 secondary schools in the Telangana state. Objectives of the project The project revolved around the central question: How to leverage the pedagogical affordances of IFP devices in the classrooms to enhance the quality of interactio ns, inclusivity and overall pedagogy? During the pi lot, the university faculty identified OER s across four subjects and conducted classroom teac hing in two schools as a demonstration exercise along with teachers. Based o n the experiences of the pilot study, the project a imed to curate 500 OERs across seven subjects for grades 8, 9, and 10. However, from the outset it was known that OERs wil l not be readily available for all the textbook concepts and that not all topics lend themselves to be effectively taught using the IFP. Therefore, the OERs were to be mapped to select concepts/topics in the textb ook. These OERs would then be integrated into class room teaching across the state of Telangana. It was anticipated that the integration of curated OERs, along with teachers' professional development, will lead to the three outcomes: improved quality of classroom interactions; improved inclusivity in classroom; and, improved learning outcomes. Curation as design The foundation of our work on this project lies in a clear understanding of Open Educational Resources  (OERs) and what curation of OER involves. According to UNE SCO which has been advocating and promoting open educational resources around the world, “OER a re learning, teaching and research materials in any  format and medium that reside in the public domain or are unde r copyright that have been released under an open l icense, that permit no-cost access, re-use, re-purpose, adaptation and redistribution by ot hers.” (UNESCO, 2019). However, creating resources is an intense process a nd requires multiple specialized skills which makes  it an expensive proposition. Especially in a resource-constrained public education system, it seems both inefficient and unsustainable to create digital educational resourc es or to buy licensed digital content for every dig ital intervention. Therefore, the idea of OER and openne ss in the context of education is fundamentally abo ut democratizing access to knowledge and promoting inc lusion, equity and enr ichment to foster quality education for all (CETE, 2023b; UNESCO-MGIEP, 2019). There are many OER resources and repositories available on the world wide web.

Research shows that in India enormous amounts have been spent on digital content and resource creation or purchase of licensed content (Mulla et al., 2023; Sharma,  2022). Moreover, the evolving nature of digital/software technologies gives rise to issues of compatibility and interoperability which render the old versions of digital resources obsolete and unusable  (Sharma, 2022). Therefore, for this project where a large number of digital educational resources were to be provisioned to a large-scale public education system, it seemed both viable and efficient to curate OERs rather tha n create resources afresh. Nevertheless, it is import ant to recognize that the process of adaptation and contextualization should be a serious exercise so that the resultant experience fulfills the intended learning goals. The process of identifying, evaluating and selectin g pedagogically appropriate resources which meet th e defined learning objective is called curation (CETE, 2023b).

As depicted in Fig ure 1, the curation process is an iterative process whi ch entails thoughtful considerations of pedagogical, technological, produ ction and administrative aspects and involves multi ple steps and skillsets. Consequently, the curation process w as guided by a rese arch and practice-backed, comprehensive framework (RAP Curation Rubric) to assess the quali ty of digital resources across three parameters-Relevance, Affordance and Pedagogical practice (CETE, 2023a). For each of the seven subjects, the design principles were drawn based on the RAP rubric and respective disciplinary learning objectives as outlined in the  official textbook and curriculum framework. The ru bric for English, for instance, focused on eliciting student expression through speaking, writing, and enacting activities to promote critical literacy and context-based meaning-making. In mathematics, it was about making mathema tical thinking visible, enabling students to explore concepts through multiple representations such as drawings, graphs, and real-life modeling. Whereas the social studies aimed to foster multidisciplinary approach and promote historical thinking through reference to multiple sources, mapping and visualization through interactive timelines. Negotiated space in a research-practice partnership As a large-scale collaborative project, the OER curation turne d out to be a complex and multilayered exercise. The subsequent sections discuss the key challenges faced during the curation process and the strategie s used to overcome them.

Challenges in design The design of teaching-learning resources is inherently a collaborative ex ercise – it requires experts in learning design and pedagogy to work in concert with teacher s, school leaders and educationists. This collabora tion is critical as it bridges the gap between pedagogical principles and the realities of  specific classroom interactions. Designing in isolation, on the other hand, can lead to resistance from teachers, as illustrated by the following two scenarios:

1. Misalignment with the prior knowledge of students:  We might design an age-appropriate pedagogically sound

problem-based activity in science for class 8, thinking it could work well for any student of a specific curri cular board. However, complexity of task may not align wi th the prior knowledge of student s in a particular region/school/classroom context. In such cases, teachers might revert to familiar methods rather than adopting the recommended new activity.

2. Misalignment with the prior knowledge/current practices of teachers: Even when teaching strategies are backed

by research, teachers may be hesitant to implement them due to uncertainty about their effectiveness i n their specific classroom context and a lack of knowledge or confidence in their ability to execute them. These scenarios highlight the importance of undertaking design as a collaborative activity. However, this approach is time-intensive and may not always align with the mutuall y defined timelines between the state and academic partners. Therefore, one must be prepared for revisions and negotiations throughout the process. Curation vs. design The discussion of design challenges naturally leads  to the question: Why focus on design when the prim ary task is curation? Curation in education is often perceiv ed as the process of identifying relevant resources, organizing them according to a specific hierarchy/sequence and  presenting them in a manner that enables effective  access and use by teachers and learners. However, in the a bsence of specific design guidelines created with r esearchbacked pedagogical processes/practices in mind, the  process of curation could end up becoming a subjec tive exercise.

Yet another consequence of an undefined curation ex ercise will be lack of coherence in the resultant l earning experiences for students. Inadequacy of open resources in social sciences and Indian languages The task of curating OER involved identifying resources that not only aligned with our design principles but also mapped to the official textbook. This process revea led a significant challenge: the dearth of relevant  open resources for Indian languages and Social Sciences significantly impeded our curation efforts and often required the adaptation or creation of resources to fill the se gaps. In such circumstances free/libre open-source software (FLOSS) tools helped us create OERs with lower cost and better efficiency. An ecosystem of such FLOSS tools has enormous potential to enrich the OER ecosystem in non-English languages and Southern contexts.  We recognized the importance and urgent need for governments, educational technology companies and organizations to adopt open standards and open license mechanism to maximize educational benefits of technology and bridge the multidimensional digital divide.

## Teacher preferences and pedagogical implications

Another challenge we encountered was the mismatch between teachers' preferences for certain types of resources and the pedagogical goals of promoting critical thi nking within/across disciplines and active learning  (where students actively think about what they learn). Our experience revealed that teachers often gravitate more towards content-delivery formats like videos, rather than interactive elements (such as science simulations) that encourage scientific thinking through interactivity in studen ts. Videos are also easier to use for teachers who may not b e comfortable with interactive resources, especially in time-constrained classroom settings. Interactive resourc es require more technical know-how and preparation, which some teachers may find c hallenging. However, researchers and learning designers in the project impressed upon teachers that making use of only passive formats like videos pose a challenge to enhancing classroom interactions and thinking skills.  In contrast, interactive resources were demonstrated to offer valuable oppor tunities for students to actively engage with the m aterials, formulate hypotheses and develop problem-solving skills. This required much negotiation with  educators and teachers at [practice partner] to use a blend of interactive resources in addition to videos and quizzes. Addressing this challenge requires sustained professional deve lopment support for teachers, demonstrating the ped agogical value of interactive resources, their relevance and  potential to impact learning. However, since the p roject concluded in December 2023, the University could not be involved in the planned professional development effort to support teachers in effectively integrating OERs for active teaching-learning processes. Nonetheless, towards the end of the project it was refreshing to see many teachers started exploring the interactive features of IFPs and OER to foster active learning and other intended project outcomes. This can be attributed to sustained negotiations through co-designing approaches. This also highlights that co-designing could be a powerful strategy for effective teacher professional development.

## Key insights and conclusion

The project highlighted that curation is not just a bout identifying and selecting relevant resources; it is an act of design. This involves determining the types of reso urces to select and adapting or creating new resour ces as needed, based on the guiding design principles. Designing the curation process o f teaching-learning resources should be a collaborative effort involving experts in learning design, pedagogy, teachers, and educationists. This collaboration helps bridge the gap between pedagogi cal princ iples and classroom realities. For a sustained intervention teacher buy-in is crucial. The project revealed that teachers m ight resist new teaching strategies or resources if they lack the prior knowledge in inter preting/creatively using them or are uncertain abou t their effectiveness in their classroom context. Pau city of contextually relevant OERs especially in non-English languages and Global South contexts demands urgent attention from key stakeholders in the public education system. Therefore, the design team needs to be prepared for significant adaptation of existing resources as well as creation of new resources to fill the gaps. To effectively integrate interactive resources into classrooms, one needs to account for professional development for teacher s. Only through co-designing and demonstrating the value of diverse resource types we can envisage a pedagogical shift among teachers. And lastly, the involvement of experts from state-curricular agency, the research partner and a quality co ntrol panel draw attention to the importance of maintaining high standards and avoiding conflicts w ith regards to expectations in OER curation through  a negotiated space.

These lessons underscore the complexity of OER cura tion and integration in educational settings and highlight the need for a multifaceted approach that  comprehensively considers pedagogical principles, technological affordances, prior knowledge and soci o-economic background of students, teacher competenci es, current practices and local contexts.
