# Developing Learning Science Competencies and Curriculum for Teacher Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This design-based case study follows the design and implementation of Science of Learning and Development (SoLD) competencies across two successive Competency-Based Education (CBE) programs, the first a STEM focused licensure program and the second a program focusing on design, equity, and justice for in-service teachers. The goal of positioning the Science of Learning and Development as a core competency and integrating it within the two programs was to support educators in becoming well-equipped to support their students’ learning and development through the whole child approach. We offer insights on the conceptualization of an overarching framework and competencies, development of assessments and curricular designs, and strategic supports/learner interactions as a possible model for SoLD–focused teacher preparation/education. Furthermore, this approach reinforces the importance of SoLD and communicates that SoLD is not distinct from pedagogy which marks a shift from how SoLD is often presented to pre and in service teachers.

Purpose While institutional changes in schools may progress  slowly, meaningful change can be realized at the c lassroom level. We advocate for educators to adopt an inquir y-based approach, viewing their classrooms and school s as places for exploration and experimentation, as sugg ested by Cochran-Smith and Lytle (1999). They propose that effective teaching knowledge emerges when educators  engage in purposeful investigation within their ow n classrooms and schools, while also critically examining the knowledge and theories provided by learning science research as “generative materials for interrogation and interpretation” (p250). We contend that Science of Learning and Development (SoLD) research findings can serve as valuable "generative materials." The objective o f anchoring teacher education in SoLD is to empower teachers to apply this knowledge adaptively, focusing on the most relevant aspects for their students' learning and development while adjusting to evolving school and classroom contexts. Rather than relying on slow and  widespread school reforms to promptly bridge the d ivide between SoLD research findings and current teaching and assessment practices, we propose that teacher education offers a more promising avenue for effecting change within classrooms. This paper argues for the introduction of SoLD findings into teacher education to equip the n ext generation of teachers with the knowledge to ap ply these findings in their own classrooms. Although research  in the learning sciences has been interdisciplinar y and far reaching to conceptualize a whole child approach, “ Challenges to implementation include limitations of curriculum available to address the range of goals articulated here and the breadth of knowledge that teachers need to learn to adapt such curricula in ways that address students’ needs” (Darling-Hammond et al., 2020).  This design-based case study explores the implementation of SoLD competencies across two consecutive CompetencyBased Education (CBE) programs: a STEM-focused licensure program and a ma ster's program emphasizing design, equity, and justice for in-service teachers. The primary aim was to position S oLD as a fundamental competency in both programs, equipping educators to  effectively support students' holistic development through a whole child approach. This approach underscores t he integral relationship between SoLD and pedagogy. ## Conceptual framework

The Science of Learning and Development Alliance fr amework (Darling-Hammond et al., 2020) was chosen as the basis for this work. This framework suggests pr inciples of practice for educators to support whole  child development in four domains: Supportive Environment, Productive Instructional Strategies, Social and Emotional Development, and System of Supports. These domains are depicted as quadrants of a circle that envelop the “Whole Child.” The SoLD Alliance framework was chosen because it presents a  more c ohesive model of development that frames it as a multidimensional process that unfolds over time (Darling-Hammond et al., 2020). W e modified the SoLD Alliance framework (Figure 1) with  the addition of  a larger concentric circle to visually accentuate the interaction of sociopolitical and historical contexts with learning and development as well as the importance of being mindful of how they shape educators’ biases, influence students’ experiences, and inform curriculum and school policies. This outer system serves as a lens through which educators should critically evaluate research on learning and development and reflect on implementation of this research. As such, this modified framework emphasizes the interplay among the three ne sted systems, rather than foregrounding one and it suggests that SoLD research must be understood and applied in context.

## The work discussed here is situated in the context of

two Competency-Based Education (CBE) programs (Table 1). CBE is a model where students progress by demonstrating mastery of specific outcomes at their own pace, guided by personalized learning experienc es (Patrick, Kennedy, & Powell, 2013). P rogram 1 is a master's and licensure program designed for prospec tive middle and high school math and science educat ors. It comprises ten "challenges" that structure the learn ing and assessment of distinct competencies related  to real-world teaching challenges. Program 2 is an in-development master's program centered on design thinking, equity, justice, and educational innovation for practicing educators. It includes a foundation institute that emphasizes core competencies followed by two project cycles during which learners explore and implement personalized d esign questions to enhance their teaching practice.  Both Program 1 and Program 2 were designed through a “backwards design” approach, a best practice in CBE, wh ich involves defining the competencies (outcomes) f irst, followed by assessments, and finally the curriculum and learning experiences (Bushway, Dodge, & Long, 2018). ## Findings

The following findings are derived from the three-phase design cycle and offer insights on the conceptualization of competencies, development of assessments and curricular designs, and strategic supports/learner interactions. Developing competency framework The first finding describes how the conceptual fram ework was operationalized through five SoLD learnin g objectives. These learning objectives are innovativ e because they defined and communicated the type and level of skill we expected from learners and our  program values. The learning objectives are concret e statements describing a set of behaviors expected for demonstration of the competency which include “Drawing on connected concepts and principles from across the Science of Learning and Development, eva luate whether a practice supports the multidimensional development of all le arners” and applying research based approaches that  a) support motivation, self-directed learning, and deep conceptual understandin g within a content domain, in light of their specific learners’ experiences, goals, and  contexts, b) develop conditions that provide learn ers a sense of safety and belonging and that foster trust and stro ng relationships, c) support the social-emotional skills, habits, and mindsets that lead to confident, independent learners, and d) leverage the multi-disciplinary resources, support teams, and personnel within and beyond the formal l earning environment in order to meet the needs of l earners and address learning barriers.

Authentic assessments The second set of findings focuses on authentic ass essments within the SoLD framework t hat enable teacherlearners to demonstrate both knowledge and practica l application. Two assessment approaches were devel oped, assessing not only learners' use of research-backed strategies in authentic contexts but also their comprehension of the research within the broader SoLD framework. This design aimed to prompt thoughtful evaluation a nd reflection on the application of these strategies in individual teaching contexts. It positioned SoLD research as "generative material," integral to pedagogy and crucial for practice improvement. Both assessment strategies rely on explicit connection to SoLD competencies through  reflection and additional faculty coaching for lea rner support. The first strategy, labeled curated proble ms of practice, was implemented in Prog ram 1 for preservice teachers. Assessments were structured as scenario-based challenges covering themes such as classroom culture, content-specific pedagogy, differentiation, collaborative l earning, and assessment. SoLD was consistently assessed within each theme. Learners identified rel evant SoLD concepts and explained their rationale f or leveraging these concepts. Feedback and support wer e provided by members of the faculty team. While appropriate for pre-service teachers, this strategy  lacked some sophistication  as it did not account for  all the contextual demands that classroom teachers contend with. The second assessment strategy, employed in Program 2, took the form of a design problem. Here, practicing teachers applied SoLD learning objectives to address design issues in their classrooms or schools, allowing for  greater autonomy and contextualization and a riche r, more cognitively sophisticated demonstration of application of knowledge. The design problem included two milestone assessments: (1) the Project Proposal, outlining ho w learners planned to apply acquired knowledge, and  (2) the Experiments, Iteration, and Reflection assessment, where learners reflected on the experiment's implem entation and any changes made. In both assessments, learners  specified SoLD objectives and concepts, documentin g and reflecting on their application. Each learner was assigned a coach to provide support throughout their design cycle. Guided support and content integration The third finding addresses the design of guided su pport and interaction with content, taking into con sideration the diverse needs and experiences of novice pre-service teachers versus experienced in-service teachers. For novice pre-service teachers, empha sis was placed on broad exposure to a range of SoLD  concepts alongside essential pedagogical skills, preparing them to recognize a range of possible applications for SoLD concepts. In contrast, for experienced in-service teachers, the focus shifted to prov iding agency for deeper engagement with SoLD, encouraging the application of SoLD concepts and strategies to enhance their practice. In the first strategy, implemented in P rogram 1 for novice pre-service teachers, learners participated in challeng es where they interacted with learning experiences and resources related to relevant SoLD concepts. This approach su pported learners in progressively learning, practicing, and  demonstrati ng SoLD concepts across various contexts throughout the program. The second strategy, employ ed in P rogram 2 for in-service teachers, involved a two-pronged approach to learner interaction with SoLD. Initially, learners underwent a six-week intensive foundation institute, collaborating with a teaching team to de lve into SoLD competencies and their applications  to authentic problems of practice. Subsequently, during an eight-week project cycle, learners applied SoLD by design ing learner-focused projects based on individual interests. Thr oughout this period, learners accessed SoLD learning modules tailored to their specific design questions and projects, facilitating the seamless integration of SoLD into their work. Close collaboration with faculty and project coaches ensured personalized support, including module recommendations, visible connections between proje cts and SoLD concepts, and encouragement for critic al reflection. The team-teaching approach between faculty and project coach es provided learners with the unique advantage of learning from multiple sources of expertise and perspectives.

## Discussion

We offer several design considerations and insights.  Using a structured framework grounds educators' knowledge and practice, fostering both coverage and reflectio n. Both programs emphasize explicit connections bet ween educators' knowledge and SoLD competencies through instruction, coaching, and student reflection. Recognizing the tension between covering fundamentals and going  in-depth, we tailored two approaches for pre-service and in-service teachers to strike this balance based on their distinct needs, contexts, and interests. In terms of authentic assessment strategies, we introduced scenario-based challenges for pre-service teachers, acknowledging their limited control in implementing solutions. For in-service teachers, we positioned them as designers and researchers within their own classrooms and school communities. Finally, both programs adopted a collaborative teaching model, moving away from one course-one instructor models. Learners had access to multi ple faculty members, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of competencies. Faculty served as coaches, guiding students through challenges and design cycles, offering diverse perspectives.
