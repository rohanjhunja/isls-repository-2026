# A Meta-analysis of Integrating 3D Virtual Learning Environment in Language Teaching

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
3D virtual learning environments (3DVLEs) have become increasingly popular in language teaching, yet there are few meta-analytical studies examining their effectiveness from the teacher-in-the-loop perspective. This study explored the overall impact of 3DVLEs on language skills and affective achievements while investigating potential moderators. Examining 32 articles published between 2013 and 2023, and sourced from the Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar, our study compared 3DVLE approaches to traditional approaches and indicated that 3DVLEs had a large impact on language skills and a low-medium impact on affective achievements. Although none moderators were identified, this study offered valuable insights due to variations in effect sizes. This study provided a detailed discussion of these findings and their implications for language teachers to integrate 3DVLEs into language teaching.

## Introduction

In recent years, there has been a notable increase in the demand for flexible learning experiences, pr ompting innovation in the design of learning environments t o enable learners to actively construct their indiv idual knowledge representations (Jonassen, 1 991). Three-dimensional virtual environments (3DVLEs), commonly utilized in the computer gaming industry, offer the  potential to harness the advantages of 3D technolo gies o enhance educational experiences.

The affordances of 3DVLE, such as tangibility, visibility, and manipulabili ty, make them particularly suitable and widely adopted in educational contexts. In the realm of  classroom language teaching, numerous studies have investigated the efficacy of 3DVLEs in  enhancing learners’ language skills (Papin & Kapla nRakowski, 2021) and emotional achievements (Ustun et al., 2022). As the long history of research on educational tech nologies has demonstrated, the mere use of a new, supposedly innovative tool without considering inst ructional aspects does not automatically result in better learning (Buchner & Hofmann, 2022). Therefore, from the teacher's perspective, it's cru cial to consider how to incorporate 3DVLEs into everyday language teaching.

Previous meta-analysis and the need for this study Several studies have conducted meta-analyses to evaluate language learning outcomes in 3DVLEs. Alshumaimeri and Mazher (2023) conducted a systematic review and  meta-analysis to investigate the integration of augmente d reality (AR) in teaching and learning English as a foreign language. Their coding framework included s tudy design, participant numb er, setting, interest and attitude effect of using AR in learning. Cai et al. (2022) carried out a meta-analysis focusing on the effects of AR on language learning outcomes and student motivation. Their coding scheme encompassed language type, target lan guage, language skill, learner educational level, d evice, pedagogical approaches, instructional strategies, a nd intervention duration. Qiu et al. (2023) conduct ed a metaanalysis examining the impact of both high-and low-end virtual reality (VR) on EFL learning methods. T heir coding framework included learners' backgrounds, le arning theory, training duration, VR immersion, lea rning activities, learning materials, gam e element, linguistic gains, and affective gains. C hen et al. (2022) conducted a meta-analysis to explore the effects of extended reality (XR) on language learning, examining moderating factors. Their coding scheme included educational level, target language, technology type, and language skill. Wang et al.

(2020) conducted a meta-analysis evaluating the effects of 3D virtual world s (3DVWs) on language learning

outcomes. Their coding framework included years of publication, educational stages, settings of 3 DVW, target languages, target language skills, and learning loc ations. However, there seems to lack a meta-analysis from the perspective of integrating 3DVLEs into teaching, wi thout distinguishing between language types and 3DV LE types, analyzing the effects of 3DVLEs on language skills and affective achievements at the same time.

To address theresearch gap, there is an urgent need  for a comprehensive meta-analysis to validate the effectiveness of 3DVLEs in language teaching classr ooms and to identify effective instructional design  features, including 3DVLE type, immersion levels, teaching mo dalities, and educational levels. Such an analysis would facilitate the promotion of 3DVLEs in language teac hing and offer valuable insights into how to better  integrate 3DVLE into language teaching.

## Research questions

The present meta-analysis held a twofold purpose: the first aim was to evaluate the eff ectiveness of 3DVLE in language teaching. The second aim was  to identify potentially effective instructional considerations when integrating 3DVLE into language teaching. Therefore, two research questions were posed as follows: RQ1: What are the overall effects of 3DVLEs on lang uage skills and affective achievement s, respectively?

RQ2: What, if any, factors moderate the effect of 3DVLEs on language skills and affective achievement, respectively?

## Method

To address the research questions, we conducted a m eta-analysis following PRISMA (Moher et al., 2009).  This section provides an overview of the search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, coding, and analysis procedures. Literature search We conducted a literature search using three electronic databases: the Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar to capture articles related to the integr ation of 3DVLE s in teachers’ language  teaching. Search terms were identified through database thesauruses and keyword s used in sample papers. The search string was (“la nguage teaching” OR “language learning” OR “language cours e” OR “language classroom” OR “second language” OR “language educati on” OR “language acquisition” OR “ foreign langue”) And (“virtu al reality” OR “virtual learning environment” OR “head mounted display” OR “mixed reality” OR “synthetic environment” OR “virtual classroom” OR “augmented reality” OR “immersive lea rning environment” OR “simulation”). The search was carried out in June 2023, which resulted in 7284 articles. Criteria for inclusion and exclusion The inclusion criteria includes: studies that were (1) reported in English language; (2) peer-reviewed journal articles; (3) related to the use of 3DVLEs in language teaching; (4) randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or quasiexperimental designs; and studies that had (5) at l east one comparison of 3DVLEs  with traditional teaching approaches; (6) adequate quantitative analysis results to cal culate effect sizes. The exclusion criteria includes: studies that (1) did not adopt a controlled experimental design;  (2) participants in all experimental conditions learned in 3DVLEs; (3) there was no teacher-in-the-loop process during the experiment; (4) investigated the effect of 2D online virtual environments instead of 3DVLEs.

Two researchers conducted the inclusion and exclusion process, resolving all disagreements at each level. Duplicate articles were removed firstly. Then, the title and abstract of these articles were manual ly scanned to eliminate irrelevant studies and articles were added manually from Google Scholar. Next, all the remaining articles were skimmed to ensure they met the criteria. After the final filtering process, 31 studies, which met all the criteria for inclusion, were included in the meta-analysis to extract effect size statistics. Figure 1 summarized the search procedure.

Coding the studies The coding scheme was built upon categories and the  factors  identified in previous meta-analysis studies. Considering the common categorization of outcomes in to cognition and affect and the variables measured in the included articles, we categorized teaching outcomes as language skills and affective achievements. For language skills, the subtypes included vocabulary, listening, speaking, reading, and writing skills. If a study  measured teaching outcomes encompassed two or more of these skills, it was  coded as a comprehensive English skill. Regarding affective achievement, the subtypes included learni ng experiences (comprising anxiety, enjoyment, interest, attachment, presence, and perceived usefu lness), motivation (encompassing self-efficacy, learning willingness, intrinsic motivation, and extrinsic m otivation), and attitude (consisting of engagement and learning attention).

To investigate the impact of crucial factors on language skills and affective achievements, we coded four main moderators: 3DVLE type, immersion level, teach ing modality, and learner's learning stage. 3DVLE type was categorized as AR-enhanced 3DVLE, VR-enhanced 3DVLE, and MR-enhanced 3DVLE. Immersion level was classified as non-immersive (operating on small screens, such as comp uters, mobile devices, etc), semi-immersive (using electronic large screens), and fully-immersive (utilizing headsets and similar devices). Teaching modality included  offline, online,  blended which combines the strength of both offline  and online teaching. Concerning learner ’s learning stage, we categorized it as kindergarten, primary school, middle school, high school, and college.

Additional to the categorizations above, reference information of each article including title, author, and year for publication were collected, together with quantitative data (e.g. mean, standard deviation, a nd sample size) which was used to calculate effect sizes. And  t o ensure reliability, the included studies were cod ed independently by two researchers. The agreement between the two coders was tested using Cohen’s Kappa, which was above 0.9 across all codes. Any differences were discussed until a consensus was reached. ## Data analysis

We used Meta-Analysis (CMA) Version 3.0 to compute the evaluation of publication bias, effect sizes, the test of heterogeneity and the analysis of moderators. F irst, we calculated indices of publication bias, including Funnel plot, Egger’s test, Classic fail-safe N, and Kendall’s Tau rank test. Then we conducted meta-analyses on language skills, and affective achievements, respectively. Finally, we tested the moderating effects.

## Meta-analysis results for language skills

The 32 articles included in the meta-analysis were published from 2013 to 2023 with a to tal of 71 studies. Table 1 describes the frequencies and percentages of studies for of each code. Publication bias for language skills We used a funnel plot to evaluate potential publica tion bias's impact. Figure 2  shows that most effect sizes were evenly distributed in the lower part of the funnel plot, suggesting a low risk of publication bias. To further assess this, we conducted Egger's test (p =.153) and Kend all's Tau rank test (p =.25), both of which showed nonsignificant results. The Classic fail-safe N test indicated that more than 2051 additional studies would be required to nullify the overall effect size, well beyond the threshold of 5k + 10 (i.e., 145). In summary, the results strongly suggest a minimal risk of publication bias.

Overall effects on language skills For the meta-analysis of language skills, we computed a total of  30 independent effect sizes from 23 articles. Effect sizes ranged from-0.641 to +3.154, with only four studies showing neg ative effects. Table 2 presents the overall effect size analysis and the heterogeneity test results. Under the random-effects model, the integration of 3DVLEs in language teaching exhibited a large and positive effect on language skills (g = 0.938, SE = 0.150, 95% CI [0.643, 1.233], p <.01). The heterogeneity test  resulted in a Q statistic of 29 (p <.01), with an I-Squared statistic of 86.216%, indicating high heterogeneity.

## Discussions and implications

The overall effects of 3DVLEs in language teaching Effect size for language skills The results of this study indicate d that 3DVLEs had a large effect on language skills (g = 0.938), suggesting 3DVLEs’ ability to improve learners’ language skills compared to traditional teaching approaches, which extended previous argument that 3D immersive environment div ersified teaching resources, promoting  learners’ language skills (Yuan et al., 2023). The innovative multimedia learning environment of 3DVLEs enables learners to interact with virtual information or physical settings, which significantly shaped learners’ cognition  (Pouw et al., 2014). Learners who engage in a higher level of interaction  also tend to have a hig her level of meaning construction. Moreover, 3DVLEs present teaching content to learners visually, audi bly and even tactilely, triggering learners’ vivid memories and improves their content comprehension. Therefore, the multimodal and authentic features, as well as interactive representations enable 3DVLEs to significantly enhance learners’ language skills. Effect size for affective achievements The meta-analysis result for affective achievements yield ed a small to  medium, positive effect (g = 0.448). 3DVLEs assist teachers in creating an appropriate context for language learners, enhancing motivation, learning attitude and immersing learning experiences. In 3DVLE s, learners perceive a deep and authentic connection to the real world, having a great sense of presence, which  leads to a high level of interest and self-efficacy which in turn contributes to learning (Luke et al., 2023). Moreover, in simulated learning environments, learners tend to be less concerned about external perception, such as  embarrassment or criticisms, increasing their learning enjoyment. Thus, the positive learning experience motivates learners to actively engage in the learning process, ultimately improving their affective achievements.

Moderator variables Moderator analysis for language skills While 3DVLE type didn’t significantly predict the effectiveness for language skills, AR-enhanced (g = 1.293) 3DVLEs and MR-enhanced 3DVLEs (g = 1.126) demonstrated larger effect size s. The results ensured that AR’s potential to present learning materials by annotati ng real-life objects with  multimedia, fostering meaning connection with learning environment. Only one article  (Lan et al., 2018) included in this study adopted MRenhanced 3DVLE to enhance learners’ language skills, so further exploration is needed. As observed in previous studies, MR-enhanced 3DVLEs had the ability to offer adjustable simulations mirroring real-word experiences, immerse learners and facilitate natural interactions (Lindberg & Jönsson, 2023). The positive impact of VRenhanced 3DVLEs was relatively modest (g = 0.673). Previous study suggested that, with VR support, learners only achieved lower-level reading skills  (Wang et al., 2021). However, VR  usage in language leaning offers benefits such as increased engagement and attentive ness in lifelike settings, aiding vocabulary acquis ition (Alfadil, 2020), which can’t be overlooked.

Although immersion level wasn’t a significant moderator, semi-immersive 3DVLEs (g = 1.126) showed the largest effect, followed by non-immersive 3DVLEs (g = 1.107) and fully-immersive 3DVLEs (g = 0.474). The results provided evidence about the potential of MR  creating a more authentic  interactive learning environment, enhancing presence, and facilitating embodied cognition (Ohta & Tamura, 2014). Non-immersive 3DVLEs, particularly utilizing  AR with 3D graphics to represent abstract learning concepts metaphorically, proved effective in cultivating learners ’ language skills. Nonetheless, fully immersive 3DVLE s presenting challenges such as high cost, potential motion sickness, and isolation from the physical world, didn’t significantly enhance learners’ language skills.

Furthermore, neither teaching modality nor learner ’s learning stage was a moderator. Each teaching modality positively impacted learners' language skills (g > 0.8). This finding encourages extensive integration of 3DVLEs into diverse language teaching classrooms. Notably, 3DVLEs had a substantial impact (g = 0.83) on primary school students (g = 1.323), with a relativ ely smaller impact on college students (g = 0.652) and middle school students (g = 0.622).  Previous studies identified 3DVLEs more closely associated with  lower-order cognitive skills (Majid & Salam, 2021). Consequently, primary school students, still developing their fundamental language skills, might gain more from 3DVLEs. It’s important to mention that all three articles included in this meta-analysis focusing on middle school students utilized fully-immersive 3DVLEs, potentially influencing the measured learning skills.

Moderator analysis for affective achievements While none of the  moderators reached statistical significance for affective achi evements, thought-provoking findings emerged. AR-enhanced 3DVLEs (g = 0.641) outperformed VR-enhanced 3DVLEs (g = 0.412), consistent with immersive level  impacts: non-immersive 3DVLEs  (g = 0.642)  had a higher impact compared to fully-immersive 3DVLEs (g = 0.340). AR-enhanced 3DVLEs allow learners to scan objects, displaying language learning information  on-screen, which enhances interactivity and enjoyment, motiv ating learners for a more satisfying learning experience. While VR-enhanced 3DVLEs support situational and interactive learning but mainly rely on conventional input devices, which do es not conform to the natural norms of interpersona l social interactions in the real world  (Persky et al., 2009). Therefore, learners achieved relatively lower affective achievements in VR-enhanced 3DVLEs in this study.

Although teaching modality wasn’t a significant moderator, it revealed that employing offline (g = 0.580) and blended  (g = 0.558) teaching modalities within  3DVLEs had a greater impact on learners' affective achievements than the online teaching modality (g = 0.207). This may be attributed to the fact that, in comparison to common digital tools such as English learning apps and online English courses, w hich have gained popularity as online activities and are favored by learners, 3DVL Es have a relatively hig her entry barrier and are more susceptible to technical challenges. Therefore, learners in 3DVLEs may have a greater need for offline guidance from teachers in addressing technical issues and adapting to the new environment.. In addition, learner’ s learning stage didn’t serve as a significant moderator. The effect size was most pronounced on middle school students (g = 0.885) and least on college students (g = 0.336). 3DVLEs facilitate learning by playing, but with higher technological barriers. Consequentl y, middle and high school students tend to achieve better affective achievements in comparison with younger students. While college students exhibit less enthusiasm for utilizing 3D technologies in language learning, possibly due to their higher exposure to innovative technologies (Garzon & Acevedo, 2019).

## Conclusions and future research

This study conducted a meta-analysis to evaluate the effects of 3DVLEs compared to traditional language teaching approaches on language skills and affective achievements  and to explore potential moderators. The findings suggested that 3DVLEs  outperform traditional teaching approaches, which encourages language teac hers to integrate 3DVLEs into language teaching. Despite no  found moderators, examinations of variables uncovered insightful findings. Notably, non-immersive AR-enhanced and semi-immersive MR-enhanced 3DVLEs had higher positive impact than fully-immersive VR-enhanced 3DVLEs. This supports the idea that less invasive learning environments enhance naturalistic learning interaction and support better learning outcomes. Regarding teaching modality, all three modalities had a similar impact on language s kills, but the online modality had the least positive impact on affective achievement s. This insight inspires language teachers to provi de more face-toface teaching and assistance for learners during th e initial stages of implementing 3DVLEs, so to help  learners more smoothly adapt to the new learning environment. Additionally, 3DVLEs proved highly effective for primary school students in developing language skills. If with sufficient support from teachers  to enhance their affective achievements, the language teaching outcomes of primary school students could be even more impressive. Middle and high school students also benefit ed from 3DVLEs, whereas college students appeared less suitable for language learning in 3DVLEs.

This meta-analysis has several limitations. First, the limite d sample size m ight introduce bias, as some subcategories of moderator variables had small or u nbalanced samples. Second, the impact of 3DVLEs on learners' thinking capacities, which closely related to language skills, was not analyze d due to a lack of studies, highlighting the need for further research in this area. Finally, the study did not delve into the sub-dimensions of language skills and affective achievements, indicating the importance of more in-depth research to provide more practical recommendations for teachers to integrate 3DVLEs into language teaching.  Thus, further research is necessary to include more moderator designs and explore more det ailed subcategories, providing additional insight into the effective application of 3DVLEs.
