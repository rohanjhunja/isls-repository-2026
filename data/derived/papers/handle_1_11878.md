# CocoNote: Agents-aided Collaborative Learning Environment Enhances Socially Shared Regulation

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study aims to develop CocoNote, an agent-aided collaborative learning platform that enhances students' Socially Shared Regulation (SSRL) in collaborative learning. CocoNote integrates reactive agents to facilitate students' planning, monitoring, and reflection alongside a proactive agent that actively intervenes to provide metacognitive, socio-emotional, and motivational support. A quasi-experimental study involving sixth-grade students examined their SSRL abilities in a STEM course. Quantitative findings indicated that students using CocoNote (n = 38) demonstrated significant improvements in SSRL and exhibited more balanced group interactions compared to the control group (n = 40). Qualitative data analysis illustrated how agents guided discussions, increased participation and improved metacognitive processes. These findings highlight the effectiveness of AI-assisted tools in fostering SSRL abilities and suggest design principles for agent-aided collaborative learning environments.

## Introduction

Collaboration is a key modern learning method that significantly enhances academic performance, knowle dge construction, and complex problem-solving skills (Miller  & Hadwin, 2024). However, compared to individual learning, it is more complex, presenting challenges such as free riding, dominant members, and difficulty reaching consensus in group activities (Zheng  et al., 2019). Researchers have identified  a lack of Socially Shared Regulation (SSRL), which is essential for effective  collaboration, as a key factor contributing to these issues (Järvenoja et al., 2020). Despite its importance, many learners lack regulation skills for productive collaboration and need structured guidance (Järvelä et al., 2013).

With the rapid advancement of technology, technolog y-based support has become indispensable in promoting SSRL. Individual and group planning tools with group awar eness features have been embedded into complex collaborative platforms (Miller & Hadwin, 2015). These tools reduce the need for manual collection and organization of chat histories, thereby decreasing the cognitive load of collaboration (Janssen et al.,  2011). Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) offers additional opportunities  to enhance this process.  L everaging GenAI’s capabilities in natural language processing, content generation, and interactive dialogue (Peres et al., 2023), researchers have developed GenAI-driven agents that function as companions, assistants, and mentors (Fidan & Gencel, 2022). However, the potential of GenAI-powered agents to provide dynamic regulatory support for learners is still emerging, and there has been limi ted exploration of how these agents can advance SSR L research (Järvelä et al., 2023). This study introduces CocoNote, a GenAI-driven agent-assisted SSRL collaborative learning platform, and investigates how integrating reactive and proactive agents impacts SSRL in collaborative settings. The research addresses the following questions: RQ1: Can CocoNote enhance students' SSRL abilities in collaborative learning? RQ2: In what ways does CocoNote influence the dynamics of SSRL among learners? Theoretical foundations Socially shared regulation of learning Originating from self-regulated learning (SRL) theory, SSRL refers to how  group members metacognitively manage their cognitive, behavioural, motivational, and emotional states through cycles  of planning, monitoring, evaluating, and regulating phases (Hadwin et al., 2017). In the planning phase, the group sets shared goal s and devises strategies to accomplish tasks. During the monitoring phase, they coordinate actions and assess progress, while in the reflection phase, they evaluate their performance and outcomes to inform  future actions (Hadwin et al., 2017). CocoNote's AI agents are designed to facilitate and sustain  the SSRL process by providing regulatory support in planning, monitoring, and reflection (Järvelä et al., 2016). Trigger regulation framework The Trigger Regulation Framework (Järvelä & Hadwin, 2024) was applied in the design of proactive agents within CocoNote. Triggering events—situations or behaviours that hinder learning, such as low engagement or negative emotions—offer opportunities to support metacognitive processes (Edwards et al., 2024). CocoNote’s proactive agents aim to enhance group-level metacognitive awareness and engagement  by giving  metacognitive, socio-emotional, and motivational support.

CocoNote overview CocoNote comprises several key components designed to support SSRL: a hypermed ia whiteboard, a group chatroom, three reactive agents, and a proactive agent. The hypermedia white board enables learners to create, modify, and link content—such as text, images, and videos—as hypermedia notes. These non-linearly organized notes foster knowledge construction and creative problem-solving. The group chat facilitates real-time communication for discussing tasks, sharing ideas, and effective collaboration. Additionally, the plat form offers collaborative scaffolding tools and a learning analytics dashboard to support ongoing knowledge construction and reflection (Xin et al., 2024). A study with students from Hong Kong and Mainland China showed that CocoNote effectively enhances collaborative knowledge building (Xin et al., 2024). Agents design for CocoNote CocoNote incorporates three reactive agents and one proactive agent to support the SSRL process. Reactive Agents. The planning assistant, monitoring assistant, and reflection assistant provide structured support for different SSRL phases —planning, enactment, and reflection. These assistan ts are developed using prompt engineering techniques. When activated by th e group leader during a specific phase, they offer teache rprepared discussion questions to provide detailed s upport. The planning assistant supports setting goals and assigning tasks, the monitoring assistant prompts progress ev aluation, and the reflection assistant guides performance assessment. Moreover, these assistants can autonomously analyze  student discussions and present results in structured tables. These tables can be transferred from the chatroom to the hypermedia whiteboard, enabling all group members to view their roles, responsibili ties, and the group's progress, prompting dynamic reflection. This fosters awareness of individual ac tions and overall group dynamics, enhancing metacog nitive awareness.

Proactive Agents. The proactive agent in CocoNote intervenes when specific triggering events occur during collaboration. For example, if a group membe r is inactive, the agent may prompt them to share their thoughts or suggest ways to contribute. Additionally, it provides socio-emotional support by addressing negative emotions or conflicts within the team, fostering a positive collaborative environment. The proactive agent operates through three interconnected modules: real-time data collection, data analysis, and intelligent regulation. The data collection module gathers information on the group's collaboration every three minutes. The data analysis module processes this information and detects triggering events. Upon identifying a trigger, the agent provides feedback via an interface and notifies learners by flashing a "lightbulb" icon in the chatroom's bottom right c orner. Once learners view the prompt, the lightbulb stops flash ing until the next trigger.  These interventions help maintain balanced, efficient team dynamics, ensuring all members actively engage in regulating the learning process.

## Research context and data collection

This quasi-experimental study involved 78 sixth-grade students in a STEM course. The experimental group (38 students, divided into 8 groups of 4-6 students each) used CocoNote equipped with three reactive agents and one proactive agent, while the control group (40 studen ts, divided into 8 groups of 4-6 students each) used a version of CocoNote without agents but retained identical w hiteboard and chatroom functionalities. To control for variables, the teacher provided the control group w ith the same questions posed by the reactive agents  in the experimental group. The experiment took place in a computer lab over a 90-minute session, during which students collaboratively completed the STEM project task "Design a Robot." Data were collected using multiple methods to ensure comprehensive evidence:  The Group Metacognition Scale (Biasutti & Frate, 20 18) was administered before and after the intervention to assess students’ group metacognitive abilities; Chatroom conversation records were collected to analyze interaction patterns; Collaboration network graphs from CocoNote’s learning analytics dashboard was used to  examine students’ collaboration networks; Six groups were interviewed —three from the experimental group and three from the control group—to explore qualitative insights. Data treatment To address RQ1, statistical analyses were conducted. A paired sample t-test was used to compare changes in each dimension for the experimental group before and aft er the intervention (see Table 1). An independent sample t-test was conducted to compare the differences between the experimental and control groups across all dimensions (see Table 2). Additionally, CocoNote platform data —including chatroom conversations and collaboration network graphs from the learning analytics dashboar d (see Figure 1) —were analyzed to triangulate the questionnaire re sults. For RQ2, semi-structured interviews with six groups (three experi mental, three control) provided qualitative insights into SSRL dynamics.

## Findings

RQ1. Can CocoNote enhance students' SSRL abilities in collaborative learning? Paired sample t-tests revealed that using CocoNote with reactive and proactive agents significantly improved the experimental group's SSRL abilities across several dimensions, notably in Evaluation (t(37) = 3.54, p < 0.001) and in Knowledge of cognition and Planning (p < 0.05). In contrast, the control group showed minimal progress. Independent sample t-tests indicated that the experimental group scored significantly higher than the control group in Reflection (t(76) = 2.06, p = 0.043) and outperforme d them in Knowledge of cognition, Planning, and Monitoring, demonstrating the positive impact of CocoNote's agent integration. To corroborate these findings, interaction behaviours were compared between Group 1 (experimental) and Group 3 (control), which were selected based on teacher observation  and recommendation. Frequency statistics indicated full participation i n Group 1 (6/6 members), with 28 out of 33 (85%) task-related messages  guided by SSRL assistant prompts. Conversely, Group 3 had a higher message volume (60) but lower participation (3/6 members) and fewer t askrelated messages (37/60, 62%).  Collaboration network graphs (see Figure 2) further  illustrated balanced connections in the experimental group, reflecting a ctive and equitable participation, while Group 3 sh owed imbalanced node sizes and connections. These data underscore CocoNote’s effec tiveness in fostering SSRL abilities through agent-supported, balanced, and productive interactions. RQ2.In what ways does CocoNote affect the dynamics of SSRL among learners? We analyzed the interview data using a thematic ana lysis approach. The findings revealed three key way s CocoNote influenced SSRL dynamics: Agents guide and support discussions, enhancing task understanding and participation Reactive agents prompted groups to plan and allocat e tasks, while proactive agents offered timely, fri endly prompts to encourage participation. This improved task comprehension and reduced conflicts. Previously, we lacked clear goals, leading to disagreements. We silently changed notes without discussion, causing repeated modifications and wast ing time. The agent-generated task distribution table reduced these revisions. (Student 1) I used to observe others’ discussions, but today, the agent encouraged me to speak up, and I gave it a try. (Student 2) Externalization tables on the whiteboard facilitate group awareness and monitoring Externalization tables on the whiteboard made each member's contributions and responsibilities visible, supporting group progress awareness and effective monitoring. The task distribution and progress tables facilitat ed communication. For instance, We could quickly identify the person responsible for a specific task. (Student 3) Real-time personalized prompts from active agent foster reflection and engagement Active agents monitored students' collaboration behaviour, identified trigger events, and provided personali zed socio-emotional support and metacognitive prompts, facilitating reflection and sustained engagement. The 'lightbulb' provided useful suggestions when I was confused, helping me clarify my thoughts. (Student 4) The active agent's prompt was friendly and timely, motivating  us to participate in discussions. (Student 5)

## Discussion, limitations and future directions

This study demonstrates that CocoNote significantly enhances students' SSRL abilities. By  integrating reactive and proactive agents, the experimental group showed  notable improvements in metacognitive processes, especially in evaluation skills. The hypermedia whiteboard's externalization tables enhanced group awareness and monitoring, fostering accountability and active involvement. Real-time personalized prompts from proactive agents encouraged reflection and sustained engagement, strengthening group cohesion. The study's small sample size of sixth-grade students limits applicability to other ages a nd contexts. The short experiment duration makes it hard to assess long-term impacts. Limited data collection methods, lack ing multimodal approaches like videos or physiological data, restricted a comprehensive understanding of learners' collaboration. Future research should expand sample size, extend study duration, and use diverse data collection and analysis methods to better explore agents' impact on collaborative learning.
