# Bridging the Distance': Developing Entrepreneurial Thinking in an International and Interdisciplinary Learning Environment

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The Covid19 pandemic presented challenges and opportunities for students' learning worldwide. An international interdisciplinary learning environment was designed to bridge the distance, address students' socio-emotional needs and to develop entrepreneurial thinking, a skill needed in times of uncertainty. Twenty-four education students from two countries took part in six online sessions. Initial results support the contribution of the project to developing the participants' entrepreneurial thinking and to the role socio-emotional aspects play in it.

## Introduction

The continuation of the Covid19 pandemic confronts higher education with challenges and opportunities such as finding ways to address the quality of distance learning instruction and addressing social and emotional needs of those who partake in teaching and learning. The importance of understanding both challenges and opportunities are significant for orchestrating future learning, dealing with crisis situations and for improving as pects of teaching and learning in the higher education systems (Engeström & Käyhkö, 2021). Elias (2003) suggested that to conduct life successfully, students need to learn how to understand and manage their emotions as well as how to resolve unexpected problems. Teaching pre-service teacher by looking at self-answering such questio ns may provide them with socio-emotional competences that lead to personal well-being and employment success (Price et al. 2020).

Developing Entrepreneurship thinking is considered an individual’s ability to turn ideas into action and involve creativity, innovation and risk-taking (EU Skills Panorama, 2014). Thus, Entrepreneurial thinking might be an asset for students at this very time of accelerated ambiguity. In this study we seek to promote entrepreneurial th inking and skills through the design of an online, international, interdisciplinary learning environme nt that is attentive to studying during the Covid19  era. The program was named “Bridging the Distance” project t o signify overcoming forced distancing caused by th e pandemic in the local communities; Yet it sought to  enable, closeness within the international communi ty, by exploring commonalities and characterizing modes of progression – similar and different in each county. Our study objectives aim to examine: 1. How does participation in an international and interdisciplinary learning environment contribute to the development of entrepreneurial thinking? 2. What are the charac teristics of learning design in an academic course that support developing entrepreneurial thinking among the participants? Our initial findings point to the potential development of entrepreneurial thinking for the students. It also points to the place that socio-emotional aspects play amon g students during the Covid19 period. Furthermore, it emphasises the importance of the projects' tasks de sign, which relate directly to the need for socio-e motional support.

## Method

To address these research questions, we designed a learning environment that spans across six synchron ous sessions, between Education students in Israel and Poland. The Israeli students enrolled in the Pedagogical Entrepreneurship course conducted as part of the Regev Excellence program for pre-service teachers at Kibbutzim College of Education and students who study pedagog y at the University of Gdansk University in Poland.  The design was led by the Center for Innovation at the Kibbutzim College in collaboration with academic and professional contributors.

Apart from the introduction and summary (meetings 1 &6), the learning design included two main teaching units. The first, invites 'inward looking'  on challenges of studying during the pandemic usin g a design task to collaboratively design a t-shirt using a composition of images and text; The second, 'outwards looking' at the challenges and opportunities of this time, creating a proposal for a social, business and/or pedagogical project presented to potential investors (Figure 1). These activities were held during online meetings in mixe d international groups and under the guidance of instructors from both institutions. We use design-based research (Barab & Squire, 2004)  aiming for both theoretical and practical contribution in the context of designing an innovat ive learning environment. Research tools included t wo questionnaires for the students (n=24); Video documentation of the six synchronously learning sessions; Student ICLS2022 Proceedings 2058 © ISLS Figure 1 Design aspects for 'Bridging the Distance' project written reflections; Interviews with six students; Students' outcome analysis and team design notes. We report on early finding from thematic analysis conducted on the interviews and outcome analysis (iteration 1, 2021).

## Results

Our findings point to the contribution of the unique learning environment to motivating participants to collaborate through discussion, design, and entrepreneurial ide as to meaningful learning outcomes. It also points to the incorporation of social interactions that were deve loped during the study sessions in small groups (3-4 participants) as well as identifying cross-cultural shared challenges among participants to do with studying during the Covid19 pandemic.

The six groups' t-shirt design reflected socio-emot ional aspects such as loneliness, the need to look forward in a positive way, and the desire to be tog ether. The entrepreneurial initiatives related to topics such as facilitating anonymous expression of mental health issues, creatively resolving international library book lending and assistive traveling-during quarantine and/or amidst covid19. The incorporation of social-emotional aspects into the learning design and into the design of tasks of the international project turned to be crucial. From the interviews, several social-emotional aspects were identified as contributors/supporters to the development of stude nts entrepreneurial thinking among them: (1) Instru ctors' respectful teamwork, acting as role model for collaboration and management. (2) International Students' personal connection and meaningful interaction in small grou ps. These aspects together with the task design, se emed to assist in facilitating students' Entrepreneurial skills: personal, technical & management (OECD, 2014). For example, S2 reflected on the connection between  people: "I think the first course took place in a zoom where the atmosphere was really really really good socially and also among the lecturers. [..] it  was important for everyone to talk and get to know." S3  related the T-shirt task design to bridging the di stance: "I believe that it was a very adequate task because it made me think about the pandemic, about what I have lost, so primarily the aspect of interpersonal relations, or  rather lack of them." Another student (S5), relate d to life improvement and innovation: "The second task was al so great because it showed me that the knowledge ab out pedagogy/ education/ psychology, which is what we a re studying can be used to improve life, to improve  the quality of life… Such knowledge can be translated into actual actions and innovations." Results from this study contribute to the design of international collaborative learning environments that promote interdisciplinary entrepreneurial thinking enabled by socio-emotional support. Such environment that are built upon students needs in the Covid19 era might help them discuss their challenges together, as well as enable them to suggest innovative ideas to social/educational problems. Further analysis of the data is needed for more precise findings to contribute theoretical and practical insights for an emergent design patterns for international collaboration around theses study aims.
