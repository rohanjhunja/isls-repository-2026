# SCoRe-Docs: An Online Environment for Student Crowd Research

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This technology innovation paper presents SCoRe-Docs, an online environment aimed to support research-based learning in large-scale groups of students from different disciplinary backgrounds that collaborate asynchronously from a distance. The environment provides a highly integrated set of tools to initiate and organize collective research processes. Core features include canvases to scaffold the collective efforts, a stage-gate-model to synchronize activities at pivotal points in the research process, a flat user role model, support for micro-contributions, and a controlled versioning system. Following up on a short introduction to the particular requirements of large-scale collaborative environments, the underlying design principles are outlined and distinctive features of the environment are presented. Initial empirical findings from a prototype trial are reported and an overview of insights and open issues is provided.

## Introduction

Throughout the last decades digital technologies not only altered the ways in which inf ormation is disseminated but also brought forward qualitatively new forms of large-scale collaboration, particip ation, collective engagement and inquiry. The development of Wikipedia, open-source software proj ects, online co-laboratories, citizen science projects, and participatory journalistic investigation networks provide  some of the most widely recognized examples of what has been labeled as mass or crowd collaboration (e.g., Cress, Jeong & Moskaliuk, 2016). These novel forms of productive knowledge work challenge established models of c ollaborative learning and call for new educational approaches that introduce students to respective knowle dge practices and harness large-scale collaboration as a potential asset for higher education (e.g., Chen, Håklev & Rose, 2021). As processes of crowd collaboration are heavily dependent on technological means in support of interactions among a plentitude of actors, usually distributed in time and space, the strive for new pedagogi cal methods is tightly bound to the need for appropriate digital technologies.

Even though the spectrum of collaborative technologies available has continuously evolved and new tools have been taken up in (higher) educational settings, there is still a scarcity of digital environments that support research-based learning in large-scale groups of students from diffe rent disciplinary backgrounds that collaborate asynchronously from a distance. While digital tools, such as wikis, real-time document editors, version control and collaborative notebook systems, can be repurposed for research-or inquiry-based forms of learning, these systems only insufficiently account for the specific dynamics of crowd collaboration. Available technologies in particular fall short to account for usage scenarios in which (a) participants cannot draw on common practices and shared epistemic frames, (b) extensive forms of meta-communication are cumbersome and unsustainable, (c) activities are not simply additive but are mutually dependent and structurally coupl ed, and (d) the individual participants are largely free to decide how and when they are going to contribute. Against this background, this paper presents SCoRe-Docs, an online environment for student crowd research, which provides a highly integrated set of tools to initiate and organi ze research processes to address a matter of collective interest. The environment has been developed and empiric ally trialed as part of a three-year joint R&D project, that brought together expertise on advanced video technologies, virtu al collaboration in interdisciplinary and large-scale groups, research-based learning, and education for sustainable development. Crowd collaboration and design principles for SCoRe-docs The development of the SCoRe-Docs environment originated from the intent of the Virtual Academy of Sustainability, a distance-education provider affiliated with the University of Bremen, to broaden the spectrum of its Massive Open Online Courses and to integrate research-based scenar ios into its portfolio. The overall aim of the corresponding R&D project, hence, has been to device and trial a pedagogical appr oach and an online environment suitable to actively engage a large number of students from all over Germany in a research-based learning process on an issue of sustainable development, making use of video technology (Groß et al., 2022). The design of the SCoRe-Docs environment was informed by the conception of the crowd as a distinct social form that differs from other large-scale social formations, such as bureaucratic organizations, communities of practice CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 413 or project teams, in that participants cannot resort neither to a set of formalized rules, a shared practice, nor a wellarticulated objective. Instead, the crowd is constituted by its participants ephem eral collaboration on a matter of shared concern (cf. Reichelt et al., 2019; Richter et al., 2022). Based on this the oretical premise and a review of corresponding examples of crowd collaboration, the development of the SCoRe-Docs environment was guided by the following design principles (for more details and theoretical grounding see  ibid., as well as Allert et al., 2021):

• DP1-Score: Provide students with generative and artifact-based scaffol ds alongside which they can

organize the collaborative process.

• DP2-Dynamic Trail Marks: Support self-documenting processes that preserve an artifact’s history.

• DP3-Vital Signs: Render recent activities visible to the crowd, providing clues for eventual follow-up-actions.

• DP4-Central Hot Spot: Create a central space for the negotiation and docum entation of collective

decisions.

• DP5-Same Page: Ensure that all (interim) results and outcomes can be unambiguously and consistently

referenced.

• DP6-Joint Quality Care: Establish transparent quality standards that can be  refined and re-negotiated

throughout the process.

• DP7-Common Resources: Ensure a sustainable, secure, and open access to all resources created

throughout the collaborative effort, taking into account suitable measures for data protection.

• DP8-Productive Diversity: Provide support for heterogeneous approaches, contributio ns, and

perspectives, as well as deliberate re-iterations and synergetic integration.

• DP 9-Collective Agency: Focus the collaborative effort, such as a collec tive research project, on a

shared matter of concern that provides a momentum for the joint endeavor.

• DP10-Social Arena: Provide means, such as an integrative communication and activity channel, that

allow participants to follow up on their own and others’ involvement and impact on the joint endeavor. SCoRe-docs and its distinctive design features SCoRe-Docs is a collaborative online environment offering a highly integrated set of tools to initiate and organize collective research processes. It provides its users with a collective research notebook allowing them to share and collaboratively work on qualitative research data in a structured yet adaptive manner. Besides the use of text and image-based data sources, the environment also integrates an online video editor, enabling users not only to share but also to collaboratively annotate, edit, and combine digital video footage. To or chestrate and coordinate the collaborative research process, the environment supports the use of artifact-based scaffolds, entails various awareness mechanisms such as on-and offline notifications, and provides m eans for process-related decision making in large-scale asynchronous settings. For analytic and research purposes, users’ intera ctions with the environment can be systematically logged and be documented in a learning record store. Figure 1 depicts the main graphical user interface of the SCoRe-Docs environment, with the collaborative working area in its center and a collapsible navigation bar on the left-and the opened communication and activity channel on the right-hand-side. The main working area is organized into a hierarchically ordered and extendable set of sections, which can be asynchronously edited, commented, and referenced throughout the environment. The sections are versioned so that all changes can be retraced and prior ver sions can be restored. Status markers additionally indicate whether a section is in progress, submitted for review, under review or completed. Looking at the SCoRe-Docs environment from a crowd-perspective, aiming to suppor t large-scale interdisciplinary and asynchronous collaboration in a substantially self-organizing manner, the following design features appear to be most noteworthy and distinctive: Artifact-based scaffolding by means of structured canvases:  To support students in the organization of their collaborative work processes and to provide them with a scaffold of the epistemic activities, they have to undertake in the course of the research process, the main working area of SCoRe-Docs is based on the concept of structured canvases. Towards this end, the canvases provide a visually organized overview of all  the (interim) results that have to be collectively produced at a certain stage of the joint research-endeavor. In that the canvases specify the format of the expected outcomes, they stress the necessit y of synthesizing activities throughout the overall process, and hence foster the collective work around shared artifacts. Synchronization and alignment of the collective research process based on a stage-gate-model: In the SCoRe-Docs environment, the use of canvases is complemented by a stage-gate-model, which divides the overall research process into a series of successive stages (such as the identification of a matter of concern, the development of a research design, the data collection and analysis, as well as the communication of results), which CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 414 are linked by gates that can only be passed collectively. The stage-gate-model thereby only specifies the (interim) results that have to be produced as well as the quality criteria these have  to fulfill in order to move to the next stage but poses no restrictions on the ways the process is organized within a stage. The stage-gate-model hence is supposed to combine the required procedural flexibility of large-scale asynchronous c ollaboration with clear incentives for joint quality care.

Participation based on a flat user role model: To encourage the equitable articulation of diverse perspectives and to foster collective agency, the SCoRe-Docs environment dra ws on a deliberately flat user role model. All participants are essentially granted the same rights regarding th e adding, editing and commenting of contributions, as well as the decision making and veto-mechanism implemented in the environment. The very permissive rights management of the SCoRe-Docs environment not only grants  participants far reaching rights and possibilities of participation but also implies a joint responsibility for the advancement and the quality of the collective process.

Encouragement of micro-contributions: To lower the threshold for participation, the SCoRe-Docs environment explicitly welcomes minor and small edits and generally invites cont ributors to work in smaller increments rather than to create or edit huge chunks of information at once, rendering it mor e difficult for others to keep track of the changes made. Technically, this is supported by the dissoci ation of authorship from the size or amount of one’s contributions as well as the systematic segmentation of the collaborative working area into separately editable and rather small sections.

Self-documenting processes and consistency by means of controlled versioning: To ensure transparency and consistency throughout the collective research environment, SCoRe-Docs not only te chnically versions all changes made on each section but also strongly prompts users to describe the c hanges they have made, so that others could follow up. This mechanism also ensures that not only final results but also interim ou tcomes can be unambiguously and consistently referenced.

Prototype trials – video-based research on sustainability issues Two fully functional prototypes of the SCoRe-Docs environment have been trial test ed in three courses offered by the Virtual Academy of Sustainability in winter terms 2020/21 and 2021/22, as well as in summer term 2021. In each of these courses the students were invited to engage in one of two di fferent research projects that either asked them to collect and analyze data on predefined topics or to develop a re search question on their own. Thematically, the projects were focused on issues of sustainability in urban spaces, covering topics such as powerrelations and inequalities at urban crossroads, the usage of urban green space s, or the logics that guide personal food consumption. Topics and research designs had been developed by members of the R&D project team who also acted as contact persons for the students on a needs-base. All projects made use of video foot age as a major source of research data. The courses had no mandatory schedules allowing students to join in or leave at any point in time, and to apply for an individual assessment. The number of participants in the different research projects CSCL 2023 Proceedings  © ISLS 415 ranged from 2 to 52 students. In all cases the students worked remotely and a synchronously in the SCoRe-Docs environment. The kind and amount of pedagogical support provided by members of the project team differed, as well as the specific research scaffolds provided for each of the research projects. Prototype trials were formatively evaluated, making use of students’ self-reports, the digital artifacts created, and the logged interaction data. The outcomes of the formative evaluations are largely in support of the suggested design principles and their implementation in the SCoRe-Docs environment. A combined analysis of the logged interaction data and the contents created showed that the artifact-based scaffolding (DP1) actually allowed students to productively build on each-others’ contributions and to synthesize their findings even under asynchronous working conditions. Toward this end the possibility for unambiguous and consistent cross-references  (DP5), a feature not fully implemented in the first trial, turned out to be essential. The analysis of th e logged interaction data also made evident that students attuned their activities to the overall state of the project and contributed in different ways (DP8), including activities that indicate forms of joint quality care (DP6). Students’ feedback as well as the selfinitiated creation of a ‘bulletin board’ within the environment support the relevance of vital signs (DP3), a central hot spot (DP4), and a social arena (DP10). With regard to the importance of dynamic trail marks (DP2), common resources (DP7), and the organization of the collaborative effort around a shared matter of concern  (DP9), the outcomes of the formative evaluations remain inconclusive. This is partly due to the fact that the trials have been carried out in an institutional setting constrained by traditional ideas of knowledge acquisition and assessment.

## Conclusions and open issues

The SCoRe-Docs environment has been envisioned and developed as an attempt to harness large-scale collaboration as an asset for higher education. Its design has been inspired by re cent knowledge practices that leverage digital technologies to promote and cultivate new forms of collective inquiry, engagement and participation. Being attuned to the particular needs of the Virtual Academy of Sustainability and emphasizing the use of video footage as a core data source, the SCoRe-Docs environment onl y provides a provisional attempt to support student crowd research in higher education.

Yet, the development and prototype trials of the SCoRe-Docs environment not only made evident that the idea of student crowd research is actually feasible but also she ds light on the need for a tight alignment of technological and pedagogical development efforts. Toward this end, the articulation of design principles played a pivotal role. Finally, the design process also revealed some limitations of c urrently available collaborative technologies, providing direction for future research and development. These includ e proper mechanisms for complex decision making in large-scale asynchronous environments, mechanisms for the collective advancement, evolution and utilization of semantic annotations in collaborative research environments, as well as interoperable specifications for tracing the evolution of shared and complexly structured knowledge artifacts.
