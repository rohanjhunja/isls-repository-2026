### Abstract

## Introduction and purpose for research

In today's data-driven society, the development of data literacy ha s become increasingly crucial (Wolff et al., 2016). For educators, pedagogical content knowledge (PCK) is as critical as subject matter knowledge (Miller & Yoon, 2023) for students’ learning experience. Howe ver, research on developing PCK for data literacy r emains limited. Therefore, this study explores:

1. What parts of the learning experiences worked well, and what parts did students struggle with?

2. How to leverage surfaced data to inform PCK for data literacy design moves?

## Methodology

Instructors and research team co-designed an online data literacy course for pre-service teachers, iteratively refining it over four semesters using a design-based research framework (Fall 2022 – Summer 2023). Qualitative data from 142 out of 173 students' self-reflections were collected and analyzed. In-vivo, axial, and focused coding and learning experience network analysis (LENA, Don aldson et al., 2024) were employed to quantify and visualize thematic connections across learning experiences.

## Discussion

This study showcases an approach of developing inst ructors' PCK for data literacy by iteratively inves tigating learner experiences in real-world classrooms using the DBR framework. The instr uctors systematically design and redesign learning experiences based on emergent  needs surfaced from the data, while tracking the effectiveness of these designs. Future research cou ld e xplore epistemic cognition and develop measures for evaluating the effectiveness of design conjectures to provide insights into their generalizability in different disciplines and contexts.
