# Embracing Diversity and Connectivity for Entrepreneurship Education Innovation: An Observation Study of Co-Creation Pedagogy for Design Thinking Capacity Building Program of Higher Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
With the growing popularity of design thinking capacity building initiatives for entrepreneurship education, educators are striving to explore technology-supported learning environments and pedagogy to achieve the inter-disciplinary, easily accessible, and studentoriented entrepreneurship education innovation. The lingering effects of unfinished learning amid the COVID-19 and mixed-mode learning have become part of a new normal, we designed and implemented a novel learning framework to put co-design pedagogical structures in place that allow educators, students, and stakeholders to form new learning experiences and create innovation together. Through the case study we designed and implemented in three universities across different regions, we propose and investigate an approach that enables micro-level analysis of knowledge creation model for student’s design thinking capacity building as well as macro-level understanding of learning dynamics for entrepreneurship education. This study presents a pedagogy-based template, and the findings have implications for the design of technology-empowered educational interventions and pedagogical innovation. ## Introduction

Under the global COVID-19 pandemic, policy makers, educators, and students are challenged by the high cost of this prolonged period of remote learning, from rising rates of student learning loss. The global pandemic has set back normal learning routine for all students. However, it is well noted that the pandemic dramatically fuels global growth of entrepreneurship program. This wide phenomenon is practically evident in higher education institutions. Entrepreneurship education has become a strategical ly important part of the university learning experience as it engages students with pressing real-world problems, develops design thinking, and broadens their life skills both for academic and non-academic advancement (Hansen &Wyman, 2021). More specifically, how to equip learners with the design thinking capacities for knowledge creation and innovation has become a great challenge facing education. The OECD has undertaken  national reviews of the impact of higher education institutions in supporting entrepreneurship. Recommendations include the encouragement of an entrepren eurial mindset, problem-based learning, enhancing interdisciplinarity, and linking students with the local, regional, and international community (OECD, 2019).

Gaps: “Can entrepreneurship be taught?” Over the past two decades, universities provided students with business entrepreneurship course and experiential programmes alongside extra-curricular activities. However, business schools struggle to teach entrepre neurship because there are not unified standards in entrepreneurship courses, such as course content, anticipated learning outcome, program delivery. Researcher, educators, a nd students think there are ambiguity in standardiz ing the curricula as it is important to tailor entrepreneurship education to specific learning environment, business contexts, and community needs.

Entrepreneurship education has been embedded in a design thinking lens, as evidenced by the increasing number of that have roots in design element (Garbui o et al. 2018; Glen, Suciu, and Baughn 2014). Yet t here is limited understanding and empirical research of the  conceptual underpinnings and the implementation of  such practices. Additionally, whether fully online, hybrid, or in-person with social distancing constrains, these learning formats represent a substantial departure from normal study year.
