# Bridging Equity and Play: Conceptual Insights into Learning and Assessment

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Playfulness is increasingly valued as a crucial design component in the Learning Sciences community. Its emphasis on learners' freedom, agency, and meaningful choices implies that playful design holds promise for promoting equitable learning opportunities. However, the precise mechanisms through which a playful approach can enhance equity in learning and assessment require further investigation. We explore theoretical connections between play and equity, focusing on how the Four Freedoms of Play intersect with contemporary understandings of equitable learning environments within the Learning Sciences and the broader literature on Play.

## Introduction

Playfulness is increasingly valued in Learning Scie nces communities, both as a design principle and a learning theory. The diverse theoretical foundations behind its use as a design element and its implementation in specific learning settings have been extensively explored. For example, Kafai (2018) demonstrates how Constructionists, such as Seymour Papert, recognize the importance of playful activities, termed "game-related activities," in youth culture. They advocate for "hard fun," challenging activities that engage learners and encourage them to invest time and effort in solving complex problems. Simila rly, Gee (2003) acknowledges the role of playful co ntexts like games in facilitating authentic problem-solving and identity development. This has led to significant research over the past two decades in areas such as game-based learning, embodied science learning (e.g., St einberg & Danish, 2022), makerspaces and creative computing (e.g., Peppler, 2022), and artful learning (e.g., Ha lverson, 2021). Advocates  of playful learning suggest that its emphasis on le arner freedom, agency, and meaningful choices can lead to more equitable learning opportunities. In this poster, we discuss our ongoing efforts to establish theoretical connections between playfulness and equity by exploring its potential as a design eleme nt in learning and assessment environments.

Conceptual connections between playfulness and equity Conceptualizations of playfulness in learning are d iverse and dynamic, often defying easy definition, as Bogost

(2016) suggests, "anything can become a game with a small shift of perspective." Despite this, there is consensus

in the literature that playful ness in learning encompasses a spectrum of experien ces with varying levels of structure (Tekinbas & Zimmerman, 2004; Zosh et al., 2017), and manifest s as behaviors, dispositions, mindsets, qualities, or styles interpretable by individuals within and outside of play (Dearybury & Jones, 2020; DeBenedet, 2018; Holflod, 2022; McDonald, 2024). Scholars continue to explore the nuanced qualities of play that influence playful learning (McDonald, 2024; Trammell, 2023), emphasizing its pleasurable, purposeful, and produc tive aspects. For instance, Parker & Thomsen (2019) asse rt that playful learning  is most effective when it is joyful, meaningful, actively engaging, iterative, and socially interactive, while Dearybury and Jones (2020) highlight the pleasure in unpredictability associated with wonder, spontaneity, imagination, humor, and sociability. However, some scholars challenge conventional notio ns of pleasure, purpose, and productivity in playfulness. McDonald (2024) argues that playfulness can include repetitiveness and deliberate meaninglessness, while Trammell (2023) calls for an understanding of playfulness beyond the White dominant gaze, encompassing rebellion against societal norms and recognition of  painful experiences.  Building upon these perspectives, we argue for a reevaluation of assessment practices to  align with the multifaceted natur e of playful learning. If we accept that playful learning can evoke both joy and  pain, assessments must provide a safe space for ad dressing negative emotions. If playful learning challenges c onventional rules, assessments should be flexible e nough to accommodate unconventional approaches  and perspectives. And if playfulness sometimes embr aces deliberate meaninglessness, assessments should allow for exploration of non-linear and at times unproductive learning paths. We see these conjectures play out in  earlier work on playful assessment by  Kim and Miklasz's (2021); based on Scot Osterweil’s (2014) Four Freedoms of Play, they  emphasize learners' experimentation, safety in failure, encouragement of diverse perspectives, and autonomy  in effort. ## Discussion and implications

In this work, we show that features of playfulness can create opportunities for equity in assessment design. While this framework is still evolving, it offers initial  design heuristics that can be refined in future it erations. Our aim is to transform these heuristics into a theoretical and design conjecture map to guide the design of playful learning and assessment, facilitating empirical investigations into their alignment with intended equity outcomes. We urge the Learning Sciences community to embrace diverse notions and facets of play and playfulness in (re)designing learning environments and educational systems, fost ering learners’ ability to navigate cultural differ ences and social injustices towards healing, resilience, and community-building. By incorporating principles of playful learning into educational assessments, we advocate for the acceptance and equitable integration of div erse ways of knowing, challenging hegemonic conceptions of learning and academic achievement.
