# Design Considerations for Supporting Youth in Developing Critical Community-Centered Artistic Data Visualizations

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study aims to investigate the design considerations and tensions in developing data visualization activities that integrate multi-disciplinary, critical, and community-centered approaches to data learning. We do so through conjecture mapping and qualitative analysis in the context of a design-based research study of an informal education program focused on data visualization. We found multiple design considerations tied to each approach, with critical approaches often deprioritized and related to tensions with the other approaches.

## Introduction and theoretical framework

Youth learning about data can be seen as interdisciplinary or transdisciplinary (Mejias et al., 2021) incorporating computing, mathematics, and art (Matuk et al., 2022). Scholars have also argued for the importance of integrating critical perspectives with data learning (Philip et al., 2016), highlighting the ethical and sociopolitical dimensions of work with data.  Additionally, work in data science education has no ted the potential of community-centered data approaches to connect to civic engagement and community action (Miller et al., 2024)  as distinct from personally-relevant data (e.g., Lee & Dubovi, 2020) and/or big data approaches (e.g., Higgins et al., 2023; Kahn, 2020). Each of these perspectives (i.e., multi-disciplinary, critical, community-centered) may lead to resonance or tensions among them in the design of activities for data learning. For instance, previous research has found tensions supporting youth to take up personally rel evant data from critical approaches that consider t he social, cultural, and political aspects of the data (Reigh et al., 2022; Stornaiuolo, 2020). We explore the following research questions: (1) What design considerations are raised in designing data visualization activities that integrate multi-disciplinary, critical, and community-centered approaches? (2) What tensions are present in this design process, and how do those tensions relate to the integration of the approaches?

## Methods and data sources

This work is part of a design-based research study working with informal education partners in Eastern Tennessee, investigating how to support youth ages 10-18 to engage with and create critical data visualiz ations about their community. We used d ata from design and facilitation teams in the fourth it eration of the informal program: materials designed and used, videotaped virtual con versations planning and reflecting on design, audio taped facilitation debriefs, and reflective analytic memo s from design and facilitation teams. A STEM teacher (2 nd author), along with two researchers (1 st and 3rd author) led the design team. The teacher (2nd author) and one of the researchers (3rd author) facilitated the program, along with mentors from the informal education partner. We analyzed data using insight-oriented qualitative methods (Scherr, 2009). After implementation, we drafted conjecture maps for data visualization activities  to support team reflection and sensemaking (Lee et al., 2022). We drew on our unpacked learning goals to identify mediating processes. Based on the mediating processes in the maps, we examined data from design conversations and analytic memos to identify design considerations. Finally, we analyzed two data visualization activities. The SpongeBob GIF usage activity used a ranked list and a treemap visualizations of the frequency of different emotions or actions in internet searches for SpongeBob GIFs (Daniels, 2020). The cardboard box city budget activity used cardboard boxes, each representing about $100,000, in a physical bar chart of amounts allocated to different uses (e.g., public safety, affordable housing) in the annual budget for the city that youth lived in. We used an analytic framework to summarize  the (a) design decision(s) made, (b) considerations in making the decision, and (c) how this feature of the design may have been connected to a mediating process. Using these summaries, we identi fied patterns in how we approached the considerations, including tensions across considerations related to different mediating processes.

## Findings

Overall, we found there were challenges anchoring d ata visualization activities in ways that had affor dances for all of our multi-disciplinary, critical, and community-centered design goals at once. Often one design goal was prioritized over others, though the goal foreground ed was different among the data visualization activ ities. The data visualization activities are summarized in the  conjecture map in Figure 1. Looking at patterns in our design considerations to understand the link between embodiment and mediating processes, we found that considerations primarily related to the mediating process youth pose and explore critical questions of data a nd uses were more often sidelined. For example, planned discussions a bout data context and its collection were rare, des pite providing contextual information about the data and  collection. We could have (in retrospect) raised that the city budget is something that (1) shows what governments  prioritize, (2) can potentially be changed, and (3) may not be accessible based on the use of tables alone (without our visualization) given the complexity of city budgets and how they are reported. With SpongeBob GIFs, the fac ilitation did not explore the source of the data or  ask questions about the implications of the collection and context. We also found design tensions that highlight tradeoffs between the critical questions mediating process and the other mediating processes, shared below. Figure 1 Conjecture Map for Data Visualization Activities First, there was a tension between supporting youth to notice and reason about patterns in the data  and to pose or explore critical questions of data and uses. In design reflections, we noted that there were consistently deliberations about the features of data visualizat ions from a critical perspective in planning, which  were then contrasted with the absence of these discussions with youth during the session.  Second, there was a tension between supporting youth to reflect on data in relation to their identities, in terests, and membership in communities and to pose or explore  critical questions of data and uses. By prioritizing connections to youth’s identities, interests, and communities through maki ng the topic of the visualization about them, it li mited opportunities for youth to use their identities, in terests, and community membership as a perspective to critique or reimagine the data and narratives of the visualization. Third, there was a tension between supporting youth to reflect on artistic media and to pose or explore critical questions of data and uses. In design reflections, we noted that some of the data visualization activities were  stronger examples of artistic media than others in  terms of the opportunities to see how their artistic features could support an artistic vision.

## Discussion

Our goals were to explore the considerations and te nsions in the design of data visualization activiti es that integrated multi-disciplinary, critical, and community-centered design goals together. We found that full integration of design features related to all goals was challenging, and it was much more common to see features related to different design goals prioritized in different data visualization activities. We found int egrating critical perspectives into data visualization activities was often deprioritized, which appear related to tensions with other design goals. Further work is needed to better understand connecti ons between other activities in the program which could act as contexts for integration or brid ging across goals. Additionally, it would be benefi cial to empirically analyze youth’s engagement during the d ata visualization activities to address the limitat ion of this study with respect to more directly observing mediating processes in action.
