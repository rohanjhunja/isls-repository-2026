# One Teacher’s Journey toward Inquiry Methods

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In response to the increasingly complex social identity development of students, China's education reform focuses on developing students' core competence. The South China PLC is a professional learning community participated by Chinese teachers, which aims to provide support for teachers' professional development, help teachers design and implement inquiry projects, so as to cultivate students' core competence and implement the requirements of education reform. This paper reports on the trajectory of one teacher, Mr. Z, who gradually developed a student-centered teaching philosophy and understanding of inquiry methods with the help of researchers and other participants. Over the course of 2 years, Mr. Z designed and implemented an inquiry curriculum, enacted it in his classroom, then revised the curriculum based on his experience.

## Introduction

Entering the 21st century, countries all over the world-including China-attach importance to cultivating students' core competencies, to prepare them for the complex and changeable world they will enter as citizens. S cholars and policymakers have calle d for education that better prepares students for t his complex society, with an emphasis on critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, collaboration, and communication (OEC D, 2016; Partnership for 21st Century Skills, 2011). In Chin a, the Ministry of Education issued new policies in  2017 and 2022 requiring efforts to be made to develop students' core competencies, and  the inclusion of interdisciplinary themes (MOE of PRC, 2017; MOE of PRC, 2022). The teaching of core competencies must emphasize authentic problems and situations where students actively participate, explore and discover under the guidance of teachers. However, most traditional classroom teaching is lecture-based, and ignores the development of students' inquiry, collaboration and communications skills. Therefore, teachers must transform their teaching approach to cultivate these competencies in curriculum design and practice.

This paper explores the journey of Mr. Z, a high school biology teacher  from southern China, who participated in a teacher professional development community for three years. Through workshops, curriculum design, enactment, reflection, and revision, we explore how this professional development served to shape  Mr. Z’s teaching and learning philosophy. We examine  his design of an inquiry project, its implementation in class, his reflection on the teaching practice, and revisions of his curriculum based on his experiences.

## Literature review

Inquiry-based learning (IBL) allows teachers and learners t o share responsibility for learning. R ooted in constructivist theories that emphasize learner agency (Archambault, 1974; Linn & Hsi, 2000; Minner et al., 2010) and curiosity-driven learning   (Dewey & Archambault, 1974), IBL encourages students to ask questions and investigate problems independently (Lee et al., 200 4). Compared to  traditional didactic epistemology, IBL requires teachers to facilitate students' active en gagement, supporting them in observation, questioni ng, collaboration, and problem-solving (Scardamalia, 2002). However, teachers are challenged in adopting inquiry as a teaching strategy (Kaiserfeld, 1999).

Incorporating inquiry into a teacher’s practice will be a significant departure from the traditional modes of teaching and learning. E specially in Chin a, under the influence of National Higher Education Entrance Examination, the classroom instruction has heavily  favored a didactic approach.  To support teachers in making such a  shift, our team organized a professional learning c ommunity (PLC). Prior research describes PLCs as groups of professionals collaborating in an ongoing, learning-oriented, critical interrogation of their practice (Toole & Louis, 2002).  It is  argued that success in establishing such a communit y requires informed and purposeful action based on learning (Hipp et al., 2008), and that there is a need for the community to be grounded in supportive structures and strategies (Supovitz et al., 2003). PLCs may offer a powerful context for professional development of teachers, with opportunities for kno wledge building, collaborative design and exchange o f resources, new curricula and teaching practices, so cial and emotional support, and continuing developm ent of one's professional identity.

In sections below we describe our professional development program, which aims to support teachers in a PLC and determine their own trajectories of change. As this work is still in progress, we submit a paper w ith a case study of one teacher’s journey. The research q uestions addressed by this study are (1) In the PLC, how has Mr. Z's understanding of inquiry-based learning changed?  and (2) In the PLC, h ow has Mr. Z  improved in the design and implementation of inquiry-oriented curriculum?

## Methods

The Critical Action Learning Exchange (CALE) is a global professional learning community (PLC) for teachers, to support their design and exchange of inquiry-oriented curriculum lessons that challenge students  with 21st century competencies and empower them  in relation to social and scientific issues like th e global climate crisis (Carvalho et al., 2021). This paper details one teacher’s experience in CALE-China, where teachers gather annually to design curriculum and discuss classroom successes and challenges from the previous year. Organized by researchers from a university in southern China and Canad a, the CALE-China hosts a six-week summer institute with weekly meetings and assignments.  Teachers collaborate in teams to create and refine curriculum, receive feedback from peers and mentors, and plan the implementation of their designs. 45 teachers gathered in 2020, 59 in 2021 (19 continued to participate) and 43 in 2022 (including 6 attend ed all three years). Veteran teachers share experiences and provide guidance. Ea ch year, teachers complete pre-and post-surveys assessing their understanding of inquiry pedagogy.  We used questionnaires and interviews, such as the STE M Teacher Workshop Participation Questionnaire, the 21st Century Core Competencies Teaching Skills Questionnaire and Interview, and the Teaching Beliefs Questionnaire, and teaching design and learning reflection doc uments to collect data and analyze teacher learning. This paper reports on the journey of Mr. Z, a teacher  with years of biology teaching experience. He attended the PLC to learn new teaching methods and sought to develop an inquiry project that would support students in learning con cepts deeply while also gaining 21st-century core competencies.

## Findings

As a biology teacher in a Chinese high school, Mr. Z traditionally relied on lecture-based methods. The third year of high school is crucial in China, with a strong e mphasis on exam preparation, and Mr. Z was selected for this important position. He was aware of the Chinese Ministry of Education's  goals regarding core competencies and interdisciplinary projects  and had some experience with extracurricular activities  involving inquiry.  Sections below detail each year of the project, focusing on Mr. Z’s professional development journey. Year one: Origins of the coastal flooding project, and Mr. Z’s own inquiry In the first year, the "IBL Project of Coastal Eros ion" was designed and implemented jointly by three schools in Macao, Shenzhen and Dongguan. The main objective of  the IBL program is for students to understand coas tal erosion, analyze factors that affect erosion resist ance through physical modeling, and propose solutio ns to delay erosion. This year, Mr. Z's school organized four teachers (English, math, geography, and biology) to participate in the project, and although some progress was made  in inquiry-based design, the implementation of the curriculum was still largely lecture-based, featuring demonstration experiments and inde pendent homework projects. Although Mr. Z didn't contribute to the initial cur riculum design in the first year, he gleaned crucia l insights by watching summer project videos, studyin g relevant materials, participating in PLC chatting  groups, and actively communicating with researchers. Throug h this process, he made adjustment s to the teaching design based on his understanding, laying the foundation to develop his own curriculum for the following year. Year two: Mr. Z Joins the PLC and reflects on his own practices In the second year, Mr. Z incorporated local issues, such as mangrove ecology, into the project with o pen-ended survey questions that encouraged students to explore climate change data, learn about STEM concepts, and think critically. Mr. Z organized field trips for students to identif y plants, participate in bird watching, observe ero ded landforms, and discuss mangrove adaptations and con servation. Working in groups of three, students int eracted with community members through activities such as display boards and brochures. Recognizing the importance of his biology course fo r exam performance, Mr. Z introduced inquiry methods by offering the curriculum unit as an "interesting class" after regular school hours for a smaller group of volunteer students. One student group from Mr. Z’s class won the first prize in a high-level innovation competition. Mr. Z agrees that he could use the curriculum in his main biology course in the coming years. But he wants to be confident in this pedagogy first, and make sure  that it is effective in supporting all students.  In this year, Mr. Z showed a strong willingness to invest time in professional developm ent and sought support from others. He actively engaged in the workshop, focusing on culti vating students' core competencies and integrating workshop materials into his curriculum implementation. Reflecting on his experience, Mr. Z emphasized the importance of core competencies in student learning.  " Through the summer workshop, my understanding impro ved. In the project implementation and students' practical activities, I will consciously focus on these ideas. " Year three: Mr. Z improves his understandings and praxis After two years, Mr. Z concluded that mangrove ecol ogy was more suitable for in-depth inquiry than coastal flooding. Hence, the project was renamed "Prospects  and Trauma-Mangrove Forests on Dongguan Coast." Mr. Z revised the curriculum to include the following major elements: assessing students' prior knowledge; introducing scenarios for reflection; exploring mangrove ecology through group activities; highlighting their beauty and functions; investigating threats; facilitating sharing and self-assessment. Mr. Z focuse d on student engagement, addressing their preconceptions, and guiding them from understanding to action with self-evaluation and reflection. The curriculum is now in its third year of implementation. In the third year's workshop, Mr. Z was highly engaged. Wh ile watching videos related to "Teaching Student Empowerment", he observed " Last year, I found this video insightful, but today, my understanding is even deeper. Empowering our students with skills like problem-solving and critical thinking is crucial for them to adapt to an increasingly complex society. We need to change our approach to educating children, as they are the future's driving force." Mr. Z actively contributed to group discussions, frequently sharing ideas on inquiry, (e.g., "The 5E inquiry model is not the same as the inquiry  in biology, but I agree that from the perspective of the curriculum, the 5E model is better. " As he redesigned his curriculum,  he said "My work is fueled by coffee, with only 2 hours of sleep daily. I revise constantly, w riting, deleting, and refining. Gradually, I'm real izing that my words finally express what I've always intended to say." Regarding student learning, Mr. Z emphasized, "We may not be able to change many realities, but we candiscuss what is behind these social problems, whether there is a lack of supervision and other deep-seated reasons. These discussions will help student s to think independently and form a critical perspective." As his comprehension of inquiry-based learning deepened, Mr. Z acknowledged taking "detours" in previous years and felt that hi s earlier courses lacked sufficient inquiry element s. Reflecting on his evolving understanding of "inquiry", Mr. Z t hought the first year’s enactment was " Not very good, t he interdisciplinary formalism''. In the second year, "many activities just allow students to 'investigate' and 'discover', and these are not really inquiries." ## Discussion and conclusion

In order to cultivate innovative talents who can ad apt to the future society in the 21st century, student-centered educational concepts and methods such as Inquiry-based learning are increasingly valued in China's educati on. Thus, teachers must learn about these  educational concepts and apply them in practice.  Any PD program must focus on teachers’ needs, focuses, prospects, emoti ons, motivations, and dreams  (Korthagen, 2017). Therefore, we've established a PLC that emphasizes knowledge sharing, collaborative design, resource exchange, and socialemotional support to help teachers understand, design, and implement student-centered inquiry. Mr. Z actively engages in this PLC, benefiting from strong support and guidance. This research has reported on  his progress, addressing challenges and collaborating on course design and reflections. Prior to joining the PLC, Mr. Z lacked knowledge of  inquiry-based learning but rapidly adapted, embracing new teaching methods in response to polic y changes and student development concerns. His foc us shifted toward on nurturing students' core competen cies. In the second year, he emphasized "learning b y doing" and encouraging practical problem-solving. By the third year, he prioritized student "thinking", challenging preconceptions and fostering reflection. He made significant strides in student-centered teaching, integrating their perspectives and cognitive processes. Transitioning  to inquiry-based learning, he designed a course recognized by PLC members. The South China PLC provides a place for teachers to communicate and learn from each other. Mr. Z's journey toward inquiry is not only about the change of teaching philosophy, but about his participation in new practices, within the social context of the PLC. Mr. Z and numerous teachers are currently enactin g their curriculum designs, anticipating another workshop experience this summer.
