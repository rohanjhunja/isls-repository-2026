# Cultivating Collaborative Wisdom: A Community of Practice Approach to Quantitative Ethnography

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
A growing number of learning scientists are applying quantitative ethnography (QE) to investigate patterns in complex behavior across a variety of contexts, constructs, and cultures. We adopt a community of practice lens to reflect on activities and principles that enable emerging and senior scholars to conduct QE research. We invite a community discussion on additional ways for lowering floors, raising ceilings, and widening walls.

## Introduction

The release of the Quantitative Ethnography book (S haffer, 2017) provided a primer for researchers int erested in a unified methodological approach to investigate mean ingful connections between human behavior, interaction logged in big data, and phenomena of interest. QE scholars hip has since grown to include over 700 publication s, several of which are award-winning studies in learning sciences and learning a nalytics. Members of the QE society are located in over 130 institutions, spanning undergraduate students to deans in over 20 countries across varied disciplines such as health professions education and healthcare, pol icy and business. As the field expands, reports hav e shifted from outlining opportunities and challenges (Kaliisa et al., 2021) to revisiti ng the foundations and fundamentals of QE (Arastoopour Irgens & Eagan, 2022). More recently, Ohsaki (2023) reported findings from a study on teaching QE as a data science method. The goal of all these public ations-though enacted at different times-has been to support members’ movement from the periphery to the center of the community. In our scholarship too, we have w itnessed this movement as we continue to take on a variety o f roles as learners and leaders. As such, in this p oster, we use a community of practice (CoP) lens (Wegner, 1998) to outline the  activities that have contributed to the growth of the QE community, including ours, and principles that w e believe are essential for QE research teams in ac ademia and industry. We hope this poster inspires conversations and reflections from/with aspiring and seasoned QE researchers whose work intersects in the learning sciences and analytics communities, as well as broader, practice-oriented discussions among other leaders in emerging scholarship communities.

## Theoretical framework

Communities of Practice (CoP) are groups of people who share a concern or a passion for a topic and co llectively negotiate best practices through interaction (Wenger, 1998). CoPs develop, curate, and share information/resources that may be useful for enculturating new members. When w ell-designed, such spaces foster interaction and collaboration among participants, especially those that are new o r underrepresented (Gee & Hayes, 2012). Support is embedded to initiate and sustain learning, such as encouraging members to discuss their work and apply their knowl edge toward actionable change. CoPs can foster professional lea rning by catalyzing connections between people, pro viding spaces for members to share individual experiences and facilitating collective insight-building (Wenger, 1998). Members learn through mentoring, coaching and self-reflection as they participate in forums of informa tion exchange and purposeful action, including communal dialogue on possibilitie s, issues, and mutually beneficial opportuniti es. Lastly, members organize to improve their knowledge and to generate new knowledge and practices that meet the evolving needs of the CoP (Wegner, 2006). A critical function of members is to narrate their work and make implicit knowledg e visible. Below, we list activities that are characteristic to the QE community and identify principles we apply in our overlapping and interdisciplinary teams.

Community building activities and principles The International Society for Quantitative Ethnogra phy (ISQE) offers an array of activities all year a round for members to understand the foundations of QE, deepen  their knowledge of QE methods and tools, and learn  from QE applications and projects undertaken by individuals  and groups of scholars. These include, but are not  limited to, the annual conference, monthly webinars and sandbox events, Spring data challenges, QE Fellows Institute. The creation of open-source codes and tools, and documentation and w orkshops-designed strategically and organically-are additional activities driven by the members and supported by the society. Participating in these activities have enabled us to build capacity in our roles as educators and researchers. For instance, in a QE sandbox event (2023), Shah (Senior Learning Scientist) laid out the following principles for leading research teams that are highly collaborative and spread across academia and industry: (1) recognizing, foregrounding and nurturing team members’ expertise, (2), scaffolding project activities like LEGO manuals, (3) creating opportunities for reflection, discussion and innova tion, (4), collaborating to foster shared understanding and av oid blind  spots, and (5) using academic and industry contexts/needs/gaps as a compass. Her key takeaways  recommended (1) including members with diverse strength s and interests, (2) structuring tasks in meaningful chunks, (3) discussing the “weird and wonderful” th roughout the research process, (4) showing and telling often, and (5) letting your North stars guide you (e.g., research purpose, theory). Furthermore, enculturating newcomers into the QE community could be distilled into principles  evident in Barany’s (Instructor) constructionist and project-based approaches: (1) teaching as a community-wide endeavor, (2) scaffolding assignments with practical value, (3) learning through peers’ journeys, (4) balancing theory and technical emphasis and (5) embracing and highlighting unsolve d challenges. Key takeaways for newcomers include (1) authentic practice as legitimate (and increasingly central) members, (2) normaliz ing and validating ongoing learning (debunking the myth of the “expert”), (3) flexible professional relationships between new and long-term members, and (4) research as an iterative, joyful, and impactful endeavor. As is the case in much of the research in the learning sciences and the computer supported collaborative learning (CSCL) communities, use of QE methods is complex and requires sustained training in multiple disciplines  and the development of tran s or multi-disciplinary teams. As such, Eagan’s (Associate Director for Partnerships and Co mmunity Engagement) philosophy of developing CoP is  guided by the following points: (1) when collaborating or mentoring, center the partner and mentee win states and align them with your own when possible (2) employ radical candor (Scott, 2019) and a radically welcoming stance towards others,

(3) use an ‘each one teach one’ and pay it forward mentality and (4) apply a cognitive apprenticeship approach to

cultivate more central members of the QE community while building capacity.

## Scholarly implications

Mitchell and Sackney (2000) framed learning communities as a living system, and they model capacity building from an ecological perspective. Specifically, in their m odel, Mitchell and Sackney (2000) describe the char acteristics and benefits of building capacity at the personal, interpersonal, and organizational levels. Our intention with this poster is to initiate a discussion with the wider QE communit y that extends into the International Society of th e Learning Sciences (ISLS). To this end, we offer the followin g discussion topics for new and returning QE schola rs: (1) Which activities have you participated in and how have they shaped your QE research in the learning sciences? (2) What are some activities that could be introduced in the fut ure to support ongoing CoP development? (3) Which o f the above principles and takeaways resonate with you in your practice as learning sciences scholars that use QE? (4) What other principles do you embrace and enact based on your role(s) as a legitimate participant in the QE community?
