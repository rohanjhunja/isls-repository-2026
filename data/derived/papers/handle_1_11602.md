# Curiosity Portals: Designing Entryways for Tangible Exploration

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Curiosity is a fundamental humandrive that propels exploration, discovery, and learning. While extensively studied, a gap remains in understanding how curiosity is sparked in social settings during initial interactions with tangible objects. This research introduces the concept of Curiosity Portals as a framework for designing and understanding these initial moments of curiosity. Through interviews with staff, artists, and attendees at the Metro Independent Comics Expo (MICE), the study identifies configurations of invitational entryways or "portals" that catalyze curiosity. These portals, categorized as sensorial, novel, accessible, social, and aesthetic, are shaped by the distinct curiosities of each participant group. The study reveals how these portals foster curiosity by providing opportunities for hands-on engagement, surprise, social interaction, and exploration. The proposed framework provides a toolkit to understand and leverage these entry points of curiosity to enhance the design of tactile objects and playthings to foster continuous exploration and deeper learning.

## Introduction

Curiosity, the intrinsic drive to explore and understand the unknown, plays a pivotal role in learning and memory, influencing cognitive and social functions (Berlyen e, 1960; Gruber et al., 2019). It is triggered by m echanisms like novelty, surprise, and uncertainty, and operates as both an individual and social phenomenon (Jirout & Klahr, 2012; Kidd et al., 2015; Sinha et al., 2017). Recen t research has identified two key processes —stretching (the impulse to seek new information) and embracing (eng agement with novel experiences)—which shape curiositydriven interactions (Kashdan et al., 2009). Despite substantial progress in understanding curiosity, particularly in informal settings like museums and libraries, there  remains a gap in examining how curiosity emerges in social interactions with tangible objects (Horn, 2018). Th is study introduces the concept of Curiosity Portal s—inviting configurations that spark curiosity-driven engagement in real-world, informal environments (Price & Falcão, 2011). Drawing from interviews at the Metro Independent Comics Expo (MICE), the study identifies five types of portals (sensorial, novel, accessible, social, a nd aesthetic) that catalyze curiosity and enhance t he design of tangible objects. These Curiosity Portals serve as  frameworks to design playful, curiosity-driven experiences, offering new insights into how objects in informal spaces can stimulate learning and exploration, prom oting deeper, more immersive engagement (Bevan et al., 2014; Tieben et al., 2011).

## Methods

The research was conducted at the Metro Independent  Comics Expo (MICE), an annual event showcasing independent comics and graphic novels, known for it s accessibility and hands-on activities. Data collection involved semi-structured interviews with 20 participants, comprising MICE staff and organizers (n=5), exhibiting artists (n=7), and event attendees (n=8). Participa nts were asked about their experiences at MICE, wha t sparked their curiosity, and their reactions to the concept of a "Curiosity Portal". The interview data were transcribed and analyzed using a reflexive thematic analysis approa ch to ident ify recurring themes related to curiosity triggers, tangible interactions, and design elements within the event.

## Findings

The analysis revealed five distinct types of invita tional configurations, or "portals," that collectiv ely shaped the curiosity-driven experiences at MICE  (Table 1). These portals were influenced by the distinct per spectives and actions of the staff, artists, and attendees.

The first category, Sensorial Portals, involved dir ect physical interaction and sensory engagement. Examples included hands-on workshops where attendees could use materials li ke screen printers, the physical quality of zines and art prints that invited touch,  and opportunities for book signings. Second, Novel  Portals operated by presenting unexpected elements or creating intriguing information gaps. A key example was an artist packaging their zine within old Game Boy cases, sparking surprise and interest, particularly among those familiar with the gaming devices. Unconventional artwork pre sentations also contributed to this portal type. Th ird, Accessible Portals focused on lowering barriers to entry and fostering an inclusive atmosphere. MICE's  free admission policy was frequently highlighted by attendees as  crucial for enabling spontaneous exploration. Open workshops, inclusive panel discussions, clear event signage, and the provision of a supportive "green room" space for artists and volunteers exemplified this portal.  Fourth, Social Portals facilitated meaningful inte rpersonal connections and shared learning experiences. These included opportunities for attendees to meet and co nverse with artists, participate in panel discussions, and engage in networking. The dynamic social environment allowed individuals to learn from others and explore collaboratively or individually. Finally, Aesthetic Portals captured attention through aesthetic appeal. Artists utilize d vibrant booth designs and strategically employed tall display stands to ensure their work was visible amidst the crowd. The overall visual design of the event, including posters and signage, contributed to an atmosphere that sparked visual curiosity. These five portal types demonstrate how intentional design choices (by staff and artists) and the intrinsic curiosities of attendees interacted to create a rich environment for exploration and engagement at MICE. ## Discussion and future implications

This study introduces and provides initial empirical grounding for the Curiosity Portals framework. The findings demonstrate how specific configurations—sensorial, novel, accessible, social, and aesthetic —can serve as effective entry points for curiosity in informal le arning settings like MICE. These portals function b y providing opportunities for hands-on engagement, surprise, low-barrier p articipation, social interaction, and aesthetic appeal, aligning with established theories of curiosity related to information gaps and joyous exploration. The Curiosity Portals framework offers a valuable t oolkit for designers, educators, and curators aimin g to foster deeper engagement with tangible objects a nd environments. By focusing on the design of these  initial "entry points," practitioners can more effectively harness intrinsic curiosity to encourage exploration, interaction, and learning. This approach holds particular releva nce in counterbalancing the prevalence of screen-based interactions by emphasizing embodied, sensory-rich experiences. Future research should aim to fur ther test and refine this framework across diverse informal learn ing contexts and continue to explore the specific a ttributes of tangible objects that best invite and sustain curiosity-driven engagement.
