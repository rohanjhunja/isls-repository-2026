# Brazilian Educators’ Perceptions of STEAM Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper investigates the perceptions of Brazilian educators of STEAM education. The analysis was based on an online survey of participants of the STEAM TechCamp Brasil program. We analyzed two open questions using the Discursive Textual Analysis method. Results indicate that participants perceive STEAM as a pedagogical approach, but have a restricted view of the sciences. This discussion contributes to the current understanding of STEAM in Brazil and to the evolution towards a self-contained conception.

## Introduction

In Brazil, STEAM education practices are still embr yonic. In a survey of STEAM approach experiences, developed and published in the country, the few records found by Maia, Carvalho & Appelt (2021) indicate that they are concentrated in four Brazilian states that  are more economically developed. Research shows th at, considering Brazil's social, cultural, and educational specificities, it should follow the example of other nations that implemented the STEAM approach in their curric ulum and have achieved promising educational result s. Therefore, innovative pedagogical practices need to be developed throughout the country, which has continental dimensions, cultural diversity and deep social ineq ualities. Brazil demands the design of specific STE AM environments and not just the reproduction of models from other countries. In search of a self-contained STEAM approach, that  is, connected to the reality and educational needs of Brazilian youth, some initiatives are being developed by educators and institutions. One of them is th e STEAM TechCamp Brasil, which aims to create a network of teachers and education professionals to articulate and improve actions aimed at active learning of STEAM in public K-12 networks in Brazil. The STEAM TechCamp has a strong potential for disseminating STEAM experiences in the country. This article aims to investigate the perceptions o f participants about STEAM practices that are being developed in the country and how they contribute to  the construction and dissemination of a unique STE AM concept connected to Brazilian reality.

The STEAM Education as an Approach Some STEAM experiences do not innovate in education, nor do they change learning experiences. In these experiences students are asked to learn and memoriz e information presented by their teachers or to dev elop projects defined by their schools or teachers. They  are asked to reproduce experiments, instead of  ob serving, identifying problems and creating solutions. Nevertheless, the STEAM approach may reinforce agency, creativity and critical thinking. It is about pedagogical prac tices that stimulate students to identify problems,  develop projects, test hypotheses and present solutions to the problems they identified. These problems are co mmonly related to the students’ context. As Sawyer (2014, p.2) highlights, to work in today’s knowledge economy, more than memorizing facts and procedures, people “need a deep conceptual understanding of complex concepts and the ability to work with them creatively to generate new ideas, new theories, new products, and new knowledge”.

## Methods

This preliminary study is based on an online questionnaire sent to the 80 participants, responsible for 41 projects that received small grants to implement projects re lated to the STEAM approach. All Brazilian states w ere represented and awarded the resources. We received 29 responses from 15 states, ensuring representation from all 5 regions of the country, and participants from each of the 4 cohorts of the STEAM Tech Camp (2018-2021). We emphasized the questions in which respondents s howed their conceptions of the STEAM approach. Two open questions were selected and their answers analyzed with elements of Discursive Textual Analys is (DTA), which is a technique that intends to verify different perspectives presented in the respondents' discourse, based on meaning units (Moraes & Galiazzi, 2011). The answers about: “How do you define STEAM?” and “How do you understand the 'S' in STEAM?” were categorized into meaning units and analyzed. Thus, as indicated by the DTA procedures, we selec ted extracts of the participants’ responses as context units, which are fragments of the respondent's disc ourse considered central and representative of the response. Then, we identified and categorized these units, wi th the intention of grouping them into categories t hat allow ICLS2022 Proceedings 1890 © ISLS counting their frequency. At the end, we defined the thematic axis of these responses. These DTA methodological procedures favor the categorization and analysis of the discourse in the responses so that we can make inferences about the data in order to make it more understanda ble and capable of formulating evidence, based on t he objectives of the study and the theoretical framework adopted.

## Results

Regarding the first question, which aimed to understand the STEAM TechCampers’ conception of the definition of STEAM, we organized the 29 responses, condensed into 4 thematic axes (educational approach; educational innovation movement; teaching and learning methodology; meaning of the acronym) from 10 units of meaning (approach; movement; methodology; science, technology, engineering, arts and mathematics; strategy system didactic-pedagogical actions; new way of interaction; innovative and inspiring; way of thinking education). A high frequency of respondents defined STEAM as a n educational approach (n i = 12) or even a movement (ni = 4) that seeks educational innovation through peda gogical practices based on interdisciplinarity, investigative spirit and creative learning. This su ggests that most of the STEAM TechCampers are not disseminating STEAM as a mere educational technolog y fad. The thematic axis – Education Approach and Educational Innovation Movement – together, corresponds to 69% of respondents. With regard to the second question, there was a pr edominance of the indication of “sciences,” due to the meaning in the acronym, followed by two occurrences related to the “scientific method.” We managed to group the 29 responses into 4 axes, namely: Natural Sciences, Subject, Scientific Method and Broad Sense. There is a balance between two conceptions of the “S” in STEAM: i) the one that understands the concept of “S” as restricted to natural sciences (34.5%), that is, a very limited view of doing science which excludes other areas and paradigms of science; and ii) one which highlights the scientific method (34.5%), or the methodological procedures inherent in investigative practice. This data shows us that the second perspective, although expanding the compression of “Science”, it was not possible to perceive whether the participants understand the methodological procedures of other areas of Science like Human Science or Social Science. This view of science in a broad sense was evident in the speech of 21% of respondents.

## Conclusions

The results show that most participants understand STEAM as an interdisciplinary pedagogical approach or even an educational innovation movement that intends to develop learning practices focused on the learner, based on scientific, investigative, creative and real-world thinking. This concept is in line with what is expected and desired for the Brazilian reality due to its educational, economic and social development demands. As for the conception of science, it is necessary that educators, including STEAM TechCamp Brasil participants, expand the scope of the areas that ma ke and produce scientific knowledge. The restricted  or more specific view of natural or exact sciences, represented mainly by subjects related to science and math ematics in schools, is probably due to the technicist perspective present and for a long time perpetuated by STEM. Because of this, the examples usually brought as applicatio n of STEAM experiments focus on projects with proce dures and fundamentals from physics, chemistry, biology a nd mathematics to the detriment of social science a nd humanistic subjects. The inclusion of the arts, in addition to the concern with design and development of pertinent soft skills, should include application in the real world, with attention to living beings and the environment, objects of study in the human, social and health sciences.
