# Science Teachers’ Ideological Shifts About Multilingual Learners

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Drawing on theories of ideologies in interaction, this poster considers the extent to which professional development supported elementary teachers’ rearticulations of monolingual ideologies toward expansive views of young multilingual learners’ capabilities in science.

## Motivation and conceptual framework

In STEM classrooms, leveraging multilingual and multimodal resources can contribute to more equitable learning environments for multilingual learners (Pierson et al., 2021). However, learning is shaped by dominant ideologies that privilege “standard” language varieties such a s (academic) English, positioning minoritized langu age varieties as deficient (Flores & Rosa, 2015) and li miting opportunities for meaningful sensemaking. This study considered the extent to which a professional devel opment (PD) project supported teachers in rearticul ating monolingual ideologies that constrained learning for multilingual learners in science. We conceptualize ideologies as powerful and politic ally non-neutral lenses used to interpret and make sense of students, classrooms, and society (Louie, 2020; Philip, 2011). Ideologies can be constraining, reproducing the status quo, and they can be expansive, challenging and disrupting dominant beliefs about the way things should be. Furthermore, ideologies are complex, fluid, and contradictory, rather than static across contexts (Louie, 2020; Philip, 2011). They can also be rearticulated through interaction (Philip, 2011; Philip et al., 2018).

## Method

This work builds on a larger research study supporting elementary teachers in implementing model-based science learning sequences. Yet equitable teaching may have  been constrained by pervasive monolingual ideologi es (Daniel, 2021). To trace teachers’ ideological sens emaking, we drew on Philip’s (2011) concept of stan ce— verbalized sensemaking dependent on the positionali ty of the speaker. Using interaction analysis (Jord an & Henderson, 1995), we analyzed moments of stance-tak ing in ideological discourse. We focus on an illust rative case of Marie M., who shifted toward expansive ideologies in the context of our design.

## Findings

We compare two moments from our PD. In both moments, Marie (all names are pseudonyms) shared with colleagues a narrative about her 4th grade student Mila’s participation in a science lesson on rainbows and light. Mila was a multilingual learner who spoke Swahili a nd had limited experience with English. We argue th at significant shifts in Marie’s retelling of the narrative illustrate rearticulations of language ideologies in our larger PD community (see Figure 1).

In our design, our research team initially failed to develop shared language around multilingual learners. As a result, our teachers most often used the term “English Learner” or “EL,” the label assigned to students by the school district. However, the use of the label was often expanded beyond students who received dis trict language services. Marie used “EL” in her first nar rative (February 2020) to refer to any student who spoke a language other than English. To address this limita tion of our design, we spent time in June 2021 disc ussing systemic labels and how they cast multilingual lear ners and their language resources as deficit. These conversations were reflected in Marie’s second tell ing of her narrative (June 2021). She noted that so me of her students were labeled ELs by the district, and some had exited out of the program, but all students were bilingual. Her clarification demonstrated a shift in her under standing of the EL label by discussing multilingual  learners with greater nuance and a greater awareness of bilingualism as a resource (Menken & Sánchez, 2019). A second shift highlighted how through their intera ctions as a community, teachers took up more expansive ways of thinking about science sensemakin g for multilingual learners. In February 2020, Mila ’s participation was positioned by Marie as looking, emulating, and copying, assuming that Mila did not understand what she was doing. This was taken up by the group as “step one”. The teachers recognized Mila’s participation but did not necessarily acknowledge that participation as science sensemaking. By contrast, Mila’s participation was framed in June 2021 as one of understanding and  engagement. Mila’s actions of looking and copying were transformed into seeing and observing, actions that  allowed Mila to “catch onto things”, leveraging he r own ICLS2022 Proceedings 1902 © ISLS abilities to draw and her peers’ thinking to engage in her own sensemaking. Rather than merely emulating, Mila’s participation was reframed as an active effort to draw on multimodal resources to communicate her understanding. The teachers’ (re)construction of Mila’s participat ion in the science activity suggests a shift from m ore constraining to more asset-based ideologies around science learning for multilingual learners. ## Discussion and implications

The examples presented here illustrate the rearticu lation of ideologies about multilingual learners. W e acknowledge that beyond our PD, other factors contr ibute to these shifts. For example, Marie was seeki ng EL certification in summer 2021, influencing the ideol ogies that frame teachers’ participation in our PD.  We also acknowledge that deficit ideologies such as hierarchical views of language varieties and a privileging of academic vocabulary persisted in our PD discourse, despite a ctivities designed to surface and challenge these i deologies. Further research is needed to understand how PD can support ideological shifts toward equitable science teaching with elementary multilingual learners.
