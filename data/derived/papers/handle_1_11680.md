# Impact of Competency-Based Education on Student Motivation: 
A Mixed Methods Study

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines the impact of a competency-based education (CBE) grading schema on students' motivation in a higher education pharmacy lab. Grounded in constructivism and self-determination theory, the study utilized a design-based mixed-methods research approach with 200 students over two semesters. Findings indicate that a CBE grading schema fosters intrinsic motivation by prioritizing skill mastery and self-directed learning. The results highlight CBE's potential to transform higher education by shifting from traditional grading to skill-based assessments that enhance engagement and better prepare students for their professional careers.

## Theoretical framework

This research is grounded in constructivism and self-determination theory (SDT). According to Piaget (1952) and Vygotsky (1978), constructivism asserts that learners actively build knowledge through experience and reflection. It emphasizes hands-on, student-centered activities. Assessments aligned with constructivism integrate real-world tasks to evaluate students' abilities. SDT serves a s a fundamental model for comprehending human behav ior and motivation across various areas. This theory sugges ts that individuals inherently require autonomy, co mpetence, and relatedness for intrinsic motivation (Deci & Ryan, 1985). When these needs are met, students are more likely to demonstrate increased intrinsic motivation, enga gement, and well-being, thus enriching the ir overall educational experience.

These theories play a pivotal role in this study. T he CBE grading schema reflects the principles of constructivism by allowing students to engage with course material and actively construct knowledge. The choice embedded in CBE aligns with SDT because students fulfill their intrinsic needs for autonomy. By leveraging these intertwined theories, the study seeks to design an educational environment where students not only actively build their knowledge but are also deeply motivated to engage and succeed.

## Methods

This study utilized a design-based research (DBR) approach to examine the CBE gr ading schema in a pharmacy practice lab over two semesters. DBR allowed iterative testing and refinement of the grading model in real-world settings (Joseph, 2004). Additionally, this study e mployed an explanatory mixed methods design to effe ctively combine the quantitative and qualitative data and gain a more comprehensive understanding of the research topic (Plano Clark & Ivankova, 2016).

The CBE grading system in this course encouraged students to engage deeply and demonstrate their proficiency through graded activities and assessmen ts. Each level was organized into "bundles" from A to F and had specific requirements to meet. For an 'A,' a  student needed a maximum of one unexcused absence, 80% or higher on 10 of 11 lab assessments, perfect scores on quizzes, passing all OSCE stations, satisfactory IPE scores, and completing all peer assignments and evaluations. Lower bundles like B and C ha d fewer requirements but still upheld a high competency standard.

Participants were students from a Tier I research u niversity with a diverse student demographic. Data was collected from 87 students in Spring 2022 and 1 13 students in Spring 2023. Open-ended survey questions gathered qualitative feedback on the grading model's support of student goals, and a modified Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI) assessed student motivation factors based on SDT principles (IMI, 2022). Qualitative data was thematically coded using MAXQD A Analytics Pro 2022 and categorized into struggles (STRUGGLE), successes (WORKED), goal achi evement (ACHIEVE), and SDT principles (THRYSDT). Inductive coding allowed for emergent th emes within these categories. The Learning Experien ce Network Analysis (LENA) method was employed to map relationships, themes, and biases in the qualitative data (Donaldson et al., 2024). Correlational analyses id entified patterns, which were visualized using UCIN et and NetDraw software, enhanced by Girvan-Newman cluster analysis. These methods created netw ork maps that revealed key components and pivot points in implementing the CBE grading schema and highlighted the interplay of challenges, successes, and SDT principles.

## Findings

The findings of this study reveal that the competen cy-based education (CBE) grading schema positively influenced students' intrinsic motivation and auton omy in a pharmacy lab setting. Using the Interest/E njoyment subscale of the Intrinsic Motivation Invent ory (IMI) as a self-report measure, students rated their intrinsic motivation at 3.34 (SD = 0.36) on a 5-point scale. The mean competency score across the cohort was 3.94 (SD = 0.81), indicating a generally high level of skill mastery. The IMI scale further explored factors contributing to intrinsic motivation. Perceived Choice, a positive predictor of intrinsic motivation, was rated at 2.87 (SD = 0.41), suggesting that improvements in offering students meaningful choices within the curriculum could enhance their intrinsic motivation. In contrast, Pressure/Tension, a negative predictor of intrinsic motivation, was relatively high at 3.75 (SD = 0.75). This finding reflects the stress associated with the high-stakes nature of some assessments. However, the qualitative analysis highlighted that the option to retake assessments without penalty and th e schema’s clarity helped mitigate stress and uncer tainty. Students reported viewing feedback as an opportunity for growth and focusing on long-term learning goals. Overall, the results demonstrate that the CBE schema reduces stress by providing clear expectations and opportunities for reassessment, aligning with SDT’s  emphasis on creating environments that support aut onomy, competence, and relatedness. This structure d approach empowers students to focus on skill mast ery rather than grades, fostering intrinsic motivation and self-directed learning. Additionally, the findings refle ct constructivist principles by encouraging active engagement and ref lection, enabling students to build deeper connecti ons with the material. With further refinement to enhance elements like perceived choice, the CBE schema can potentially increase intrinsic motivation and promote sustained engagement and long-term skill development. Potential to advance the field CBE promotes a shift from traditional grading towar d mastery-focused assessment, aligning with constructivism and SDT. However, CBE can be implemented in a varie ty of ways within the classroom without clear means  of assessing how various methods of implementation can affect student motivation. By combining the IMI scale and qualitative analysis of student reflections, this s tudy provides insights into the practical analysis of CBE grading implementation across disciplines. Using this mixed-methods approach, educators can identify evidence-based strategies to improve their implementation of the CBE grading schema to increase student motivation. By focusing on mastery and skill-based assessments, this study envisions education as a tool for societal and global impact by equipping students to address real-world challenges with confidence and creativity.
