# From Drawing to Understanding: Constructionist Approaches in Teaching Complex Biology Concepts

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This paper investigates the efficacy of constructionist learning framework in enhancing students’ understanding of complex biological concepts. Utilizing a pre-and post-survey along with practical drawing exercises, this study the learners’ experiences change in confidence and comprehension. Results indicate a significant increase in learners’ confidence post-survey. It also implies a connection between hands-on drawing activities and improved conceptual understanding.

## Introduction and background

The study addresses the challenges in biology educa tion, where instructors and students grapple with c omplex, abstract concepts. It highlights the importance of using visual aids like models and drawings to make unobservable phenomena understandable, empha sizing the need for students to not only interpret but also create visual representations (Quillin & Thomas, 2015). Despite t he proven benefits of such active learning strategi es in enhancing understanding in post-secondary education (Ainsworth et al., 2011), their  application in large introductory biology courses remains limited.

Rooted in constructionist theory, the research advocates for a learning approach where students actively engage by creating artifacts based on their designs, leading to deeper understanding and meaningful le arning (Papert & Harel, 1991). This hands-on, design-based learning process fosters a collaborative envi ronment where learners candiscuss and refine their ideas, applying them to real-world contexts.

The study investigates the impact of constructionis t methods, such as drawing, on knowledge retention in undergraduate biology courses, shifting from tra ditional passive learning models to an active, cons tructionbased approach that builds on students' prior knowledge (Papert, 1993). It aims to assess how these active learning techniques contribute to a deeper understanding of biological concepts, addressing the main research q uestions about their effectiveness in educational settings.

The main research questions are:

1.  How does the application of constructionist learning methods influence students’ understanding of complex

biological concepts, specifically the electron transport chain (ETC) and DNA structure and replication?

2.  Does active engagement in drawing and labeling mole cular structures, such as ETC and DNA, enhance

students' confidence and comprehension of these concepts?

## Methods

This research aimed to explore the impact of constr uctionist learning methods on students' understandi ng of complex biology topics, specifically the electron t ransport chain (ETC) and DNA structure and replicat ion. Utilizing a combination of pre-and post-surveys alongside an active engagement task where s tudents drew the ETC and DNA structures, the study aimed to enhance comprehension and measure confidence across these topics. Confidence levels were gauged using a five-point Likert scale, and student drawings were graded on a four-point scale to assess knowledge retention and clarity of understanding. In total, data from 198 pre-survey and 195 post-survey responses were analyzed, resulting in 112 unique responses after duplicates were excluded. The surve ys assessed students' confidence in understanding v arious aspects of the mitochondria’s structure and functio n, the process of electron flow through different s tages of cellular respiration, and the mechanics of DNA repl ication, including key differences between eukaryot es and prokaryotes, and the function of telomeres.

To deepen the evaluation of student understanding, the research required participants to redraw the ETC and DNA replication processes, emphasizing accurate depiction and labeling of critical features such as the 5' and 3' ends of DNA, and the synthesis of leading and la gging strands. The drawings were scored for their c larity and accuracy, with higher scores indicating a more profound comprehension of the subjects.

## Findings

The survey before the course showed that 51% of stu dents had taken an AP Biology course and exam, with  27% doing so in their senior year of high school. Howev er, 41% had no prior exposure to AP Biology. Initia lly, confidence in understanding the electron transport chain (ETC) was low, with less than 15% feeling confident and only 2.73% fully confident. Post-course, confidence increased significantly, with up  to 22.26% feeling fully confident and a noticeable rise in overall confidence levels regarding the ETC. Similar improvements were seen in students' understanding of DNA structure and replication. Initially, confidence was below 10% for various DNA topics, wi th a significant number expressing complete lack of confidence. Post-course, there was a substantial i ncrease, with about 41% of students feeling fully c onfident in their understanding of key DNA replication concepts.

In practical assessments, 95.89% of students perfec tly drew and labeled double-stranded DNA, though only 89% accurately added complementary nucleotide sequences. While 86.3% correctly identified strand direction, clarity and accuracy in drawing and labe ling more complex DNA replication processes saw lower scores, indicating areas of challenge for some students.

## Discussion and conclusion

The findings of this study provide insightful contributions to understanding the efficacy of constructionist learning approaches in the context of teaching complex biological concepts such as the electron transport chain (ETC) and DNA structure and replica tion. A key observation from the pre-and post-surveys is the marked increase in students' confidence in understanding these concepts. This positive shift suggests that engaging students in active learning processes, such as drawing molecular struc tures, c an significantly enhance their comprehension and confidence (Cromley et al., 2020; Tytler, 2016, 2020; Rellensman et al., 2017; Wu & Rau, 2019). The data from the pre-survey highlighted a notable lack of confidence amo ng students, especially in topics like the electron transport chain and DNA re plication. Such low confidence levels in understand ing these complex topics are not uncommon in traditional lear ning environments. However, the post-survey results demonstrated a considerable improvement in confiden ce, which is indicative of the potential benefits o f constructionist methods in biological education.

The assessment of student drawings also revealed high levels of accuracy in depicting the 5' and 3' ends of double-stranded DNA, with a majority of the students scori ng full points. This outcome underscores the effectiveness of visual and constructive learning approaches in biology education. However, the decrease in scores for more complex tasks, such as accurately drawing a template DNA strand in the 3’ to 5’ direction, in dicates areas where further instructional support may be ne eded. The varied performances across different tasks suggest that while constructionist methods are beneficial, they need to be supplemented with other instructional strategies to address more challenging concepts. This relates to the idea of constructionism which conveys that t he learner gains a deeper understanding of the concepts when they actively tinker and engage with the process of knowledge construction (Kafai, 2006).

It is important to note the substantial increase in  students' confidence in understanding the role of telomeres in DNA replication and the 5’ end problem, as reflected in the post-course survey. This improvement suggests that visualizing and recreating the se structures can aid in grasping intricate biologi cal processes, which are often challenging to conceptualize through text-based learning alone (Quillin & Thomas, 2015).

In conclusion, this study reaffirms the value of co nstructionist learning methods in enhancing understanding and confidence in biology students, p articularly in complex subject areas. However, give n the limited sample size, further testing of this techni que could yield better insights. It was also tested o n one course in the field of Biology; therefore, no general conc lusion can be drawn about the technique’s effective ness in STEM. Students reported that the adoption of drawing in their class allowed them to visualize the concept better. Like the discussion by Morado et.al; (2020) the participants were able to reflect on their learnings.
