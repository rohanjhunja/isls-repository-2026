# From Data Producer to Data Storyteller: A CS-infused Curriculum Integrating Programmable Sensors

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This case study presents computer science-infused curriculum to support middle school students in developing their data science literacy and computational thinking skills in a community garden. Using the storyline model to embed data science and computati onal practices, students investigate anchoring phenomena using programmable sensors, then provide data-informed recommendations to master gardeners on how to improve food yield. Students progress from collecting and analyzing data towards developing personally-relevant narratives, connecting community priorities through data storytelling.

This paper describes early-stage design work to develop interactive tools in a computer science (CS) curriculum to support middle schoolers to engage in multiple aspects of data science, from being data producers to data analysts to data storytellers. In this effort, students are asked to provide data-informed recommendations to a master gardener on how to improve yield in the community gardens to enhance food production. This project is closely tied to community concerns around food security. The aim of the larger project is to support computational thinking for rural students and create a sense of belonging in the community. This project combines visuals, narrative structure, and data interpretation to help teachers integrate storytelling into science lessons as a prototype for future classroom implementation. Our work-in-progress investigates the research question: What ways candata storytelling develop middle school students’ data literacy skills while engaging with community-relevant challenges?

## Theory and prior work

An approach to designing curriculum that supports place-based learning and centers students' questions and experiences is “storylining,” which is built by student-driven investigations. In the contexts of this curriculum, storylines support students in building computing and data practices through, real-world problems, positioning them as active participants in the learning process (Bhaduri et al., 2018; Reiser et al., 2021). Data storytelling is the practice of interpreting and communicating data through narrative structures (Matuk et al., 2022). Unlike traditional data analysis, which often focuses on numbers and charts, data storytelling combines data literacy (Hardy et al., 2019) with narrative techniques to create engaging, persuasive, and informative presentations that connect with audiences emotionally and intellectually (Knaflic, 2015). Further, data storytelling is a pedagogical strategy for teaching data literacy. It uses narrative and visual tools to deepen understanding and make data personally and socially meaningful. Data storytelling candevelop students’ abilities to move from being consumers to data creators and critical-creative data users (NASEM, 2023; McDowell & Turk, 2024)

## Context and methods

This study is in the first year of a three-year CS-infused curriculum development project conducted in partnership with a rural U.S. school district, a community garden, and local nonprofits. In the curriculum, students will use data science processes and programmable sensor technologies (such as BBC micro:bits) to help the local master gardener increase garden yield. This paper specifically draws on data from a one-week professional learning (PL) workshop with three middle school teachers who helped co-design the curriculum. During the workshop, teachers identified the lesson-driving questions, part of the storyline curriculum, to scaffold students’ learning of data science processes (see Figure 1): collecting data through programmable sensors (data produce rs), analyzing growing conditions (data analysts), and communicating data-driven insights to the master gardeners (data storytelling). This approach not only strengthens technical skills and critical thinking but also prepares students for future academic or industry projects where interactive data storytelling is essential. ## Case study

We present a hypothetical student’s story and a dashboard (Figure 2) that incorporate data storytelling. Each visualization answers the question, “What zone in the community garden is best to grow carrots?” The story also includes sentence starters which serve as a scaffold to help students articulate data-driven conclusions and evidence-based reasoning, such as “I used programmable sensors to…, so I could …” and “Master Gardener could maximize yield if …”.

Figure 2 Hypothetical Student Data Storytelling Products In this story, students take on the role of data scientists inspired by a personal narrative about helping a community gardener grow carrots for a local bakery. Through lesson driving questions, as data producers, they use micro:bit to collect garden data,  developing both agency and literacy about how data is gathered and what it represents. As data analysts, they interpret these measurements to determine the most favorable growing conditions for carrots, comparing patterns across different zones. Finally, as data storytellers, they create visualizations and narratives that communicate their findings, recommending the best zones for planting and suggesting improvements.

By working with real-world platforms and drawing evidence-based conclusions, students strengthen both academic skills and career-relevant competencies. Incorporating data storytelling into the curriculum offers students authentic ways to explore data and communicate their findings while enhancing creativity and community belonging. Storytelling is a powerful tool for making science engaging, yet integrating it may be unfamiliar to teachers. These examples provide a concrete pathway by combining visuals, narrative structure, and data interpretation that teachers can adopt to bring storytelling into their lessons without needing advanced training in narrative techniques. This project serves as a prototype and pedagogical model to inform future classroom trials and curriculum refinement.
