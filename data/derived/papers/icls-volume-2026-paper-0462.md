# Exploring Strategies and Difficulties in Elementary Students’ Data Visualization Construction

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study used Epistemic Network Analysis to examine 6th-grade students' (n=39) strategies and difficulties in constructing data visualizations via CODAP. Findings show that students who successfully completed data visualization tasks utilized resourcing strategies, seeking help from teachers or peers, whereas non-completed students relied on deducing and trial-and-error strategy. These results emphasize the importance of verbal activities and social interaction in developing elementary students’ data visualization literacy.

In the era of big data, students must develop data visualization literacy (DVL) to both interpret and construct visualizations (Börner et al., 2019). While prior research has focused on interpretation among university or secondary students (Chang et al., 2024), elementary students are also expected to develop DVL across various subjects to enhance critical thinking and combat misinformation (Chevalier et al., 2018). However, understanding of how younger students construct visualizations remains limited (Alper et al., 2017). Consequently, this study investigates the difficulties and strategies of South Korean sixth graders during the data construction process, specifically addressing the following research questions: ● What strategies do sixth-grade students adopt, and what difficulties do they face when constructing data visualizations using datasets related to school children, roller coasters, and automobiles? ● How do the epistemic networks of strategies and difficulties differ between students who completed the data visualization tasks and those who did not?

## Theoretical framework

The Holistic Data Visualization Literacy Framework (DVL-FW) (Börner et al., 2015) served as the theoretical foundation for this study on how elementary students construct data visualizations. This framework was proposed to teach and assess DVL comprehensively, focusing on the cognitive and behavioral processes of data practices: acquiring, analyzing, visualizing, deploying, and interpreting data (Börner et al., 2015; Börner et al., 2019). By emphasizing these p rocesses, the DVL-FW provided a lens for deeply analyzing the strategies and difficulties elementary students face when constructing data visualizations.

## Method

Participants included 39 sixth-grade students from Seoul, South Korea. Data sources included a questionnaire on strategies and difficulties adapted from Chang et al. (2024), students’ verbal explanations, and visualization artifacts created with  CODAP. Following a 4-hour intervention over 2 weeks, students completed 2 final tasks: describing the visualization process (Task 1) and constructing a graph with 3 variables (Task 2). Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) was employed to model the relationship between students' strategies and difficulties. Furthermore, researchers compared students who successfully explained and constructed visualizations (n=25) against those who did not (n=14). Data preparation followed two cycles of coding (Saldaña & Omasta, 2016). Although CODAP  supports Korean, the variables in the datasets were presented only in English. This posed a challenge for sixth-grade students, requiring them to understand the meaning of variables such as Weight, Height, Maximum Speed, Type, and Mileage.
