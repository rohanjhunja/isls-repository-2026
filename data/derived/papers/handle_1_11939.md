# Traced Dialogic Assessment in Teacher Education

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster introduces traced dialogic assessment, a computer-supported summative assessment format. Combining oral assessment with digital tracing, the approach uses AI-driven video transcription to capture, visualize, and aid assessors and students in analyzing oral exam interactions. Grounded in design-based research, we explore how theoretical insights from CSCL can inform the assessment design. This work addresses challenges in assessing talk-based interaction and in balancing relational dynamics with objective assessment criteria.

## Introduction

This poster introduces the concept of traced dialogic assessment. Traced dialogic assessment is a  computersupported summative assessment format we have designed that is both dialogic — meaning it involves talk and meaning making between students and assessors — and traced, in the sense that these interactions ar e recorded, visualized and followed over time. Here, we present the conceptual grounding for traced dialogic assessment and outline a design rationale for conducting traced digital assessment in teacher education building on core principles of CSCL (Stahl & Hakkarainen, 2021). This work is part of an ongoing design-based research study aimed at creating an infrastructure for conducting more tran sparent and robust assessment of oral exams. Central to the design is a video transcription system, powered by a Large Language Model (LLM), which produces a shared representation for enhanced tracking and analysis of student performance. Assessment formats are often chosen based on their effectiveness in captur ing and conveying internal cognitive processes and understandings that are not  immediately observable (Pellegrino, 2022). Consequently, there is a general discrepancy between a collaborative approach to learning and the individualized nature of current typical assessment practices in higher education  (Boud & Bearman, 2022). This is important because decisions teachers make about assessment influence both students' opportunities to demonstrate their learning, and how they approach their own learning processes. Assessment has been an intensely debated topic for a long time, but these debates have recently been  fueled by the rapid development of generative AI. LLM-chatbots are disrupting traditional exam formats in higher education  by allowing students to pass conventional assessments with just a few keystrokes (McGrath et al., 2024; Giannakos et al., 2024). Against this backdrop, there is a need to innovate existing assessment strategies, with oral assessment formats potentially playing a key role (Rudolph et al., 2023). A key interest in CSCL is understanding the fine-grained processes of collaboration  and collaborative learning (Suthers, 2006). Traced dialogic assessment builds upon prior work that views situations, including assessment scenarios, as collaboratively constructed by participants (Jacoby & Ochs, 1995). This perspective supports the use of video recordings and detailed transcriptions of interactions to expose subtle aspects of knowledge use (Hall & Stevens, 2015). Building on this theoretical founda tion, we examine how student assessment emerges thr ough interaction before becoming a shared product. This is a  promising analytical focus; how meaning is negotiated through interaction and developed into a shared "knowledge object" that extends beyond the immediate situation (Damsa, 2014). By introducing new tools, such as visualizations of  the meaning construction between students and assessors (Davidsen & Steier, 2024; Shapiro et al., 2024), we explore ways to enhance collaboratio n and transparency. In dialogic assessment formats, student competenc e is demonstrated through interaction, with assessors acting as participants in collaboratively co-constructing the competence being evaluated (Hontve dt et al., 2024). Traced dialogic assessment builds on the hypothesis  that oral assessment formats can be made more transparent and foster shared understanding between  students and assessors by introducing tools that t rack and visualize the dialogue. Our research question asks: What guidance does CSCL give on the development of a video transcription and analysis system that supports transparent assessment of oral exams in teacher education? Design based research project The assessment design work in this study is grounded in a teacher education context in Norway. The Scandinavian educational system has a long tradition for oral exams, and recently, an increasing amount of research has studied oral assessment as interaction in secondary and upper-secondary schools. These studies have given insight in how students and teachers do assessment (Vonen et al., 202 2), and has  showed that the teachers’ a ssessments often lack explicit connection to the students’ talk (Solem et al., 2023). Our project is guided by design-based research, characterized by theory-driven innovation in which teaching practices and r esearch inform each other (Kali & Hoadley, 2021). By leveraging  insights from  interaction analysis combined with AI-based transcriptions and visualizations, we aim to expand assessment forms t hat enhance students’ reflective integration of the oretical knowledge in dialogues. We see this as  a path to greater transparency in assessment. However,  it also brings forward important ethical considerations  and w e are working to create a solution in accordance wi th the universities’ privacy and data security regulations. Even if the tool is created to ensure students a transparent and useful assessment situation, awareness of the impact  recording may have  on students' stress levels is essential. Transparency must be balanced against potential risks to privacy with sensitivity to the implementation of such a system (Isohätälä et al., 2021).

## Results: A design for traced dialogic assessment

In a traced dialogic exam, teacher education students will engage in dialogue with two assessors about key issues, theories and concepts in a pedagogy-course. We have developed a set of assessment design principles and CSCL guidance which serve as a basis for design decisions for a traced dialogic assessment tool based on prior research and document analysis of the exam format, course ob jectives, and assessment criteria for the selected pedagogy course. ## Discussion

The contribution of this poster is to present the conceptual grounding for applying  principles and ideas from interaction analytic studies on assessment and CSCL, and explore how these ideas can inform the design of a new LLM-supported assessment tool.  Following the  design principles shown in Figure 1, we argue that teacher education students will profit from a tool that offers a concrete output from oral exams, in the form of a transcript, highlighting key concepts and connections to literature, and the assessors’ assessment and grade compiled into an anonymized PDF. This document can serve as a record of the assessment and as a resource for the student's future learning. Importantly, the technology does not evaluate stude nt performance.  Technology is used to support assessors’ decision-making and increase transparency in the process.  Robust learning involves not only familiarization with concepts and understanding pro cedures but also the ability to expand on these con cepts and demonstrate critical, independent reflection. For example, simply mentioning concepts does not guarantee such reflection, but having access to the transcript and related resources may assist assessors and students in seeing the conversational dynamics in the exam  and identifying points in interaction that are essential for the as sessment. Traced dialogic assessment brings the role of inter action to the foreground as the presented prototype  can transform the typically ephemeral interactions in oral exams into a tangible record for transparent assessment.
