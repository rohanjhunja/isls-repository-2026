# Collaborative and Adaptive Learning: Designing AI Educational Systems with and for Educators

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The COALESCE framework extends DBIR principles to AI-powered educational technology development, positioning educators as active co-creators. This study evaluates COALESCE through its application in designing an AI-assisted lesson-planning tool. Our findings show how deep educator involvement supports trust, ownership, and domain-specific improvements in AI-generated content. We discuss implications for participatory AI design and offer a replicable model for context-aware, educator-aligned innovation.

## Introduction and motivation

Scaling educational innovations to meet the diverse  realities of K –12 classrooms remains a central challenge in the learning sciences (Coburn, 2003; Penuel et al., 2016; Moundridou et al., 2024). A key design challenge lies in embedding adaptability and transparency from the ou tset to ensure alignment with teachers ’ pedagogical goals. Design-Based Implementation Research (DBIR) offers a promi sing pathway. DBIR emphasizes iterative, collaborative design between researchers and practitioners to address persistent problems of practice and support scalable, sustainable innovation (Penuel et al., 2019; Fishman et al., 2013; Russell et al., 2017; Jung & Mercier, 2023). While DBIR has been widely applied in curriculum and program design, its application to Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based educational tools remains limited. Meanwhile, generative AI (GenAI) – particularly large language model (LLM) -based tools have gained momentum in K-12 settings by promising enhanced efficiency and personalized support (Mintz et al., 2023). Howe ver, many GenAI tools lack domain specificity, cont extual responsiveness, and transparency, making them diffi cult to trust or meaningfully integr ate into teaching practice (Holmes & Tuomi, 2022; Crompton et al., 2022; Chan & Hu, 2023). To address these limitations, we propose the Collaborative Adaptive Learning Ecosystem for Syste ms Creation and Enhancement (COALESCE) framework, which extends DBIR to AI technology development by embedding educators as co-creators throughout the research and development process of AI applications.

COALESCE framework COALESCE fosters reciprocal learning between educators, rese archers, and developers, enabling educator s’ expertise to directly inform model training, interf ace design, and ongoing refinement. Engaging educat ors in the design process fosters a sense of ownership, leadin g to more effective technology adoption that prioritizes user values and ethical standards (Shneiderman, 2022). Therefore, this iterative process enhances contextual relevance, transparency, and trust in AI tools while building educators’ AI literacy. Sustained collaboration benefits all stakeholders: educators gain deeper insight into AI  systems and adapt their instructional strategies; developers refine tools based on actual classroom use; and researchers generate new theoret ical understandings of AI integration in education. To illustrate COALESCE’s workflow, this paper uses the framework to enhanc e an AIpowered K-12 lesson planning tool-a task that continues to be cognitively demanding and  time-consuming for educators (Reiser & Dempsey, 2012). Here, we investigate two research questions: RQ1: How does deep educator involvement shape satisfaction and willingness to a dopt the tool? And RQ2: How does educator-guided development influence the quality of AI-generated lesson plans? By addressing these researc h questions, our COALESCE approach offers new insights into how educ ators can be empowered to shape AI technologies tha t align with their pedagogical needs.

Empirical studies: Evaluating the COALESCE framework in lesson planning To assess the effectiveness of COALESCE, we addressed the two research questions through a two-stage process: Stage 1 involved 20 K-12 math educators in co-designing an AI-powered lesson planning prototype. Educators collaboratively defined lesson plan quality measure s (e.g., warm-up relevance, instructional rigor, cool-down assessments), reviewed AI-generated ratings of human-designed plans-initially rated by GPT-4 (Achiam et al.,

2023) - and modified both content and scores. Their feedback im proved the definitions of lesson quality and

produced 800 labeled plans to fine-tune a LLaMA-2-13b model (Touvron et al., 2023). This stage highlighted mutual learning within COALESCE, as educators interacted with  AI responses while deepening their understanding of AI-driven lesson planning.

Stage 2  tested the impact of educator-guided refinement. Sixteen teachers conducted blind, pairwise comparisons of lesson plans created by humandesigners, customized GPT-4 prompts, and the fine-tuned LLaMA2-13b model. They evaluated 529 plan pairs across les son sections using educator-defined criteria (quality and relevance of overall and each lesson plan sections:  warm-up, main tasks, cool-down). This stage focused on examining whether the iterative adjustments made th rough COALESCE in Stage 1 enhanced the AI’s  alignment with pedagogical standards and instructional relevance. ## Findings from Two User Studies

RQ1: Educator Satisfaction and Buy-In RQ2: Quality of AI-Generated Plans

(i) Educators developed a deeper understanding of

AI capabilities and valued COALESCE’s integration of domain-specific standards. They found the tool more responsive than generic LLMs and identified use cases across roles (e.g., PLC leaders, special education).

(i) Human curriculum designer-designed plans were

preferred overall (42.1%), but customized GPT-4 (30.6%) and fine-tuned LLaMA-2-13b (27.3%) showed promising acceptance – especially when fine-tuned using educator data.

(ii) Educators observed substantial improvements in both the efficiency and perceived efficacy of their lesson planning assisted by the interactive tool through the reciprocal learning design and iterative refinement stages of COALESCE.

(ii) AI performed better in certain lesson plan sections. For example, Customized GPT-4 outperformed others in cool-down tasks (37.4%), showing the benefit of embedding instructional strategies during training.

(iii) Educators expressed a strong willingness to adopt and recommend the tool. Their sustained involvement built trust and a sense of ownership, encouraging advocacy and long-term use.

(iii) AI-generated plans received greater preference at the high-school level (fine-tuned LLaMA-2-13b: 37.9%) than in elementary, suggesting higher effectiveness for advanced content when domaintuned.

## Discussion and conclusion

COALESCE extends DBIR into AI development by embedding educators as co-creators. This reciprocal process builds AI literacy, trust, and pedagogically sound tools. The framework’s iterative design led to AI-generated content better aligned with real classro om needs. Educators’ sustained input improved both usability and model performance, showing that participatory, domain-specific refinement enhances AI adoption and impact.
