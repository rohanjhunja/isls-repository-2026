# Enhancing Teacher-AI Collaboration: The Impact of Prompt Engineering on Generative AI's Instructional Effectiveness

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Little is known about the instructional value of generative AI (GenAI) outputs in response to different prompts. This study designed prompts for ChatGPT based on the Knowledge Construction (KC) framework and evaluated outcomes in three subjects using the TPACK framework. Results show that higher KC phases produced more pedagogically meaningful outputs. “Integration of technology” and “student engagement” emerged as key usage aims, highlighting the importance of theory-informed prompt engineering in enhancing teacher-AI collaboration.

## Introduction

The integration of generative artificial intelligen ce (GenAI) tools, such as ChatGPT, into educational practices has created new opportunities for supporting instru ctional tasks. However, for GenAI-generated outcomes to be pedagogically meaningful, they must align with learning goals in terms of accuracy of content and appropriateness of suggested technology (Celik, 2023). Effective pr ompt engineering is essential for generating pedago gically meaningful outcomes from ChatGPT (Cain, 2024).

In this study, we apply the Technological Pedagogic al Content Knowledge (TPACK) framework to assess the outputs generated for different subjects by ChatGPT. TPACK offers a comprehensive model for assessing how well technology is integrated into teaching in a manner that is both pedagogically and contextually relevant (Mishra & Koehler, 2006). Evaluating ChatG PT’s outcomes using the TPACK framework might allow educators to assess not only the content accuracy but als o the tool’s ability to support instructional strat egies meaningfully and enhance learning experiences (Whal en & Mouza, 2023). This evaluation is crucial to en sure pedagogical and ethical support from GenAI technologies for teachers. Therefore, in this study, we seek to deepen our understanding of how theoretically crafted prom pts based on the KC framework improve ChatGPT’s effectiveness by evaluating its outputs through the  lens of TPACK. KC framework consists of five progr essive stages (e.g., information sharing and meaning negot iation), with deeper knowledge construction occurri ng at the later, more advanced stages.

Instructional use of ChatGPT and TPACK Recent reviews indicate that ChatGPT could be beneficial for getting various instructional sup port (Celik et al., 2022; Clark et al., 2024). Such support includes: (1) organizing teaching activities, (2) integrating technology into subject matter, (3) assessment and grading, (4) enh ancing student engagement, and (5) addressing diver se students’ needs.

The TPACK framework offers a comprehensive lens for  evaluating ChatGPT's instructional outcomes by examining the interplay between technology, peda gogy, and content. Stemming from Shulman (1986)’s concept of Pedagogical Content Knowledge (PCK), whi ch integrates content and pedagogical knowledge for effective teaching, TPACK expands this framework by incorporating Technological Knowledge (TK) (Mishra & Koehler, 2006). This addition creates three combine d dimensions: Technological Content Knowledge (TCK), Technological Pedagogical Knowledge (TPK), and the core TPACK, which aligns all components to enhan ce learning outcomes.  In this study, four TPACK dimensions —TCK, PCK, TPK, and TPACK —are applied to evaluate ChatGPT’s instructional value. TCK assesses how technology supports content deli very and visualization, ensuring clarity and interactivity. TPK evaluates the integration of technology with pe dagogical strategies, focusing on differentiated instruction and student engagement. PCK examines the alignment of teaching methods with content, ensuring pedagogical  soundness and alignment with learning goals. Final ly, TPACK assesses the coherence of all three component s, ensuring technology enhances pedagogy and conten t delivery. For tasks like organizing teaching activi ties, dual knowledge (TCK, PCK, TPK) and triple kno wledge (TPACK) are critical, making this framework essenti al for guiding and evaluating AI-generated educational outcomes. This study addresses the following research questions: RQ1) How does theory-informed prompt engineering affect ChatGPT`s TPACK across selected subjects?

RQ2) How does ChatGPT`s TPACK change based on the different instructional usage aims?

## Methodology

We designed a template of ChatGPT  prompts aligned with the phases of the KC framework, leveraging prompt engineering strategies. These prompts were adapted for each session by modifying the subject, topic, and specific usage objective for ChatGPT. In each KC session, fi ve unique prompts were applied, resulting in ChatGP T generating outcomes for each prompt. Thus, from 45 sessions, a total of 225 outcomes were produced. To address the RQs, a quantitative content analysis was conduc ted on ChatGPT's generated outcomes. For this analy sis, we adapted rubric based on the TPACK framework, drawing from previous studies (e.g., Celik, 2023; Harris & Hofer, 2011). The rubric includes four main domains: TPK, TCK, PCK, and TPACK. The core TPACK domain has five indicators, while each of the other knowledge domai ns includes four indicators. To reveal the effect o f subject type on TPACK dimensions a one-way ANOVA test was performed (RQ1). We further analyzed the variation of TPACK based on different KC phases within each subj ect. For this, three more one-way ANOVA tests were conducted. The same method was used to investigate the comparison of TPACK in  different instructional aims (RQ2).

## Discussion and conclusions

This study highlights the critical role of theory-informed prompt engineering in enhancing the pedago gical and technological alignment of AI-generated outcomes, particularly through the TPACK framework (Mishra & Koehler, 2006). The findings verify prior research emphasizing the importance of tailoring prompts to align with instructional contexts (Cress & Kimmerle, 2023). By  systematically assessing ChatGPT’s performance acr oss instructional aims, this research underscores the v ariability of AI’s effectivene ss across different subjects and teaching objectives. This suggests that AI's integr ation must be planned as context-sensitive (Chen et al., 2024; Walter, 2024).
