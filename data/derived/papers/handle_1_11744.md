# Student Teacher Learning as a Means to Increase the Capacity of a Design-Centric Research-Practice Partnership

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Research-practice partnerships (RPPs) can provide effective means to design and implement school materials but can face capacity issues. This study explores efforts to integrate 10 student teachers into an RPP focused on computer science learning to increase capacity of the RPP. These efforts led to (1) increased implementation of CS-integrated lessons, (2) establishment of a pipeline for pre-service teachers to become new hires, and (3) spurred development of professional development tools used across the RPP.

## Introduction

The K-12 Computer Science Framework  in the US highlights the importance of providing all students  with opportunities to engage in computer science (CS) and computational thinking (CT) from an early age to support their participation in a digitized society and futu re workforce (K-12 CS Framework Steering Committee, 2016). Despite the adoption of state-level CS standards, many districts, particularly th ose serving multilingual learners (MLs), face challenges in implementing these reform s. Research-Practice Partnerships (RPPs) serve as a promising vehicle to address these challenges, offering pathways for research-based instructional reform through design-centric activity (Coburn & Penuel, 2016; Kali et al., 2023). However, RPPs often contend with capacity issues due to competing demands and limited resources (Henrick et al., 2017). This study examines how including student teachers in an RPP enhanced the partnership ’s ability to design and implement CT-integrated science lessons, expand educational opportunities for MLs, and address inequities in CS education. Research-practice partnerships: Aims and challenges RPPs are collaborative efforts between researchers and practitioners aimed at solving practical educat ional problems and improving school systems (Coburn et al., 2021). They are uniquely positioned to address equity issues by bringing diverse expertise to bear on problems of practice  (Farrell et al., 2023). Their effectiveness, however, is often constrained by contextual challenges, such as high staff turnover, competing district initiatives, and limited capacity in districts for curriculum design and implementation (Coburn et al., 2013). These limitations are particularly pronounced when partners are requi red to take on new design roles, which demand significant time and effor t in addition to their existing responsibilities.  RPP membership typically includes educators, researchers, and other stakeholders such as communi ty organizations, each bringing unique perspectives  to the partnership (Booker, 2023). This diversity allows f or innovative problem-solving but can lead to reso urce competition and misaligned goals (Duff et al., 2023). Effective RPPs intentionally leverage the strengths of their members to maximize their capacity for meeting shared objectives (Coburn & Penuel, 2016). Project overview The RPP examined in this study was based in the Wes tern United States and aimed to integrate CS and CT  into elementary science curricula. The partnership’s goa ls included expanding student access to CS educatio n, with a particular focus on supporting Spani sh-speaking MLs. Student teachers collaborated with re searchers, mentor teachers, and in-service teachers to design and deliver lessons that  embedded CT concepts such as algorithms, pattern recognition, and conditional logic. Lessons  also incorporated language supports, such as sentence frames and visual aids, to enhance accessibility for MLs (Jacob et al., 2020). Student teachers were positioned as active participants in the RPP, contributing to the design  and implementation of CS-integrated lessons, but also as recipients of professional development materials. Their willingness to take instructional risks and experiment with new teaching strategies distinguished them as valuable contributors to the RPP’s reform efforts.

## Methodology

This study employed Cobb and Jackson’s (2011) theory of action for instructional improvement, focusing on the development of a coherent instructional system. Par ticipants included 10 student teachers placed in district schools, who were trained to integrate CT concepts into their teaching. Data sources included pre-surveys, recorded lessons, and semi-structured interviews, which were analyzed using an iterative coding process informed by grounded theory (Charmaz, 2002). Findings were t riangulated with artifacts such as lesson plans and professional development (PD) materials.

## Results

Increased implementation of CS-integrated lessons in classrooms Student teachers significantly expanded the RPP’s c apacity to provide CS and CT learning opportunities  across the district. While in-service teachers were more limited by competing initiatives and responsibilities, student teachers implemented ten CT-integrated lessons, surpassing the project contributions of their more experienced counterparts during the same period. For example, one middle school student teacher designed a lesson on electric fields using a PhET simulation, embedding CT concep ts such as conditional logic and algorithms with sc affolds like “IF/THEN” sentence frames. Another student tea cher had elementary students explore properties of matter and supported them in creating flowcharts and using them as computational artifacts to identify unknown liquids. Notably, these and other student teacher-led lessons also helped engage cooperating teachers in CT design work, expanding the district-level reach of the RPP more quickly and effectively. Created a pipeline for pre-service teachers to become new in-service hires Two student teachers transitioned to in-service roles within the district, rejoining the RP P as active contributors in more intensive curriculum design. Their familiar ity with the RPP’s goals and methods allowed them t o contribute immediately and effectively, increasing the RPP’s design capacity. For instance, one of these teachers co-designed a unit on solids that expanded upon their prior work as a student teacher, demonstrating cont inuity and deepening expertise. This pipeline was supporte d by the distri ct, which emphasized RPP participation as a valued qualification for hiring decisions. Fosterin g a pre-to in-service trajectory not only enhanced the RPP’s design capacity but also ensured sustained progress toward its instructional goals (Coburn & Penuel, 2016). Spurred development of professional development tools used across the RPP The inclusion of student teachers also informed the development of professional learning tools used by in-service teachers. For example, the “CompSci Concept Map” initially created for student teacher PD, was refined through student teachers’ use, feedback, and later adopted in in-service teacher workshops. This activity encouraged participants to map out their understanding of CS concepts and connect them to CT. Other tools—including a CT handout and kickoff workshop—were iteratively tested with student teachers before being scaled across the RPP for broader use. Working with student teachers also helped surface key instructional challenges—such as how to avoid overloading lessons with too many CT concepts—which led to a new emphasis on designing for coherence across multi-lesson sequences. By  piloting tools with student teachers, the RPP was a ble to refine its resources for broader implementation, leading to a more ideal  alignment of PD activities with teacher learning n eeds and project objectives (Cobb & Jackson, 2011).

## Discussion and implications

The findings demonstrate that including student teachers in RPPs addresses critical capacity challenges, supports instructional coherence, and promotes equity in CS education. By providing lessons to underserved stud ent populations and engaging in curriculum design, stud ent teachers not only advanced the RPP’s goals but also contributed to their own professional growth. Their  involvement highlights the potential of RPPs to le verage emerging educators a s agents of reform while aligning their training wi th district priorities.  The study also underscores the importance of strategic membership composition in RPPs (Sjölund et al., 2023). Including diverse stakeholders, such as student teachers, enriches th e partnership’s capacity for innovation and sustainab ility. Moreover, the development of professional learning tools, informed by student teacher participation, exemplifies how iterative design processes can enhance the scal ability of educational reforms.  Finally, this work has implications for policy and practice, particularly in the preparation and professional development of teachers. Engaging student teachers in RPPs provides them wit h opportunities to participate in meaningful reform  efforts, bridging the gap between theoretical training and practical ap plication. For districts, this approach offers a pathway to align teacher preparation with local ins tructional goals, fostering long-term systemic change (VanGronigen et al., 2022).
