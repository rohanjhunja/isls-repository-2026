# Comparing AI Feedback and Teacher Feedback on Argumentative Writing

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Generative AI tools like ChatGPT have shown great potential in enhancing the effectiveness of L2 writing instruction. We compared the feedback generated by ChatGPT and teachers regarding feedback quantity and features. Key findings include: ChatGPT produced more negative feedback than teachers and more mitigating praises, general suggestions and specific solutions concerning both content and form. Teachers feedback delivered more explanations on the essay content. The findings suggested the positive impact of AI in generating feedback on student writing and promoting students’ writing ability.

Feedback is an essential component of effective writing instruction. Providing students with immediate, high-quality feedback allows them to assess their writing abilities and recognize pathways for improvement. While educators tend to offer limited or brief evaluative statements, extensive feedback generated by AI exhibits greater length in terms of word count. Moreover, Wang (2024) found that students received AI feedback can perform better than those received teacher feedback. Compared with teacher feedback, which mainly focused on issues related to content and language, ChatGPT distributed its attention equally among the three foci (content, organization, and language) (Guo & Wang, 2024). This study examine the efficacy of AI feedback compared to teacher feedback by analyzing the quantity and quality of feedback features. Forty-five English argumentative essays written by Chinese undergraduate students (female: 76%, male: 24%, mean age = 20 years) were used as essay corpus in this study. We used ChatGPT (v.3.5) to generate formative feedback for the essays and developed the evaluation criteria proposed by Paulus (1999) to design prompts regarding six dimensions: organization, development, cohesion, structure, vocabulary, and mechanics. ChatGPT acted as an English writing instructor to identify and explain the problems, and provide constructive suggestions to the essays. Teacher feedback was given by two English writing instructors who had taught English writing for more than ten years. They gave feedback based on the same criteria used by ChatGPT. Four research assistants were trained to code feedback. Any discrepancies were resolved through of multiple issues. Comments were then classified as summary, purely positive, or negative comments. Then, the negative comments were analyzed based on a coding scheme proposed by Wu and Schunn (2020). This coding scheme includes four key features of feedback: mitigating praise (i.e., praises included in negative feedback to soften criticism), explanations (i.e., explain what a problem), general suggestions (i.e., general advice about how to improve), and specific solutions (i.e., specific advice about how to improve). To explore the differences between AI feedback and teacher feedback, we calculated the amount of negative feedback and analyzed the four features included in AI feedback and teacher feedback. SPSS version 29.0 was used to conduct paired sample t-tests to determine if AI feedback was different from teacher feedback. AI feedback displayed a much higher quantity [t (44) = 24.09, p <.001, Cohen’s d = 2.26] (AI feedback: Mean = 17.89, SD = 0.75; teacher feedback: Mean = 9.78, SD = 2.12). Because prior research has observed different AI feedback and teacher feedback within the two aspects: essay content and form. For content-related feedback, the quantity of teacher feedback is slightly higher than AI feedback (see Table 1). AI feedback featured a greater quantity of mitigating praise, general suggestions and specific solutions. However, teacher feedback excelled in providing explanations. AI generated substantially higher quantity of negative feedback on form (see Table 2). AI feedback outperformed teacher feedback in the four feedback features including mitigating praises, explanation, general suggestions, and specific solutions on improving form-related aspects. AI allows for immediate responses, enabling students to reflect on and revise their writing without delays, subsequently improving learners’ motivation and promoting long-term development of writing skills. In terms of content-focused feedback, AI feedback included more mitigating praises than teachers, which indicated AI may foster a supportive environment and motivate learners to engage actively with the feedback provided. Teachers provided significantly more detailed explanations than ChatGPT, which may be attributed to ChatGPT’s limited understanding of essays and merely guessing of the meanings. Regarding form-focused feedback, AI feedback provides more negative feedback, mitigating praise, explanation, general suggestion, and specific solution, demonstrating its capacity to deliver diverse types of feedback on language form. This result highlights the potential of AI to contribute significantly to form-focused aspects of writing and the positive impact of AI on improving language accuracy in writing (Steiss et al., 2024). Our findings provide insights into how AI feedback and teacher feedback can be integrated or balanced to enhance writing instruction outcomes. Guo, K., & Wang, D. (2024).
