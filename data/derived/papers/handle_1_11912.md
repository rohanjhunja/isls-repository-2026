# Identifying Critical Points in Interprofessional Collaboration: Towards Developing a VR-Simulation

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
In this study, we identified critical collaboration points in interprofessional wound care through qualitative interviews with experts. These points highlight key moments when collaborative activities such as coordination and negotiation are essential. Based on these identified points, a virtual reality (VR) simulation will be developed with prompts to promote interprofessional collaboration. The simulation will be tested for its effectiveness in promoting collaboration, improving procedural knowledge, and improving communication and decision making across disciplines.

## Motivation: Developing a VR simulation to foster interprofessional skills

Interprofessional collaboration is widespread and e ssential to tackle complex problems, but it remains a  major challenge in professional practice such as medicine. One example is wound care, where effective care for wounds requires seamless integration of knowledge from dif ferent profession als such as nurses, physicians, and pharmacists. Common issues in these teams include, among others, inadequate documentation (e.g., findings from wound assessment) and communication gaps  (Heerschap et al., 2018). Therefore, students such as nursing, medical, and pharmacy  students would benefit from structured training opp ortunities where  professional knowledge such as conceptual knowledge and procedural knowledge (e.g., wound assessment and wound care implementation) (Förtsch et al., 2018)  can be applied to realistic scenarios. Such training opportunities ideally also allow for the practice of effective interprofessional collaboration (e.g., coordination, negotiation, regulation, joint information processing; (Meier et al., 2007; Witti et al., 2023). However, existing curricula contribute to disciplinary boundaries by teaching such interprofessional skills, if at all,  within individual profession s, even though interprofessional education (IPE) would be particularly important here as effective collaboration between healthcare professions can lead to better patient care and patient satisfaction (Cox et al., 2016). Our overall goal is to address this current curricular lacquer in the light of IPE by developing a virtual reality (VR)-based simulation for the training of interprofessional skills in wound care (see Figure 1). In recent years, VR-based simulations have been increasingly used in he althcare to measure and foster interprofessional skills (Popov et al., 2023) as they offer hands-on, low-stakes, immersive learning environments that not only address technical skills related to conceptual and procedural knowledge, but also non-technical skills such as collaboration, communication, and a deeper understanding of interprofessional rol es (Qiao et al., 2021). To design an effective VR environment, it is important to identify the critical moments in wound  care where collaboration has a strong impact on patient outcomes. We refer to these moments in the problem-solving process as critical points in interprofessional collaboration. However, understanding these critical points remains limited. Thus, we investigated the  following research question s. RQ1: What are the critical points in interprofessional collaboration in wound care? RQ2: Which collaborative activities (i.e., coordination, negotiation, regulation, joint information processing) are most relevant at these points?

## Findings and discussion

The main category “Critical Collaboration Points ” was assigned when the interviewe e indicated that relevant wound care steps need to be discussed or negotiated collaboratively. We yielded 8 subcategories. We focus on two subcategories that illustrate two distinct critical points in the problem-solving process (RQ1; see Table 1). ## Experts recommend a preliminary interprofessional discussion

at the start of wound care to avoid mistakes. A structured exchange of previous knowledge and experience of the wound and the patient's history creates a common understanding and provides the basis for an informed management strategy. The course of treatment is jointly negotiated so that all relevant information is shared and taken into account to ensure coordinated and effective care. The focus is on sharing wound documentation and discussing dressing change intervals and the selection of appropriate dressings, so that any necessary adjustments to treatment can be implemented in a continuous and coordinated manner.

## Experts recommend an interprofessional discussion

on the choice of wound dressing in order to make an informed decision. All relevant characteristics of the wound, such as wound type, size, degree of exudation and the stage of wound healing, should be taken into account. It is also recommended to weigh up the effectiveness, workload and cost-effectiveness when making the selection in order to ensure optimal and economical wound care. The decision-making process should be carried out by an interprofessional team.

Anchor example: “Here, the professional groups should exchange information regarding potential improvemen ts related to dressing changes, intervals, or the selection of wound dressings.” (PH1, Pos. 3) Anchor example: “Reference to economic aspects in the selection of wound dressings, shared decisionmaking with regard to wound type/size, exudation, stage of wound healing, economic aspects” (PH2, Pos. 1) These two critical points can be linked to  different collaborative activities (RQ2; Meier et al., 2007; Witti et al., 2023), namely coordination (i.e., a structured exchange of previous knowledge and exper ience of the wound), negotiation (i.e., experts recommend an interprofessional discussion about the choice of wound dressing in order to make an informed decision), regulation (i.e., so that any necessary adjustments to treatment can be implemented in a continuous and coordinated manner), and joint information processing (i.e., so that all relevant information is shared and taken into account).  We plan to  design the VR simulatio n on the basis of these critical points by integrating prompts at these points to encourage interprofessional collaboration. For example, a note saying: “New information: Patient is allergic to certain dressings (e.g., plaster allergy). Discuss with your team how to proceed” could be displayed. This would prompt students to engage in an interprofessional  team discussion to determine the best treatment option, emphasi zing the need for effective communication and collaboration. Identifying and fostering these critical collaboration points and related collaborative activities in a VR-based training not only helps to foster interprofessional collaboration in wound care educa tion but also has the potential to advance IPE in other knowledge-rich domains.

Further steps First, the simulation will be tested for its usabil ity, authenticity, and ability to improve interprof essional communication, decision making, and teamwork across  professions, with expert feedback used to make des ign improvements. Second, a validation study with medical, pharmacy, and nursing students will assess the effects of the VR training on students’ wound care knowledge (procedural knowledge) and professional self-efficacy. Third, an experimental study will investigate the effect o f collaboration prompts in the VR environment with different levels of support on the quality of students’ inter professional collaboration, considering whether stu dents recognize critical collaboration points and engage in relevant collaborative activities.
