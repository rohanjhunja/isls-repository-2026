# Research-Practice Partnership Fostering Educational Change – Case: Digital Citizenship Competencies Roadmap

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This practice-oriented paper seeks to examine the role of Research-Practice Partnerships (RPPs) in promoting educational improvement in the digital era. The RPP co-development project described here aimed to bridge the gap between research and practice by providing school practitioners with research-informed knowledge while simultaneously offering researchers insights into practical development processes. The study focuses on a case school participating in the RPP co-development project to enhance its development initiatives through additional support in the form of knowledge and resources. The university partner played a pivotal role by supplying research-based knowledge, resources for data collection and analysis, and ongoing mentoring throughout the iterative co-development cycle. This two-year collaboration resulted in significant structural and pedagogical advancements within the school, as well as substantial professional growth for participating teachers. Key areas of development included enhancing home-school collaboration, effectively utilizing digital tools for internal communication, and fostering students’ well-being and sense of belonging.

## Introduction

The objective of this study is to conduct an analys is of the role of Research-Practice Partnerships (RPP) as delineated by Coburn and Penuel (2016) and Penuel a nd Gallagher (2017) in design-based initiatives aimed at enhancing educational practices within the context of digitalization. This paper  focuses on analyzing how the schools’ development needs can be fostered by an RPP initiative that provides both research-based and actionable knowledge for practitioners and simultaneously fosters researchers’ understanding of the participating schools’ realities (Nelson et al., 2015). This research was conducted as part of a systemic school research and development project, Growing Mind, which took place from 2018 to 2023. T his project sought to integrate academic research with collaborative partnerships involving education al practitioners to facilitate transformative educa tional practices in the capital region of Finland.

In this study, digitalization is conceptualized as a systemic transformation of school culture, necessitating investigation within the specific educational conte xts where emergent epistemic, social, and material processes are operationalized (Korhonen et al., 2021; Silander et al., 2022). The limited effectiveness of technology-driven or researcher-led interventions prompted us to adopt a participatory approach, specifically through the framework of RPP, to foster iterative improvements in school practi ces. The school improvement initiatives outlined in the systemic educational development project entailed a comprehensive assessment of the developm ental needs of schools, followed by  the promotion of iterative enhancements in pedagogi cal and organizational practices in collaboration with school practitioners, including school leaders and teachers. This co-development process was realized through the integration of design-based research (DBR) methodologies and cyclical dev elopment frameworks (Bryk et al., 2011). This cyclical model of development facilitates in cremental advancements by school practitioners, which are subsequently analyz ed and refined in partnership with researchers (Bry k et al., 2011).

Identifying developmental needs: A participatory approach The Growing Mind project encompassed six work packages (Figure 1) designed to facilitate personal, social, and institutional renewal in the digital era. The consortium of project partners comprised thre e universities from Finland, alongside the Education Division and numerous schools within City of Helsinki. Additionally, the RPP project established a collaborative framework desig ned to enhance partnership among the stakeholders and to secure support for the development initiatives from the Education Division of City of Helsinki. This collaborative framework is depicted in Figure 2 (Korhonen et al., 2024). This paper adopts a practice-oriented approach, concentrating on the co-development efforts undertaken within work package 5 (Figure 1). We present a case study that illustrates the implementation of RPP collaborative activities at one of the participating schools. In collaboration with our research team, the Education Division of City of Helsinki, and the schools involved, work package  5 aimed to promote systemic enhancements in school culture. This was achieved by identifying the initi al developmental needs of each participating school  and documenting their participatory processes of school  impro vement across iterative developmental cycles. The specific case examined here is a small primary school  aiming to develop its digital practices. Concurrently, City of Helsinki  initiated a special digitalization program intended to assist schools i n their efforts to advance the digitalization of learning and teaching.

In addition to the city level  digitalization program, t he school leader (Author 1) acknowledged the necessity for a comprehensive plan to systematicall y advance the digitalization of existing educationa l practices. However, concerns regarding student wellbeing, raise d by the guardians, also emerged as a significant developmental theme.  Hence, t his plan encompass ed not only teaching and learning methodologies but al so collaborative practices among teachers and between the school and homes. The successful exe cution of these initiatives necessitated a foundation of research-based knowledge, developed collaboratively in a man ner that recognized the professional agency of teachers and fostered their sense of belonging within the educat ional community.

In alignment with the participatory approach and to ensure the active involvement of teachers in the development process, an initial mapping phase, that is assessment of the developmental needs of the school, was undertaken in collaboration with our research team to incorporate the perspectives of all educators wi thin the school (Tiippana et al., 2024). This mapping phase employed a range of data coll ection methods, including surveys, interviews, and workshops. Subsequently, a preliminary analysis  of the ga thered data was performed by the researchers (Authors 3 & 4), which was followed by a workshop aimed at prioritizing and further elaborating on the identified developmental needs. Participation in these events was inclusive, with all teachers from the scho ol actively involved. Drawing upon a comprehensive analysis of data gathe red from educators, concerns articulated by students' guardians, and the strategic objectives e stablished at the municipal level, three primary th emes for development were delineated: A) enhancement of teachers’ communication practices through digital applications, B) advancement of digital educational practices of teachers and students, and C) refinement of digital assessment methods. The themes were interrelated and mutually reinforcing. The co-development initiative sought to engage the entire teaching community through a series of workshops conducted over a two-year period. However, the primary facilitators of t he pedagogical development efforts were the school leader and a development t eam composed of three teachers. This team operated in close partnership with our research team. In addition to the described RPP activities, the school leader participated in a 60 ECTS specialization program “Learning and teaching in digital environments” organized by the University of Helsinki and supervised by Author 2. As a part of this program, the school leader received additional support for the development work, including access to contemporary research and expanded professional networks. Implementation of the RPP project During the two-year RPP project, the school initially concentrated on establishing digital collaboration practices, facilitated by our research team. These novel practices served as a foundational basis for subsequent pedagogical development efforts. The evolution of digital pedagogy and assessment practices encompassed the integration of the school’s existing curriculum focused on student well-being and the promotion of digital competencies among learners. These pedagogical development objectives were collectively framed under the designation of the Digital Citizenship Competencies Roadmap. This initiative involved a comprehensive exploration of the applications and tools that had been utilized by educators within th e school. This exploration was complemented by an investigation of future labor markets and the competencies required therein, as well as a survey for guardians to gauge their views and perceptions concerning digita l competencies within the home environment. The dat a collected from these efforts informed the implementation phase of the project. Throughout this implementation phase, our research team provided multifaceted support to the school, fostering a close collaborative relationship. Autho r 3 played a significant role by participating in m ost of  the development workshops with the school’s development team and serving as a mentor to the school leader (Author

1) during the project. The researcher  team contributed to the creation of surveys, the analysis of collect ed data,

and the provision of the latest research-based insights. Meanwhile, the school practitioners  designed the development activities from a pedagogical standpoint, with the school leader ensuring that the development team was allocated sufficient time for this work.

The implementation of the Digital Citizenship Compe tencies Roadmap necessitated the involvement of multiple stakeholder groups. Firstly, it was impera tive to ensure the active participation and engagem ent of the entire teaching community. This aspect was inherently integrated in to the RPP initiative and was particularly emphasized within this school through a series of workshops and knowledge-sharing sessions. Furthermore, the newly established digital collaboration applications and practices facilitated the seamless involvement of educators in developmental activities, even during the periods of distance teaching necessitated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Secondly, the school sought to enhance the engagement of guardians in home-school collaboration. It is a prevalent observation in Finland that the e ffectiveness of digital communication tools in fost ering homeschool collaboration is often not so successful among guardians from immigrant backgrounds. This challenge was acknowledged in the school. Consequently, to better  assist students in integrating into the digital so ciety, school practitioners devised innovative strategies aimed at reaching out to guar dians from immigrant backgrounds and dedicated time to educate them in the use of digital applications that are commonly employed in home-school collaboration in Finland. Thirdly, this co-development initiative regarded students as active participants, thus emphasizing the importance of fostering their involvement in school development efforts. Given that the students in this school were relatively young (ages 7-11), the digital teaching and learning practices developed considered the potential limitations associated with their age. Nonetheless, it is noteworthy that many students in Finland receive their first mobile phone when starting school at the age of seven, and from the first grade onward, digital tools and applications are routinely u sed in education. Additionally, recognizing the signifi cant variability in digital competencies among students, a special role  of "digital student agent" was established. Studen ts who expressed a willingness to assume this role were provided with training and a specific mandate to assist their peers as “digi-agents.” During the ongoing co-development initiatives with three significant stak eholder groups, additional forms of development were also pursued. The primary objec tive of the school was to integrate the digitalization of pedagogical practices with the school ’s wellbeing curriculum, thereby creating a cohesive  structure aimed at enhancing students’ digital citizenship competencies comprehensively. The wellbeing curriculum encompassed four thematic areas: physical activity, nurture, rest, and culture. These themes were rotated annually, allowing for an in-depth exploration of one theme per academic year. The educational practitioners aimed to cultivate students’ digital competencies in conjunction with these wellbeing themes. This initiative was underpinned b y the aspiration to evolve teaching and learning practices toward more authentic learning experiences and to expand students’ learning environments beyond the borders of the traditional classroom. Moreover, the incorporat ion of gamification as an innovative pedagogical st rategy has been shown to enhance learning effectiveness by eng aging students in novel ways (Caponetto et al., 201 4). In light of these considerations and as one of the concrete outcomes, a new educational game was developed in the school, merging practical and research-based insights. This collaborative game development effort was conducted in partnership with a business-oriented university of applied sciences in Finland,  where students specializing in coding designed this educational game as part of their diploma pro jects. The idea behind the game was to make students explore various strategies to enhance their well-being, while the bear character actively "follows" their progress and invigorates (Figure 3). The game is facilitated through an application that provides map-like playing in inside and outside environment. The designed play area for this well-being game encompasses the school, the schoolyard, and the surrounding areas.

Figure 3 The Bear-Character’s Invigoration in the Wellbeing Game RPP project outcomes The RPP project outlined in this paper involved a multi-tiered collaborative framework, resulting in divers e outcomes that can be divided into school (practitioner) and university (researcher) levels. At the school level, the initiative not only fostered the establishment of c ollaborative educational structures and practices b ut also enhanced the digital competencies of educators and students. Additionally, school practitioners—including school leaders, teachers, and administrative staff—recognized the necessity of providing targeted guidance to guardians regarding the utilization of digital applications used by the school to facilitate effective home-school collaboration. Presently, digital collaboration with guardians has become significantly more prevalent than it was five years ago, and both teachers and administrative personnel have  acquired the skills necessary to further promote t his digital engagement. Moreover, the expansion of the “digi-agent” network among students has significantly enhanced the implementation of peer support and mentoring initia tives. Collectively, the outcomes derived from practitioner perspective underscore the complex dimensions of di gitalization in the context of school culture devel opment. Furthermore, these outcomes illustrate how research-based knowledge can effectively inform and facilita te the initiation and modification of development processe s within educational settings.  Teachers’ e mphasis on  the importance of research-based information, particularly about their own school, highlights how essential it is that researchers take roles that support the RPP partners in practice (Nelson et al., 2015). For the university partners involved in this initia tive, the outcomes derived from this case study encompass an enhanced comprehension of procedural aspects related to RPP and a deeper understanding of school practitioners’ perceived opportunities to engage in and contribute  to school culture  development. The outcomes associated with RPP intrinsically integrate data fr om the co-development efforts undertaken by all participating schools within Growing Mind project. Our research team has conducted analyses of the data gathered from this case school and emphasize that, although RPP as a co-development model aspires to involve the entire sch ool community in developmental activities, it is imperative to focus more intently on the methods of this engagement. The findings from this case indicate similar results to those of our other studies; teachers occupying specialized developer roles within their schools demonstrate hi gher levels of engagement in RPP initiatives and pe rceive greater opportunities to influence the development processes and objec tives than teachers without a designated developer role (Reinius et al., 2024; Tiippana et al., 2024). Furthermore, these findings emphasize the importance of the RPP and participatory practices  in systemic school development projects, reinforcin g insights from prior studies (e.g., Korhonen et al., 2024). In light of these results, we emphasize that researchers should play a crucial role in facilitating collaboration and knowledge sharing among educators from the outset of partnership planning. To conclude,  a suitable methodological framework is necessary to  support RPPs as dynamic processes, with potential solutions integrating RPPs with cyclical educational design research  and enhancing the agency of all involved actors.
