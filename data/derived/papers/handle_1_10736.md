# The Effects of Prompt Scaffolding on Learning to Write Arguments with ChatGPT

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Although ChatGPT can be useful for writing, it is not certain whether ChatGPT is beneficial for learning to write particularly when ChatGPT carries out the task instead of learners. To enhance the positive effect of ChatGPT in learning to write, teachers should provide students appropriate scaffoldings on creating prompts, which are crucial in interacting with ChatGPT. This study aims to investigate the effects of prompt scaffoldings on learning to write arguments with ChatGPT. In this study, 179 Korean high school students participated, and they were assigned into experiment and control groups. This study found that the experiment group that received prompt scaffoldings outperformed the control group in regards to self-efficacy, interest, knowledge, and performance in argumentative writing. This study implies that ChatGPT can be beneficial for learning only when learners effectively interact with it.

## Introduction

The ability to write arguments is being highlighted in the field of education as a core competency for solving complex problems and mak ing important decisions. Argumentative writing is a multifaceted process through which learners integrate existing knowledge with ne w information to construct persuasive arguments. Argumentative writing is a challenging task for the majority of students (Goldman et al., 2019). ChatGPT, with its superior natural language process ing capabilities, is increasingly being used to ass ist students in their argumentative writing (Su et al.,  2023). ChatGPT can be utilized by students to faci litate the process of generating ideas, obtain personalized feedback, and enhance their ability to express thoughts and ideas. Yet, critical viewpoints also point out that  over-reliance on ChatGPT could impede the development of  higherorder thinking skills, such as creativity and critical thinking, or hinder self-directed problem solving (Atlas, 2023; Ibrahim et al., 2023). Therefore, when integrating ChatGPT in education, it is necessary to recognize potential concerns that may arise for learners, including negative impacts and ethical considerat ions, while providing educational support to enable effective  outcomes. Recent research explores the ne ed for teachers to assist the effective creation of prompts when using ChatGPT; in other words, a focus on pedagogy-based approaches is key to utilizing ChatGPT in a way that aids learning  (Farrokhnia et al., 2023).  The role of teachers in guiding and moderating students' use of ChatGPT  prompts thus appears  critical to ensure its positive impact on learners' argumentative writing abilities.  Scaffolding support can serve as the bridge that he lps learners achieve higher understanding and foster a more active engagement b ased on Constructivist theories. Consequently, considering previous studies have demonstrated  scaffolding’s positive effects, the current study  aims to investigate  how scaffolding prompts for ChatGPT use during argumentative writin g affects learning outcomes  such as self-efficacy, interest, knowledge, and performance.

## Literature review

Recent studies investigate that ChatGPT effectively enhances learner motivatio n and engagement in language learning contexts (Muñoz et al., 2023). Additionall y, it has been shown to significantly improve learn ing achievements in technology learning. However, learners still experience difficulties in making effective prompts when using ChatGPT.

As AI such as ChatGPT advance in complexity, the qu ality of their outputs depends not only on the algorithms and data but also on the effectiveness of the prompts they receive.  This has led to the emergence of 'prompt engineering' as a new digital literacy skill (Bozkurt, 2023). Prompt design determines how effective they are in AI interactions; well-structured prompts provide meaningful replies, wher eas poorly-designed ones generate irrelevant outputs. Current research focuses on prompt optimization to improve AI interactions, including methods such as careful word selection, specificity, and context providing (Atlas, 2023), as well as the 'CLEAR' framework, which emphasizes conciseness, logic, exp licitness, adaptability, and reflectiveness  (Lo, 2023). To write appropriate prompts and enable effective interaction, teacher support has been underlined (Rahman & Wastanobe, 2023; Su et al., 2023) and this support is referred to as ‘prompt scaffolding’ in this study.

## Participants

A total of 179 Korean high school students, 90 male s and 89 females, were included in a quasi-experimental investigation. To investigate the impact of ChatGPT  prompt scaffolding on argumentative writing, the c lasses were randomly allocated to either the ex periment or control groups. As a result, the experi ment group included 98 students, while the control group included 81 students.

## Procedure

The research spanned five class sessions, with each  session having a duration of 50 minutes. The initial session encompassed the conducting of pre-test surveys and assessments. During sessions two t o four, the students participated in argumentative writing tasks utilizi ng the ChatGPT.  The procedure was initiated by the teacher introducing a topic, followed by the students engaging in questioning to enhance their comprehension. After that, the students developed their positions and constructed outlines, which were further elaborated into comprehensive argumentative essays using ChatGPT.  The fifth session contained the conducting of post-test surveys and assessments. The surveys were utilized to estimate levels of self-efficacy and interest, and the assessments were used to evaluate levels of knowledge and performanc e in the argumentative writing. The experiment group was provided with intervention in the prompt  scaffolding, whereas the control group participated  in argumentative writing using ChatGPT without scaffolding.

## Data collection

The research instrument used in this study was a mo dified version of the writing self-efficacy survey created by Shell and Bruning (1989), which had 26 items using 6-point Likert scale. The overall reliability of the self-efficacy survey items was high (Cronbach’s alpha=0.89). Furthermore, the survey measuring writing interest was modified to suit the situation from a survey presented by Bong et al. (2012) and consisted of 5 items using 6-point Liker scale. The reliability of the writing interest survey items was very high (Cronbach’s alpha=0.94). We gave students essay writing assignments to measure their argument ative writing skills. To assess their independent argumentation skills, participants completed these activities without the assistance of ChatGPT during  both the pre-test and post-test per iods. The essays addressed issues such as the deplo yment of strong AI and the identification of parties responsible for AI-related concerns. ## Data analysis

In the study, a repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to investigate the influence of ChatGPT prompt scaffolding on learning outcomes. Th e variables of self-efficacy, interest, knowledge, and performance were quantified through total scores. Performance was assessed jointly by a researcher and a teacher, focusing on content, form, and expression, and the inter-rater reliability (Cohen’s kappa) of these evaluations was high (content: 0.84, form: 0.92, expression: 0.80).

## Results

Self-efficacy The repeated measures ANOVA showed a significant main effect of time, indicating differences between pre-test and post-test self-efficacy scores, F (1, 177) = 27.81, p <.001 (see Table 2). There was significant interaction effect between groups and time in self-efficacy of argumentative writing, F (1, 177) = 19.39, p <.001. This finding suggests that there were diverging improvements in self-efficacy scores between the experiment and control groups, with the experiment group showing a more substantial improvement compared to the control group. This finding underscores the efficiency of the experimental intervention in boosting self-efficacy.  However, there was no significant main effect of group when considerin g the experiment and control groups separately, F (1, 177) = 3.19, p =.076.

Interest The repeated measures ANOVA revealed a significant main effect of group, F (1, 177) = 7.79, p =.006, indicating distinct interest scores between the experiment and control groups (see Table 2). Additionally, a significant main effect of time was observed, F  (1, 177) = 41.63, p <.001, reflecting an overall shift in interest from pre-test to post-test. The results indicate a significant group-by-time interaction, F (1, 177) = 16.87, p <.001, suggesting that there was a different changes of interest scores over time between the experiment and control groups. ## Discussion

Significant interaction effects were observed acros s all assessed variables with the application of a repeated measures ANOVA.  The findings of the study indicated that the group that receiving prompt scaffolding  had significant enhancements in self-efficacy, interest, knowledge, and performance. These results indicat e that the use of organized prompting with ChatGPT has the potential to cultivate an effective educational outcomes. A comprehensive incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies in educational settings should go beyond just delivering material. It is important  to prioritize both the subject content and the app lication of technology in order to effectively engage students and enhance the effectiveness of learning. These fi ndings support the suggestion that teachers should success fully manage the utilization of ChatGPT and impleme nt strategic pedagogical approaches, such as prompt  scaffolding, in order to improve student engagement  and enhance learning outcomes in the argumentative writ ing. The effectiveness of integrating ChatGPT into educational environments depends on its pedagogical  implementation, which necessitates educators to in struct students on how to  utilize ChatGPT not only as a tool, but also as an approach to promote enhanced cognitive involvement and the building of knowledge. The adoption of these pedagogical strategies is anticipated to enhance learning results and provide novel approaches for u sing ChatGPT into the argumentative writing education. This study has a few limitations because it examined the effectiveness of prompt scaffolding in the Korean high school context. Considering the possibility that other learning contexts may show different results in terms of the impact of prompt scaffolding when using ChatGPT, it is imperative to extend the application of prompt scaffolding across diverse array of educational settings. Broadening prompt scaffolding to encompass considera tions of AI ethics such as user data privacy and the potential for bias is needed. In addition, investigating variables that significantly influence learning outcomes including students’ prior writing experience or fam iliarity with AI tools  and incorporating qualitative research method could provide deeper insights of scaffolding effects.
