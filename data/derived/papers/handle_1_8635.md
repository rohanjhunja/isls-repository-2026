# Co-Design of AI-enhanced Learning Analytics: Considerations for Ensuring Diversity, Equity, Justice

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The majority of the participatory/co-design research has somehow ignored the considerations tackling the diversity, equity, and justice challenges in the design process of learning analytics. We suggest four considerations: (1) while determining the needs and expectations of targeted learners, consider all subgroups of populations (2) determine the transparent and inclusive algorithm for meeting the needs (3) consider diversity and equity for model training data (4) consider heterogeneous participants while testing prototypes of learning analytics.

## Introduction

Although learning analytics has huge potential in serving personalized learning opportunities, there are also prevalent concerns on crucial ethical issues such as discrimination and fairness. Specifically, these concerns are related to the degree to which learning analytics can provide different learners from numerous socio-cultural and economic backgrounds with fair intervention (Ha nsen & Reich, 2015). Due to the integration of arti ficial intelligence (AI) into learning analytics, algorith ms and automated computational methods have been wi dely utilized. In this regard, algorithmic decision-maki ng of learning analytics has raised some questions on inclusiveness and discrimination levels of these algorithms (De Cremer & De Schutter, 2021). In order to effectively overcome diversity and equi ty challenges, the possible solutions require an interdisciplinary collaborative approach involving all stakeholders who benefit from AI-enhanced learn ing analytics systems (Dollinger & Lodge, 2018). This a pproach is closely related to the participatory/co-design process. Although important research and initiatives are focusing to (co)- design effective and efficient learning analytics systems with particular learners, the maj ority of this participatory/co-design research has somehow ignored the considerations /demands of tackling the diversity, equity, and justice challenges in early design and testing process of learning analytics systems (Holstein & Doroudi, 2019).  The purpose of the current paper is to determine considerations for ensuring diversity, eq uity, and justice in the co-design process of AI-en hanced learning analytics.  Researchers and developers have utilized the co-design approach to give a voice to learners. Nowadays, co-design has become a well-established a pproach to facilitate the inclusion of the differen t stakeholders` needs in the design process. In the c urrent study, the main components of co-design are shown in four iterative steps. The relevant components with descriptions are as follows:  Empathize & Problem Definition (1): identification of target learners, description of contextual factors Ideate (2): decision on learning approach, identification of learning variables and data modal ity, determination of AI methods, role of AI method s in the learning analytics tools. Development of Prototypes (3): selection of low fidelity and high-fidelity proto types. Test (4): implementation of usability test, deployment of LA tools in a real classroom setting.

## Methodology

The considerations for co-design of AI-enhanced lea rning analytics were determined by reviewing litera ture from various disciplines namely learning analytics,  responsible AI use in education, and co-design pro cess for educational technologies. Through the review of rel ated literature, the consideration was selected bas ed on the diversity, equity, and justice from the well-known publications in each research area. The considerations for codesign of AI-enhanced learning analytics were deter mined by reviewing literature from various discipli nes namely learning analytics, responsible AI use in ed ucation, and co-design process for educational technologies. Through the review of related literature, the consi deration was selected based on the diversity, equit y, justice from the well-known publications in each research area (e.g., Hernández‐Leo et al., 2019).

## Results

The preliminary results as considerations are prese nted in this section. Initially, we determined four  important considerations (C1, C2, C3, and C4).

C1: While determining the needs and expectations of targeted learners, consider all subgroups of populations As researchers and developers interview or meet wit h learners to grasp their needs, the participants s hould represent all learners for equity. In this step, th e gender, race, socio-economic characteristics of g roups should be formed as equal as possible. ## Conclusions

Personalized and adaptive approaches of learning an alytics highlight the advantage of enhancing the eq uity of learners at different stages. For maximizing this advantage, researchers and developers should perform some codesign considerations. In the current study, we def ined four preliminary considerations for ensuring d iversity, equity, and justice in the learning analytics syste ms. Learning analytics developers might integrate t hese considerations when they carry out the co-design process.

C2: Determine a transparent and inclusive algorithm for meeting the needs and expectations The prediction results of algorithms should be chec ked not only for accuracy but also inclusiveness. Transparency of algorithms is also important for ju stice. Transparency is one of the common ethical us e principles and considerations of many AI-based syst ems (Kitto & Knigt, 2019). Transparency makes AI algorithms of learning analytics more accountable and, thus, the outcome of learning analytics such as adaptive feedback might be more visible and measurable (Anan ny & Crawford, 2018). C2 can be performed in the ideate (2) step of the co-design process.

C3: Consider diversity and equity for model training data In order for AI algorithms of learning analytics to  make fair decisions, training data should properly  represent targeted learners. Before training AI algorithms based on the data, developers must know how the learn ers are distributed in the training data set (De Cremer & D e Schutter, 2021). The discrepancy could stem from an unbalanced training set within some subgroups due t o historical bias. Also, it is more likely that res earchers might be biased when they focus conclusions derived  from skewed data privileging behavior patterns of more prepared, lower need students (Meaney & Fikes, 2018). C3 can be applied in the ideate (2) and development of prototypes (3) steps of the co-design process.

C4: Consider the heterogeneous participants while testing prototypes of learning analytics In the testing step of the co-design process, the f irst versions (prototypes) of developed learning an alytics are utilized by learners. Deep understanding is needed to improve prototypes; therefore, the participants testing prototypes should be as diversified as possible. If  any learners from different sub-groups have no cha nce to provide comments, the last version might be lacking  their views, intentions and expectations.
