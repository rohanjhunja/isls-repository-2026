# Where Material Impact Matters: Fostering Equitable Teaching Practices through Working Differences in Elementary Science Methods Course

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This conceptual paper empirically illustrates the journey of preservice science teachers' becoming, presenting narratives of working differences. It involves juxtaposing differences to disrupt the sameness ontology prevalent in science education and broader societal contexts. We map preservice teachers' paths of becoming in an elementary science methods course, sharing key juncture points where transformative science teaching practices emerge. This is achieved by noticing ways to reconfigure their classroom environment that significantly impact their teaching and their students’ learning experiences.

## Background and objectives

The purpose of this proposal is to empirically explore the concept of elementary preservice teachers’ diffracting noticing through the lens of "working difference" (Ellsworth & Miller, 1996, p. 179) in education and follows lines of flight (Deleuze & Guattari, 1987) of preservice teachers’ becoming during an elementary science methods course. Utilizing a posthuman concept of diffraction and becoming (Barad, 2007), we seek to create a space where elementary preservice teacher s of science engage in diffracting noticing (Jeong & Steele, 2023) such that fluid and dynamic notions of equitable teaching practices may emerge.  In doing so, o ur aim is to reimagine a more equitable, inclusive pedagogy for elementary preser vice teachers of science  and sustain such practices by leveraging areas where material impact matters the most in their teaching. Building on the work of the Jeong & Steele (2023), we advocate for using science and science education as a tool to address wicked problems disproportiona tely affecting marginalized populations  and delve into transformative, equity-centered elementary science teacher education. We e xplore how science educators can facilitate elementary preservice teachers' engagement with their teaching praxis by examining their navigation of dynamic differences (Barad, 2007). To this end, we aim to reconceptualize the journey of elementary pr eservice science teachers’ becoming (which is always and already fluid and continually evolving) as narratives of "working difference" (Ellsworth & Miller, 2014) as they engage in diffracting their teacher notices. “Working difference” is not about working through the differences or working with the differences or working past the differences; this engagement of “working difference” involves juxtaposing differences in order to disrupt the hegemonies rooted in the ontology of sameness, prevalent not only in science education but also in broader societal contexts (Roth, 2008). To that end, we map the paths taken by elementary preservice teachers in their becoming as they juxtapose their differences.  In mapping their becoming, we share their juncture points in their journey where meaningful and transformative s cience teaching practices emerge. The research ques tion guiding this exploration is: How do instances of "working difference" manifest when preservice teachers actively diffract their teacher notices in order to foster evolving conceptions of equity  and envision a more just  and inclusive pedagogy?

Theoretical assumptions and concepts Jeong & Steele  (2023) advocate against conditioning learners to a single, static being in equitable science instruction.  In this proposal, we draw on Barad's (2007) concept s of becoming and diffraction to empirically illustrate the notion of teacher noticing, specifically explor ing the "situated working of multiple and fluid identities" in the context of elementary preservice teacher education (Ellsworth & Miller, 1996, p. 181). Applying Barad’s concepts of diffraction and becoming (2007,  and 2014), diffracting noticing, as theorized by Jeong & Steele (2023), is a relational-ontology-oriented concept that ruptures and challenges ontological concerns about stable entities. Thus, we argue in this paper that "working difference" involve s engaging with and responding to the fluidity and malleability of identities and dif ferences, rejecting fixed and static categories of sameness or permanent otherness (Ellsworth & Miller, 1996, p. 181). As part of a larger NSF IUSE grant, the iteratively designbased elementary science methods course focuses on equity-oriented pedagogy and fostering multiple epistemologies, moving beyond simplistic notations of comparing similarities or contrasting differences in both students' and teachers’ identities  or their becomings. Understanding one’s becoming through posthuman perspectives, employed in this paper, helps us reconceptualize stable beings as effects p roduced from lived experiences, interactions, and entanglements that a re always being assembled and re-assembled. Differences are juxtaposed when preservice teachers diffract their notices; they work differences  to produce new insights, entanglements and assemblages that are organized and re-organized. To this end,  we investigate elementary preservice teachers' becoming by emphasizing dynamicity, multiplicities, and differences and how the preservice teachers engage in “working difference” as a process of "revealing, interrupting, and reconstructing meanings and power relations" that would otherwise remain fixed (Ellsworth & Miller, 1996, p. 181).

## Method

Orienting our inquiry from a posthuman perspective,  we employed a method of inquiry that assume s the subject as irreducible, multiple, and continually re-assembled through social-discursive-material relational entanglements with other entities (Jeong & Steele, 2023). In doing so, we reject “tree logic” that c alls for rigid, systematic, and linear protocols for reproducibility  and transparent analysis (Sherman & Teeman, 2020). Instead, we employed rhizomatic analysis that embraces the uncertainty of not knowing where the “analysis” will lead, recognizing that we cannot prescribe an analysis plan for beings who are essentially people-who-are-yet-to-come or in the process of becoming. We, as science educators, desired to work identities and d ifferences with our preservice teachers through their lived stories, connecting with “the l iving of a life to the processes of theorizing and analyzing ‘differences,’ to the processes of ‘reconfiguring what will count as the world’ (Butler, 1993). To that end, we began our posthuman inquiry with employing ethnographically-informed methods of analysis, for every research methodology has an entry point and an initial anchor: Using this method, we collected preservice teachers’ work samples and artifacts of teaching produced from assignments such as reflecti ons, concept maps, asynchronous discussion posts, lesson  plans, video recordings of their teaching, preserv ice teachers’ doodles and musings, and artifacts of dis cussion during class that included the course instr uctor’s doodles and musings. Author 1 taught the course at a public, research intensive university in the Mideastern region of the United States. The course was designed such that elementary preservice teachers engaged in mult iple opportunities to: 1) articulate their ideas about equitable and inclusive teaching and juxtapose their teaching ideas in relation to others, 2) reflect on and re-articulate the discursive-material impact left by these differences, and 3) notice and “see” their instructional approaches thr ough the lens of others to produce new experiences,  thought, and ideas about equitable teaching.

As a method of analysis, w e used mapping (Deleuze & Guattari, 1987) to repres ent our preservice teachers’ becoming. Employing a rhizomatic mode of inquiry (St. Pierre,  2004), we aimed to examine material-discursive assemblages by juxtaposing artifacts of preservice teachers teaching and examined how prese rvice teachers engaged in working differences, as we read, juxtaposed, and slip-slid against the text of the artifacts of preservice teachers’ work.

## Findings

Here, we mapped the paths taken by elementary preservice teachers in their becoming as they worked their differences. In mapping their becoming, we share  the juncture points in their journey where meaningful a nd transformative science teaching praxis emerges. We empirically illustrated that:

1. Elementary preservice teachers began to notice and recognize ways in which they were capable of

reconfiguring their classroom environment.

2. Elementary preservice teachers articulated varied ways of recon figuring their classroom environment

that had a material impact on their teaching practices.

3. Elementary preservice teachers began to shift their  teaching praxis to create different material reali ties

or material entanglements towards a more equitable and inclusive science teaching for their students.

## Discussions and contribution

We live in a world of messy, entanglements of diffe rences. The persistent refusal to cultural métissag e (Roth,

2008) and thus hybridity and heterogeneity of differing expectations, desires, and assumptions often lead to social-material-discursive spaces of injustices and inequities: And a science classroom is no exception.  As science educators, w e respond ed to the advocacy of using science and science education  as a tool to address wicked problems disproportionately affecting marginalized populations. We delved into transformative, equity-centered elementary science teacher education. In this vein, it is an ethical imperative for science education researchers to consider concepts that assume the subject as irredu cible, multiple and continually re-assembled through social, discursive, material relational entanglements. Thus, we examine d how the notion of “‘situated working’ of ‘multiple and fluid’ identities” and difference s can become useful in research in science teacher ed ucation (Ellsworth & Miller, 1996, p. 181) towards helping elementary preservice teachers reim agine a more equitable and inclusive science pedagogy.

While thinking with posthuman concepts outlined abo ve (St. Pierre, 2013), we employ ed a rhizomatic mode of inquiry (St. Pierre, 2004) and examine d material-discursive assemblages by juxtaposing artifacts of preservice teachers teaching. In doing so, we illus trated what “working difference” could look like  based on our findings:

1. Engaging in this process became a way of “seeing” a nd noticing their own teaching practices

differently and noticing differences and therefore becoming differently.

2. Working difference led to preservice teachers’ engaging in a dialogic process of reimagining a kind

of effect-on-practice that was based on relational ontology and differences.

3. The culmination of their working difference manifes ted in implementing varied instructional

approaches to reconfigure their classroom environment that would foster different material realities that their students could experience thereby impacting the students’ becoming in the space: A socialmaterial-discursive assemblage that would be accessible and inclusive of all of their students. We turned our inquiries into differing material-dialogic assemblages that are fostered where preservice teachers’ “working difference” allow ed them to be prepared to  respond to their students’ science becoming with fluidity and malleability. Preservice teachers were able to imagine  ways to reconfigure their classroom environment to have a material impact on not only their teaching p raxis but on students’ learning. This work compels us to rethink preservice teachers’ multiple and fluid becom ing, as an assemblage that was “constructed in and through the dynamics of our engagement with each other over  time” (Ellsworth & Miller, 1996, p. 181). In doing  so, working difference foregrounds the possibility and significance of reading and performing difference s as workin-progress with the potential to have a material impact and creating different material realities for the students.
