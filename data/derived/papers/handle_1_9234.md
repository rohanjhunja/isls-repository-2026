# The Influence of Knowledge-Building-Based Collaborative Argumentation Instruction on High School Students' Online Collaborative Argumentation Performance and Behavioral Patterns Regarding Socio-Scientific Issues

**Conference:** ISLS 2023

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Argumentation on socio-scientific issues (SSI) is challenging for high school students, this study attempted to adopt the knowledge-building (KB) pedagogy in SSI-based collaborative argumentation instruction design and further explored its effects on high school students' SSI-based online argumentation. This study revealed that the students in the experimental group benefited more from the KB-based instruction in their online SSI-based argumentation performance. Also, two groups of students revealed different online collaborative argumentation behavioral patterns after instructions.

## Introduction

Previous research has revealed that SSI-based argumentation is complicated and challenging for high school students. They have difficulties generating arguments for different purposes. Besides, they often treat argumentation as a win-lose debate rather than a collaborative idea ref inement (Wu et al., 2017). As one of the initial attempts, a KB-based collaborative argumentation instruction was designe d in this study to address the above issues. The KB pedagogy emphasizes that all "ideas" proposed by students should be treated as improvable, and the purpose of creative knowledge work in school is to advance the state of knowledge in the “classroom community” through progressive and collective discourse (Bereiter & Scardam alia, 2014). The adoption of KB pedagogy in argumentation instruction design could be promising in transferring the original win-or-lose type of classroom argumentation into a more idea-centered and community-based collaborative argumentation. Also, this study explored the influence of KB-based collaborative argumentation instruction on high school students' online argumentation performances and behavior patterns regarding socio-scientific issues. The research question of this study is: After implementing SSI-based argumentation instructions, how do the two groups of students with KBbased collaborative argumentation instruction and conventional argumentation instruction diff er in their online collaborative argumentation performances and behavior patterns?

## Participants, learning context and research design

The participants were 78 first-year high school students who volunteered to  join an argumentation workshop adopting two topics: nuclear power plant building and genetic modification food (GMF). This study used a quasiexperimental design to explore the effects of KB-based argumentation instruction. The participants were randomly assigned to the Experimental (n=34) and the Control groups (n=44) with two different argumentation instructions. Both instructions had two-hour sections twice and were taught by the same teacher. There were three stages for this study: (1) Mini-lessons for discuss ion on an online argumentation platform (CoArgue); (2) Argumentation instructions for two weeks (the control gr oup: a conventional argumentation instruction (AI); the experimental group: a KB-based collaborative A rgumentation Instruction (KBCAI)); (3) Online argumentation (all participants of the two groups were  randomly assigned into pairs and asked to argue another SSI topic on CoArgue).

## Data collection and analysis

To evaluate the two groups' students' argumentation performance, the framewor k proposed by McAlister et al.

(2004) was adopted. All the students’ arguments were categorized into six ty pes: Proposing arguments (A1),

Questioning (A2), Challenging (A3), Reasoning (A4), Supporting (A5), and Others (A6), indicating their argumentation performance. Chi-square analyses were conducted to examine if the students’ argumentation performance in the pretest and the post-test were associated with the two groups. ## Conclusion

This study initially adopted KB pedagogy in SSI-based argumentation instruction and the results revealed the effectiveness of the KB-based argumentation instruction. The findings of this study may provide important insights for future research and future development and implementation of SSI-based argumentation instruction.
