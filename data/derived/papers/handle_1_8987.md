# Using Delivery Simulation Experiences in Hiring in the Technology Field

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
Technical assessments and interviews are a unique facet of the technology field. Unfortunately, they have gaps that lead to frustration for candidates and result in companies missing top talent. I propose using delivery simulation experiences to better assess and meet the needs of a modern workforce.

## Introduction

The recruiting and hiring process is rife with challenges. Some of the issues are found in all fields; for example, over and underemployment (Kulkarni et al, 2013), inclusion (Nota et al, 2013), and implicit bias (Fisher, 2018) are not unique to the technology field. However, there are some problems that are more unique to the technology field. One such problem is technical interviews. Using the connectivist l ens, this paper will examine the traditional technical interview process and a theoretical alternative-the delivery simulation experience. Why use connectivism Developing software and the problems being solved within the te ch industry are complex problems. As Siemens

(2006) points out, with increased complexity, in order to solve prob lems, the knowledge to do so resides in the col-lective. Connectivism addresses the pace at which knowledge is cons tructed and changes in a modern society (Siemens, 2006). Delivery simulation experiences Delivery simulation experiences are mock environments where candidates work alongside their prospective team for some fixed amount of time and the work being done reflects the actu al work that would be done and problems that would be solved in the job for which the candidate is applying. Ta ble 1 presents a comparison of the traditional technical interview compared to delivery simulation experiences through the lens of connectivist principles. Learning is a network of specialized nodes that requires a diversity of opinions to choose the best approach and knowledge may reside in non-human appliances, and learning is enabled/facilitated by technology.

In take home technical challenges, the candidate may use existing technological resources like previous projects. In in-person technical interviews, the candidate is expected to have all the knowledge to solve the problem or answer the question (Behroozi et al, 2019; Chrupcala, 2021; Morahg, 2021).

Candidates may use any of the technological resources typically used during the team’s day-to-day activities as well as tools with which the candidate is familiar. They may also take advantage of the team and specializations (e.g. product specialists and designers) to inform decisions.

Learning and knowing are constant, on going processes (not end states or products). Currency (accurate, up-todate knowledge) and capacity to know more are the priority and the intent of all connectivist learning activities.

Capacity is focused on and limited to what the candidate currently knows (Behroozi et al, 2019; Chrupcala, 2021; Indeed Editorial Team, 2021; Morahg, 2021).

The intent of a DSE is to assess how a candidate uses and seeks knowledge to fill knowledge gaps. The team and candidate are engaged in the process of learning as they work through the problem together in search of a solution.

The right answer now may be wrong tomorrow.

Technical assessments are presented largely in isolation. The purpose is to test the candidate’s individual knowledge. Presented with a problem that gives the candidate an opportunity to showcase these skills, this can be done in a traditional technical interview (Chrupcala, 2021; Indeed Editorial Team, 2021).

The work being done in a DSE reflects authentic work because DSEs are collaborations. There are opportunities for the candidate to demonstrate connections and adjust tonew information not only with fields and ideas, but with the hiring team.

## Conclusion

Delivery simulation environments can't replace the entire hiring process. They can provide an opportunity for both candidate and team to assess one another on more realistic and holistic terms. DSEs have the potential to be a better fit from a connectivist perspective than the traditional technical task or technical interview. More research is needed in order to explore and collect evidence to better understand what DSEs have to offer.
