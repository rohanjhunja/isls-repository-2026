# Efficacy of Dialogic Teaching for Learning with Multiple Representation Simulations in Secondary Physics Classrooms

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study investigated how dialogic teaching supports physics learning with multiple representation simulations. Three secondary teachers and their 268 students were recruited from China with a two-month intervention. The present results indicated that teachers in the treatment groups employed more various talk moves than comparison groups. Students in the treatment had significantly higher discursive engagement and positive emotional engagement than the comparison group. The findings contributed to the effectiveness of dialogic teaching on multiple representations.

## Introduction and literature review

Teaching and learning Physics can be challenging and requires students to mentally visualize abstract or complex concepts (Kjeldsen & Lützen, 2015; Lützen, 2011). T o address this difficulty, physics teachers use var ious representations, such as graphs, to aid students' u nderstanding. However, if used inappr opriately, these representations can impose an additional cognitive burden on students. Multiple representations refer to utilizing concret e representations (e.g., tables, plots) to elucidate the direct relationships between abstract principles and everyday phenomena (Ainsworth, 1999). Employing multiple representations is cost-effective and succinct in providing additional info rmation, resolving interpretative challenges, and fostering deeper comprehension (Ainsworth, 1999). Student engagement plays a mediating role between classroom interaction and student learning outcomes (Appleton et al., 2008; Virtanen et al., 2015). Student discursive engagement with others is the de gree to which students are involved with others in conversational interactions to learn about the subject matter within a domainspecific context (Chen, Zhang, et al., 2020). Students’ positive emot ions, such as enjoyment in class, are an important part of the school experience and are associated with their learning and achievement (Pekrun et al., 2006; Frenzel et al., 2018).

For dialogic instructions, the academically productive talk (APT) framework outlines four pedagogical goals supported by specific talk moves: (a) elaborating, (b) reasoning, (c) lis tening, and (d) thinking with others (Michaels et al., 2010). The framework highlights teacher guidance, engaging  in reasoning discussions through elaborations, debating with others, and expressing opinions and views, which adjust students’ emotions (Chen, Zhang, et al., 2020), thereby enhancing students’ learning processes and outcomes. In this study, we utilize dialogic teaching to support multiple representation-based learning and examine their influence on classroom discourse, students’ c lassroom engagement, and their physics learning out comes. Dialogic instruction is designed as supporting mate rial provided to teachers to enhance their teaching  strategies in accordance with the related multiple representat ions simulations, thereby fostering increased stude nt engagement. Our study addresses the following four research questions:

(1) Do the treatment and comparison participants have different classroom discourse?

(2) Do students in the treatment and comparison classrooms have different classroom engagement?

(3) Do students in the treatment and comparison classrooms have different physics learning outcomes?

(4) If so, how and why do the classroom engagement and student learning outcomes of the treatment and

comparison groups differ?

## Methods

This mixed-methods study involved 3 physics teachers and their students (268 students from six classes), from a school in the northwestern city of mainland China. Each teacher coded and randomized two comparable physics level classes into the treatment group (n = 134; 67 girls, 67 boys) and the comparison group (n = 134; 67 girls, 67 boys). Teachers in the treatment group used the prepared instructional design materials based on dialogic teaching ways with multiple representation  simulations, while teachers in the comparison group use materials based on monologic teaching ways with the same simulations. This is an ongoing study designed to last two months, and we partially completed the lesson video, questionnaire collection, and related data analysis. Lesson videos for coding and analyzing with the Classroom Discourse Analyzer tool (CDA; Chen, Chan, et al., 2020)  for RQ 1. We assessed cognitive engagement, discursive engagement, and emotional engagement with six-point Likert scale questionnaires according to the literature (Chen, Zhang, et al., 2 020; Fredricks, et al.,

2016) in pre-, middle-, and post-tests. Student academic achievements were collected at various stages, including

pre-tests, monthly tests, and post-tests. We developed the test papers with two  to three items to test the learning outcomes of every interventional topic lesson according to the textbook and homework. The t-test is conducted on paired pre-tests and post-tests for RQ s 2-4. Semi-structured interviews would be conducted with selected students as well.

## Discussion and significance

The preliminary findings from this ongoing study in dicated that classroom discourse, when based on multiple representations, has the potential to contribute to physics educatio n. We anticipate discovering a statistically significant difference between the treatment and co mparison groups after the post-test. From a theoretical perspective, our goal is to identify the effectiveness of using dialogic teaching to support multiple representations in teaching and learning. We aim to address the lim itations associated with the high cognitive load of  multiple representations and contribute to the literature on student eng agement in dialogic teaching. Practically, we hope to offer pedagogical insights for physics teaching and learning, enhance the alignment of simulations within teaching practices, and inform the design and innovative use of educational technologies.
