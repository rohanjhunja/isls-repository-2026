# Intertwining Research and Practice in Higher Education: Presenting LPI’s Learning Sciences Master Program

**Conference:** ISLS 2022

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The LPI and Université de Paris have developed the AIRE Master program to train future change-makers and researchers in response to the transformation happening in our world. This innovative program offers high-level training through a learning-by-doing-based curriculum and social implications. It aims to embark on a rich discussion between various stakeholders on how research and hands-on practices can be combined in such a way to transform our higher education system.

## Introduction

Today’s world is changing rapidly, becoming more in terconnected, technologically complex, and economically competitive (Sawyer, 2014). Meanwhile, we are facin g global and social challenges that require us to t ake immediate action. Yet, the traditional pedagogies have failed to train our society to overcome such challenges. To succeed in confronting these challenges, we need to co-construct and share new ways of teaching, learning, doing research, and mobilizing collective intelligence. In other words, we need a “learning transition” that enables us to learn how to learn and support the learning of individuals everywhere and anytime. In response to this challenge, LPI (Learning Planet Institute) and Université de Paris have developed a master’s program to train future changemakers and researchers concerned with the way we teach, learn, and conduct research as well as work, create, and collaborate. The program, namely AIRE (Interdisciplinary Approaches in Research and Education), offers highlevel training in learning sciences and intertwines research and practice, the collaboration of multiple stakeholders, and an interdisciplinary approach to transforming our way of teaching and learning. General Overview of The Program The program aims at enabling students to comprehend  the concept of teaching and learning harmonized wi th needs and changes, design and implement effective l earning environments, and bridge research and practice to address problems through an evidence-based approach. One of the significant objectives of the program is to develop the ability of students to face and deal wi th a diverse range of analyses. As (Doise, 1986) pr oposed, human actions, such as learning, can be explained through the conceptual framework of the four-level of analysis: intra-individual processes (level 1), inter-individual but intra-situational dynamics (level 2), effec ts of social position in a situational interaction (level 3), and intervention of general beliefs (level 4). In this perspective, the program enables students to understand and experience themselves at these different levels through being exposed to various disciplines and being involved in diverse types of (a) community, (b) ecosystem, and (c) in ternships. In line with (Renn & Arnold, 2003) and based on Bro nfenbrenner’s Ecological Systems Theory (1986), the program strives to let students comprehend that any learner is in the “center of concentric rings of environments”, ranging from immediate (microsystem) to most distal (macrosystem) contexts. Curriculum The curriculum aims at providing students with a gl obal understanding of the transformation happening in education, and it encourages them to design relevant and challenging entrepreneurial projects. Based on the figure of social-scientist-entrepreneur, the interdisciplinary teaching units aim to transmit the fundamental  knowledge of sub-disciplines related to learning sciences and  develop associated skills. The courses are offered  in a block mode to increase engagement and accelerate learning  (Zepeda & Stewart, 2006). It starts with an intens e fourweek bootcamp where students discover the basics of  learning sciences and develop initial reflections about learning. Following this, a variety of courses (e.g., instructional design, learning theories, game de sign, critical thinking, data science, etc.) and activities are offered to enrich students’ learning paths through theory and knowhow, practical knowledge, seminars and masterclasse s, research methodologies and statistics, and proje ct management foci. Moreover, as part of the curriculum, students do three internships in research labs, NGOs (Nongovernmental Organizations), or companies to gain hands-on experience working on learning-related issues. Pedagogy The pedagogical modalities of the program are devel oped to enable students to acquire knowledge and cr ucial ICLS2022 Proceedings 1956 © ISLS skills and apply them to everyday life. It is based on the concept of “learning-by-doing” to enable students to gain knowledge and skills through working in real-life c ontexts. For instance, internships and various cour ses (e.g., learning by doing curriculum and learning by doing research) provide students with opportunities to ac quire practical knowledge and transversal skills through experiencing firsthand. Moreover, it offers project-based learning by bringing bachelor’s, master’s, and doct orate students in the LPI ecosystem together to add ress challenging interdisciplinary problems. For example, the 'creative week’ is organized every year for master’s and doctorate students from diverse backgrounds to work collectively on an interdisciplinary project and present it to the LPI community. Also, the program values peer-to-peer learning as it develops a spirit of collaboration amongst learners and lets them enhance their in-depth knowl edge by learning from each other. Lastly, it attach es great importance to mentorship as it provides continuous improvement throughout the process.

## Social Implication

The program not only aims at enriching students' knowledge and skills but also equipping them intellectually to be able to reflect upon their own actions and how t hese actions affect their immediate social and phys ical environment. In an attempt to compose a learning environment and curriculum that support students in functioning in social reconstruction, the program put emphasis on three significant elements: (a) diversity, (b) national/international networks, and (c) sustainable development goals (SDGs). Regarding diversity, it welcomes each year a cohort of passionate students from dive rse backgrounds and nationalities. For example, 14 students from 12 different countries (e.g., China, France, Russia, India, USA, UK, etc.) across the globe were admitted to the program for the 2021-2022 academic year, and their majors range from business to psychology, art and design, education, social sciences, and engineering. Students' desired career paths in the program vary as the y strive to hone relevant skills and knowledge in the area of their greatest interest. Graduates the program may choose, for example, a career in educational research within or outside academia, work in a company (e.g., e-learning manager, instructional designer, etc.), in NGOs, as creators of educational materials or educational consultants, and so on. Almost 35% of our alumni in an academic year apply for a Ph.D. in the field of learning sciences. Furthermore, the program attaches great importance to sharing be st practices, experiences, and knowledge with large  and enthusiastic international and national networks, learning from each other to achieve goals that cannot be achieved alone.  For instance, students get immersed into th e LPI ecosystem where they work collectively with o ther stakeholders such as scientists, researchers, stude nts, and individuals who are willing to bring about  a change. Lastly, the program cultivates a sense of belonging  to humanity and responsibility to take action to a ddress changing societal needs, thus fostering mutual understanding and orchestrating SDG-oriented activities towards sustainability.

## Conclusion

We need a "learning transition" that transforms the  way we teach, learn, and do research to keep pace with the fast-changing world. In light of this, the AIRE Learning Sciences Master program aims to intertwine research and practice, the collaboration of multiple stakeholder s, and an interdisciplinary approach through innova tive curriculum, adaptive pedagogy, and social implication to train future change-makers and researchers.
