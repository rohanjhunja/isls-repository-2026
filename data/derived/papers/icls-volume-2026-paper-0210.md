# Embedding Epistemological Pluralism in Explorers of Planet Ioio: A Roleplay- Based Data Science Curriculum

**Conference:** ICLS 2026

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
We explore how undergraduates may engage with multiple epistemological values related to data science in Explorers of Planet Ioio, a pilot, roleplay-based data science curriculum designed to support epistemological pluralism: awareness of multiple, valid ways to construct knowledge with data. Much existing data science education emphasizes singular sets of epistemological approaches to data inquiry, contrasting how data scientists approach problems from different epistemological perspectives. We present findings from a design-based research case study to describe processes in Explorers of Planet Ioio that preceded awareness of epistemological pluralism. We theorize which kinds of processes allowed the learners to better understand foundations of epistemological pluralism in the Explorers of Planet Ioio context. We also share how we interweave epistemological reflection within our data science curricular materials.

In this paper, we embed principles of epistemological pluralism in Explorers of Planet Ioio, a set of intentionally designed undergraduate data science lessons, to better understand how undergraduate learners may come to understand multiple ways of constructing knowledge with data. We define epistemological pluralism in this context as coming to understand multiple, valid ways of constructing knowledge (Andreotti et al., 2011; Miller et al., 2008; Turkle & Papert, 1992). This philosophical framework contrasts with much of traditional data science pedagogical design, which emphasizes procedural problem-solving strategies. As such, data science education may implicitly communicate a singular set of epistemological values to learners, contrasting with how data scientists approach problems with differing epistemological values and ways of constructing knowledge with data (Hicks & Irizarry, 2018; Memarian & Doleck, 2024). Our curricular materials aim to better prepare learners to navigate the plurality of data science approaches through embedding a conceptual foundation of epistemological pluralism in its design. We pose the following research question: How do learners come to recognize that they can solve a data-driven problem with multiple possible analytical approaches? We present results from a design-based research case study and develop two design cycles of data science curricular activities where learners explore how they, and others, may solve the same complex data science problem differently. We share our initial design conjectures to facilitate reflection on personal ways of constructing data knowledge and the possibility of multiple, valid data knowledge construction approaches. Our analyses link these conjectures to initial findings through a grounded theory process (Creswell & Poth, 2018), where we describe how our learners may learn to construct knowledge with data from multiple epistemological perspectives. We theorize what kinds of processes allowed the learners to better understand foundations of epistemological pluralism in the Explorers of Planet Ioio  context. Our conjectures guide the design of future Explorers of Planet Ioio materials to support learning of multiple epistemologies within data science education.

## Theoretical framework

The term  epistemology broadly encompasses beliefs and values related to knowledge characterization, development, and construction (Desai et al., 2022; Sandoval, 2005). We draw from literature around student epistemologies, which characterize student-held individual beliefs (Alsumait, 2015; Hofer & Pintrich, 2012) and values related to knowledge (Kuhn, 2012; Lowrie, 2017; Zahle, 2019). Specifically, we attend to student-described values associated with data knowledge construction, which we refer to as data epistemological values, including accuracy (Kuhn, 2012), efficiency (Lowrie, 2017), and interpretability (Somers, 2025) of data knowledge. To contend with the multiplicity of these values in an analytic context like Explorers of Planet Ioio, we design our activities around epistemological pluralism: a philosophical framework that contends there are multiple legitimate ways of knowing or understanding something (Turkle & Papert, 1992; Miller et al., 2008; D’Ignazio & Klein, 2023). This principle holds two broad implications for designing learning environments. First, distinct ways of constructing knowledge can complement each other when understanding complex problems (Miller et al., 2018). Epistemological pluralism encourages scientists to consider how one approach to knowledge construction can support another approach, resulting in a more comprehensive understanding of a 1532 problem. Second, epistemological pluralism does not absolutize subjectivity (D’Ignazio & Klein, 2023; Hoggan-Kloubert & Hoggan, 2023). Although learners understand the world through subjective interpretations, their knowledge of the world can still misalign with objective reality. Epistemological pluralism seeks to understand diff ering processes of knowledge construction, irrespective of whether the knowledge aligns with objective reality. Given the roleplaying context, we attend to the learners’ processes of constructing knowledge over the accuracy of knowledge constructed in our analyses. Our analyses draw from a constructionist framework, where understanding how a learner constructs an artifact sheds light on how a learner constructs a mental schema in relation to the artifact (Ames, 2018; Papert, 1991). Researchers better understand how learners construct knowledge by studying how they construct mediated artifacts (Ackermann, 2001). We view data science materials as mediated artifacts, and study processes of data model construction in Explorers of Planet Ioio to better understand learner knowledge construction and associated epistemological values. Our constructionist framework helps us link data construction processes, such as selecting features in a data model, to knowledge construction processes, guided by data epistemological values. Explorers of Planet Ioio design We designed Explorers of Planet Ioio around three conjectures toward a data science pedagogy centered around supporting epistemological pluralism. First, we aimed to cultivate awareness of data epistemological values through role-play pedagogy. In Explorers of Planet Ioio activities, learners are asked to roleplay as one of two fictional “Explorer” characters (Figure 1a) with distinct sets of interests, beliefs, and values pertaining to data science and epistemology (Bowman, 2014; Jones & Conner, 2021; Shapiro et al., 2021). After answering written reflective prompts about their role-sheet, learners first complete a lab-based data science lesson around decision tree modeling (Figure 1b). We encouraged learners to complete the lesson as if they were their Explorer character. We asked learners to “think-aloud” as if they were their character, sharing thoughts, emotions, and feelings that their character might have (Reinhart, 2022). Our second lesson instructs two learners, assigned different role-sheets, to construct a joint decision tree, or explain why they may not be able to do so. Here, we hypothesized that the learners might experience a contextual implication of epistemological pluralism in data science: the chance to collaboratively solve (or choose to not solve) a complex problem with multiple, differing solutions. After completing each role-playing exercise, learners answer written reflective questions as themselves. Together, we hypothesized that Explorers of Planet Ioio  would help the learners witness a contextual  application of epistemological pluralism in data science: collaboratively solving a complex problem with multiple, differing solutions. Second, we sought to expose learners to thinking with different analyses on the same data. Activities involve a fictional, pre-curated dataset with a variety of data types and variables, hinting at an underlying, narrative conflict (Hardy et al., 2021; Wise, 2022). We hypothesized that different learners may emphasize different variables in their data science models, based  on the values implicated by their role-sheets. With a dataset to support several possible analysis configurations, learners might explore different analyses on the same dataset (Taber, 2008; Foley et al., 2023). Third, we sought to disrupt notions of absolute objectivity and absolute subjectivity from constructed data knowledge. Through post-roleplay self-reflection on personal processes of knowledge construction, learners may more clearly recognize when constructed data knowledge misaligns with evidence from the dataset. This self-reflection may help learners identify data knowledge construction habits prone to misinformation, balancing methodological rigor and diverse ways of constructing data knowledge (D’Ignazio & Klein, 2023). Together, these three conjectures might help learners to identify the multiplicity of analytical approaches in a single context. ## Study design and data analysis

1533 We implemented two pilot design cycles of Explorers of Planet Ioio in Winter and Spring of 2025. We recruited twelve undergraduate participants (8 female, 4 male) from a large, research-focused university in the Western United States, with a convenience sampling approach. Most learners held affiliations with interdisciplinary, datadriven research labs, including the primary labs of both authors. Learners completed lesson activities across two sessions, lasting approximately 1.5 hours each. Seven learners c ompleted the first session remotely, and five learners completed the first session in person. All learners completed the second session remotely. Following consent, we recorded each learner’s audio, web camera, and screen over Zoom, which transcribed all captured audio recording. A “Mission Commander” (the study’s lead author) served as an activity facilitator and answered basic questions about activity instructions and concepts, while also taking observational notes. Analyses follow Creswell and Poth’s grounded theory phases (2018). In the open coding phase, the lead author applied inductive, open codes to all learner think-aloud transcripts and written reflection responses. Within the axial phase, the lead author initially focused on a broad emergent coding category, Context Processes, which described interactions with curricular materials, such as making summary statistics. One Context Processes category, Considering Alternatives, stood as central to the others. In the selective coding phase, the author identified three kinds of processes which comprised Considering Alternatives: Orientation, Dissatisfaction, and Satisfaction. From these processes, we theorize how learners came to understand the possibility of multiple data knowledge construction approaches in Explorers of Planet Ioio lessons. Within Orientation processes, such as reviewing a role-sheet, learners imagine how their character may construct their model(s). In doing so, they characterize data epistemological values of importance to themselves and their character. For example, learners who emphasized model accuracy constructed decision tree models and tweaked model parameters to increase or decrease model accuracy to a preferential level. Orientation activities also shed light on how these data epistemological values influenced their data knowledge construction. While in character, Student 11 connects her sheet’s data epistemological value of efficiency to how she interpreted decision tree visualizations: So my beliefs about efficiency made me try to find a way to think about data such that this kind of specific parts of data could give us a more or less larger decision rather than needing to go through multiple stages. So I guess that I focused a lot on the efficiency part, and that made me kind of zone into specific aspects of the table or specific aspects of the data that could have had a more larger relationship with the decision versus more focus on the accuracy part. In this example, the learner (in character) explicitly attributes a data knowledge construction process — zoning into specific parts of the data — to her character’s (and her personal) data epistemological value of efficiency. Through exploring how learners like Student 11 center her model construction around a data epistemological value of efficiency, we contextualize how learners who value efficiency might construct data knowledge. In these ways, we may be able to classify enacted and perceived data epistemological values within the Explorers of Planet Ioio context.

As learners construct their models, they experience moments of Dissatisfaction, which often precede moments where the learners find and acknowledge new ways of constructing the data model. One notable form of Dissatisfaction emerged as a recognition of assumptions. Student 7 (in character) describes how awareness of these assumptions related to an awareness of alternative and multiple ways of constructing data models: When you make assumptions, you're kind of impacting the model with your own biases. I think, to reconcile that would simply just be trying every, while using, of course, knowledge. But try and being smart with how you approach creating a model. But then also being open to including different features in the model and looking at well. Because there's probably like thousands and thousands of variations of models you can make just with those 6 or 7 features of the data frame. Student 7 acknowledges the multiplicity of possible models and ways to approach the particular data multiple possible models to address a problem serve as a response to awareness of personal assumptions. Through highlighting that prior data knowledge construction approaches yielded incorrect knowledge, the learner became willing to consider differing and multiple data knowledge construction approaches. Interestingly, different outcomes followed a recognition of Dissatisfactions  pertaining to assumptions depending on if the learner was actively constructing a model. Following reflections on assumptions during model 1534 construction, several learners observably changed their model construction approach. In other cases, learners reflected on possible assumptions during post-activity reflections, which did not precede observable changes of model construction. This may have occurred because learners had already constructed complete models and fulfilled their model construction goals. We theorize, then, that processes that generate Dissatisfaction, such as exposing knowledge constructed through assumptions, must occur  during active model construction if the learner is to consider multiple valid data knowledge construction approaches. We define processes of Satisfaction where the learners address and resolve their Dissatisfactions. Often, these Satisfactions link to data epistemological values.  Throughout his collaborative lesson, Student 2 maintained data epistemological values of accuracy and readability throughout his roleplaying, which led to Dissatisfaction in these areas. Students 1 and 2 constructed a finished model which reflected both values. Interestingly, Student 2 would later share that his role-sheet reflected many data epi stemological values that he did not hold before the exercise. However, through constructing a model that reached a Satisfactory model interpretability, he came to also see the value of readability: Yeah how much I valued readability against accuracy when it came to data typically I would want the data to be as fitted or as accurate as possible  but when playing a character that cared more about the story behind the data I found myself also caring about the readability  and relatability to the data which is really cool moving forward. Student 2 first Oriented his data model and knowledge construction objectives around model accuracy and interpretability. Later, he encountered Dissatisfaction  through constructing models that he did not deem as satisfactorily accurate or readable. Eventually, he achieves Satisfaction after constructing a model that he deems both accurate and readable. From our findings, we theorize that learners like Student 2 came to recognize that they can solve a data-driven problem with multiple possible analytical approaches as they reach moments of Satisfaction, through overcoming moments of Dissatisfaction in relation to data epistemological values established during Orientation activities.

In this work, we present three insights on how learners may understand multiple, valid ways of constructing data knowledge within Explorers of Planet Ioio. First, we highlight how enacted data knowledge construction processes shed light on personal knowledge construction processes. The first implication to support learners toward seeing the plurality of data epistemologies in data science pedagogy like Explorers of Planet Ioio is to help learners characterize their existing, underlying data epistemological values. We see how different forms of Dissatisfaction pertaining to the data epistemological values precede the learner exploring alternative and multiple ways of constructing the model and data knowledge. If the learner — or their character —  does not emphasize making a readable model, for example, highlighting a model’s lack of readability may not precede an observable change. Our second implication, then, is to include diverse types of Dissatisfaction, in future pedagogical applications, aligned to different data epistemological values. Finally, the timing of Dissatisfaction also matters; we suggest that learners must be actively constructing a new data model for this Dissatisfaction to impact their process. As we align data epistemological values to moments of  Dissatisfaction and new forms of Satisfaction, we theorize learners came to recognize the possibility of multiple data model and data knowledge construction approaches in our activities. We express several limitations of this pilot work. By our convenience sampling, several participants held associations with interdisciplinary research labs, including the same labs as the “Mission Commander” (who did not supervise the participants). Severa l learners may have had substantial experience reflecting on epistemological development before the study. We acknowledge how our pilot implementation, occurring primarily over a Zoom environment, may have influenced learner behaviors and reactivity effects. Lastly, we put forth how our own ways of constructing knowledge influence limitations in the analysis. Altogether, this work shows promise in cultivating learning environments that value multiple ways of constructing knowledge. As we encourage our learners to imagine how their data epistemological values influence data knowledge construction,  we encourage learning scientists to critically examine and identify which epistemological values influence the learning environments they design, and how these values work toward, or against, a field that values multiple ways to construct knowledge.
