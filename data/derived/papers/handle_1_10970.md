# Emerging Findings on Learning Transfer Between Novel Roles for Working Learners and Learning Workers

**Conference:** ISLS 2024

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
The study of learning transfer from a cognitive and sociocultural perspective has a long history. Nonetheless, there is a need for a deeper understanding and consideration of supporting learning transfer in contexts where working and learning co-occur. This qualitative study describes how learning transfer to novel roles is experienced in a multi-national consulting firm.

Research background The ability to rapidly transition between roles and types of work is becoming increasingly necessary (Dede, 2018). Within a job, employees have to adapt and pivot to novel roles and tasks more quickly as demands shift. To maintain employability, people need to be able to t ransfer learning between novel role types with rela tive ease (Dede, 2018).

Supporting learning transfer, where working and lea rning happen intertwined and involve learning workers and working learners, needs to be better un derstood and considered. Fortunately, this idea is naturally observed in many consulting firms, where workers ad aptively apply skill sets across different types of  projects and roles and are motivated to do so due to the str uctures of consulting career progression. For examp le, it is common for a consultant to be a Quality Assurance T ester one day and then a Training Instructor, Communications Designer, Private Equity Due Diligen ce Analyst, or Project Manager the next. Leveraging interviews with 60 consultants at a multi-national consulting firm, this qualitative study de scribes emergent features of how learning is transferred to novel roles. Conceptual framing Learning transfer is the phenomenon in which learni ng, knowledge, or skills from one context are lever aged in another (Perkins & Salomon, 1992; Bransford, Brown,  & Cocking, 1999). Transfer has been studied for ye ars, primarily as a theory of common element s, and yet there is limited empirical evidence that  it occurs that way. Sociocultural scholars proposed an alternative view  suggesting that learning transfer is a process of negotiation and interpretation between individuals and their social and cultural contexts  (Pea, 1987; Voss, 1987). In this view of learning transfer, learners bring different expe riences and approaches to novel problems, often fac ilitated through social interaction in the sociocultural context in which the learning transfer occurs. More modern revivals into the study of learning transfer have primarily continued to take this sociocultural view and have found that learning transfer is more than common elements, but  instead that it involves the motivations and dispo sitions of the individual learner (Loboto, 2012; Perkins & Sal omon 2012), peers and instructors (Engle, 2012; Zhu  et al 2022), context (Grotzer, 2015), and the recognition that learners may spontaneously transfer learning from a task or prior knowledge and achievement (Chin et al, 2019).

In-progress study The study focuses on employees in a multinational c onsulting firm's North American practice. The sampl e included 60 mid-career and semi-tenured employees. Between them, these employees ha d completed more than 475 transitions to novel roles. The breadth of roles each participant completed is significant, and to the outside world, it would appear to be vastly different jobs.  The study involved 90-minute semi-structured interviews and included questions about participants' careers, how they learn in a work context, and specific transitions between new and different roles. Participants were asked hi gh-level questions about their role transitions, questions about particular transitions, and hypothetical future tra nsitions (Weiss, 1994). Although not wholly, the a nalysis was primarily based on a grounded theory approach  (Eme rson et al., 2011). Open coding allowed for emergen t features of successful novel role transition. Axile  coding led to those being clustered before selecti ve coding revealed themes (see table 1).
