# How Individual Preparation Before Collaboration Shapes Learners’ Verbal Communication Patterns: An Epistemic Network Analysis​

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This study examines how individual preparation (IP) before collaboration influences learners’ verbal communication during collaborative learning. By comparing immediate collaboration with collaboration following IP, this study employed the Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) to investigate the differences in learners’ verbal discourse patterns. The results indicate that while IP before collaboration fosters more structured communication, marked by enhanced task coordination and responsiveness, it reduces spontaneous negotiation. The findings suggest a trade-off between structured preparedness and flexibility, with implications for optimizing collaborative learning design.

## Introduction

Collaborative learning often depends on both effect ive communication and the integration of diverse id eas (Wilczenski et al., 2001). Unstructured group inter actions can lead to inefficiencies in information exchange and knowledge co-construction (Barron, 2003). Individual preparation (IP) before collabo ration, grounded in the Preparation for Future Collaboration (PFC) paradigm  (Lam & Kapur, 2018), enables learners to activate prior knowledge and structure their ideas before collaboration (Chen et al., 2024; Mende et al., 2021). Although IP has been shown to improve argument quality and task foc us, it may also constrain flexibility in integratin g peers’ perspectives (Tsovaltzi et al., 2015). This study investigates through Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) whether individual preparation shapes key aspects of verbal  communication, by comparing groups that collaborat e immediately with those who engage in IP before collaboration.

## Method

Forty-four university students were paired into 22 dyads to complete a product ideation task under randomize d conditions: 1) Immediate Collaboration: Dyads begandiscussing without delay for 7 minutes; 2) Collaboration following Individual Preparation (IP): Participants first had 2 minutes of silent individual preparation before a 5minute discussion. All sessions were conducted in a university laboratory, with participants individually seated at computers and accessing a shared online interface. The participants’ verbal interactions were audio-recorded, transcribed, and manually verified. Content analysis was conducted with fully annotated utterances as the unit of analysis. A coding scheme, adapted from established interacti on analysis models, captured eight task-oriented behaviors (i.e., offer ideas, ask for help, respond, agree, coordinate task, and check progress) (Wang et al., 2020), which is widely applied to investigate collaborative behavioral patterns (Lan et al., 2012; Lucas et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2022). Two experts independently coded 15% of the data (Co hen’s Kappa = 0.872), after which GPT-4o coded the remaining discourse. Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) was applied using a four-line sliding window to capture dynamic co-occurrences, with Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) enabling condition comparisons, and Mann-Whitney U tests assessing statistical differences.

## Findings

The ENA visualizations revealed marked differences in communication across conditions (see Figure 1). In the immediate collaboration, “Offer Ideas” (A1), “Disco ver Uncertainty” (A6), and especially “Negotiate” (A5) cluster near the control centroid, often co-occurring with off-topic discussion (A9). In contrast,  the individual preparation (IP) before collaboration condition is marked by more organized communication, with “Respond” (A3), “Agree” (A4), “Task Coordination” (A7), and “Task Progress Regulation” (A8) prominently positioned on the right. The statistically significant divergence  on the horizontal axis (MR1) of the ENA space (p <  0.05) underscores the trade-off, where immediate collaboration is characterized  by stronger negotiation (A5), whereas the group with individual preparation shows higher levels of task coordination (A7) and progress regulation (A8). ## Discussion and conclusion

The findings suggest that individual preparation pr ior to collaboration promotes structured, task-oriented communication, which enhances coordination and clar ity, allowing learners to organize their contributi ons effectively (Chen et al., 2024; Mende et al., 2021). However, t he reduced frequency of negotiation and adaptive dialogue in the individual preparation (IP) condition indicates that such preparation may also limit the exploration of diverse perspectives (Korde & Paulus, 2017). For educators, these r esults underscore the need to balance preparatory activities with opportunities for open,  flexible discussion, potentially by incorporating reflection or integration phases post-preparation. Future research should extend these in sights by integrating multimodal data to capture additional cognitive and affective dimen sions of collaborative learning. This study advance s our understanding of the trade-offs inherent in collaborative design and provides practical guidance for optimizing learning environments.
