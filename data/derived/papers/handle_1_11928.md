# Adaptive Dashboard System for Reflective Assessment in Knowledge Building Pedagogy

**Conference:** ISLS 2025

## Abstract & Introduction

### Abstract
This poster examines students' evaluations of the analytics and visualization tool, Software KBAT, designed to facilitate reflective self-assessments within Knowledge Building communities supported by Knowledge Forum. Adopting a design-based research methodology, data were collected through a survey assessing students’ perceptions of the tool's value. The study involved 42 students from an educational research course at the University of Granada. This poster highlights students’ view on KBAT and identifies opportunities for improvement.

## Introduction

Knowledge Building is an educational model that empowers students to collaborate effectively in enhancing their understanding of a subject (Bereiter  & Scardamalia,  2016). Students typically engage in hybrid environments supported by Knowledge Forum (KF) to transcend clas sroom boundaries. During collaboration, students’ question, inquiry, and refine ideas shared in the KF to address authentic knowledge problems (Gutiérrez-Braojos et al., 2024). Scientific literature provides evidence of the benefits of concurrent and reflective assessment sessions in the knowledge-building process. These sessions help generate a metadiscourse on how to coordinate individual efforts to improve shared knowledge (Tong & Chan, 2023). Researchers and practitioners have collaboratively developed technologies to assess collaborative know ledge building in KF  (Zhu & Kim, 2017). While several widely used tools exist, we think that, staying true to KB principles, there is always room for improvement. While communities go through similar stages (de Jong, 2020), they differ in their initial conditions and the ways in which they navigate these stages. Therefore, we think this margin lies in creating software that does not  provide predefined dashboards but allows users to choose among them to create a dashboard tailored to the circumstances the educator deems most appropriate for the communi ty. This poster explores whether KBAT supports the progression of discourse within the Knowledge Forum, as well as students’ perceptions of its practical  use and how these perceptions can inform future improvements.

## Method

Participants were 42 students enrolled in an educational research program at the University of Granada, Spain. The KB was implemented in a hybrid format, supporte d by Knowledge Forum (KF) and reflective assessment dashboards generated by the KBAT (Figure 1  & 2). Dashboard data enabled students to reflect on their participation, challenges, and progress, proposing improvements and recognizing shifts toward collaboration and community leadership.  On the one hand, to examine whether KBAT fosters di scourse progressio n, student contributions on KF  were analyzed across three cycles using a coding scheme.  On another hand, d ata were collected through a Likert scale (four dimensions: awareness, motivation, ethics, technical aspects; adapted from Gutiérrez-Braojos, 2023) and three open-ended questions to identify strengths supporting KB  and suggest improvements. ## Results

On one and, the analysis of the quality of the disc ourse produced by the students shows a improvement in their contributions throughout the course  (Figure 3). On another hand, the analysis of the Likert respons es indicates that students rated the dashboard positively across all dimensions: it facilitates awareness, does not interfere with motivation, meets ethical standards, and satisfies technical us ability criteria (Figure 4). Open-ended responses corroborated these positive findings, highlighting the software's value; some students even recommende d its use from the beginning of the course alongside KF  “At first, we thought it was like other courses, whe re you just do assignments that repeat content, but here what real ly matters is whether you collaborate and contribut e to the community. We didn’t really understand that at the beginning. It was hard for u s to learn, and both the teacher and KBAT really helped us see what we were being assessed on. That’s why it would be better to use KBAT from the start of the course”.

## Conclusions

Students showed a clear improvement in the quality of their contributions throughout the course. KBAT proved to be a valuable tool in supporting this progress, helping students monitor their participation, disco urse patterns, and conceptual development. Survey results indicate d that students perceived the dashboard positively across all dimensions—awareness, motivation, ethics, and technical usabil ity—while open-ended re sponses reinforced these findings, highlighting its usefulness in maki ng assessment criteria more transparent. These outc omes align with previous research on the benefits of reflective assessment (e.g., Tong & Chan, 2023; Gutiérrez-Braojos et al. 2023). Although KBAT already supports Knowledge Buildin g goals effectively, students emphasized the importance of introducing it earlier in the course to maximize its impact.
