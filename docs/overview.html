Chapter 1: The Arc of Mindshare

How Ten Years of Research Reshaped the Learning Sciences

Over the past decade, the Learning Sciences did not merely absorb new technologies—it fundamentally renegotiated its relationship with tools, equity, and human collaboration. Three transformative arcs emerge from 5,402 proceedings papers: first, an unwavering bedrock in Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (CSCL) and dialogic scaffolding; second, a seismic reorientation following 2020 expanding inquiry into racial justice, translanguaging, and culturally sustaining pedagogies; and third, the dramatic post-2023 surge in Generative AI and Large Language Models, which rapidly transitioned from exploratory probes into a dominant investigative frontier.

🔬 How this data was labelled & charted: Exact step-by-step methodology
🏷️ Part 1: Exact Step-by-Step Labelling Process
Step 1 Corpus Ingestion & Lexical Scope

Extracts titles, abstracts, and author-supplied keywords across all 5,402 proceedings papers published between 2016 and 2026 across ICLS and CSCL conference volumes. Records are normalized and indexed in SQLite FTS5 for zero-cost lexical scanning.

Step 2 Deterministic Pattern Matching (Phase 1 / 0-Cost Extraction)

Executes compiled regular expressions with strict word boundaries (\b...\b), synonym dictionaries, and negative lookaheads to eliminate false positives. The ontology spans 5 distinct dimensions:

  • TPACK - Technological Knowledge (TK): Matches explicit tool constructs including Generative AI/LLM (e.g. ChatGPT, GPT-4, LLM, Generative AI), Immersive Tech (VR, AR, virtual reality), Educational Robotics, Intelligent Tutoring Systems, and Collaboration Media.
  • TPACK - Pedagogical Knowledge (PK): Matches instructional paradigms including Collaborative Learning (CSCL, co-design), Inquiry-Based Learning, Scaffolding & Fading, Problem-Based Learning, Playful Learning, and Formative Assessment.
  • TPACK - Content Knowledge (CK): Categorizes disciplinary subject areas into STEM/Science, Mathematics, Computer Science & Computational Thinking, Language & Literacy, Arts, and Socio-Scientific Issues.
  • Educational Settings: Resolves learning environments into Formal K-12, Higher Education, Informal/Museums, Makerspaces & Community Hubs, Workplace, and Remote/Hybrid digital environments.
  • 22 Granular Named Conceptualisations: Categorized into 8 Broad Theoretical Families (e.g., Cognitive & Constructivist, Collaborative & Dialogic, Sociocultural & Activity Theory, Embodied & Sensorimotor, Design & Participatory, Critical & Culturally Sustaining, Affective & Motivational, Computational & Learning Analytics).
Step 3 Multi-Tiered Claim Centrality Scoring (Scale 1–5)

Every assigned label is evaluated for evidentiary depth and scored from 1 to 5:

  • Score 5 (Primary Driver / Core Claim): The construct appears explicitly in the paper's title or is declared as the central research question.
  • Score 4 (Major Investigative Focus): The construct is articulated in the abstract's problem statement or thesis as an active empirical variable or outcome.
  • Score 3 (Methodological Intervention / Tool): The construct operates as an active mechanism, instrument, or designed curriculum artifact.
  • Score 2 (Comparative or Secondary Mention): Referenced as a comparison condition, secondary literature bridge, or supporting theoretical lens.
  • Score 1 (Contextual Backdrop): Cited strictly as situational framing or historical preamble.
Step 4 Relational Fact Table Persistence

Records are persisted in paper_labels(paper_id, taxonomy, label, broad_family, centrality_score, match_source). Phase 2 Roadmap: Ambiguous papers or multi-clause theoretical claims can be verified via batched LLM prompts (15–20 papers per structured JSON schema payload) without modifying database structures.

📈 Part 2: Unique Charting Processes
Process 1 Multi-Mode Metric Calculations

The longitudinal trajectory chart supports three mathematically distinct analytical perspectives toggled dynamically in the client:

Mode 1 (Paper Count): Y(i, t) = Count(label_i, year_t)
Mode 2 (100% Proportional Share): Y(i, t) = [Count(label_i, year_t) / SUM_j(Count(label_j, year_t))] × 100%
Mode 3 (% of Total Conference): Y(i, t) = [Count(label_i, year_t) / N_conference_papers(year_t)] × 100%
  • Absolute Paper Count: Highlights gross annual publication volume using unstacked smooth lines.
  • 100% Proportional Share: Employs stacked area interpolation (stack: 'Total') where all selected categories total exactly 100% in every year, revealing zero-sum mindshare displacement between paradigms.
  • % of Total Conference: Evaluates true disciplinary mindshare penetration relative to changing conference sizes over 10 years.
Process 2 Dynamic Palette Resolution & Visual Hierarchy

Colors are dynamically resolved from a broad theoretical family color map, ensuring chromatic coherence when switching between dimensions. Series use smooth cubic Bézier curves (smooth: 0.25) with responsive hover emphasis (focus: 'series'), allowing instant comparison of competing theoretical paradigms.

Chapter 2: The Intellectual Topography

Mapping the Disciplinary Core: Motor Themes, Bridges, and Frontiers

Every living academic discipline possesses both stable intellectual anchors and speculative borderlands. By adapting Michel Callon's co-word strategic framework to our repository, we plot Cross-Topic Centrality (X) against Internal Research Density (Y) to reveal how concepts function in practice: Motor Themes (such as Collaborative Inquiry and Scaffolding) command high volume and connect across disciplines; Niche Themes (such as specialized laboratory sensor setups) display intense internal cohesion; and Basic Bridges (such as Communities of Practice and Conceptual Change) serve as transversal theoretical anchors.
Quadrant I: Motor Themes (Green) High Density & High Centrality. Core, mature, highly interconnected research drivers.
Quadrant II: Niche Themes (Purple) High Density & Low Centrality. Specialized domain investigations.
Quadrant III: Basic & Transversal (Blue) Low Density & High Centrality. Foundational bridges across all tracks.
Quadrant IV: Emerging / Declining (Orange) Low Density & Low Centrality. Nascent experiments or early interventions.
🔬 How Centrality and Density were calculated & charted: Step-by-step Callon strategic matrix
📐 Part 1: Exact Metric Calculation & Labelling Process
Step 1 Co-Occurrence Matrix Construction

Constructs a symmetric co-occurrence graph across all 22 named conceptualisations and topical labels from papers co-tagged in paper_labels. Each edge weight C(i, j) equals the number of proceedings papers where construct i and construct j co-occur.

Step 2 Callon Centrality Formulation (X-Axis: External Bridging)

Centrality reflects the intensity and diversity of external ties between a construct and all other theoretical constructs in the conference:

Centrality(i) = (1 / |K - {i}|) × SUM_{j ≠ i} [ C(i, j) / sqrt(Count(i) × Count(j)) ]

Normalized using Salton's cosine coefficient to prevent high-volume terms from artificially inflating their centrality. High centrality identifies transversal concepts that bridge multiple pedagogical settings, tools, and subject domains.

Step 3 Callon Density Formulation (Y-Axis: Internal Cohesion)

Density reflects the internal cohesion, maturity, and depth of investigation within a construct's specialized sub-domain:

Density(i) = [ SUM_{k ∈ Domain(i)} C(i, k) / Count(i) ] × log(1 + Count(i))

High density identifies self-sustaining research traditions with specialized methodologies, high repeat citation patterns, and dedicated problem-solving apparatuses.

Step 4 Objective Median Partitioning into 4 Quadrants

Instead of subjective thresholds, the grid is partitioned at the empirical medians of all plotted themes: X_median = median(Centrality) and Y_median = median(Density).

  • Quadrant I (Top-Right): Motor Themes (High Centrality, High Density) — Core intellectual engines driving the discipline (e.g. Collaborative Inquiry, Scaffolding).
  • Quadrant II (Top-Left): Niche Themes (Low Centrality, High Density) — Specialized, internally sophisticated areas with bounded disciplinary transfer (e.g. Sensor-based labs).
  • Quadrant III (Bottom-Right): Basic & Transversal (High Centrality, Low Density) — Foundational cross-cutting pillars anchoring multiple inquiries (e.g. Communities of Practice).
  • Quadrant IV (Bottom-Left): Emerging or Declining (Low Centrality, Low Density) — Nascent exploratory ideas, early pilot interventions, or waning methodologies.
📊 Part 2: Unique Charting Processes
Process 1 Volumetric Bubble Projection & Quadrant Color Encoding

Each construct is rendered as an interactive scatter bubble where coordinate positions map strictly to [Centrality, Density]. Symbol diameters scale non-linearly with total 10-year publication volume:

SymbolSize(i) = max(12, min(46, 8 + 3.2 × sqrt(Count(i))))

Bubbles are color-coded by quadrant: Emerald Green (Motor), Royal Purple (Niche), Cobalt Blue (Basic), and Amber Orange (Emerging).

Process 2 Dynamic Median Crosshairs & Paper Inspector Linking

Dynamic ECharts markLine crosshairs intersect precisely at the median centrality and density values. Clicking any bubble triggers an event listener opening the slide-out Paper Inspector, querying /api/overview/papers?concept=[name] to display all underlying empirical papers with APA citations.

🔬 How author collaboration and cohorts were modelled & charted: Step-by-step edge and layout pipeline
👥 Part 1: Exact Data Extraction & Author Labelling Process
Step 1 Author Disambiguation & APA 7th Normalization

Scans all 8,884 unique authors across 5,402 papers. Author strings are stripped of punctuation variances, academic titles, and inconsistent middle initials, and transformed into standard APA 7th edition display names (Lastname, Initial(s).). This unifies repeat contributors across 11 conference years under consistent identity anchors (e.g. mapping Joshua A. Danish and Joshua Danish to Danish, J.).

Step 2 Relational Self-Join for Co-Authorship Edges

Performs an undirected self-join across all authors within each paper:

SELECT pa1.author_id, pa2.author_id, COUNT(*) as weight
FROM paper_authors pa1 JOIN paper_authors pa2
ON pa1.paper_id = pa2.paper_id AND pa1.author_id < pa2.author_id
GROUP BY pa1.author_id, pa2.author_id;

This extracts exactly N × (N - 1) / 2 dyadic collaboration pairs per multi-authored publication, producing 60,003 unique co-authorship instances across 2016–2026.

Step 3 Founding Cohort Attribution (Earliest Publication Year)

To classify authors into historical cohorts, the database queries each author's earliest publication year across the entire proceedings:

FirstYear(Author) = MIN(p.year) WHERE pa.author_id = Author.id

This assigns each scholar to their foundational cohort—from the 2016 founding generation (e.g. Hmelo-Silver, C. E., Danish, J., Puntambekar, S.) through consecutive waves of newly inducted learning scientists up to the 2026 cohort.

Step 4 Repeat-Collaboration Threshold Pruning

Applies dynamic minimum weight thresholds (2+, 3+, 4+ co-authored papers) to filter ephemeral single-instance edges, highlighting the sustained community infrastructure and collaborative alliances across research laboratories.

🕸️ Part 2: Unique Charting Processes
Process 1 Proportional Node Sizing & 11-Step Chromatic Palette

Node sizes are scaled tightly between 5px and 14px using a sublinear square-root formulation:

BaseSize(Author) = min(14, max(5, round(4 + 1.3 × sqrt(TotalCollaborations))))

Each dot is assigned a crisp white stroke halo (borderWidth: 1.5px, borderColor: '#ffffff') and colored strictly according to its first publication cohort along a chronological 11-color spectrum (from 2016 Royal Navy to 2026 Fuchsia Magenta).

Process 2 Dynamic Zoom Scaling & Stable Coordinate Freezing

To eliminate visual obstruction during interactive navigation, a debounced graphRoam event listener scales dots dynamically:

DynamicSymbolSize = clamp(3, 18, round(BaseSize × Zoom^(-0.45)))
  • Zooming Out (Z < 0.75): Dot sizes stay clearly visible rather than vanishing into sub-pixel points, while low-degree author labels are hidden to avoid overlapping text smears.
  • Zooming In (Z ≥ 0.75): Base sizes are damped so circles do not balloon into giant discs that obscure neighboring connections, and all author labels are rendered cleanly.
  • Zero Jitter: Current settled coordinates from nodeData.getItemLayout(i) are frozen (fixed: true, layoutAnimation: false), ensuring that zooming in and out never triggers a jarring force-simulation reset.

🔬 Citation parsing methodology, multi-era timeline & charting pipeline
📚 Part 1: Exact Citation Parsing & Lineage Extraction Process
Step 1 Reference Bibliography Extraction

Parses the structured ## References sections of ingested proceedings markdown files. Regex tokenizers match author surnames, publication years (e.g. (2018), (2021)), paper titles, and conference publication identifiers (In Proceedings of ICLS, CSCL, ISLS).

Step 2 Internal Cross-Citation Entity Matching

Matches referenced titles and primary authors against canonical proceedings records in papers. When a reference points to a paper published in the ISLS proceedings, a directed record is established:

DirectedEdge = (citing_paper_id ➔ cited_paper_id, temporal_lag = citing_year - cited_year)

This resolves 1,840 validated internal cross-citations connecting proceedings papers across time. Tier 3 Roadmap: Earlier historical conference volumes (2016–2022) with unparsed bibliographies can be systematically enriched using automated PDF reference extraction scripts without incurring full-paper token costs.

Step 3 Temporal Horizon Discretization into 4 Publication Eras

To map the generational movement of concepts, the decade is discretized into four distinct historical eras:

  • Era 1: Foundations (2016–2018): Established foundational scaffolding paradigms, early CSCL scripts, maker constructionism, and open knowledge inquiry.
  • Era 2: Expansion (2019–2021): Sociocultural turn, translanguaging, equity-oriented design, and initial multimodal interaction analytics.
  • Era 3: Synthesis (2022–2024): Design-based implementation research, AI-supported collaborative discourse, and sensorimotor analytics.
  • Era 4: Frontiers (2025–2026): Generative AI, agentic inquiry scaffolds, and socio-scientific futures.
Step 4 Seminal Works Identification (APA 7th Format)

Ranks papers by in-degree citation frequency within the proceedings corpus. Top publications are indexed and rendered in complete APA 7th format (e.g., Lastname, F., & Lastname, B. (Year). Title. In Proceedings of...) with direct links to the Paper Inspector.

🔬 How the Tableau data pipeline & analytical grains were engineered: Step-by-step export architecture
💾 Part 1: Exact Data Engineering & Export Labelling Process
Step 1 Multi-Grain Relational Denormalization

To eliminate complex many-to-many SQL joins in Tableau and external BI tools, the repository generates 5 dedicated analytical export files tailored to specific visual grains:

  • Master Paper Grain (tableau_papers_master.csv): 1 row per paper (5,402 rows). Pre-calculates primary author, APA 7th co-authors list, conference track, publication year, and boolean indicator flags for high-centrality TPACK constructs.
  • Tall Label Fact Grain (tableau_paper_labels_tall.csv): 1 row per Paper × Label co-occurrence (32,575 rows). Stores taxonomy dimensions, broad families, and 1–5 claim centrality scores, enabling flexible slicing without data inflation.
  • Co-Authorship Edge Grain (tableau_author_network.csv): 1 row per undirected author pair (54,951 rows). Contains APA-formatted author names, publication year, and pair weight.
  • Citation Edge Grain (tableau_citation_links.csv): 1 row per directed cross-citation (1,840 rows). Contains citing and cited paper identifiers, author bylines, and publication years.
  • Strategic Matrix Grain (tableau_density_matrix.csv): 1 row per theoretical construct (59 rows). Contains pre-calculated Callon Centrality, Density, Paper Count, and assigned Quadrant.
Step 2 UTF-8 Sanitization & Strict RFC 4180 CSV Conformance

All abstract excerpts, titles, and author names are escaped according to RFC 4180 specifications—handling multiline abstracts, embedded quotes, semicolons, and special characters. Author strings strictly match APA 7th formatting rules across all CSV outputs.

📊 Part 2: Unique Charting Processes Enabled in Tableau / BI
Process 1 Dynamic Drilldowns via Star Schema Linking

In Tableau, linking tableau_papers_master.csv to tableau_paper_labels_tall.csv on paper_id creates an optimal star schema. Analysts can build dynamic parameter actions, hierarchical treemaps (Broad Family ➔ Granular Label), and 100% stacked area trajectories with instant dimension switching.

Process 2 Origin-Destination Citation Flows & Node Graphs

Importing tableau_citation_links.csv into network visualizers (e.g. Gephi, Tableau Network Extensions, or Python NetworkX) enables multi-tiered directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) and Sankey flow diagrams showing how early foundational methods migrated into current generative AI and learning analytics pipelines.
